TL;DR

  • What it is: Sapporo's owned, members-only social site for Yebisu beer fans, set up as a fictional "town" named after Ebisu, the Tokyo district that took its name from the beer. It pre-opened on 25 Feb 2022 (Yebisu's birthday), added community features on 16 Sep 2022, and reached about 150,000 registered "residents" and 65,000 posts by early 2025. It is still active in Nov 2026 (Sapporo, 2025; site).
  • Why people come back: a mostly 50-plus core audience gets a calm, off-topic-friendly place to post daily life and photos, plus daily hooks: an omikuji lottery that can win a case of beer, monthly login and like badges, point and badge ranks that unlock merchandise, and seasonal hashtag campaigns. Brand staff, from the brand manager (the "mayor") to the head brewer, post under their own names.
  • What the company feeds it: the "staff talk BAR" (担当語りBAR), the YEBISU BEER COLLEGE online lectures (seven editions by Sep 2026) with quiz "degree" badges, co-creation votes that become real products (craft goods; a beer cocktail served in every YEBISU BAR), and a volunteer executive committee that meets brand staff about monthly.
  • Biggest weakness: engagement depends heavily on incentives. Badges are handed out for a like or a single comment, which produces one-word replies ("楽しみ", "looking forward to it"). The headline feature this catalogue first listed, members mapping bars that serve Yebisu, is a minor corner: 647 posts in three years against more than 100,000 chat and photo posts. Sapporo has published no business-impact figures, and no member count since Feb 2025.
  • The one lesson: seed the space with your hottest fans first, let them talk about their lives rather than only your product, and have real, named employees show up. Then use daily lightweight rituals to turn a campaign visit into a habit. Sapporo's Jan 2023 daily-draw lottery brought in ~18,000 sign-ups in a month and was designed for retention, not just registration (coorum case study).

What the community is

YEBISU BEER TOWN (ybt.sapporobeer.jp) is a free, age-gated (20+) community website. Signing up creates a "YEBISU Members" ID, which also covers Sapporo's newsletter and sticker-point campaign site (Sendenkaigi, 2022). It runs on coorum, the fan-community SaaS from Asobica (page assets are served from community.dl-asobica.com), and is operated by Sapporo's Yebisu brand group with Dentsu Digital as the agency running it (Dentsu Digital, 2023).

The site layout in Nov 2026 (site top):

  • Talk Lounge (トークラウンジ): Free Talk (46,103 posts) and YEBISU Photo (56,592 posts). Before the Feb 2025 renewal it was "Minna no 'En' Kaijo" (みんなの"縁"会場, a pun on 宴 "banquet" and 縁 "bond"), with themed rooms for photos, gourmet and cooking, travel, lifestyle, culture and "Yebisu mania".
  • Staff talk BAR (担当語りBAR): posts from the "mayor" (the brand manager), the brewer, the developer, the "restaurant support captain", the designer and Sapporo's social-media team.
  • Yebisu shops (ヱビスなお店): member-posted tips about places that serve Yebisu (647 posts), plus YEBISU BAR news and Sapporo's certified "Zeppin Yebisu" bars.
  • Campaigns, readings, Beer College, and the "YEBISU LOG 2026" badge program.

Staff run the town as fictional municipal departments: 住民課 (resident affairs, the operations office), 企画課 (planning) and 街づくり課 (town planning). There is a mayor (町長). Fukuyoshi Takashi held the post from launch until 16 Sep 2023; brand manager Okii Takako has held it since (mayor-change notice).

Unofficial spaces: no fan-run subreddit, Discord or Facebook group was found. The Arctic Shift Reddit archive requires a subreddit or author for searches, and Brave turned up no Yebisu community subreddit, so this is "no evidence found". Fan activity outside the site takes the form of member-organized offline meetups announced inside it, and individual blogs (see Member voice). Sapporo runs a separate company-wide fan community, SAPPORO STAR COMPANY (since Dec 2021, 4 staff), which is not Yebisu-specific (Fanbase Company note, 2025).

Origin and strategy

  • The problem: "Beer had become distant from customers" (Mantani Hiroyuki, Yebisu brand group). The market is an oligopoly of four big brewers that compete on taste and mass advertising, so functional value has become a commodity. Sapporo's answer was to build emotional value (Nikkei Cross Trend, 2024). Yebisu sales fell year on year from 2017 through 2023 (Nikkei Cross Trend, 2025).
  • Asking first: in summer 2021 the brand surveyed its newsletter subscribers and received "thousands" of passionate replies. The most common request was to connect with other Yebisu fans and share how they enjoy it. Specific asks: bar recommendations, which foods other fans pair with Yebisu, and hearing from the people who make it (Dentsu Digital; coorum).
  • Champion: Fukuyoshi Takashi, senior media planning manager, who called it "my half-delusional idea" (Yebisu Magazine, 2023, Wayback). Brand manager Okii Takako backed it, drawing on earlier in-person fan gatherings where strangers bonded over Yebisu.
  • Research-led design: Dentsu Digital applied its "Fan Farming CX" framework. Free-text research produced 98 fan origin stories ("narrative journeys") and a 200-page strategy "compass". The key finding: Yebisu loyalty is inherited, from parents and relatives or from senior colleagues at work. The concept word "ご縁" (bond, connection) came out of this research (Dentsu Digital).
  • Business goals stated at launch: 200,000 YEBISU Members, a 20% monthly active user ratio on BEER TOWN, and newsletter open rates kept above 40% (Sendenkaigi, Dec 2022). Later framing: keep heavy users buying, and mine their zero-party data (their stated motives, original Yebisu experiences and values) to design marketing for light and mid users. Resident voices are used in three places: (1) planning inside the community, (2) campaigns outside it, and (3) tracking loyalty change through a sign-up questionnaire plus an annual "resident awareness survey" (Nikkei Cross Trend, 2024; summarized by countand1, 2024).
  • It sits inside Sapporo's 2022+ medium-term theme of "fusing real and digital". The physical side is YEBISU BREWERY TOKYO, opened Apr 2024, and the YEBISU BAR chain (Shibukei, 2022).

Trajectory. Registrations grew quickly: 70k at six months, then roughly +10k per quarter through 2024. Growth then slowed: +10k in the four months to Jan 2025, and no figure since. Posting accelerated from ~10k total (Sep 2023) to ~3,400 posts a month through 2024. Estimate: since the Feb 2025 renewal the live rooms have added at least ~38k posts, about 1,700–2,500 a month depending on how many posts sit in the archived rooms. That suggests posting has plateaued or eased, though it is still active. The engaged core is small next to the registered base. Big staff announcements draw roughly 150–250 reactions from ~150k "residents", about 0.1–0.2% (estimate). The 20% MAU target has never been reported against. Residents skew 50+, men and women (ITmedia).

What keeps people coming back

  1. A place to be yourself, not just a brand board. Fukuyoshi deliberately avoided posting about beer so he wouldn't "frame" what residents said. Members post castle visits, convenience-store ice cream, even rivals' beer (Yebisu Magazine part 1). Dentsu's Takagi credits this freedom for the growth: "if you could only talk about Yebisu… post counts could have trended down." After an earthquake, the first place one resident came to ask "is everyone okay?" was the town (coorum).
  2. Daily rituals. An omikuji modeled on the real Ebisu shrine next to Sapporo's headquarters can be drawn once a day. It shows a fortune, a food pairing and Yebisu trivia, and the rare "atari daikichi" wins a case of beer (1 per month, 10 in 2026) (omikuji notice). The Jan 2023 New Year lottery showed a different rabbit illustration, in the style of the Chōjū-giga scrolls, every day, so even losing draws were shareable. "People came for the lottery, looked at other posts while there… and for some it became a habit" (Fukuyoshi, coorum).
  3. Badges, points and ranks. Badges for activity and limited events have existed since Sep 2022. A point program launched in Sep 2023, lapsed, and "returned" with the Feb 2025 renewal (PR TIMES 2023; renewal notice). In 2026 it became YEBISU LOG 2026: one badge per month for logging in and one for liking someone else's post (up to 11 of each), flavor badges for posting each Yebisu variant with its hashtag, check-in badges for visiting YEBISU BAR or the brewery, plus event badges. Ranks run Silver (3 badges, phone wallpaper), Gold (15, sticker), Platinum (35, charm) and Diamond (45, tote bag). Being featured in the weekly pickup email earns a "featured post" badge (YEBISU LOG 2026). Fukuyoshi says badges also start conversations ("you went to that event?") and that he collects them himself.
  4. Seasonal prompts. Early on there were few posts, so staff set a hashtag theme each season (#桜 cherry blossoms, #花火 fireworks, #夏ヱビス2026 summer). Residents then invented their own, such as "#ハー部" (herb club) for home gardening (Yebisu Magazine part 1).
  5. Identity. Members are "residents" (住民), there is a mayor, staff hold municipal titles, and in-jokes follow ("it's more a city than a town now," Okii wrote at 140k).
  6. Parasocial ties to named staff. The BAR puts the brand manager, head brewer Aritomo, developer Endo and "restaurant support captain" Makihara in front of members. Fukuyoshi values being able to explain decisions "in my own words, with warmth" instead of as an official corporate statement (coorum). Residents send farewell and welcome messages when the mayor changes.
  7. Peer welcome and real friendships. "Old-timers" like newcomers' posts (ITmedia p.2). Residents meet at the YEBISU BEER HOLIDAY festival, and some organize their own regional meetups ("Hokuriku branch" in Kanazawa, Nov 2024; a "second unofficial town meetup in Kanazawa," Jun 2025) (meetup post).
  8. Voice that leads to action. After the Jan 2024 Noto earthquake, residents asked to help, and a posting campaign that sent donations to the affected area followed (ITmedia p.2).

The content engine

  • YEBISU BEER COLLEGE: members-only lectures. They began as live online events in June 2022, including a live link to a buyer choosing hops in Germany for the second edition, and a stream from a YEBISU BAR. Later editions are articles and videos with a "degree exam" and badges: the 5th (Sep 2024) toured the new brewery, and the 7th (Sep 2026) interviewed chefs from the four finalist restaurants in the Beer Gourmet Grand Prix (PR TIMES 2024; college post). Analysis showed Yebisu fans prefer "high-context," substantial articles over light ones (Dentsu Digital).
  • "Readings" and the Yebisu Library: brand history, factory visits, and articles from a mook co-produced with Pen magazine.
  • Staff posts in the BAR: product launches (e.g. brewery-exclusive beers in Aug and Sep 2026), events, the opening of the YEBISU BEER HALL TOKYO in Ginza (Oct 2026). Posting cadence in autumn 2026 was roughly weekly (site top).
  • Co-creation as content: the "Yebisu monozukuri project" with Tokyo craft workshops drew 4,809 votes for items costing over ¥10,000 each, and the winners were sold (coorum). A beer-cocktail vote picked a drink served at all YEBISU BARs from 11 Nov to the end of Dec 2024. Members submitted YEBISU BAR menu ideas in 2026.
  • Member-made content is curated into the weekly pickup email and onto the top page. A blogger studying for a beer certification ran a "maniac beer quiz" thread in the Talk room every 2–3 days (Hatena blog, Jun 2025).
  • Most publishing goes through the agency and operations office (住民課, 企画課). There is no podcast or YouTube series dedicated to the community (no evidence found).

People, programs and governance

  • Staffing: two Sapporo people, including Mantani, handle daily post checks and patrol the site (ITmedia p.1). Dentsu Digital provides strategy, building and operations. The two companies held a weekly meeting that "sometimes ran over five hours" (Dentsu Digital). The platform vendor is Asobica's coorum. Fukuyoshi picked it over off-the-shelf tools so the vendor would "evolve with us".
  • Executive committee (実行委員, superusers): first recruited from Beer College attendees before the Sep 2022 launch, by "directly approaching the most fanatical fans". About 30 members over the first year (Yebisu Magazine part 2), around 20 active, meeting brand staff about monthly (countand1 citing Nikkei). They propose campaigns, post actively, and give feedback on tone and manner. Their first offline meetup was at YEBISU BEER HOLIDAY in Aug 2023. Recruiting opened again in Sep 2024 (room notice list). The visible perks are recognition, access and meetups; no cash or free-beer perks were found.
  • Rules: community guidelines, revised 29 Jan 2026 to add rules on posting about living creatures and on links unrelated to Sapporo or Yebisu, and to spell out how violations are handled (notice). Users can mute each other.
  • Feedback loop: executive committee meetings, resident proposals (the Noto donation, the bar-tip feature itself), votes, and annual surveys. Mantani's lesson after year one: "Rather than looking at data, look at the fans in front of you" (ITmedia p.3).

Growth loops

  • Seed hot, then widen. Mantani first targeted only "the most passionate fans" because "if there's no heat, widening the base just adds dormant members." Content from that core was then pushed outward (ITmedia p.2).
  • Owned touchpoints: banners on "Kototoki" (the sticker-point prize site) and the Yebisu Magazine, and the shared YEBISU Members ID.
  • Real-world to digital: event attendees and YEBISU BREWERY TOKYO visitors are added as LINE friends and then steered into the town (ITmedia p.2). The brewery drew ~570,000 visitors in its first two years (alwayslovebeer, 2026). Check-in badges close the loop back into the site.
  • Prize campaigns built for stickiness, like the daily New Year lottery (~18k sign-ups in a month), a mystery hunt for the first anniversary, and a site "treasure hunt" at the 2025 renewal.
  • Not much outward word of mouth: content is gated behind registration and an age check, so search and social sharing reach is limited. No referral program was found.

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: little. Sapporo has published no retention, purchase-frequency, LTV or MAU numbers for residents. The 20% MAU target has never been reported against.
  • Company claims: the community is "working very well as a place to hear real voices" (Fukuyoshi). Registrations grew "faster than the original plan" (Yebisu Magazine part 2). Insights from residents' origin stories ("first beer with my parents on my 20th birthday") fed the YEBISU BAR rollout and the brewery experience (countand1/Nikkei).
  • Context (correlation only): Yebisu sales rose in 2024 (101.9% of the previous year), the first increase in eight years, and in Jan–Sep 2025 (101.1%), while premium beer overall fell (84.4%). The brand credits redefining "premium" plus experiences such as the brewery and the CREATIVE BREW line, not the community specifically (Nikkei Cross Trend, 2025). Co-created goods and the cocktail did produce sales, but no figures were disclosed.

What they did right

  1. Asked before building. A newsletter survey confirmed demand and set the feature list (bar tips, pairings, staff voices) (Dentsu Digital).
  2. Allowed off-topic talk. Growth and daily posting rest on residents' whole lives, not on product talk (coorum; Yebisu Magazine).
  3. Named, senior staff in the room. The brand manager is the mayor, and the brewer and developers post. When a buyer was needed live from Germany, they negotiated with the purchasing department head to make it happen (coorum).
  4. Retention-designed acquisition. Daily draws with shareable losing results instead of one-shot giveaways; the New Year lottery turned 18k sign-ups into habits (coorum).
  5. Superusers before scale, recruited from event attendees, meeting monthly, and later meeting in person (Yebisu Magazine part 2).
  6. Co-creation with real output that members can buy or drink (4,809 votes; the cocktail in every YEBISU BAR).
  7. Survived its founder leaving. The mayor handed over after one year with a public baton-pass, and the town kept growing (mayor notice; member counts).

What they did wrong

  1. Shallow, incentive-driven engagement. Badges for a single like or comment produce replies like "楽しみ" and "いいですね" (13 replies on the Sep 2026 Beer College post, mostly one-liners) (college post). Engagement on big posts runs ~0.1–0.2% of the registered base (estimate). Consequence: the headline count of 150k overstates the active community, and Sapporo stopped publishing member counts after Feb 2025.
  2. The bar-mapping feature never took off. It launched in Sep 2023 to build trusted word-of-mouth bar listings that restaurants could use too (coorum), but it holds 647 posts against 100k+ chat and photo posts. The restaurant link is now carried mostly by staff-run content (YEBISU BAR, the Grand Prix).
  3. Mechanics switched on and off. The point program lapsed and "returned" in 2025, the omikuji was "restarted" in Mar 2026 with an end date of 15 Dec 2026 and nothing confirmed after it, and a member complained "I couldn't draw the omikuji for 4–5 days" (renewal thread). When habits depend on mechanics that run as campaigns, they reset each time a mechanic stops.
  4. Top-down room consolidation. In Feb 2025, five themed rooms (culture, lifestyle, travel, gourmet, Yebisu mania) were frozen read-only and folded into two. Comments were congratulatory and no backlash was found, but niche sub-identities lost their own spaces.
  5. First-year metric obsession. Mantani admits "the first year we were too conscious of the numbers in front of us" (ITmedia p.3).
  6. Tokyo-centric, weekday-heavy events leave regional members out ("I'd love to go but I live in Shiga"; "please consider holding it on a weekend too").
  7. Older audience. The core is 50+, while the brand's stated aim is to reach younger drinkers (Nikkei 2025). The community deepens existing loyalty more than it recruits new drinkers.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Validate demand with your existing list before building, and let the answers set the first features.
  2. Seed with superfans by hand (recruit from your most engaged event attendees, meet them monthly, act visibly on what they say) before you open the doors wide.
  3. Make the product the shared password, not the only topic. Off-topic, everyday posting keeps daily traffic up. Brand staff should post less "marketing" and more as people.
  4. Design acquisition campaigns for day-2 retention: daily draws, shareable losing results, collection badges. Tie badges to real-world actions too (store check-ins, posting each product variant).
  5. Put senior, named staff in a dedicated space (the "staff BAR") and give it identity props (a mayor, residents, departments) that make the place feel inhabited.

Avoid these

  1. Paying per like or comment. You will get empty one-word engagement and inflated totals. Reward contributions that help others (bar tips, guides), and report active users, not registrations.
  2. Running core habits as campaigns that start and stop. If a daily ritual matters, make it permanent and reliable.
  3. Assuming members will build your directory. Crowd-sourced utility features (here, the bar map) need seeding, structure and a reason to contribute, or they stay empty while chat thrives.

Score justifications: retention mechanics 4 (rich daily, seasonal and status loops, though leaning on prizes); content engine 4 (staff BAR, seven Beer College editions, co-creation output, weekly pickup email, but no regular show); member ownership 3 (executive committee, member-originated campaigns and meetups, but the platform and room structure are set top-down); staff investment 4 (brand manager as mayor, cross-functional staff posting, an agency team with weekly meetings, though only ~2 in-house operators); business impact evidence 2 (only qualitative claims, no metrics); durability 4 (4.7 years, survived a leadership handover and a redesign, active Nov 2026).
