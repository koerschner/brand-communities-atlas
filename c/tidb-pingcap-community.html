TL;DR

  • What it is. TiDB is PingCAP's open-source, MySQL-compatible distributed database (GitHub since September 2015). Its community has three parts. The center is a Chinese Q&A forum, AskTUG (founded July 2019; about 45,000 members by the end of 2025), with a member-written column, volunteer moderators, MVA/MOA honors and city meetups. Around it sit a contributor community on GitHub (about 2,400 contributors, CNCF projects TiKV and Chaos Mesh) and a much thinner English layer (Discord, Slack, Devpost hackathons, a Champion program).
  • Why people come back. The forum answers production problems for free. PingCAP's annual reports claim a 90–95% resolution rate and 10+ replies per question. On top of that sits a dense, China-style gamification layer: XP levels V1–V11, daily check-in streaks, points you can spend on swag and certification exams, badges, monthly honors and lotteries. Recognition is also very personal: every volunteer is named in an annual "wrapped" post.
  • What PingCAP feeds it. Weekly Infra Meetups (125 by March 2020), DevCon (750+ attendees in 2019), source-code reading series, Talent Plan courses, essay contests, Book Rush e-books, a monthly magazine, city events run with volunteer organizers, and a named community-manager persona, "表妹" (Billmay, "cousin").
  • Biggest mistake. The English community never stuck. PingCAP launched an English Discourse forum (c. 2022), padded it in June 2024 with roughly 8,000 GPT-translated Chinese threads, pointed users to Stack Overflow, where TiDB questions get closed, and then closed the forum in August 2025 for Discord and Slack. Meanwhile the Chinese forum's expert core is thinning: people supplying accepted solutions fell from 483 in 2022 to 100 in 2025.
  • The one lesson. Pay volunteer experts with status, small material rewards and career value (exam vouchers) in one coherent ledger, and make it a public, yearly ritual to name them. But a community built for one language and market doesn't transfer by translating or mirroring it. Each market needs its own home, staff and a stable place to ask questions.

What the community is

Official spaces (2026):

  • AskTUG (asktug.com, now served at pingkai.cn/tidbcommunity/forum). A Discourse Q&A forum created on 2019-07-03 (about.json) and announced in August 2019 as the place where "TiDB User Group members, experienced architects… and PingCAP R&D and DBAs" answer questions (Zhihu, 2019-08-21).
  • Linked tidb.net properties. A blog/column (专栏), a member center with points, events pages, TUG pages and the MVA roster (tidb.net/member/rules, tidb.net/tug/mva).
  • Rebrand under PingKai. Since 2025-11-24 all of this sits under the PingKai brand at pingkai.cn, which merged several domains and official sites (announcement). The forum footer now says it is "hosted and operated by 平凯星辰（北京）科技有限公司" (forum).
  • GitHub. Code, issues and governance (TOC, teams, earlier SIGs) live in pingcap/community, plus GitHub Discussions. TiKV entered the CNCF in August 2018 and graduated on 2020-09-02 (CNCF).
  • English real-time spaces. The Discord (server created 2023-03-09 by snowflake ID; 2,080 members and 96 online on 2026-11-07 via the invite API) and Slack with English and Japanese channels (docs).
  • Ambassador programs. TUG MVA/MOA in China, and the TiDB Champion Program internationally since 2024.
  • Local groups and events. Local groups include TiUG Japan and the TiDB India User Group; events include hackathons and DevCon/HTAP Summit/SCaiLE.

Closed or dormant spaces:

  • ask.pingcap.com. The English Discourse forum, with snapshots from December 2022. Its last pinned topic was "We're Moving to Discord and Slack!" (August 2025); it timed out on 2026-11-07 (Wayback, Dec 2025).
  • r/tidb. Created in October 2017; 369 subscribers and 298 posts lifetime. It had 79–82 posts a year in 2018–2020, almost all from a few accounts, one of them jinqueeny (matching PingCAP's Queeny Jin). It has had 0–7 posts a year since 2022 (Arctic Shift).

Status today. The Chinese forum is active but contracting. The English community lives in chat apps and hackathons. Related entity: PingCAP is a founding-board company of the OurSQL Foundation, launched 2026-05-27 with Percona, PlanetScale, VillageSQL and Alibaba to support MySQL independently of Oracle; PingCAP's Sunny Bains sits on the board (The Register, ChannelLife). That is a separate community and belongs under the MySQL/OurSQL entry, not this one.

Origin and strategy

PingCAP was founded in 2015 by Max Liu (刘奇), Ed Huang (黄东旭) and Cui Qiu (Baidu Baike). The strategy was open source from day one, with "radical transparency": "All our code, bugs, and discussions were publicly on GitHub… transparency built deeper trust" (Ed Huang, 2026-08). In 2017 Huang described the community explicitly as the go-to-market engine: "Open-source is viral in nature, which is how we're able to cover China's entire developer community with almost zero marketing expenditure" (Harbinger China, 2017-11-26).

The community was built in layers:

  1. Developer meetups (2016–). PingCAP started Saturday-morning "Infra Meetups" for infrastructure engineers on 2016-03-05 (Sohu, 2018).
  2. Contributor governance. Maintainer, Committer and Contributor roles came first. In December 2019 PingCAP restructured: it added "Active Contributor" (8+ merged PRs a year), Reviewer and Special Interest Groups, and formally brought the user community (TUG) into the structure, because "the TiDB user community… was obscured in the old community structure" (Jian Zhang, 2019-12-09).
  3. User community (2019–). TUG was framed as "an independent, non-profit, third-party organization sponsored and managed by TiDB users", with five regional chapters at first: Beijing, Shanghai, Hangzhou, South China and Southwest (PingCAP). AskTUG launched as its online home.
  4. Education (2018–). Talent Plan began in December 2018 as courses "to train students, contributors, new hires, and existing employees" (GitHub). PingCAP Education and certifications followed in October 2018 (forum).

Business ties. Each layer maps to a business need:

  • Adoption: Huang credits GitHub discovery for a Japanese payment company's switch (2026 essay).
  • Support deflection: the forum FAQ says it "has no commercial service support or response guarantee" and directs urgent production issues to paid 24x7 support (FAQ).
  • Hiring and talent supply.
  • Promotion of paid products: in 2025 the community ran a trial campaign for the commercial 平凯数据库 (TiDB Enterprise) "agile mode", with free certification as the reward (Ti 星球 news, 2025-09).

Internally, the visible Chinese champion is the operator "Billmay表妹" (黄漫绅), who writes the annual reports and a manifesto for the "ideal open-source community" (2024-04-29). The English champion since 2023 is community manager Ray Paik, formerly of GitLab and the Linux Foundation (Medium bio; Crunchbase).

Trajectory. Membership still grows (about 5,000 a year), but the helping core and question flow are shrinking. These are my calculations from PingCAP's own cumulative figures:

  • New questions: about 4,000 in 2024 and about 2,000 in 2025.
  • New replies: about 77,000 in 2024 and about 47,000 in 2025.
  • Pace now: about 85 topics a month (≈1,000 a year annualized).
  • Solution providers: 483 (2022) → 396 → 155 → 100 (2025).
  • New contributors: +183, +183, +74 a year (2023–2025).

The forum's own figures disagree in places: the 2022 report counts "45.4K topics" while later reports count "quality questions", and Discourse reports 49,201 accounts against 44,914 in the annual review. Treat them as directional. A moderator still called it "the best database community among Chinese databases… 100+ active users every day" in September 2025 (Ti 星球 news). Peak activity was 2022–2024. Now it is a mature, contracting support forum.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Answers to production problems. This is the core value. The reports claim a 90% resolution rate (2023) and about 95% (2024), with 10+ replies per question (2023, 2024). Many members run TiDB community edition in production without a support contract, so the forum effectively is their support channel.
  2. One ledger for XP and points. The member rules define it:
    • Levels: V1–V11 with titles (V6 "junior contributor" at 1,000 XP, up to V10+ "honorary contributor").
    • Daily check-in streaks: 2, then 3, then 5 points, resetting when you miss a day.
    • Rewards per action: +5 to ask, +8 to answer, +15 for a best answer, +2 per like, +5/10 when quoted, +10/20 when pinned.
    • One-time onboarding rewards: for adding company details, binding GitHub (which auto-grants a Contributor badge), binding email (which unlocks a weekly digest) and binding a phone.
    • What points buy: swag, PCTA/PCTP exam seats, lottery entries, downloads, and the right to "summon a community expert". Points are real currency: members open threads like "积分不够怎么来凑？" ("not enough points, how do I make up the difference?", Nov 2026).
  3. Status, honors and named recognition.
    • Monthly MVA (≥3 quality pieces a year) and MOA (≥10), announced in forum posts (MVA; May 2023).
    • Other awards: a "Most Solutions Award" for 200+ solutions, trophies for regional organizers, honorary-moderator status for people who step down, and joke "easter-egg" titles on profiles (a top moderator's title reads "you've unlocked another TiDB community easter egg").
    • The yearly "wrapped" post names every person with 100+ replies and every article author in word clouds (2025). It singled out one moderator who visited 356 days in a row, solved 428 problems and received 1,300 likes (2022).
  4. Rituals.
    • The "TiDBer 唠嗑茶话会" (chat tea party): an open discussion prompt with prize draws, run roughly weekly through 2024–2025 (no. 111 around April 2024, no. 164 in April 2025). It slowed in 2026: no. 194 in March, no. 201 in November (search).
    • Annual cycles: essay contests, the year-end review with lottery, and the October Champion class.
  5. Identity and belonging. Members are "TiDBers". The forum is "Ti 星球" (Ti Planet), and default usernames are TiDBer_xxxx. The staff persona "表妹" ("cousin"; a "表弟" also appears) gives the company a familiar face. Annual reports describe the community in heavily emotional language ("warm home").
  6. Career value. Certifications (PCTA, PCTP, PCSD) can be earned through points or free campaigns (2026 free certification posts). The forum and column serve as a public portfolio for DBAs, and MVA status is "certified technical pioneer".
  7. Switching costs. A shared knowledge base: 221 curated user case studies (2024), 2,400+ member articles, and the forum search PingCAP says answers 80% of questions (FAQ).

The content engine

Company-produced:

  • Infra Meetups, weekly on Saturdays from 2016 (no. 128 by July 2020) (archive).
  • Annual DevCon. In 2020 it moved online, with 80+ speakers (DevCon 2020).
  • TiFlash/TiCDC source-code reading series (14 episodes, 13,000+ subscribers in 2022) and paper-reading sessions.
  • A monthly community tech magazine (TiDB 社区技术月刊) and the "Ti 星球新鲜事" news digest.
  • Monthly online meetups in 2025: a five-part "TiDB vs MySQL" series, then TiDB vs Oracle and vs SQL Server (2025-09).
  • City events: 9 in 2024; in 2025 Xiamen, Jinan, Xi'an (100+ attendees), Nanjing, Shenyang and Suzhou.
  • Talent Plan courses and PingCAP Education certifications.
  • In English: the blog, dev.to and Hashnode posts, the "Data in the Hallway" podcast (55+ episodes by October 2025), Devpost hackathons with $30k+ prizes (2024, AgentX 2025), and OSS Insight (2022), a GitHub analytics site built on TiDB Cloud that doubles as content marketing.

Member-produced and how it is rewarded:

  • The column (专栏). Launched in February 2022 out of an AskTUG "tech articles" category. Articles earn 100–600 points plus creation badges (reward FAQ; contest). A volunteer "布道师组委会" (evangelist committee) reviews submissions. Output was 358 articles in 2022, 295 in 2023, 382 in 2024 and 605 in 2025.
  • Essay contests. The 4th edition was co-run with the database portal 墨天轮/Modb, with prizes such as Bose headphones and DJI gimbals (tidb.net).
  • "Book Rush". Collective e-books written against a new release: for TiDB 6.0, 129 sign-ups, 37 completed trials and 46 articles; contributors got Apple Watches (2022).
  • Community-written book. "TiDB in Action" (TiDB 实战) was written by the community (GitHub).
  • Peer mentoring. A peer-mentor "upgrade squad" filled 20 mentor slots in one day (Billmay).
  • AI. Since March 2025 a "TiDB AI 小助手" (a GraphRAG bot built on TiDB Vector and trained on 2,400+ docs) answers on the forum (2025-09).
  • Translation. In June 2024 PingCAP mirrored the Chinese forum into English with GPT. Thousands of topics carry the note "translated from a Chinese forum by GPT and might contain errors" (example).

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. In China, a small operations team (Billmay表妹, "社区小助手" accounts, the "TiDB官方" account) plus PingCAP engineers who answer on the forum. English admins on ask.pingcap.com in 2025 included Ray Paik, Hazel Zhang and others, plus a translator_bot and a TiDB-AI-Assistant moderator (Wayback about page). Exact headcount: no evidence found.
  • Volunteers (China).
    • Moderators: grew from a few to 15 in 2022, with 15 in 2024 and 24 in 2025 (7 of them honorary).
    • Evangelist committee: 11 members (2025).
    • Regional organizers: about 30 (2025), who moved events "from officially organized to TiDBer self-organized" (2022).
    • MOA/MVA: 36 in 2023.
  • Champions (international, 2024–). Nomination only, by employees or current Champions. Status lasts one year, with a new class announced each October. Perks: exposure and speaking slots, early roadmap access, cloud credits, direct product-team access, swag, and coaching in speaking and writing (program page). The 2025 roster lists 11 Champions in India, the US, Brazil, Sri Lanka, Thailand, Portugal and Japan.
  • Contributor governance. The TOC coordinates across companies; teams and SIGs run modules, and the Active Contributor → Reviewer → Committer → Maintainer ladder applies (pingcap/community). TiKV's maintainers at graduation came from PingCAP, Zhihu, JD Cloud and Yidian Zixun (CNCF). Top outside contributing organizations in 2024: Tencent, Alibaba, ByteDance, Microsoft and NUS (2024 summary).
  • Rules and feedback. The forum FAQ bans spam and trolling and asks for background details before posting. Since 2025 an AI bot auto-filters ads. Product feedback flows through "product improvement reward" notices, tea-party threads collecting suggestions, and roadmap Q&A threads such as TiProxy (Billmay).

Growth loops

  1. GitHub discovery to adoption. Developers find TiDB through GitHub, CNCF credibility and Rust/TiKV buzz (Huang).
  2. SEO from forum answers. An English user noted "Google is indexing and showing results from ask.pingcap.com pretty well" (2025-05). The Chinese column is syndicated to Juejin, OSChina, InfoQ, CSDN and Modb.
  3. City events hosted by user companies. Examples: "TiDB community visits 360", Rakuten Dalian, Luckin Xiamen. These double as reference-customer showcases for the domestic-substitution (国产化) market.
  4. Talent pipeline. Talent Plan, university hackathons and certifications produce TiDB-skilled engineers who then choose TiDB at work. Talent Plan alone led to 15 PingCAP hires by 2020.
  5. AI hackathons as top of funnel for TiDB Cloud. About 2,100 participants signed up for TiDB Serverless/Starter in 2024 (PingCAP).

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence. Weak. No retention, churn or NPS data tied to the community was found. PingCAP is private, so there are no SEC filings. Funding: a $270M Series D in November 2020 (TechCrunch) and a reported $3B valuation in 2021 (third-party Tracxn).
  • Company claims.
    • Open source let PingCAP reach China's developers at "almost zero marketing expenditure" (2017).
    • Most customers "can install PingCAP's products themselves, so our pipeline growth is very healthy" (same source).
    • Contributor companies include Tencent, ByteDance and Alibaba.
    • The community serves as a pre-sales channel for the enterprise edition and TiDB Cloud (2025 campaigns).
  • Counter-signal. In March 2023 PingCAP made layoffs that Chinese tech media read as the limit of "monetizing open-source technical traffic". One widely shared post argued that "B-side users are extremely rational… why pay real money for the enterprise edition when the free community edition works?" (Zhihu, 2023-03; Maimai). That is the classic open-core tension: an excellent free peer-support community can lower the urgency of buying support. Whether community team members were cut: no evidence found.

What they did right

  1. Built a complete incentive economy, not just badges. XP, levels, streaks, points, a store and exam vouchers sit in one ledger, with clear point values for asking, answering, accepted answers and being quoted (rules). Result: 1,400–1,700 members a year helped others in 2022–2024.
  2. Named recognition at scale. The annual reports list hundreds of usernames, and moderators get honorary status when they retire. It is cheap and evidently motivating: members reply to the review threads listing their own "battle record" for the year (2025 review replies).
  3. Handed governance to volunteers early. Moderators, the evangelist committee that reviews articles, and regional organizers who self-run city events (from 2022). This scaled events to 30 organizers and many cities with a small staff.
  4. Turned users into authors. The column grew to 2,406 articles by 419 authors, through contests, Book Rush and MVA thresholds. Member articles are the knowledge base and the SEO.
  5. Education as a community layer. Talent Plan (11k GitHub stars), certifications that can be bought with points, and source-code reading series created career reasons to stay.
  6. Contributor governance with a clear ladder and neutral homes. SIGs and the Active Contributor/Reviewer ladder (2019) plus CNCF donation of TiKV and Chaos Mesh made outside companies real co-owners (CNCF).

What they did wrong

  1. An English community with no stable home. Questions were sent to GitHub Discussions, then to ask.pingcap.com (c. 2022), then to Stack Overflow via a banner, then to Discord/Slack, and the forum closed in August 2025 (Wayback). A user pointed out that Stack Overflow had only two TiDB questions in 2025 and closes TiDB questions as off-topic (thread). Consequence: a 2,080-member Discord, unsearchable chat, and SEO lost.
  2. Padding with machine translation. About 8,000 GPT-translated Chinese topics were imported in a few days in June 2024. This is my estimate from topic IDs: about 1,700 before, up to 9,700 by 23 June, and only about 490 organic topics from July 2024 to August 2025. The import made the English forum look busy without creating English members; it closed 14 months later.
  3. The expert core is thinning, and nobody has said why. Solution providers fell from 483 to 100 between 2022 and 2025, while membership kept growing. The 2023 report admits that "deep-water" problems "may exceed the ability of moderators and community members". An AI answer bot (2025) may further crowd out human answerers; that is an untested hypothesis.
  4. Heavy dependence on one staff persona. When a member asked in April 2026 why the tea party had paused for over a month, the operator answered "最近太忙了~~" ("been too busy lately") (thread). The 2024 annual report shipped with an unfilled placeholder, "[具体数量]" ("[specific number]") (2024).
  5. Commercial and corporate restructuring of a community that claims to be "independent". TUG is described as user-run, yet the forum is now "hosted and operated" by 平凯星辰, re-hosted under the commercial PingKai brand (November 2025) and used to promote the enterprise edition.
  6. Trust shock (2026). Between 2026-09-09 and 2026-10-07 an attacker used a compromised GitHub account and exposed credentials to copy TiDB Cloud internal data and source code (PingCAP CEO note). The leak hit the Hacker News front page (433 points), where commenters called the company's statement downplaying (HN, 2026-10-20). This isn't a community failure as such, but it hits the trust that "radical transparency" was supposed to build.
  7. Abandoned channels. r/tidb was used as a broadcast outlet in 2018–2020 and then left dormant (Arctic Shift).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. One coherent contribution economy. Publish exact point values for asking, answering, accepted answers and being quoted. Let points buy things that matter: certification, swag, expert access. Add a gentle streak (2/3/5) rather than a punishing one.
  2. A yearly "wrapped" that names people. List every helper and author by handle, give honorary titles to people who step down, and turn the post into a thread where members share their own year.
  3. Ladders that hand real power to volunteers. Moderator → honorary moderator, author → evangelist committee reviewer, attendee → regional organizer, contributor → reviewer → maintainer. Each step should come with a visible artifact: a trophy, a badge, or moderator luggage.
  4. Make users authors on a schedule. Use release-tied collective e-books (Book Rush), essay contests with partner media, and monthly honors with low thresholds (≥3 pieces for MVA) so new writers can qualify.
  5. Make career value part of membership. Courses, certifications you can earn through community activity, and a public body of work turn a support forum into a professional home.

Avoid these

  1. Don't move the community's home every couple of years. Pick one searchable, durable place to ask questions per language or market and staff it. Chat (Discord/Slack) can supplement it but not replace it.
  2. Don't fake activity with machine-translated content. Mirrored threads don't create members, and a "might contain errors" label erodes trust. Seed with native staff answers instead.
  3. Don't let the system rest on one beloved operator, and watch the core. Track the count of people giving solutions, not just registrations. Plan a succession and a load-sharing structure before the "cousin" gets too busy.

  • OurSQL Foundation / MySQL community (Percona, PlanetScale, PingCAP, VillageSQL, Alibaba, 2026–): a vendor-neutral foundation formed out of frustration with Oracle's stewardship; outcome too early to judge.
  • OceanBase community (Ant Group): TiDB's main Chinese distributed-database rival, which per one TiDB member is "following TiDB's community route"; a good comparison.
  • CockroachDB community (Cockroach Labs): Western distributed-SQL peer whose license changes tested its community.
  • YugabyteDB community (Yugabyte): another distributed-SQL vendor community with Slack and an ambassador-style program.
  • Vitess / PlanetScale community: a CNCF MySQL-sharding project whose sponsor dominates it.
  • Modb / 墨天轮 (云和恩墨 Enmotech): a Chinese database content platform that co-runs vendor essay contests; a meta-community for Chinese DBAs.
  • Talent Plan / TinyKV (PingCAP and universities): an education-as-community program worth a standalone look.
  • Chaos Mesh (PingCAP → CNCF): a second PingCAP project donated to a foundation.
