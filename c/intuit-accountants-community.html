TL;DR

  • What it is: Intuit's public Q&A forum for professional tax preparers who use Lacerte, ProSeries, ProConnect Tax and EasyACCT, at accountants.intuit.com/community. It descends from the 2011 in-product "Live Community", ran on Lithium/Khoros from 2019 and moved to Gainsight on 15 June 2026. Next to it sit an Idea Exchange, the Champions program and a few user groups.
  • Why people come back: tax season makes it necessary. When a form isn't released, an e-file is rejected or Lacerte goes down in March, preparers need an answer within hours. Volunteer veterans (IRonMaN, sjrcpa, BobKamman, abctax55, George4Tacks, Just-Lisa-Now-) answer faster than phone support, and they have built a gruff, funny, decades-deep culture.
  • What Intuit feeds it: staff-posted News & Updates boards for each product, moderators who route bugs to developers (800+ tags, a "Monitor Wall", a weekly internal newsletter), an Idea Exchange with a product-manager review cadence, free CPE virtual conferences (Empower, Elevate), webinars and the Tax Pro Center content site.
  • Biggest mistake: a long pattern of taking things away from the members who carry the forum: shutting TaxAlmanac (2014), removing the in-product Live Community widget (2018), offering superusers a 16-page Champions contract they refused (2022), and three platform migrations. The June 2026 Gainsight move drew "this place really sucks" from the top contributor and a goodbye from another regular. It also landed during a round of 23–54% price increases.
  • The one lesson: in a professional B2B community, a handful of expert volunteers is the product. Measure containment if you must, but protect their tools, their status and their reading flow, and never make them sign away their right to criticize you.

What the community is

  • Official forum. accountants.intuit.com/community has product boards (Lacerte Product Discussions, ProConnect Product Discussions, ProSeries Product Discussions, EasyACCT, Intuit Tax Advisor, hosting), staff-written News & Updates boards for each product, and two "Industry Discussion" boards: Tax Talk for technical tax questions and Practice Advice. In November 2026 the navigation shows these topic counts: ProSeries 29,676, Lacerte 11,262, ProConnect 8,819, Tax Talk 3,076, EasyACCT 726, Practice Advice 561, plus small boards. That is about 54,700 forum topics carried over and added (community home, via r.jina.ai, 7 Nov 2026). It is "Powered by Gainsight" with uploads hosted on insided.com.
  • Idea Exchange: 1,613 ideas, filterable by Lacerte, ProConnect or ProSeries. As of November 2026 "Idea submission is temporarily unavailable. We're taking a step back to improve this experience from the ground up" (Idea Exchange).
  • Groups and events: a Lacerte SDK Group (it already existed in 2019), "The Lounge" and "ProSeries Early Access" (Groups). The Events tab showed "No events to display" in November 2026, even though Intuit runs free CPE conferences elsewhere (Events).
  • Volunteer program: Intuit Community Champions, which shows as a badge on posts (for example Just-Lisa-Now- and Jim-from-Ohio). It replaced the earlier AllStars title.
  • Self-description: members are "fellow software users (except for the occasional moderator/employee that wanders by)" (May 2026 thread). The guidelines' mission is "to empower accounting and tax professionals to connect, share knowledge, solve problems" (Guidelines, Jun 2026).
  • Sister space: Canadian ProFile users have a separate community (profile-en.community.intuit.ca).
  • Important unofficial spaces: LTUG, the Lacerte Tax Users Group, is an email list founded by customers on Yahoo Groups and now at groups.io/g/ltug. In 2006 CPA Practice Advisor noted that Intuit's Lacerte community "was set up independently on Yahoo! Groups … created by customers for customers" (CPA Practice Advisor, 2006). TaxProTalk.com was founded in 2014 by a TaxAlmanac member, "ChrisV2", for "TA refugees" (TaxAlmanac user talk page). r/taxpros has 78,309 subscribers and ~25,000 posts since June 2014 (Arctic Shift, Nov 2026); there, Intuit's pro products are debated against Drake, UltraTax and CCH.

Why Intuit built it. The record points to three motives:

  1. Support deflection. The 2022 Khoros award entry frames success as "a 93.5% Containment rate with customers not having to call us" (Khoros 2022).
  2. Early warning for product. Tax season is time-critical: "one of the forms that is needed for this group of clients will NOT be ready for another month … your client decides to go somewhere else" (same source).
  3. Feedback. "This group of professionals is always vocal about how we can improve our products" (Khoros 2021).

TaxAlmanac also had a quieter strategic logic: "In return the company gets free tips and advice from a roster of tax specialists that help improve its products" (Fast Company, 2012).

Champions inside Intuit. Toby Joplin (TaxAlmanac, 2005). Matthew Knell, "Principal PM, Out of Product Experiences and Intuit Communities" (2021). Betty Jo Parra, "Program Manager, Community" (2022), later "Customer Experience Lead for Personalization within Out-of-Product Help and Community" (RocketReach); she announced the 2026 Gainsight launch on LinkedIn ("Migrating a community of this scale — while keeping the lights on — takes a village"). The org-chart language places the community under out-of-product help, not marketing or customer success.

Business context. ProTax, which covers Lacerte, ProSeries, ProConnect, ProFile and ProTax Online, earned $599M in FY24, $621M in FY25 and $647M in FY26, about 3% of Intuit's revenue, and most of it lands in Q2–Q3, the tax season (Intuit Q4 FY26 fact sheet). Since 1 August 2025 ProTax has been folded into Intuit's Consumer segment alongside TurboTax. Members notice their low priority: "We are pimples on the face of the moon. The core customers are TurboTax subscribers" (BobKamman, Aug 2025).

Trajectory. Activity peaked in 2020–21, when COVID-era law changes (stimulus, Form 8915-E/F, PPP) and a new platform brought in about 80k messages a year. It then roughly halved to about 46k in 2022–23 and fell to about 33k a year from 2023 to 2026. These message-ID estimates assume Khoros IDs are global and sequential, which is consistent across all the data points above. Off-season traffic after the migration is about 7 topics a day, low next to the 350,000-customer base. The QuickBooks community's sister migration showed about 19 topics a day (see quickbooks-proadvisor report).

What keeps people coming back

  1. The tax calendar forces return visits. Every January to April, preparers hit the same new forms, e-file rejects and software bugs within days of each other. The community is where "is it just us?" gets answered: "Every workstation in our office is down. Is this just us or is everyone having an issue?" (Lacerte 2022 down?, Mar 2023). Staff post outage notes in News & Updates and point members to them ("Subscribe to News and Updates to get notified for posts like this", Mar 2022). Form-release timing is the core need that the 2022 award entry describes.
  2. Peer answers beat phone support. "If people have a question, often times, they do get solved - much more quickly and accurately here on this forum than with Support (sad to say)" (itonewbie, Apr 2020). Members also say they come because support is thin: "since Proseries provides virtually a zero level of professional technical support, we are forced to work with the proseries community" (JWM, Aug 2025).
  3. A veteran in-group with its own voice. Signatures act as identity markers: "Slava Ukraini!" (IRonMaN, "a coyote"), "The more I know the more I don't know" (sjrcpa), "HumanKind... Be Both" (abctax55), "Don't yell at us; we're volunteers" (qbteachmt), "Answers are easy. Questions are hard!" (George4Tacks). Threads such as "Lacerte History" draw 1970s war stories about service bureaus, carbon-paper forms and a KayPro running early Lacerte (Oct 2023). Members joke with each other ("Is this a bad time to bring up the fact that you haven't paid your dues yet?"), which feels like belonging for regulars and can feel like a clique to newcomers.
  4. Status ladders. Rank levels run from 1 to 15, and the top answerers sit at Level 15. Likes ("Cheers"), Best Answer, a weekly "Most helpful members" leaderboard, and earlier a Khoros "Top Kudoed Authors" list (2019) all show who stands where. The AllStar title mattered enough that ex-AllStars kept "Still an AllStar" and "Another Former AllStar" in their signatures after the program changed (Dec 2022).
  5. Switching costs that the community reinforces. When members threaten to leave over 23–54% renewal increases, a Champion argues the other side: "I just need to prepare 1 or maybe 2 extra returns to cover my increase, that still seems more affordable than switching to a different program and having to relearn the logistics" (Just-Lisa-Now-, Jul 2026). Peer know-how about workarounds makes staying easier, and the forum is a place where Intuit's case for staying gets made by peers.
  6. In-product hooks, mostly lost. The Live Community widget put the forum inside the software from about 2009. ProSeries removed it in 2018 and promised a "next generation widget" ("we anticipate an improved design that you will be able to count on in the years to come"). After the 2026 migration, members reported that Help > Browse Online Community returned a 502 error (Jun 2026).

The content engine

  • Staff-written News & Updates boards for each product, with roughly 114–145 topics each on the new platform. They cover release notes, system requirements, login changes ("Action Required: Update to ProSeries required to Login", 2026) and deadline roundups ("Top Help Articles & Resources for the July 15th Deadline", Jul 2020).
  • Seasonal expert events, mostly in the past. In 2012 there were two weeks of daily expert-hosted topics on Schedules K-1, C, F and E and on depreciation (Accounting Today). In 2018–19 there were three Tax Reform AMA days ("Thursday, Tax Reform AMA day … 2-4 P.M. CST … still 2 more to attend. March 4th and April 11th", archived thread). I found no evidence of community AMAs after 2019, and the Events board is empty.
  • Member spotlights: "Meet Our AllStars", a weekly feature on top contributors hosted by staffer Austin, ran in 2019 (itonewbie feature). I found no evidence that it continued.
  • Off-forum education: free virtual CPE conferences, Empower (1 day, up to 5 CPE and 2 CE, 10 Nov 2026) and Elevate (13–14 May 2026) (Tax Pro Center; webinars). There are also product webinars, the Tax Pro Center editorial site with practitioner authors, and ProAdvisor Academy courses for ProConnect. The community home page promotes Empower with a banner, but the community itself is not the venue.
  • Member-made content: member-written how-tos inside threads, file-sharing on LTUG ("If you have files to upload and share, we welcome your sharing", groups.io), and, in TaxAlmanac's era, a full wiki. Nothing on the official forum rewards long-form member guides beyond likes and rank.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff: a small moderation team with named handles: Kathi_at_Intuit ("Community Manager" badge, 2025–26), "Intuit John", IntuitKatieB (2019), Intuit_Devin (2019), yw_sh (2021) and Betty Jo (2021, "I will make sure that we get this back to the team", Mar 2021). The team's size is not public: no evidence found.
  • Feedback loop: Khoros CARE tagging (800+ tags), a Monitor Wall dashboard and a weekly internal newsletter to Customer Success, Product and Marketing teams. These escalated 80+ issues in one season (Khoros 2022). The Idea Exchange had an "induction process" and a recurring review cadence with the product managers of three teams; 20+ ideas shipped in five months of 2021 (Khoros 2021).
  • AllStars → Champions. AllStars (c.2019) were top contributors featured weekly. The cross-Intuit Community Champions program went "LIVE across all of our Intuit Communities" in 2020 (Are you a Champion?, Nov 2020). Its terms of agreement run 16 pages and need a signature. Benefits include possible meetups, use of the "Intuit® Community Champion" designation, a certificate, a private forum, "gifts of nominal value, including Intuit swag", limited access to employees and product discounts. In return, members must "promote Intuit Services, in a manner which reflects favorably at all times" and take legal responsibility for claims arising from their posts (Dec 2022 thread). Several top AllStars declined: "A number of us who were AllStars decided not be Champs" (sjrcpa). A few veterans accepted, such as Just-Lisa-Now- and Jim-from-Ohio.
  • Rules: the 2026 guidelines are generic (be respectful, stay relevant, protect privacy, use judgment, and post nothing you wouldn't put "on a road-side billboard"). Moderators remove links and contact details, and promotional links from competitors were "[Link removed by Moderator]" in a 2026 pricing thread.

Growth loops

  • SEO. Public, Google-indexed threads on niche tax and software questions are the main way people arrive. Members admit search is the real interface: "you can search these forums by using a google search; not by using the search function provided at the top of these pages" (qbteachmt, Apr 2020). Many visitors arrive by mistake; veterans keep a stock reply for lost TurboTax filers: "You seem to be lost on the internet. You've come to a Peer User community…" (Dec 2020).
  • Product-choice threads. Threads such as "How do you decide between Lacerte Tax, ProConnect Tax, and ProSeries Tax" (Apr 2026) and "Proseries vs ProConnect" turn peers into pre-sales advisors, and often steer people between Intuit's own products rather than to competitors.
  • Acquisition loop: I found no evidence of one. There is a customer referral program on the marketing site, but it is not tied to the community.

Evidence of business impact

  • Company-reported, via vendor awards (soft): 93.5% containment; 80+ escalated issues including 3 P1s; +21.8% accepted solutions year over year (Khoros 2022). 800+ ideas, 20+ implemented, 75k+ visitors to the Idea Exchange (Khoros 2021). These are self-nominations to the platform vendor's award program: they are unaudited and use no control group.
  • Hard: none found. No 10-K, earnings call or case study ties the forum to ProTax retention or churn. EDGAR describes ProTax only as products.
  • Indirect: ProTax revenue grows slowly (+4% in FY26) despite steep price increases, which suggests high switching costs. Members name relearning and file conversion as the reason they stay, and the forum lowers the cost of staying by solving problems.

What they did right

  1. Instrumented the forum as a sensor. Tagging plus a Monitor Wall plus a weekly newsletter turned scattered complaints into P1 escalations and changes to form-release priorities, a strong use of a support community in a deadline-driven trade (Khoros 2022).
  2. Closed the idea loop, for a while. Replacing an ignored UserVoice with an Idea Exchange that had a product-manager cadence produced 20+ shipped ideas in months (Khoros 2021).
  3. Let veterans set the tone. Moderators stay light-touch and tolerate sarcasm and criticism, so the same Level-15 names have answered for 7+ years (itonewbie, IRonMaN, sjrcpa and abctax55 appear in 2019 and 2026). Peer answers are often rated better than phone support.
  4. Recognized people by name. The 2019 "Meet Our AllStars" weekly profiles, which included business links for the featured pros, cost little and created warmth ("Thank you, Austin for the intro").
  5. Used staff posts for real-time incident news, which is what members actually return for in March.

What they did wrong

  1. Killed TaxAlmanac (2014). Nine years of tax-pro wiki content and discussion went offline, and the community founded TaxProTalk.com instead of staying with Intuit. Eight years later the dead in-product link was still a grudge: "The fact that Intuit killed TaxAlmanac is solid proof that they don't give a about their customers, and every dead link is a great reminder of that" (Jul 2022).
  2. Removed the in-product door. The Live Community widget was pulled in August 2018 with a promised replacement that never arrived, and in-app access broke again after the 2026 move (502 errors).
  3. Asked superusers to become brand ambassadors on corporate terms. A 16-page contract with an obligation to reflect "favorably at all times" and an indemnity clause. "I like the ability to criticize when necessary and I really don't want to run around with a muzzle and a leash on" (IRonMaN, Dec 2022). The program lost its most credible voices.
  4. Replatformed three times, without the core users. The 2026 Gainsight move hid unread state and last-poster names and left "Migrated Content — No text available" stubs. The pre-migration announcement now redirects to a "deleted" category with "Access Denied". Idea submission is paused. Veterans: "This is the third (I think) revamp I've gone thru; it's far & away THE worst. Good thing 'they' asked for our thoughts/comments during development… /S" (abctax55). Another regular, strongsilence: "I'm out. Goodbye everyone" (Jun 2026).
  5. Used the community as a substitute for support. "Stop pawning users off in community forums to solve issues" (Apr 2020). Containment as the headline metric rewards exactly this.
  6. Turned the forum into a complaints desk for pricing. In June–July 2026, just after the migration, renewal increases of 23–54% (Fast Path from $545 to $813) set off threads about leaving for competitors. Staff did not respond publicly in that thread: "I'm almost positive someone from Intuit will respond — or maybe not" (IRonMaN).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Tie the community to the moments your users must have answers. Deadlines, outages and releases. Staff posts on status and known issues give everyone a reason to check in.
  2. Instrument it for product. Tag every thread, put the counts on a dashboard product managers see, and send a weekly digest, so the forum becomes your earliest bug detector.
  3. Run an idea portal with a real review cadence and visible "implemented" outcomes. Retire it publicly if you can't staff it.
  4. Give expert volunteers visible status (ranks, weekly leaderboards, profile features with business links) and let them keep their voice, including criticism.
  5. Keep a door to the community inside the product. An in-app widget beat any SEO funnel for keeping professionals engaged while they work.

Avoid these

  1. Don't shut down or move member-built knowledge (wikis, archives, threads) without a migration that its builders help design. TaxAlmanac's closure spawned a competitor community and a grudge that lasted a decade.
  2. Don't turn superusers into contracted brand ambassadors. Recognition with strings attached ("reflect favorably at all times", indemnities) drives off your most credible members.
  3. Don't make containment the main success metric. It rewards substituting peers for paid support, and when price rises land in the same space, the forum becomes the place where churn talk spreads.

Score justifications:

  • Retention mechanics 3: strong seasonal necessity and a veteran in-group, but few designed rituals since 2019 and lost in-product hooks.
  • Content engine 3: steady staff news and free CPE conferences, but AMAs and spotlights lapsed and events live off-forum.
  • Member ownership 4: peers do most answering, and LTUG and TaxProTalk show members will run their own spaces.
  • Staff investment 2: a small moderation team, three replatformings imposed from outside, idea submission paused.
  • Business impact evidence 3: specific but vendor-award, self-reported metrics, with no financial linkage.
  • Durability 4: continuous since c.2009–2011 (TaxAlmanac 2005), surviving three platform moves, but on a declining activity curve.
