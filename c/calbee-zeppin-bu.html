TL;DR

  • What it is: A closed, free, anonymous-handle fan community for Zeppin Kappa Ebisen, Calbee's premium shrimp-cracker line made to go with alcohol. It opened in June 2022 on the Japanese SaaS platform Commune and has always been small: 618 members in Nov 2023 and 1,600+ in Feb 2026 (Calbee/PR Times, Dec 2023; Calbee, May 2026).
  • Why people come back: a light office role-play. Members join a "department", file a "絶品日報" (daily Zeppin report) and "晩酌報告" (evening-drink report), earn points that promote them up an online business card ("promoted to new hire!"), drink with the brand team at online parties, and, most of all, help choose the next flavour. Three fan-designed flavours shipped labelled "ファンとの共創の味" (a flavour co-created with fans) (Dec 2023, Feb 2025, Sep 2025).
  • What Calbee puts in: very little headcount. One brand marketer spends 10–20% of their time on it and replies to every post, three colleagues back them up, and Commune's customer-success team runs a monthly strategy meeting with them (Commune webinar report, Jan 2024). On-pack QR codes, the brand site, X and the Calbee loyalty app bring in new members.
  • Biggest mistake: putting everything on one third-party platform. The 5–7 Oct 2026 Commune breach leaked members' emails and profiles, and the community has been offline for at least a month. The breach also hit the community's high-trust, staff-as-peer feel. There's no sign of a migration or restart plan (Calbee notice, 9 Oct 2026).
  • The lesson: a niche, high-passion product can run a sub-2,000-member community as a standing co-creation and research panel at low cost, and credit on the package makes the payoff visible to members. Treat the community's data, and how long it lasts, as yours to own, not your vendor's.

What the community is

Zeppin-bu ("Zeppin department"), full name 絶品部「やめられない、とまらない」課 (the "can't stop, won't stop" section, after the Kappa Ebisen jingle), is a members-only site at zeppinbu.jp, built on Commune's community platform. Calbee pitches it as "a new department staffed by fans, where ikeoji who love fine drinks and snacks gather" (Zeppin brand site). In Calbee's wording Ikeoji means "a lovable guy who works hard every day for his drink", not a handsome older man. Calbee adds that women who love drinking and Zeppin are "respectfully called ikeoji too" (PR Times, Dec 2023). Registration is free and members use handles; the launch tweet says "匿名登録制" (anonymous registration) (@CalbeeKappaCP, 10 Aug 2022).

Spaces and formats:

  • Boards: 絶品日報 (post whenever you eat Zeppin, spot a new SKU in a store, or try a topping or pairing); 晩酌報告 (any evening drink, snack or favourite bar); イケオジ企画会議 ("ikeoji planning meeting": polls, naming contests, event ideas); and a "#絶品見つけた！" ("found Zeppin!") store-sighting tag (Commune case study, archived Dec 2023; Commune webinar, Jan 2024).
  • Gamification: likes, comments and posts earn points that change the job title on a member's online business card. Points can also be exchanged for original goods (Commune webinar, Feb 2023; AdverTimes, 1 Dec 2023).
  • Events: irregular members-only online drinking parties, online tasting sessions with prototypes mailed to members' homes, and one in-person fan meeting (30 seats) at Commune's own Commune Marché festival in Tokyo on 13 Sep 2025 (Commune Marché report; zeppinbu.jp post, Sep 2025).
  • Unofficial spaces: no evidence found. There is no fan-run subreddit, Discord or forum, and the community is Japanese-only. Public chatter on X is tiny: a full-archive X search returned 19 non-retweet posts mentioning 絶品部 and Kappa Ebisen between June 2022 and Sep 2026.

Status today: suspended. Calbee's 9 Oct 2026 notice says the service is stopped pending Commune's investigation (PDF). On 7 Nov 2026 zeppinbu.jp still redirected to Commune's "臨時メンテナンス中" (temporary maintenance) page, which matches community-zukan's check on 6 Nov (Community Zukan F01).

Origin and strategy

  • Brand problem: Kappa Ebisen (launched 1964) has 99% awareness and near-universal distribution, but by about 2018 sales had stalled. It lacked preference: few people asked for it by name, and it was being dragged into discount price wars. Brand marketer Yuto Odawa (小垰勇人), who joined from a drinks maker in 2019, heard from sales that the brand "might disappear from shelves". He framed the fix with Tsuyoshi Morioka's awareness × distribution × preference model (THE CALBEE note, May 2025; Commune webinar, Feb 2023).
  • Product first: Zeppin launched in April 2020 as a thicker, harder, shrimp-heavier cracker built on a "premium salt × shrimp × ingredient that goes with drinks" formula. Its target, unusually for Calbee, was a narrow demographic: men in their 50s drinking at home during COVID (AdverTimes, Dec 2023).
  • Signal of passion: calls to the consumer hotline showed fans buying by the case, emptying shelves, or touring "30 stores with an empty bag in hand". A research survey that asked for photos brought back many beaming middle-aged men holding beer and Zeppin, and the photos were reused in sales decks (Commune case study).
  • The push: Yo-Ho Brewing's fan-marketing lead told Odawa, "Have you actually met these fans? Meeting fans has a hundred benefits and no harm" (百利あって一害なし). Calbee's marketing division was also pushing digital fan marketing at the time. Odawa picked Commune after "seeing their TV commercial by chance" and comparing two or three vendors. He liked Commune's pitch that a lasting community raises fan passion over time, and its UI, simple enough for ikeoji who aren't SNS-savvy (Commune case study).
  • Business model link: this is a low-involvement product sold through retailers, with no direct customer relationship. Calbee says the community does four things: (1) deeper voice-of-customer than paid surveys, faster and cheaper; (2) proof for retail buyers that a new SKU has repeat fans ("using fan voices in negotiations raised our adoption rate"); (3) co-created products that drive PR and sales; (4) morale for the brand team (Commune webinar, Jan 2024). Calbee explicitly does not chase member counts: "rather than 1,000 registrants, 100 passionate fans" (Commune webinar, Feb 2023).
  • Champions: Odawa ran it as "ブランド担当のだわちゃん" ("Dawa-chan from the brand team") until he moved to new-business development in April 2025 (THE CALBEE note). Takahiro Shiozaki, Kappa Ebisen brand manager since 2022, spoke for it in 2024. Mariko Kimura of the Kappa Ebisen team fronted the first co-created launch (AdverTimes).

Trajectory: steady growth of about 400–500 members a year, roughly doubling a year after the first co-created SKU and the on-pack recruitment that came with it. What keeps people coming back

  1. A job, not a forum. The office conceit (部/課, 日報 daily reports, 企画会議 planning meetings, a business card with a job title that rises as you earn points) gives middle-aged salarymen a playful identity they already understand. Members show it off: "Got promoted to new hire in the Kappa Ebisen Zeppin department (lol)… #my business card is ready" (X, 16 Nov 2023).
  2. Low-effort daily rituals. You don't need to write anything clever: snap your evening drink and post it to 晩酌報告, or report a store sighting. These posts double as free shelf-availability data for Calbee.
  3. Co-creation cycles with real stakes. Each round runs on the same funnel: an open call for flavour ideas (50, 47, 74 entries), Calbee shortlists 4 (3 when Suntory's brewer joined), members vote online down to 2, prototypes are mailed to project members' homes for an online tasting, and a final vote picks the winner. Members also vote on packaging. Each round takes about a year, so there's always a "next one" (PR Times, Aug 2025; Commune webinar, Jan 2024). The pack carries a co-creation badge, so participants can point to the shelf.
  4. Staff as drinking buddies. Online parties "start with a kanpai, beer in hand, with no company-vs-customer distinction". Odawa posted under the nickname 「だわちゃん」 and shared failures from development and behind-the-scenes stories from talent shoots. Calbee replies to every post (Commune case study).
  5. Tangible belonging. Branded polo shirts went to members: "The Zeppin-bu polo arrived. It really feels like a school club" (X, 4 Oct 2023). There's a point-exchange catalogue, and the brand site promotes the community.
  6. Feedback visibly closed. Members' "want to eat it again" poll (Aug 2024) brought back the first co-created flavour (scallop) for a third run in May 2025 (Calbee, May 2025). Calbee also promised to post "here's how we used your input" when it renewed the packaging (Commune webinar, Jan 2024).

What's missing: no streaks, no peer-led groups, no regular weekly cadence (events are "irregular"), no regional meetups, no ambassador tier, no in-product hook beyond the on-pack link. Switching costs are nil, and so are member-built assets apart from posts.

The content engine

  • Company-made content inside the community: brand-manager posts (product news, development stories), polls and surveys (favourite flavour, pack designs, community name), co-creation calls (for example "【超重要✨コメント募集】共創品企画第３弾始動！", "super important, ideas wanted: co-creation round 3 kicks off", which promised idea-submitters a seat in the project, about a year before an autumn launch (zeppinbu.jp, Sep 2024)), and event invitations.
  • Outside content about the community: Calbee's corporate note blog (THE CALBEE), press releases that end with a recruitment blurb and member count, and a steady flow of Commune-produced webinars (Feb 2023, Jan 2024, re-run Feb 2025), case studies and articles. Much of what the public knows about Zeppin-bu is vendor marketing, which this report treats as company claims.
  • Member-made content: photos of evening drinks, pairings (for example Ghana chocolate with Kappa Ebisen (X, 30 Oct 2023)), store sightings, flavour ideas. Points reward it; the strongest reward is getting your idea or vote onto a national product. Calbee reuses member photos and voices in sales decks and in a media briefing where fans spoke to journalists (Commune webinar, Jan 2024).
  • Cadence: low and lumpy. Engagement peaks around co-creation calls, tasting sessions and launches.

Content engine score 2/5: a friendly staff voice and surveys, but no regular programming, education or library of member content.

People, programs and governance

  • Staffing: the Kappa Ebisen brand team runs it with no dedicated community manager. In Feb 2023 the team was three brand managers, "none of them dedicated". By Jan 2024 one brand marketer led it at 10–20% of their time, more during co-creation projects, with three others following along (Commune webinars, Feb 2023 / Jan 2024). Commune acts as a "strategic partner": monthly meetings, ideas for raising activity, help planning events. R&D staff join co-creation tasting calls, but there's otherwise "no major collaboration with development". Staff investment 2/5.
  • Partners: Suntory's brewers co-judged round 2, a flavour designed to go with Kinmugi 75% sugar-off. Calbee chose that beer because it was Zeppin's #1 co-purchased item in point-of-sale data. Calbee had assumed premium beer until community chatter showed most fans drank cheap "third beer"; POS data then confirmed it (AdverTimes, Jan 2025; Nissyoku, Feb 2025).
  • Ambassador or superuser program: none formal. Co-creation project members (~40 in round 1) work as a de facto inner circle. Member ownership 4/5: members pick flavours and packs that ship nationally with credit, but don't run anything themselves.
  • Rules and moderation: no public evidence found beyond anonymous handles and Commune's standard tools.

Growth loops

  • The product is the ad: "Most inflow comes from the product." Calbee "doesn't advertise much", and from early 2023 the back of the pack has carried a Zeppin-bu invitation (Commune webinar, Feb 2023). Other routes in: the Kappa Ebisen and Zeppin brand sites, @CalbeeKappaCP, and push notices from the Calbee Ruby Program loyalty app.
  • Co-creation PR: every co-created launch got national trade and consumer press (Sankei, AdverTimes, Impress Gourmet Watch, Nikkei Compass). Calbee says round 1 brought ad-equivalent value of 200%+ compared with the same year's smoked-chilli SKU, and that new buyers and new members arrived because of it (Commune webinar, Jan 2024).
  • Cross-brand reach: the Suntory collab put Zeppin in beer aisles and in front of Kinmugi drinkers in their 30s–50s (AdverTimes, Jan 2025).
  • Word of mouth: members said they recommended the co-created flavour to friends and bought several bags. SEO and social sharing are negligible because the community is closed and the public X footprint is tiny.

Evidence of business impact

Hard evidence: none independently verified. Everything below comes from Calbee or Commune and is unaudited.

  • First co-created flavour (瀬戸の塩と帆立貝柱味, Dec 2023) sold 130% of the same year's 燻製唐辛子味 (smoked chilli) limited edition, with ad-equivalent value of 200%+ (Commune webinar, Jan 2024; Commune/PR Times, Feb 2025).
  • The scallop flavour was re-released in Sep 2024 and again in May 2025 after topping a member poll. It entered a Merchandising-On new-product ranking (28 May–3 Jun 2025) (Calbee; ichioshi).
  • In a recommendation-intent survey that included Calbee long-sellers and competitors, Zeppin ranked near the top despite ~30% awareness. Calbee concedes it "can't say this is because of the community" (Commune case study).
  • Calbee says it found that members with high passion and recommendation scores buy far more Zeppin per year, but no numbers were published.
  • The series itself survived. The company frames keeping a new line alive inside a 60-year brand as "the biggest result" (THE CALBEE note).
  • No evidence found of churn, LTV, NPS figures, earnings-call mentions or academic study. Business impact evidence 2/5.

What they did right

  1. Started from real, observed passion. They built a community after hotline calls and photo surveys showed fans were already obsessive, not to manufacture interest (case study).
  2. Picked a sharp identity. "Ikeoji" plus office role-play is specific, funny and self-flattering. The tongue-in-cheek inclusion of women kept it from being exclusionary (PR Times).
  3. Gave the community a recurring, consequential job. Three national co-created SKUs in about two years, with visible on-pack credit and a clear funnel where Calbee filters for feasibility and fans make the final pick (PR Times, Aug 2025).
  4. Used the community as a research instrument and checked it against hard data. The "third beer" finding was triangulated with POS data, and that led to the Suntory partnership (Commune webinar, Jan 2024).
  5. Measured passion, not headcount. Choosing "100 passionate fans over 1,000 registrants" kept a tiny team focused on replying to everyone (Commune webinar, Feb 2023).
  6. Turned the community into trade-marketing ammunition. Fan voices helped get the SKU onto retail shelves, which is the real bottleneck for a snack.

What they did wrong

  1. Total dependence on one vendor, and the breach that followed. Between 5 and 7 Oct 2026 an attacker obtained a login-signing key shared by every Commune community, posed as admins, and pulled member data. Commune's later count was 166,505 member records across its clients (125,601 including emails). Zeppin-bu members' emails, display names, usernames, bios, profile fields and group memberships were exposed (Calbee notice; piyolog; Human Hacker, Oct 2026). Commune rolled every community's database back to 6 Oct 18:00, with DMs, likes and new sign-ups not restorable. It began reopening communities on request from 8 and 16 Oct, but Zeppin-bu was still down on 7 Nov. For a community whose value is staff–fan trust, this is the most serious setback in its history, and Calbee didn't say how many members were affected.
  2. No owned exit or data strategy. Calbee's notice routes members' questions and withdrawal requests to Commune's contact form. Calbee's other snack communities are meanwhile consolidating onto its own Ruby Program app plus Asobica's coorum: Kataage moved in 2024, and Jagarico relaunched as じゃがりこじま in Sep 2026 after starting on Commune in 2023 (Asobica, Jul 2024; Gourmet Press, Sep 2026). Zeppin-bu sits on an island.
  3. Thin staffing and dependence on one person. It runs as a side job for the brand team, and the founder-host "だわちゃん" left the team in April 2025. Parasocial ties built on one marketer are fragile, and later posts lost the named persona.
  4. Momentum is unclear after the 2026 relaunch. In May 2026 the line was rebranded from "salt × ingredient" to "Japanese ingredient × flavour" with new packaging, and neither 2026 SKU (May, Aug) is labelled co-created (SSNP, May 2026; Yahoo/SSNP, Aug 2026). No fourth co-creation round was found. Community-zukan likewise "found no 2026 activity" (F01).
  5. Evidence lives in vendor marketing. Most metrics come from Commune webinars and case pages, and Commune has since taken its case-study pages down (404 as of Nov 2026). Calbee itself has published no hard numbers on retention or repeat purchase.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Recruit from the product itself. An invite on the pack or in the app brings in actual users, not sweepstakes hunters, and needs no ad budget.
  2. Give the community a recurring decision with visible consequences. Run the idea call → company shortlist → member vote → home test → final vote loop on a yearly cycle, and credit members in the shipped product. Split the roles: the company filters for feasibility, members make the final pick.
  3. Wrap participation in a role-play your audience already understands. Department, daily report, job title on a business card: status and rituals that cost almost nothing to build.
  4. Have a named staff host who replies to everything and drinks with members. Treat members as peers ("同志", comrades) and share failures and behind-the-scenes stories.
  5. Use the community as a fast research panel and check it against hard data. Then use the evidence externally, with retailers, partners and the press.

Avoid these

  1. Don't let a SaaS vendor hold your members' trust and data with no exit plan. Know where your member list lives, how you'll contact members, and how you'd restore the space if the vendor goes down or is breached.
  2. Don't hang the community on one person's side-job time. Plan the handover when the founder-host moves on, or the bond and the cadence go with them.
  3. Don't let co-creation stop when the product strategy changes. A repositioning is the moment to bring the community in, not to go quiet.
