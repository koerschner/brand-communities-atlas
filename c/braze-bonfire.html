TL;DR

  • What it is. Braze's practitioner community for lifecycle and CRM marketers who use its customer-engagement platform. It launched in Q4 2019 as a Slack workspace, grew to 5,300+ members and 180,000+ messages by mid-2022, then moved in October 2022 to a web forum at community.braze.com (Khoros), and again in April 2026 to Higher Logic Vanilla. Around it sit Bonfire Meetups, AMAs, the Customer Champions advocacy program, monthly "Marketer of the Month" features and a "Bonfire Community Leader of the Year" award at the Forge conference (braze.com, Jun 2022; welcome post).
  • Why people came back. In the Slack era, the habit of a tool already open on every marketer's desktop: fast peer answers to fiddly technical problems (Liquid templating, Connected Content, Canvas journeys), topic channels, and AMAs where "the product managers [are] in there." Members also used it to hire and to find jobs.
  • What Braze feeds it. A staffed team (five people in 2026, under a VP of Community & Learning), AMAs, Masterclasses, a Book Club, video "Firesides" with Braze's president, a ranks-and-badges system, monthly leaderboards, challenges with Forge tickets as prizes, regional meetups, certifications, and a points-based advocacy program that turns active members into references and speakers.
  • The biggest mistake. Shutting down a busy Slack community for a web forum. Message volume fell by more than an order of magnitude (from roughly 8,000 Slack messages a month in 2021–22 to roughly 135 forum posts a month in 2022–26; estimate). The Slack history was locked away ("Access is Denied"), members kept asking for Slack back through 2026, and Braze stopped putting a member count in its 10-K after January 2023.
  • The one lesson. For a B2B practitioner community, where it lives is the retention mechanic. Chat in a tool people already have open gives speed and ambient presence that a destination forum can't replace with badges. If you have to move for searchability, data ownership or SEO, keep a real-time layer, and migrate the history and the regulars first.

What the community is

Braze (Nasdaq: BRZE) sells a customer-engagement platform (cross-channel messaging, journey orchestration, AI decisioning) to 2,609 customers as of January 31, 2026 (FY2026 10-K). Its users are CRM, lifecycle, growth and marketing-technology people at consumer brands. "Braze Bonfire" is the umbrella brand for every space where they gather:

  • Bonfire Slack (Q4 2019 to about Oct 2022): the original community at brazebonfire.slack.com, with channels such as #campaigns-and-use-cases, #braze-product-hub, #tech-stacks, #ama and #book-club (SMB article, Nov 2021; Book Club post, 2020).
  • Bonfire web forum (Oct 19, 2022 onward) at community.braze.com, first on Khoros (the "/t5/" URLs), then on Higher Logic Vanilla from April 13, 2026 (links resolve via braze.vanillacommunities.com). Today's categories: Channels, Data & AI, Orchestration, Braze Learning, Braze News, Connection Lounge, The Job Board, Ask Me Anything, plus private groups such as the Bonfire Expert Council, a "Small But Mighty" group for small CRM teams, the Creative Collective and a Tech for an Equitable Future group (categories page; community groups).
  • IRL: Bonfire Meetups in NYC, San Francisco, Chicago, Berlin, London and Melbourne, often paired with Braze Learning workshops, plus a Bonfire takeover of the Braindate lounge at Forge 2026 (SF meetup 2025; Berlin; Melbourne 2024; Forge 2026 agenda).
  • Advocacy: Braze Firebrands (300+ customers in 2021–22), now the Braze Customer Champions program, where points for advocacy unlock perks (S-1; Champions page).
  • Unofficial spaces: Email Geeks Slack, where a member said in 2023 there were "over 700 Braze specialists" (thread), and a "Braze Slack Community" started by a London-based Braze partner-success employee, which "just ticked over 60+ Braze users" in 2026 (LinkedIn). The subreddit r/braze is unrelated (a Lithuanian school meme sub with 92 subscribers; Arctic Shift). No fan-run Braze subreddit exists.

Status: active and staffed, but small. The coordinator's description ("practitioner community and events for B2C marketers, a peer of Klaviyo's") holds; start year verified as 2019.

Origin and strategy

Braze (formerly Appboy) launched Bonfire in Q4 2019. An early job ad framed the reasoning: "In past years at dozens of events, we've seen our customers learn so much by trading strategies and interacting with one another… Braze Bonfire is the fabric which knits all of our customer events and relationships together into a cohesive whole." The community manager was to work with Marketing, PR, Customer Success, Sales, Partnerships, Customer Education and Product, and to "drive more engagement and leadership from our Firebrands (Customer Advocates)" (job ad, via r.jina.ai). The name was deliberate: "a bonfire is an invitation to gather together and share our stories… the more people contribute to a bonfire, the greater and the brighter it burns" (braze.com).

The business case runs through product adoption and advocacy, not support deflection. Braze sells high-touch enterprise contracts, and its revenue depends on net retention and expansion. In the 2021 S-1, Braze listed "Customer Engagement Expertise and Highly Engaged Community" as a strength, with Bonfire members using it "to give direct feedback to our product and engineering teams," and Firebrands "serving as references to prospective customers" (S-1, Oct 2021). Then-CMO Sara Spivey credited Bonfire with "building strong relationships between our customers," especially during the pandemic (MoneyInc, Nov 2021).

Champions inside Braze: the first community manager was Maddie Bertschmann (credited by members as "the OG"), then Maggie Brennan, who "run[s] all things Community at Braze" in 2022 (intro post; Allan Heo, LinkedIn, Oct 2023). In March 2023 Elizabeth Kinsey, who had built the Slack Community at Slack and the Mobile Growth Community at Branch, joined to lead Community & Advocacy (post, May 2023). In 2026 the function sits under Will Crocker Hay, VP of Community & Learning (later titled VP, Growth), who in August 2026 was hiring a Senior Director to own "our Bonfire community and its meetups, the Customer Champions advocacy program, and our executive Customer Advisory Boards" (LinkedIn; job ad).

Trajectory. Slack grew steadily: about 115,000 messages in the 14 months from April 2021 to June 2022, roughly 8,000 a month (estimate). On the forum, Khoros message IDs ran from about 14 to about 5,580 between October 2022 and March 2026, about 135 posts a month, and that count includes staff announcements, event posts and job ads (estimate). After the April 2026 relaunch, discussion IDs went from 5,680 to about 5,939 and comment IDs from about 5,646 to about 6,015 in six to seven months, so roughly 40 new threads and 55 replies a month (estimate). Chat messages and forum posts aren't equivalent units, but the gap is far too large to explain by format alone. Peak activity was mid-2022. Since then the community has rebuilt a small, steady core but has never come back to that level.

What keeps people coming back

Slack-era drivers (2019–2022).

  • Speed and ambient presence. "Within an hour, he got a direct message from another community member who figured out the fix he needed" (Sean Collins, Bilt Rewards, on a Liquid problem; Braze, Jan 2023). After the move one member summed up what was lost: "Slack was easier because it was on our desktops and telephones" (Jan 2024).
  • Searchable shared memory. "One of the benefits of a Slack channel is you have historical documentation… I use the search feature all the time" (Barry James, YNAB; Nov 2021).
  • Access to the vendor. "I love the AMAs. No other Slack channel has AMAs or has the product managers in there" (Griffin Bohm, Pinger; same source).
  • Cohort rituals. The Bonfire Book Club (April–May 2020) met every two weeks in #book-club, with a customer speaker each session (Delivery Hero opened), structured around acquisition, engagement, retention and loyalty (Braze). AMAs and Masterclasses followed through 2021 on topics such as holiday campaigns, travel in 2021 and iOS 15 (AMA; iOS 15 Masterclass).
  • Careers. Members hired through it ("It's been a great source of hiring for me," Georgia Price, Pressed) and peers from other companies set up knowledge-sharing calls (Flink).

Forum-era drivers (2022–).

  • Status and progress. An 11-step rank ladder from New User to Visionary (6,000 points), with points for questions, replies, accepted answers and reactions, badges on profiles, and since May 25, 2026 a Top Contributors leaderboard (ranks article). Power users responded: "my goal was to get to the Authority rank as soon as possible… there were ranks Practitioner 1, 2, and 3, as well as Specialist 1, 2, and 3 💀" (Allan Heo; LinkedIn, Oct 2023).
  • Recognition. A monthly "Around the Bonfire" recap names gold, silver and bronze champions and a list of top posters (April 2026). A Torchie award for "Bonfire Community Leader of the Year" goes to the most helpful regular at Forge: David O'Reilly in 2024 ("nearly 150 posts and 285 sign-ins this year"), Allan Heo in 2025 (15 Braze certifications) and Nirnay Polaboina in 2026 (2024; 2025; 2026 press release). "Marketer of the Month" articles and live hangouts run every month (Oct 2026).
  • Challenges with real prizes. The Canvas Challenge (Jul–Aug 2026) offered a limited-edition badge, a week on the homepage and Customer Champions points. An August challenge offered Forge tickets for posts, and a Halloween challenge offered a speaking slot at the first Bonfire Digital Meetup (Forge challenge; Sept recap).
  • Credential stacking. Bonfire, Braze Learning certifications (5,000+ certified; 890 certifications earned onsite at Forge 2025) and Champions points are being wired into one ladder: "learner → community participant → advocate → champion" (Braze Learning; Forge; job ad). For consultants the badges sell work: Heo says "Bonfire is the reason For Now Marketing exists."
  • Switching costs. Mostly personal ones: certifications, rank and reputation tied to Braze skills, and a job board for Braze-skilled people. No evidence that community participation feeds the product itself.

The content engine

  • Live formats: Bonfire AMAs (Slack #ama, then a forum category with 58 AMA threads, including email deliverability with Braze's deliverability principal), Masterclasses, the four-part Book Club, Marketer of the Month hangouts and APAC roundtables (AMA category; events).
  • Video: "Bonfire Firesides" (2023), interviews of customer CRM leads (Flink, Bilt, Pressed, CarpeDM) by Braze President and CCO Myles Kleeger (Wayback, Jun 2023).
  • Editorial: monthly Marketer of the Month articles on braze.com, the monthly "Around the Bonfire" recap (since April 2026), product-launch roundups (e.g., Forge 2026 launches), and Braze Learning roundups (BFCM prep) cross-posted into the forum.
  • Education: Braze Learning paths and certifications, including 2026 BrazeAI Engagement Specialist and Technical Marketing Specialist certifications announced inside Bonfire, and free certifications for laid-off marketers (Braze Learning article).
  • Member-made content: the "Campaigns That Made You Look" gallery and community blog (2022), the Creative Collective blog run with Braze's 427° Innovation Lab (Nov 2024 on), and a library of Canvas Challenge entries with real architectures (a Disney Streaming monthly recap built with Catalogs and Canvas Context; a real-money-gaming loyalty engine) (Creative Collective; Canvas winner). The Canvas Challenge was conceived by members Anne Schultheis and Allan Heo, not staff (results post).

Cadence is steady (monthly features, monthly recap, AMAs a few times a year, quarterly meetups), but most of it is Braze marketing content reused for the community rather than content born in it. Content engine 3/5: well produced and regular, but thinly connected to everyday peer threads.

People, programs and governance

  • Team: in 2026, five people: digital community, advocacy (a principal and a specialist), and community and learning events (a manager and a specialist), under a VP of Community & Learning (job ad). A "Community Manager, Digital" role to "own strategy and execution for our digital community, Braze Bonfire" was posted in October 2025 (Greenhouse). The current moderator, stefanie.baldwin, runs the recaps, challenges and feedback threads.
  • Turnover: the forum shows earlier leads Maggie Brennan and Elizabeth Kinsey as "Braze Alumni" by November 2026. Kinsey announced the April 2026 migration herself, and Braze was recruiting a Senior Director to lead the function from August 2026, which suggests a leadership change in mid-2026 (inference).
  • Superuser layers: the Bonfire Expert Council ("our most active community members trusted to provide feedback"), Customer Champions (points for speaking, case studies, reference calls and reviews; perks include early access, VIP invites, swag and free event passes), and the Supporter rank (500 points), which comes with an invitation to the Champions program (Champions article).
  • Rules: a Code of Conduct dating from 2019, community guidelines, and terms of use for the community platform (Oct 2022). New users can't send direct messages until a moderator approves their first post (ranks article).
  • Feedback loop: an "Ideas for Braze" tag, a "Features of the Future" poll (36% voted for a monthly Digital Meetup, which became the November 2026 pilot), and a member's suggestion to match experts to unanswered questions that became "Follow Topics I'm Interested In." Product feedback to Braze's engineers is claimed in every 10-K, but there is no public ideas portal with statuses.

Staff investment 4/5: a dedicated, multi-role team for seven years, three platforms and a global meetup program. Member ownership 2/5: members build challenges and answer questions, but every platform decision was imposed against what vocal members asked for.

Growth loops

  • Customer onboarding: Bonfire is pitched to every customer ("When you partner with us, you join thousands of forward-thinking marketers"), so growth tracks Braze's customer base rather than bringing in new customers (Wayback 2021).
  • Career network effects: the Job Board is the most-followed category (61 followers) and draws posts from outside Braze's customer base (freelance gigs, bank roles). Practitioners who change jobs bring Braze into new companies; that's plausible, but no evidence quantifies it.
  • Advocacy to acquisition: active members become Champions, then references, case studies, Forge speakers and G2 reviewers. In December 2022 the community team asked members to review Braze on G2 in exchange for a gift (post).
  • SEO: the forum era makes answers indexable (threads such as "Migration from SFMC to Braze" rank in search), which Slack never could. At about 1,450 threads, the effect is small.
  • Events: meetups co-hosted at customers' offices (Delivery Hero in Berlin, Cloudflare in San Francisco) and the Forge conference (about 2,700 attendees in 2025 by a third-party estimate; Vendelux).

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: none public. Braze has never disclosed churn, NRR or expansion for Bonfire members versus non-members. Its own 2026 job ad says the programs are "healthy, growing, and genuinely loved… What they are not yet is fully wired into how Braze retains and grows customers," and lists "support deflection measured" and "share of at-risk and expansion accounts touched by community" as goals to build, so they weren't measured at the time (job ad).
  • Filings: the S-1 and FY2022–FY2023 10-Ks give member counts (3,000+, 5,000+, 7,000+) as a competitive strength. From the FY2024 10-K on, the number becomes "thousands," which suggests the post-migration figure was no longer flattering (inference) (FY2023 10-K; FY2026 10-K).
  • Company-curated anecdotes: saving "at least two hours" on one problem (YNAB), two hires (Pressed), solving a 40,000-SKU catalog import (Wine.com), Content Blocks automation learned from a peer startup (Flink) (SMB article; expansion article).
  • Adjacent recognition: Braze's customer success team won Customer Success Team of the Year in 2022 (CS Collective), not specific to Bonfire.

Business impact evidence 2/5.

What they did right

  1. Started where the users already were. Slack in 2019 got Bonfire to 2,700 members and 65,000 messages within about 18 months and 180,000 messages by mid-2022, with almost no tooling cost.
  2. Put product people in the room. AMAs with PMs and the deliverability team were the feature members named as unique ("No other Slack channel has AMAs or has the product managers in there").
  3. Made helpers famous. A stage award at Forge, monthly champion lists, Marketer of the Month, homepage spotlights and Forge tickets give status that's worth something on a résumé. Heo built a consultancy on it, and each year's winner is a regular, steady answerer rather than a celebrity customer.
  4. Let members design rituals. The 2026 Canvas Challenge came from two members, and the Digital Meetup came from a poll. Both show the team listening.
  5. Tied the community to careers. The job board, LinkedIn login (2022), certifications and hiring stories made Bonfire a professional network for "Braze people," which makes the community useful even between product questions.
  6. Hired seasoned community leadership. Kinsey came from building Slack's own community, and the 2026 role is explicitly framed around KPIs (engagement, advocacy influence dollars, at-risk accounts touched).

What they did wrong

  1. Killed the Slack community (Oct 2022) without keeping a real-time layer. Members said so plainly: "I'm really sad the Braze Slack community was disbanded. Getting ideas or help in Bonfire is very difficult, it seems like the audience is less engaged or the entire Slack community didn't move over" (Aug 2023). Braze replied that it had no plans to go back to Slack and closed the thread to replies (Jan 2024). The same complaint came back after the 2026 relaunch: members still miss "the amount of different channels," and one account executive's customers "miss the old Slack community" (feedback thread, Apr 2026). The result: activity fell more than tenfold (estimate), the forum had only 1,160 members in May 2023 against 5,300 on Slack, and the most valuable regulars partly left for Email Geeks and an employee-run Slack.
  2. Lost the history. Members asking for old Slack threads were pointed to a "Bonfire Archive" that returned "Access is Denied" (Jun 2023) (thread). Three years of searchable answers, which members had called the main benefit, became unreachable.
  3. Two platform moves in under four years. Braze moved from Slack to Khoros (2022) and then to Vanilla (2026). Braze admitted that the Khoros forum "can be clunky. Sometimes logging in is challenging. Sometimes it's tough to find what you're looking for" (Mar 2026). The second move meant password resets, retired groups and a week of read-only, and it launched without tag-following.
  4. Metrics went quiet. The 10-K went from precise counts to "thousands," and public member and post counters on the homepage disappeared after 2024. That makes it hard to hold the program accountable, inside or outside the company.
  5. Programs at small scale. The headline 2026 challenge drew nine entries from eight people in a customer base of thousands of companies. The AMA category's last post was June 2025, so AMAs, the format members praised most, appear to have lapsed until the Digital Meetup pilot.
  6. Leaning on advocacy asks. Gifts for G2 reviews and points for reference calls blur peer community and marketing pipeline. There's no documented backlash, but it reinforces the sense that the community serves Braze's go-to-market.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Launch in the chat tool your users already have open. For practitioners in Slack all day, a Slack community gets to thousands of members and a daily habit fast. Bonfire's best growth came before it had any forum features.
  2. Make product staff a scheduled, visible presence. Recurring AMAs with PMs and specialists (deliverability, iOS changes, holiday peaks) were the format members called unique.
  3. Give helpers status that's worth something outside the community. A stage award at your conference, monthly named champions, homepage spotlights and free conference tickets cost little and create career capital, which keeps the top 1% answering.
  4. Let members invent the rituals. Member-designed challenges and poll-chosen formats give regulars ownership. Start with them.
  5. Tie learning, community and advocacy into one ladder (certification, then participation, then champion), but measure it against account health from day one rather than retrofitting it years later.

Avoid these

  1. Don't shut a lively chat community down for a forum. If you need search, SEO or data ownership, add the forum alongside, sync or export the history, and move the regulars before you switch anything off. Bonfire's activity never recovered.
  2. Don't lose the archive. Members valued searchable history most, and an "Access is Denied" archive tells them their contributions were disposable.
  3. Don't stop publishing your numbers. When the metric quietly vanishes from filings and the homepage, it's a sign the program has no internal scoreboard. Define engagement and retention KPIs before a migration so you can see what it costs.
