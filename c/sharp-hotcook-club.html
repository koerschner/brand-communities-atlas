TL;DR

  • What it is: Sharp's official owners' community for the Healsio Hotcook, a waterless automatic cooking pot. It runs as a white-label site and app on Commune, a Japanese community SaaS, and opened on 25 May 2020. Anyone can read it; posting needs a Sharp COCORO ID with a registered Hotcook. It grew to 26,000+ members by August 2025 (Commune case).
  • Why people come back: a recipe appliance goes stale once the owner has cooked the booklet. Other owners' recipes, photos ("フォトくっく") and troubleshooting answers revive use. Sharp ties this into the product: member recipes can be downloaded to the pot as cloud menus, and 15 have become numbered official menus (No. 501–515) (tarosuke.jp).
  • What Sharp feeds it: staff prompts from a mascot account ("クックにゃん"), online cooking classes with chefs and creators, a few lottery-seat fan meetings, comics drawn from members' stories, cross-brand collaborations (Ajinomoto Cook Do) and a Guinness World Records stunt for the 10th anniversary (194 owners opening their pots at once online, 8 Nov 2025).
  • Biggest mistake: total dependence on a single outsourced platform, with no dedicated team. When Commune was breached on 5–6 Oct 2026, every Commune community went into maintenance. A month later (7 Nov 2026) Hotcook Club is still offline and I found no public statement from Sharp.
  • The lesson: make the community the product's content pipeline (member recipe → device-downloadable menu → official menu), and you get retention and R&D from a tiny team. But own your data, your fallback channel and your incident communications.

What the community is

  • Owned space: ホットクック部 ("Hotcook Club", literally "Hotcook club" in the Japanese school-club sense: members are 部員, "club members"). It lives at hotcook.commmune.com and in the Commune app (community code "hotcook"). Its sections are Recipes (original, arrangements of official recipes, beginner, "heavy-rotation meals", fermentation and low-temperature), Photo-cook (Instagram-style photo posts), Talk (tips, free talk, troubleshooting), surveys, news, columns and a "Hotcook Club improvement committee" (ホットクック部向上委員会) box. I took this layout from the page's embedded config in an archived snapshot (Wayback, Sept 2025).
  • Access model: reading is public and open to people who don't own a Hotcook, on purpose, so shoppers can browse. Posting requires a COCORO ID with a Hotcook registered by model and serial number (Sharp PR, May 2020; jp.sharp/hotcook/community). Login is single sign-on through Sharp's COCORO MEMBERS identity service.
  • Who runs it: at launch, SHARP COCORO LIFE's solution development unit (Nakano) with an admin persona "れいちぇる＠SHARP" (Sharp blog, Jul 2020). Later it moved to the domestic kitchen product-planning team (Masako Nishihashi, Mari Yoshida). It is run "as one of the product-planning department's duties, not by a dedicated team," with Commune's customer-success staff co-running it (Commune case).
  • Unofficial spaces: X and Instagram hashtags (#ホットクック, #ホットクック部), recipe-book authors and creators. The largest is the couple behind "たろすけさん家" (blog, YouTube, X; 40,000+ combined followers per tarosuke.jp), whom Sharp hires as guests rather than competing with. There's also a fan-built "Hotcook Recipe Finder" that indexed about 280 Hotcook Club recipes (X @hotcook_finder). Reddit presence is negligible: the product is Japan-only, and only stray mentions exist in r/japanlife.
  • Status today: the community grew steadily through 2025. Since 6 Oct 2026 it has been offline because of the Commune breach (see "What they did wrong").

Origin and strategy

  • The product problem. The Hotcook launched in November 2015 as a new category. Sharp says the first 3–4 years were a struggle. Early buyers skewed older rather than the young families Sharp had targeted. Sharp added Wi-Fi and COCORO KITCHEN cloud recipes in 2017, and word of mouth ("convenient, hands-off, tasty") took off after tasting events (Business Insider Japan; Commune webinar report).
  • The pitch came from the vendor. Commune's CEO Yuya Takada says some of his own staff owned Hotcooks they couldn't use well, so Commune proposed a community to Sharp (Sharp blog, Jul 2020). Sharp's reasons, in Nakano's words: connect beginners with veterans, give prospective buyers a place to see what the pot can do, and make the club a "place where problems get solved". He also called it a lower-friction alternative to the support desk and a direct line for owner feedback and surveys.
  • The customer-journey framing (Nishihashi, June 2024). Prospects fear the unknown ("what can it even make?"). New owners can't follow the manual or get bored with the menus. Veterans want to share recipes, and few of them ever contact support. One community addresses all three (Commune webinar).
  • Timing. It launched in May 2020 during Japan's COVID stay-home period, when Hotcook sales tripled year on year and owners were already swapping recipes on social media (Kaden Watch; Commune webinar).
  • Business-model tie: the Hotcook is a one-time hardware sale with no subscription. The community's job is satisfaction, usage frequency, word of mouth, accessory and second-unit sales, and product input. Sharp also gets a branding benefit: "we don't just sell and walk away" (売りっぱなしじゃない) (Commune case).

Read-through. Membership is about 4% of units shipped (26,000 of ~650,000+; my estimate). That's a high share for an appliance, given that posting requires registering the device. Growth was linear rather than viral: about 4,000–5,000 net members a year. Sharp has published no posts-per-day, DAU or MAU figures. Commune's post IDs are global across tenants, so they can't serve as a volume proxy. The leaders say outright that headcount isn't their goal: "just growing the number means nothing… we want deep relationships" (Commune webinar).

What keeps people coming back

  1. The "recipe rut" cure (core loop). Sharp's own diagnosis: owners try lots of menus after purchase, run out of ideas and stop using the pot. Seeing another owner's "you can also do this" post makes them want to cook again (&Fans, Feb 2026). The community exists to fight churn in usage, which is the leading indicator for word of mouth and repurchase.
  2. A product hook: member recipes run on the device. Since the autumn 2020 models, owners can download Hotcook Club recipes through COCORO KITCHEN and have the pot cook them automatically (BCN+R, Sept 2020; jp.sharp). Fifteen member recipes have been promoted to numbered official menus 501–515, labelled "(ホットクック部)" (tarosuke.jp). This is the YNAB-style "content inside the product" pattern, except that the content is member-made.
  3. Points and badges. The welcome post explains that activity earns points and badges, up to a "5,000-point badge" (welcome post snippet). These are Commune's stock gamification features. I found no evidence that members care much about them.
  4. Identity and inside jokes. Members are 部員 ("club members") and the space is a 部 ("club"). Second units are "welcomed" (お迎え) like a family member, and Nishihashi calls the resulting chain of copycat second-unit purchases the "chain of happiness" (Commune case). The cat mascot クックにゃん runs the staff account ("Hotcook Club manager"), appears at events and has inspired fan-made art such as icing cookies (member post, May 2021).
  5. Peer support and a "someone is watching" feeling. Nishihashi contrasts the club with social media: on social media, posting feels risky and no reaction makes people anxious, while in the club "you feel someone will definitely see it" (Commune case). Over time Q&A shifted from staff-led to members answering each other (Commune webinar).
  6. Belonging and loneliness. Sharp frames cooking as lonely, obligatory work and the club as turning it into entertainment: "I'm not alone" (Commune webinar). At the online fan meeting "nobody left even after the scheduled end time" (&Fans).
  7. Scarce IRL access. Lottery seats at Sharp's HEALSIO CAFÉ fan meetings, with product planners explaining parts, tastings, quizzes and prizes, and members who knew each other online meeting for the first time (PR Times; Mynavi).

What's missing: no fixed weekly or monthly ritual, no streaks, no seasons and no formal superuser tier. Retention runs on utility and occasional events, not habit design. (Retention mechanics score 3/5: a strong product hook and identity, but weak cadence and status systems.)

The content engine

  • Member-made (the bulk): recipes with tags (#初心者向け, #低温調理), photo posts and tips. Prolific posters include recipe-book author なお ("頑張らないホットクックレシピ"), whose "paid-quality" recipes appear in the club (tarosuke.jp). A Hotcook creator notes that publishers' Hotcook recipe books peaked around 2020, which he links to how much recipe sharing moved to users (tarosuke.jp).
  • Staff prompts: themed calls such as "heavy-rotation meals", "failure stories" and "#私とホットクックの出会い" (how I met my Hotcook), posted by the admin or mascot account (Kaden Watch; post, Mar 2025). Commune proposes monthly post and campaign ideas to the busy Sharp owner (Commune case).
  • Stories → comics → marketing: in the August 2021 campaign "#わたしのホットクックストーリー", owners posted why they bought a Hotcook (via X or the club). Selected stories were drawn as manga by an Instagram illustrator who is herself an owner, and winners received a new Hotcook (Sharp campaign page; @SHARP_JP). Sharp then posted the manga on Instagram, turning "my story" into "everyone's story" as authentic acquisition content (Commune webinar).
  • Live and online classes: Sharp's "Healsio Kyoshitsu" online classes double as club events. Examples are the たろすけさん家 fan meeting (Sept 2023), chef Inada's Motto Cook curry class (270 sign-ups), an Ajinomoto "Cook Do" collaboration (19 Apr 2025) cross-promoted in Ajinomoto's own Commune community (Ajinomoto community; Sharp event page), and a one-day Hotcook curry house at Erick South (6 Sept 2025) (tarosuke.jp).
  • Tentpoles: a booth at Commune Marché (12–13 Sept 2025) with a vote on popular recipes (tarosuke.jp), and the 10th-anniversary Guinness record, survey microsite and #わたしのホットクックリレー relay campaign on X and Instagram (Sharp news).
  • (Content engine score 4/5: rich, varied and member-led, with a unique path from member content to device menus. It's held back by event cadence and by the lack of a dedicated producer.)

People, programs and governance

  • Staffing: a part-time duty for one or two product planners (Nishihashi also runs in-store promotion and social media), plus Commune's customer-success team. Nishihashi says she couldn't watch daily operations alone and that Commune's support was essential (Commune case). Her internal advice: recruit sympathetic colleagues and "trust them 300%" (Commune webinar). (Staff investment score 2/5.)
  • Moderation and tone: members describe the club as well policed and peaceful, with almost no trolls (tarosuke.jp). Sharp first held back so members would speak freely, found that "if we don't get involved, conversation doesn't take off", and moved to events plus low-branding comments from the mascot (&Fans).
  • No ambassador or MVP program. Instead Sharp borrows existing creators (たろすけさん家, the influencer Miho, the author なお) as guests.
  • Feedback loop (strong):
    • Motto Cook (もっとクック), a spatula-style stirring accessory launched in Oct 2023, came from veterans asking for "more serious cooking". Sharp surveyed the club on the concept, had members test prototypes, and members later asked for a reissue after it sold out (Commune webinar; Business Insider Japan).
    • The inner pot moved from stainless steel to a fluorine (non-stick) coating after "easier cleaning" feedback (&Fans).
    • Other Sharp teams now ask "can we do this in Hotcook Club?" when they need surveys or monitors (Commune case).
    • Leaders admit the trade-off: requests from the core don't always appeal to mainstream buyers (Commune webinar).
  • (Member ownership score 4/5: members produce most of the content and answer most questions, and their recipes ship as official menus. There's no formal member leadership.)

Growth loops

  1. Public recipes as a showroom: browsing without an account lets prospects "picture cooking" before they buy. In-store catalogs and displays pitch "there's Hotcook Club, so you're safe", and inserts in the box once advertised it (Commune webinar; Sharp blog).
  2. The second-unit loop: a member posts about "welcoming" a second unit, others copy, and they post too. Sharp says it never asked anyone to buy (Commune webinar).
  3. Owner word of mouth: a quarter of owners recommend the Hotcook to others, and more than 30% of women in their 20s–30s do (Business Insider Japan). In the 10th-anniversary survey, 55.3% said another user's recommendation prompted their purchase. That sample was recruited through the club and X, so it's biased (Sharp 10th survey). One fan-meeting attendee had persuaded 10 acquaintances to buy (&Fans).
  4. UGC exported to social media: manga, relay campaigns and the Guinness event get PR coverage (Nikkei, ITmedia, Mynavi) far beyond the club.

Evidence of business impact

Hard-ish (verifiable product outcomes): Motto Cook and its 1.6 L follow-up (Aug 2025) were co-developed with members; the non-stick pot change came from feedback; 15 member recipes ship as official cloud menus.

Company claims and surveys (soft):

  • In the Oct 2024 fan-meeting survey of 34 lottery-chosen superfans, 71% said they liked Sharp more and 67% felt more attached to the Hotcook since joining. "About 40%" said the club made them keep using the pot (PR Times).
  • Use three or more times a week rose from ~60% to ~80% "in the latest survey", which Sharp attributes "at least partly" to the club (&Fans). The 82.7% figure comes from the Sept 2025 survey (N=242), which was recruited through the club and X (Sharp 10th survey), so selection bias likely explains much of the rise.
  • Satisfaction is about 90% overall per Sharp's research, and 97.9% in the club-recruited survey.
  • Internal standing: senior management says "Community, nice! The current member count is enough; cherish core customers". Middle management still asks for cost-effectiveness justification (Commune webinar, Jun 2024).

Missing: no revenue, attach-rate, churn or cohort data linking members to non-members, and no filings (Sharp doesn't break out the Hotcook). (Business impact evidence score 2/5.)

What they did right

  1. Wired the community into the device. Member recipes download to the pot and the best become official menus. Contributing has real utility and prestige, and Sharp gets free menu R&D (jp.sharp; tarosuke.jp).
  2. Read-open, write-gated by ownership. Prospects can browse freely, and only verified owners post. Members credit this for a troll-free space (tarosuke.jp).
  3. Aimed at the real churn point. The club targets the post-novelty "recipe rut", not acquisition vanity metrics (&Fans).
  4. Closed the loop visibly. Concept surveys, prototype testing and shipping Motto Cook showed members their input matters (Commune webinar).
  5. Borrowed reach instead of building it. Sharp brought in chefs, YouTubers, an illustrator and another brand's community (Ajinomoto) rather than paying for an in-house content team.
  6. Turned members' stories into marketing with consent and reward (the manga campaign with a free Hotcook as the prize).
  7. Was candid about the right level of involvement. Sharp adjusted from hands-off to light-touch, mascot-voiced participation (&Fans).

What they did wrong

  1. Single-vendor dependency, now an outage. On 5 Oct 2026 attackers abused Commune's private-community invite links and a stolen login signing key to register and impersonate admins. Commune put all communities into maintenance on 6 Oct, rolled every community database back to 6 Oct 18:00, and confirmed leaks affecting a cumulative 166,505 member IDs across tenants (Commune 9 Oct; Commune 21 Oct). Communities have reopened since 16 Oct, browser-only and with features restricted, but only at each client's request (Commune 16 Oct). On 7 Nov 2026 hotcook.commmune.com still redirected to the maintenance page, and I found no Sharp notice to members. Sharp does not appear among the organizations publicly reporting leaks (Security Lab tracker), so I can't say whether Hotcook Club data leaked. Either way, six years of recipes and relationships sit on a third party's infrastructure with no visible fallback channel. Sharp also suffered its own e-commerce breach in 2024, which raises the cost of reassuring members (Sharp notice, Oct 2024).
  2. Under-resourced by design. It's a side duty for one product planner and leans on Commune for monthly ideas and tricky member inquiries (Commune case). That makes the program fragile to staff rotation. The owner has already changed at least once (Nakano → Nishihashi/Yoshida), and growth stalled at around 13,000 members before the new owner re-energized it.
  3. Heavy signup friction. To post, an owner needs a COCORO ID, then the model, serial number and purchase date, often through the COCORO HOME app, whose Wi-Fi and device registration owners frequently struggle with (tarosuke.jp; owner blog, Mar 2023). That likely caps conversion from ~650,000+ owners to ~26,000 members (an inference).
  4. Lost some members to Instagram. While Sharp was working out how the club and social media should split roles, some members moved their activity to Instagram. Sharp chose to accept this rather than fight it (&Fans).
  5. Weak ROI case internally. Middle management keeps asking for cost-effectiveness numbers, and the published evidence is still small superfan surveys (Commune webinar).
  6. Thin evidence and transparency. The public metrics are almost entirely the vendor's marketing (Commune case studies and press releases). Sharp publishes no activity figures.
  7. Possible cannibalization by AI. The July 2026 pro models add "Cook Talk", a generative-AI assistant that answers usage and recipe questions (Sharp news, Jul 2026). That's the same job peer Q&A does in the club. It isn't a mistake yet, but it's a strategic tension Sharp hasn't publicly addressed.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Make member content executable in your product. Let users' best creations (templates, recipes, workflows) run inside the app or device, and promote the best into official content with credit.
  2. Aim the community at your usage cliff. Find when users stall (here, after the booklet recipes run out) and design the feed and prompts to re-spark use at that moment.
  3. Read-open, write-verified. Public browsing turns the community into a sales tool, and an ownership gate keeps quality high and trolls out.
  4. Show the feedback loop. Survey the community on a concept, let members test the prototype, ship it, and announce it there first.
  5. Borrow reach. Invite your fan creators, chefs and adjacent brands to co-host events rather than building an in-house media team.

Avoid these

  1. Don't let a SaaS vendor be your only channel and only operator. Keep exports of your data, a fallback channel (newsletter, social media) and your own incident playbook. A platform breach shouldn't silence you for a month.
  2. Don't run it as a side job. Without a dedicated owner the program stalls when staff rotate, and you can't build the ROI case management will ask for.
  3. Don't put three accounts and a serial number between a happy customer and their first post. Gate on ownership with minimal friction (for example, verify after the first post).
