TL;DR

  • What it is: Orange France's official customer-to-customer help forum at communaute.orange.fr. It has run on Lithium (now Khoros) since August 2013 and replaced an older official forum that members date to about 2009. It covers home internet, Livebox, TV, mobile and, above all, the Orange email service. It had 812,601 registered members on 7 Nov 2026, up from 634,938 in March 2021 [1][23].
  • Why people come back: most visitors arrive once from Google, get an answer and leave. The people who return are a micro-community of "TopMembres": long-time customers, mostly retirees by Orange's own description, who give about a third of all answers [2]. They are kept by status (a #TopMembre badge and a public leaderboard), a private salon with staff, insider previews "in confidence", and, from 2014 to 2019, a yearly MeetUp in Paris with executives [2][7][10].
  • What Orange feeds it: paid webconseillers ("web advisers", now also called "webspécialistes") from customer-service sites in about eight French cities. They have staff badges and access to customer systems, and they pick up threads the volunteers can't solve [12][17]. Orange adds a marketing blog, contests, and staff threads for big changes such as the 2026 Mail Orange redesign [1].
  • Biggest mistake: launching the 2013 platform by wiping the old forum's history "practically without notice". Volunteers called it contempt for "years of voluntary contribution" [5]. The quieter mistake came later: the volunteer program stopped visibly renewing. There is no public MeetUp after #8 in May 2019, the newest wiki tutorials are from 2023-24, and message volume fell by roughly 60% from its 2014-2020 level [8-10][1].
  • The one lesson: a support forum lives on a dozen superusers and a few paid staff who can open the customer's account. Give the volunteers status, access and face time. Pair them with staff who can actually fix the account. And never throw away their archive, because it is both their legacy and your SEO.

What the community is

Communauté Orange is an owned Q&A forum for anyone with an Orange account, customer or not. Reading needs no login. To post, you sign in with your Orange (orange.fr) ID and pick a pseudonym [3]. The homepage organises it into six areas: Internet à la maison, TV d'Orange, Mobile Orange, Mail Orange, "La communauté" (onboarding) and the Lounge Orange social area ("espace de convivialité") [1]. Next to the forum sit a member-written "Trucs et astuces" wiki of tutorials and the "Orange et Vous" blog of product news, games and promotions [1][3].

Who answers [3][12][13]:

  • Members and TopMembres: unpaid customers. Orange stresses that TopMembres "are passionate volunteers, not Orange employees" [10], and moderators edit any post that suggests otherwise [14].
  • Webconseillers / webspécialistes: Orange employees with a "Staff" badge and an Orange logo, working from customer-service sites in Clermont-Ferrand, Rouen, Aix-en-Provence, Anglet, Paris, Villeneuve-d'Ascq, Tours and Narbonne. They "have access to the IT applications to look concretely at each customer case" [2][12].
  • Community managers and moderators: a small team. One CM, Ibtisème, ran both the Orange and Sosh forums in 2025 [2].

The forum sits inside Orange's customer-service stack. It is linked from the contact page ("find the answer on the Orange community forum or ask other users") [29], and its header carries the 2026 "Élu Service Client de l'Année" badge [1][17]. Orange runs a separate sister forum for its low-cost brand, communaute.sosh.fr, which has existed since Sosh launched in 2011 [2][37].

Status, Nov 2026: active. On 7 Nov 2026 the homepage showed 812,601 members and about 1,200 people online. The newest blog posts were Halloween 2026 and a back-to-school challenge, and threads about the redesigned webmail were busy [1][40].

Origin and strategy

  • Before 2013: Orange (the former France Télécom, which absorbed the Wanadoo ISP brand) already had an official help forum. Ibtisème says you would have to "go back to the history of Wanadoo" to trace it [2]. Long-time TopMembres date themselves to "2009, the time of the first forum" [14]. That older forum had a reputation for thin staff presence. In January 2014 one member wrote that on "the old official Orange forum few Orange advisers answered", which is why people used the unofficial forum-orange.com [18].
  • August 2013 relaunch on Lithium: the CM Stéphanie's account was created on 1 Aug 2013 [32]. The first Wayback capture (19 Aug 2013) shows a brand-new Lithium site ("Découvrez votre nouvel espace communautaire") with only a handful of posts per board and a Forum / Wiki / Blog / Concours structure [4]. Members later traced page assets to lithium.com [31]. The old forum's content was not carried over (see "What they did wrong") [5].
  • Strategy: Orange France has never published one. The design and the staffing point to support deflection and SEO self-service. Orange's 2025 description of the user journey: "most people arrive on the forums from their favourite search engine", ask once, and get answered by members, with "web specialists" stepping in when a case needs "business tools" [2]. Sister Orange operations say the goal outright. Orange Romania's community team told Khoros its "main goal is to decrease costs with calls and chat/messenger (call & chat/mess deflection)" [30]. Orange Spain won a 2016 Lithy (Lithium's customer award) for moving to social support on Lithium [30].
  • Organisational home: the forum is staffed from Orange France's digital customer-care ("équipe web réseaux sociaux") [9]. That team also ran @Orange_conseil on Twitter/X from 2010 until Orange stopped support on X on 10 Nov 2025 and moved it to Messenger and Instagram [26]. The forum survived that cut. A separate Social Room inside Communication (8 people, 3 CMs, c. 2020) does listening and crisis monitoring [27].

Trajectory. Volume peaked at roughly 300,000-370,000 messages a year from 2014 to 2020, with a COVID spike, then roughly halved by 2023. It now sits around 120,000-140,000 a year, about 60% below the peak (estimate). New registrations also slowed: about 36,000 a year in 2021-23, about 34,000 in 2024, and about 17,000 from January to early November 2026 (from the member counter [23][1]). The forum is not dying. Search traffic and Orange-side changes keep feeding it, such as the 2026 webmail redesign [40] and the copper/ADSL switch-off. But fewer people now talk to each other there.

Reddit is irrelevant here. r/Sosh had 3 subscribers and 11 posts in total (Arctic Shift, Nov 2026) [38]. French Orange customers who want a second opinion go to lafibre.info's Orange boards or UFC-Que Choisir's forum [25].

What keeps people coming back

The forum serves two populations with very different reasons to return.

1. One-off askers (the vast majority). They come back only when something breaks. The main pull is that the answer already exists and ranks in Google. Orange's CM describes the funnel as search engine, then an existing thread, then sign-up to ask [2]. Unofficial guides say the same: the community is "the right reflex for known outages and fine-grained settings: the problem has often already been documented by another subscriber" [25].

2. TopMembres and regulars (the core). The mechanics that hold them:

  • A named elite status. Orange set up a "TopMembres" group "to clearly identify" its most active helpers (the label was in use by the 2017 MeetUp; the explainer post is dated 31 Oct 2018) [8][34]. Members carry a #TopMembre badge, and the homepage leaderboard of top solution authors mixes TopMembres with staff [1]. Below that sits a long rank ladder based on activity: Nouvelle Recrue, promeneur, contributeur occasionnel, contributeur, contributeur confirmé, passionné, fan, super fan, star, super star, incollable, Orange Star (names observed on posts; full thresholds not published). Members joked from the start: "C'est quoi après ? Super Star Intergalactique ?" [33]; "soon we'll have to sew stripes on our clothes like the military" [6].
  • Insider access. TopMembres "have access to a private salon" with staff. They "are in a certain confidence about new things and the right moment to talk about them" and get pointed to Orange's research panels (cafés numériques run by Le Lab) [2].
  • Face time with Orange. The MeetUp program was announced in May 2014 as "a new programme for contributors… to meet and share more with the most active of you". The first was held on 20 June 2014 at Orange France in Arcueil [7]. There were themed extras, such as a February 2015 meeting with TopMembres and Homelive power users [11]. By MeetUp #6 (May 2017) the event included a Social Room visit and Orange speakers [8]. #7 (June 2018) brought webconseillers from Twitter, Facebook and the Sosh forum [9]. #8 (22 May 2019) had marketing leads demo "Maison Connectée" and the head of Orange's internet-crime unit brief members, plus a session gathering forum suggestions [10]. Orange framed the TopMembres as "a bit your spokespeople" [10]. No MeetUp after #8 turns up anywhere (search found none).
  • Kudos, solutions and badges. Lithium kudos are renamed "Top" ("Une réponse vous a plu ? N'hésitez pas à toper son auteur !"), and badges arrived around 2015 [20][39].
  • Ties to staff. Members know the CMs and webconseillers by first name. In 2014 a regular started a thread just to defend the CM Stéphanie against people who thought she had "a magic wand" [19].
  • Tenure as identity. Core members define themselves by how long they've been there ("members since 2009") and police newcomers' status-seeking: "sitôt arrivé, il veut être T.M" (barely arrived and he wants to be a TopMembre) [14]. In 2025 Orange described them as mostly retirees, unlike Sosh's working-age "Top Contributeurs" [2]. That is a durable core, but an ageing one.

The content engine

  • Member-made: "Trucs et astuces" wiki. TopMembres write tutorials such as Thunderbird setup, Windows backups, mail attachment handling and the Windows Mail client [1][3]. The wiki's all-time contributor list is led by TopMembres (PhilDur, JuanLucas38, fredolerouge) next to former CMs [34]. But the five newest tutorials on the homepage date from July 2023 to December 2024, and several are now credited to "Ancien Membre" (deleted accounts) [1]. The pipeline has stalled.
  • Orange-made: the "Orange et Vous" blog. Mostly marketing and engagement: Black Friday deals, Advent calendars, an Halloween 2026 Playzer game, a "Défi Rentrée" challenge, the Marketplace launch, a monthly "On regarde quoi ce mois-ci" streaming pick, and posts celebrating ESCDA awards [1][17]. Earlier it ran member features, such as a 2014 piece on a long-time fibre user [41]. Posts draw a few thousand views (6,552 for the Nov 2025 ESCDA post) [17].
  • Staff utility posts: pinned "À lire avant de poser une question" threads, written by a TopMembre in 2013 [4]. Change-management threads for big product shifts, such as "Bienvenue dans le nouveau Mail Orange" (2026) [1], and earlier the Pages Perso shutdown and the move to OVH [15].
  • Missing: no podcast, webinar series, video academy or regular live format turns up for the forum. Orange's YouTube videos introduce the webconseillers [12], but don't teach the product through the community.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff: CMs (Stéphanie 2013 to about 2019, then Léa_T, Raphael_M, Margault, Ibtisseme_B, Rania_s; many now carry "Ancien CM" badges), anonymous moderators ("Modérateur_A") and rotating webconseillers [1][14][34]. The 2025 interview names one CM for both the Orange and Sosh communities, which points to a lean team [2].
  • Superuser program: TopMembres. Orange chooses them; there is no published application or criteria. When a newcomer asked "what studies do you need?", moderators deleted parts of the thread so as "not to mislead" readers about paid work [14]. Perks: badge, private salon, embargoed previews, Lab study invitations and (until 2019) the MeetUps [2][10]. No cash, gifts or product perks turn up for France. Orange Sénégal, by contrast, paid weekly top contributors a 5,000 FCFA top-up card [35].
  • Powers: limited. A 2021 thread shows members believing TopMembres delete posts. An Orange Star corrected them: "only the moderation teams, or even the community managers, are authorised" [14].
  • Feedback loop: MeetUps included a "life on the community" session whose suggestions were "taken into account" [10]. Webconseillers escalate cases. A 2019 CM reply shows a portal bug "identified and corrected by our technical teams following your messages" [21]. There is no public ideas board or "you asked, we built" log.
  • Moderation: strict about politeness and off-topic posts. Regulars scold rude posters ("BONJOUR quand même… vous ne saluez pas les gens ?") [20]. Long-running complaints about "censure" mostly concern moderation of Orange's news-portal comments, which the support forum ends up absorbing. One such thread runs to 70+ pages [21][22].

Growth loops

  • SEO. The one real loop. Millions of searchable solved threads bring visitors in, a share register to ask, and the answers become new search results [2][25]. The ~2M messages still in the main boards are the asset [24].
  • Orange's own channels send traffic there: the contact page [29], assistance articles, and third-party guides such as Selectra, which tell readers to "go to communaute.orange.fr" [25].
  • Outliving competitors. The unofficial forum-orange.com / livebox-news forum, long the alternative to the official one [18], closed by August 2026. It now redirects readers to "the Orange community… its direct successor" (alongside affiliate offers) [25].
  • No referral, ambassador or acquisition loop. The community's job is retention through service, not acquisition.

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: none published. No deflection, cost, NPS or churn numbers for the French forum turn up in Orange press releases, the Khoros/Lithium awards archive or academic work.
  • Company claims: the communities have "about one million members" and TopMembres give "about a third of answers" [2]. Orange credits its "webspécialistes… on Facebook via Messenger" and "on the help forum" for being Élu Service Client de l'Année 2025 and 2026, an award based on mystery-shopper tests [17][28].
  • Indirect signals: the paid staffing has lasted 13 years, with webconseillers spread across eight cities [12]. During the 2020 lockdown Orange sent "engineers, project managers (like me)" into the forums to relieve them [13]. The forum also survived the November 2025 shutdown of X support while other channels were consolidated [26]. Inside Orange the forum is treated as core support infrastructure, not a marketing experiment.
  • Group context: Orange Romania (deflection as the stated goal) and Orange Spain (Lithy 2016) show the same operating model across the Orange group [30].

What they did right

  1. Paired volunteers with staff who can open the account. The webconseillers can access customer systems, so a public thread can turn into a resolved account issue. Members noticed the change from the old forum: "now we have web advisers who go through the forum" [18][2][12].
  2. Turned top volunteers into a named, met group. Six MeetUps between 2014 and 2018, then #8 in 2019, put long-time helpers in a room with marketing, security and care staff. Participants called them "very enriching" [7][8][9][10].
  3. Kept the same platform and URL for 13 years. Unlike Telekom's 2024 move to Sprinklr (see the telekom-hilft report), Orange has stayed on Lithium/Khoros since 2013. Thread URLs from 2013 still resolve, so the SEO archive keeps compounding [5][6][1].
  4. Gave members an authored, durable output. The wiki gave TopMembres something lasting to own beyond individual answers [1][34].
  5. Set norms early. Politeness, "search first", and "start a new thread rather than reply to an old one" are spelled out for newcomers and enforced by regulars [3][20].

What they did wrong

  1. Wiped the old forum at the 2013 launch. Users found "the questions, the messages of the old forum… everything has disappeared. We start from zero." Another wrote: "all the knowledge bases accumulated over all these years are lost… I don't see your interest except overloading the technical service on the phone." The sharpest reaction: dropping "years of voluntary contribution… practically without notice and without giving contributors time to save what they wanted. What waste and what offhandedness… enough to disgust the best!" [5]. Consequence: the community restarted from zero. Orange spent 2014 rebuilding trust with the new contributor program [7].
  2. Let the superuser program go quiet. No MeetUp after 2019, a stalled wiki, CM turnover visible in a string of "Ancien CM" badges, and one CM covering two brands by 2025 [2][1][10]. With a retiree-heavy core [2], nobody replaces the volunteers who leave. Activity fell about 60% from its peak (estimate).
  3. Status without clear criteria breeds resentment. Because TopMembre selection is opaque and the leaderboard is public, members accuse others of chasing "the high score in number of posts, not serving the community… it's good for his ego" [15].
  4. Staff response is uneven, and members say so. "As for the Orange webconseillers who sometimes step into this forum, they don't seem to be in a hurry" (2025) [16]. When Orange celebrated its ESCDA award on the forum, the comment thread turned into a list of complaints: "a masquerade", "Orange answers fictitious situations perfectly. Completely useless otherwise" (2026) [17].
  5. The forum became the complaint desk for everything Orange. Portal homepage redesigns, news-comment moderation and 70-page "censure" threads crowd into the support forum's lounge [19][21][22]. Volunteers spend energy defending Orange on topics they can't fix.
  6. The forum leans on marketing content, not learning content. The blog is mostly promotions and games [1]. It does little to make members better users of the product, which is the YNAB-style content engine Nathan asked about.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Put staff with account access in the threads. Peer answers cover how-to questions. Staff who can see the account close the rest and make the forum worth trying before the phone line [2][12].
  2. Give superusers a name, a private room and a yearly in-person day with real executives. That costs little next to the third of answers they supply [2][10].
  3. Treat the archive as capital. Keep URLs stable across platform years, and pin "read before asking" guides written by members [4][1].
  4. Let volunteers author durable artefacts, such as a wiki or tutorials, and credit them by name [34].
  5. Write and enforce norms early (greet, search first, new thread per problem). The regulars will enforce them for you [3][20].

Avoid these

  1. Never launch a new platform by deleting the old community's content. You hand the people you most need a reason to quit [5].
  2. Don't let the superuser program go quiet. Rituals that stop (no MeetUp since 2019) and a shrinking CM team let an ageing core shrink without anyone to replace it [2][10].
  3. Don't make the support forum the dumping ground for unrelated brand grievances or a channel for promotions. Members read promo posts and award self-congratulation as tone-deaf when their own problem is unsolved [17][21].

Score notes. Retention mechanics 3: real status, a private salon and meetups, but the rituals lapsed after 2019. Content engine 2: a member wiki that has stalled and a promo blog. Member ownership 3: volunteers give about a third of answers and write the wiki, but have no moderation powers or say in the roadmap. Staff investment 4: paid advisers in about eight cities for 13 years, plus CMs. Business impact evidence 1: no published numbers, only award claims. Durability 4: 13 years on one platform, still growing in registrations.
