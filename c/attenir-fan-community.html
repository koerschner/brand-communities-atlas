TL;DR

  • What it is: a free, members-only forum for Attenir customers (mostly women in their 30s to 50s). It is built and co-run by the community vendor QON on its "Beach" platform, opened in December 2015, and had 238,670 registrants and 465,155 posts in November 2026 (homepage).
  • Why people come back: a points system pays for every post, comment, vote and "stamp", and the points enter monthly prize lotteries (Attenir products, ¥500 coupons for 200 people a month). Members can also be picked as reviewers for new products. A friendly staff persona, "Tōko", posts several prompts a week, and peers who share the same skin worries and life stage chat freely, often about things unrelated to beauty (points page; Kyoto Shimbun/PR TIMES STORY).
  • What the company feeds it: daily official topics, interviews with product developers, quizzes, trial salons, co-creation votes (for example the colours of a free gift), an annual "Attenir general election" product vote, fan meetings and lab tours, and an anniversary festival. Executives read the posts, and they are shared with every employee in an internal chat room (Sankei, 2022).
  • Biggest weakness: behind the large registrant number sits a small active core. The flagship annual vote drew 1,427 votes, about 0.6% of registrants. New registrations have flattened since 2022, and side spaces ("Blooming with Attenir", "My Happiness Garden") have been opened and then closed. Almost all of the success story comes from advertorials paid for by QON.
  • The lesson: treat the community as a research and insight engine first and an acquisition channel second. Keep the brand's sales voice out of it, and give executives a ritual for reading it. Attenir credits this, rather than member count, for the hit product that drove its record sales.

What the community is

The Attenir Fan Community (アテニア ファンコミュニティ) is a closed-registration forum on beach.jp, the multi-brand community platform of QON Inc. (founded 1996 as Avec Laboratory; Beach launched in 2000) (QON organization page). The terms of use are QON's, not Attenir's (registration/ToS). Members must register with the same email address they use for the Attenir online shop, so Attenir can link community activity to purchase records (homepage). Anyone can read most official topics, but posting needs registration.

Its spaces (as of Nov 2026):

  • おしゃべりカフェ (Chat Café): the main official board, hosted by "スタッフの透子" (staff member Tōko), a recurring persona. It holds official prompts, campaigns, monthly point lotteries and free-form member topics. One evergreen member thread, "Are you eating breakfast?", has 1,774 comments (topic list).
  • おためしサロン (Trial Salon): reviews of products sent to selected members, and photo albums (homepage).
  • Member circles: user-created sub-groups, many off-topic, e.g. "Let's talk about anything, 40s and 50s" (109 members), "Adults who can't make friends" (183), "50s fighting for beauty & health" (82), a KinKi Kids fan circle (54) (circle list).
  • Time-limited official rooms: "私のHappinessガーデン" (My Happiness Garden, opened 4 Oct 2022 as a "women's empowerment" talk room) (PR TIMES), and the secret circle "Blooming with Attenir", which ended service on 30 Jan 2026 (circle list).
  • An earlier メンバートピック (Member Topics) board from 2016–2018 is now closed to new participants (circle).

No meaningful unofficial spaces were found. No subreddit exists for a Japan-only mid-priced cosmetics brand, and a full-archive X search for the community's name returned zero posts (X API, 7 Nov 2026). Outside the forum, the brand's other customer touchpoints are its @cosme brand page, LINE and an app; the community promotes the LINE account (topic list).

Origin and strategy

  • The crisis. Attenir (founded 1989, slogan "first-class brand quality at one-third the price") grew until 2007, then fell from 2008 to 2013 as the market split into prestige and cheap products and left its mid-priced range stranded (Diamond Chain Store, Jan 2024).
  • The champion. Tomoko Saito, then head of the sales-strategy office, felt that one-way advertising was dying and wanted a "circular" system in which recommendations spread on their own. She read QON founder Takashi Takeda's ソーシャルメディア進化論 (2011), cold-called QON, and launched the community in December 2015 (Forbes Japan, Feb 2020). She became president in March 2019 and is now a FANCL executive officer. Successors: Kōji Haruta ran it, then became Attenir president on 27 Mar 2026 (Kokusai Shogyo); Yoshiaki Shinkai, head of mail-order sales, has led it since 2024 (Sankei, Nov 2025).
  • How the goal changed. The goal moved from amplification to insight. Haruta says it began "to spread recommending voices into the world" but "before we knew it, it became the starting point for pulling customer voices into the company" (Kyoto Shimbun/PR TIMES STORY, Oct 2022). In 2025 he said that early on they chased registrant numbers and LTV, but later decided that "the community is already a market and a database; the question is not how to steer it but how to use it" (Sankei, Apr 2025).
  • Business-model fit. Attenir is mostly a direct mail-order business (plus some shops). Shinkai: "unlike shoppers in stores, mail-order customers are lonely." Their own events showed how much customers valued meeting other users (Sankei, Nov 2025). Saito's stated payoff is retention without discounting: "without a community, retention measures tend to rely on coupons… with it, that isn't necessary" (Forbes Japan).
  • Self-described phases (Kyoto Shimbun/PR TIMES STORY):
    1. 2015–16: grow registrants and prove the correlation with purchases.
    2. 2017–18: raise the share of members who act (claps, comments).
    3. 2019 onward: the community becomes "indispensable" to product and service decisions.

Trajectory (my calculations from the table):

  • New registrants: about 45k a year from 2016 to 2019, about 16–18k a year in 2020–21, then only about 4–8k a year from 2022 to 2024. There was a bump of about 14k in 2025 around the 10th-anniversary and new-member campaigns, then about 1.7k from March to September 2026. The count fell slightly (−265) between September and November 2026.
  • Posts: about 60k a year in 2018–19 and again in 2024, about 30k a year in 2025–26, so roughly 80 posts a day now.
  • Claps: total claps kept climbing fast (3.1M in Dec 2019 to 11.5M in Jan 2025, about 1.6M a year). That suggests lightweight reactions, which also earn points, became the main form of activity. The clap counter disappeared from the homepage after January 2025, when claps appear to have been renamed "きもちスタンプ" (feeling stamps).
  • Active core: pinned staff prompts typically get 50–375 comments (topic list). The real active core is probably low thousands, which is about 1% of registrants (estimate).

What keeps people coming back

  1. Points paid for every action, spent on lotteries.
    • Points are earned for voting in one-click polls, starting topics, commenting, receiving comments, giving and receiving stamps. They are capped at 1,000 a day and never expire in aggregate.
    • Each month members can spend 500 points on the "Attenir Select" prize draw (a new product each month) or 100 points on a ¥500 shop coupon (200 winners a month). Everyone who joins also gets a welcome coupon (points page; Chat Café welcome).
    • Prizes require an Attenir shop account, which ties the habit to the store.
  2. Being chosen. Members can be selected as reviewers for new products in the Trial Salon, for pre-launch monitor programmes, and for lab tours. In 2018 one member wrote that her goal for next year was "to be selected as a reviewer" (PR TIMES, 2018).
  3. Social reinforcement. Claps and stamps on your own posts matter. One member wrote: "I even got claps on my own comments… fulfilling days" (same source). Haruta credits the 2017–18 push on clap and comment rates with "activating" the community (Kyoto Shimbun/PR TIMES STORY).
  4. Peers who share your situation. Members are mostly women aged 30–50, Attenir's target age is 40s, and many are at a life transition (marriage, childbirth, returning to work) (Forbes Japan; Sankei 2022). Haruta: they post here rather than on SNS because skin worries are "hard to talk about with friends," and finding people with the same worries is hard. The member circles show strong off-topic belonging, from work stress and parenting in your 50s to friendship-seeking.
  5. A staff persona and a mascot. "Tōko" hosts every official topic. "Tenny" (テニー), a white-bear mascot, was designed and named by member vote. The company hides Tenny across topics in recurring "find Tenny" campaigns to teach newcomers to browse (topic; Kyoto Shimbun/PR TIMES STORY).
  6. Seasonal rituals.
    • The "Attenir general election" every year-end and New Year: a vote on best new item and best overall product, with "推しコメント" (fan-favourite comments) published.
    • Winter coffret and lucky-bag threads, monthly point lotteries, and campaigns tied to product renewals.
    • The 10th-anniversary festival (4 Aug–20 Oct 2026) with a "Beauty BINGO" relay of prompts (koubo listing; topic list).
  7. Seeing that your voice changed something.
    • Members rename products ("Primer-san" for Primer Shot), and the nickname becomes campaign copy.
    • They vote on the colours of free gifts.
    • They spot renewals of packaging and fragrance that were made after their complaints (Sankei 2025; netkeizai, Dec 2025; Sankei 2022).
  8. Mixing new and loyal customers. Attenir deliberately puts newcomers in front of heavy users. Examples: a "your Attenir memories" prompt, rooms where current users and non-users of Dress Snow and Dress Lift talk around a renewal, and a time-limited newcomers' talk room (Forbes Japan; Sankei 2025; netkeizai).

Switching costs are mild: point balances, a posting history, friendships in circles, and the status of a long-time reviewer. The staff account shows "3,756 days since joining / 993 posts" (staff profile).

The content engine

  • Official prompts several times a week from the Tōko persona.
    • Beauty-routine questions ("which eye-makeup technique do you find hardest?").
    • Lifestyle questions ("eco activities?", "do you eat breakfast?").
    • Quizzes about ingredients.
    • One-click polls and "教えて！アテニアさん" Q&A on makeup worries (topic list).
  • Interviews with developers and staff, often tied to a launch or renewal: the "story of Skin Clear Cleanse Oil's evolution" (Oct 2019), staff relay columns (e.g. the Inner Effector developer), a planner interview for the Dress Snow renewal (topic; topic). Saito: fans "are very happy when they see the life-size Attenir" (Forbes Japan).
  • Events.
    • In-person fan meetings, e.g. an "ageless eye makeup" session, and research-lab tours (events page).
    • Online fan meetings, e.g. the Zoom meeting for the 35th "founding day" on 18 Feb 2024, with members' memory stories, face-cleansing research and a prize quiz (Attenir).
    • A large real-world 10th-anniversary event was planned for 2026 (Sankei, Nov 2025).
  • Member UGC. The core content is product reviews, routines and photos (e.g. "night white CC mille-feuille layering"). Rewards are points, stamps, featured "推しコメント" (fan-favourite comments) in result posts, and being quoted by the company.
  • Purpose-led rooms. The "My Happiness Garden" room ("anti-ageing care for the heart", unconscious bias) and "Blooming with Attenir" were limited-time experiments (PR TIMES 2022).

What is missing compared with YNAB-style how-to engines: no structured curriculum, no long-form tutorials, and no member-authored guides library. The education is small, conversational and attached to products.

People, programs and governance

  • Who runs it. Attenir's mail-order sales department runs the community: Shinkai (lead), Marina Kawakami (operator), and the Net CRM group manager Yae Kinoshita. QON supplies the platform, facilitation know-how and analytics (Sankei 2025; Sankei Nov 2025). QON says it has close to 100 staff working on fan communities (QON partner page). Team size at Attenir is not disclosed (no evidence found).
  • Facilitation is the main governance tool. Rules exist ("お部屋を楽しむマナー", room manners) (events page nav), but leaders mostly describe managing the tone of the room:
    • "If the corporate colour comes out too strongly, the freedom and fun disappear" (Haruta).
    • "If we say 'please use it', customers pull back" (Kawakami).
    • Saito: a commercial message can make the mood turn bad suddenly.
    • Company staff appear "as one consumer who loves the product" (Kyoto Shimbun/PR TIMES STORY; Sankei 2022).
    • Negative posts are allowed, and other members often defend the brand (Digiday Japan, Jun 2024).
  • Ambassador or superuser programme: none found. Recognition comes through reviewer selection, prize draws and featured quotes, not titles.
  • Feedback loop.
    • Posts are shared daily into an all-employee chat room, "アテニア好きと繋がりたい" (I want to connect with Attenir lovers).
    • Saito kept asking staff "did you look at the community?" until the habit stuck.
    • Product planners now cite community voices as evidence in proposals ("'customers want this' beats 'I want to do this'") (Sankei 2022; Sankei 2025).
    • Shinkai deliberately reads comments one by one for their "stories" rather than clustering them like survey data (Sankei Nov 2025).

Growth loops

  • Owned channels push customers to join. The shop's top page, product-page review excerpts, and newsletters (Kyoto Shimbun/PR TIMES STORY), plus a permanent recruitment page on attenir.co.jp (Attenir) and cross-posts on its @cosme brand blog (@cosme, May 2018). New-registration prize campaigns run alongside launches (e.g. Wrinkle Liner, Nov 2026).
  • Search. Saito (2020): "Now if you search 'Attenir', the community's voices come up" (Forbes Japan). Because the boards are publicly readable, they act as review content.
  • Members' words reused as advertising. Community language fed the Dress Snow "I don't choose / I want both" campaign starring Manami Konishi, and the "Primer-san is coming" copy (Sankei 2022; Sankei 2025). This loop works through product and advertising, not peer referral.
  • Paid acquisition at launch (unverified detail). A points-reward site, Sugutama, listed an "Attenir Fan Community opening campaign" (search snippet; page returned 503). This suggests some early registrations were incentivised. Since then the company says it does not use coupons to drive registrations and no longer targets registrant counts (Sankei Nov 2025; Digiday).
  • No member-to-member referral programme was found.

Context from FANCL's investor-relations filings (independent hard numbers):

  • Attenir revenue was ¥11.5bn in FY3/2019, rising 4.3% that year after double-digit growth in FY2016–17 (Nichiryu interview).
  • Attenir cosmetics fell 2.5% to ¥13.4bn in FY3/2023: domestic sales rose 3.1%, overseas fell 41% under China's lockdowns (FANCL FY3/2023 results).
  • Attenir revenue was ¥15.19bn in FY3/2024 (+13.0%) (bci headline snippet).
  • Since Kirin consolidated FANCL, Attenir's sales are no longer disclosed. FANCL's 2025 report says the Attenir brand "performed well" at home and abroad (Tsuhan News, Mar 2026).

Caveats.

  • The comparisons between users and non-users are correlational and almost certainly suffer from self-selection: loyal buyers join.
  • Recovery started in 2014, before the community opened (Diamond), so the community was one part of a broader repositioning around "one-third the price".
  • Recent growth also leans on Chinese inbound tourists: inbound sales were 2x 2019 levels in 2024 (Kokusai Shogyo, Jul 2025).
  • No academic study of this community was found (OpenAlex search returned nothing).

Score: business_impact_evidence = 3. There are several quantified, consistent claims over a decade, but none is audited or causal, and most come from advertorials.

What they did right

  • An executive sponsor who reads the forum, and a ritual to match. The CEO who started it read it constantly and made "did you look at the community?" a company habit. Posts flow daily into an all-employee chat (Sankei 2022; Sankei Nov 2025). Three successive leaders have kept it going for 11 years; durability = 4.
  • Using it for insight, not advertising. The clearest win, Dress Snow, came from an unmet trade-off ("I don't want to choose between whitening and anti-ageing"), not from a feature request (Kyoto Shimbun/PR TIMES STORY).
  • Keeping the sales voice out. Every leader names a hard rule against "selling" inside the room, and allows complaints (Digiday).
  • Reusing members' words. "Primer-san" and "I don't choose" came from members, which gave the company confidence ("if it came from fans, it can't be wrong") (Sankei 2025).
  • Linking accounts to purchases. Requiring the shop email made it possible to measure LTV from day one.
  • Allowing off-topic belonging. Circles about breakfast, work stress and parenting in your 50s keep people around between purchases.
  • A clear, cheap reward loop. Points for small actions, monthly lotteries and reviewer picks are a cheap, predictable cadence. Retention_mechanics = 4, content_engine = 4: a dense weekly cadence of prompts, interviews and events, though little evergreen education.

What they did wrong

  • A small active core and stalled growth.
    • Registrants grew by only about 4–8k a year in 2022–24, fell slightly in autumn 2026, and posts slowed to about 30k a year (Wayback series above).
    • The biggest yearly vote drew 1,427 votes from about 237k registrants.
    • The "200k+ women" headline overstates participation by roughly two orders of magnitude.
  • Activity that may be bought with incentives.
    • Points are given for receiving stamps and comments, up to 1,000 points a day, and lottery prompts dominate the pinned topics.
    • The flood of claps (11.5M) next to modest comment growth suggests point-farming taps.
    • No evidence was found that Attenir checks the quality of incentivised posts.
  • Rooms opened and closed without explanation. "My Happiness Garden" was time-limited, "Blooming with Attenir" closed on 30 Jan 2026 with a one-paragraph notice, and Member Topics was frozen (circle list). The purpose-led "women's empowerment" extension did not stick.
  • Content quietly removed. Cumulative posts fell from 191,539 (Dec 2019) to 187,476 (Jun 2020), probably a purge or archive during the 2019 platform renewal. No announcement was found.
  • Locked to the vendor, and fragmented.
    • The community lives on beach.jp under QON's terms and needs a separate login.
    • Community coupons don't appear in the shop's My Page (FANCL FAQ).
    • Members asked for an app in March 2018 (thread). By 2025 leadership still listed tighter integration with the official site and app as a to-do (Sankei 2025).
  • Depends on a few skilled people. Shinkai admits that operating the community and extracting voices "depends on the ability of the person in charge," and that it lacks quantification and repeatability "as people and structures change" (same source). The staff persona hides this but doesn't solve it.
  • The public evidence comes from the vendor. Nearly every success story (Forbes Japan, three Sankei pieces, Digiday) is paid content (提供：クオン, "provided by QON"), or written by Attenir (PR TIMES STORY). Independent coverage is thin (a paywalled Nikkei piece, Apr 2023). This makes outside verification hard; member_ownership = 2, because members shape the conversation but own no programmes, roles or governance.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Tie community identities to customer accounts from day one, so you can measure spend and retention by cohort instead of guessing.
  2. Make reading the community an executive ritual: a daily feed into an all-company channel, leaders who ask "did you see this?", and proposals that cite member quotes as evidence.
  3. Mine it for trade-offs and words, not feature votes. Look for "I don't want to have to choose" tensions and members' own nicknames, and put them straight into the product and the advertising.
  4. Use a consistent host persona with a sales-free voice. Staff show up as fellow users. Allow complaints and let members answer them.
  5. Run a light reward loop (points for small actions, monthly lotteries, reviewer selection), plus yearly rituals such as a members' product vote. Give newcomers deliberate on-ramps: welcome rooms, scavenger hunts, and rooms that mix them with veterans.

Avoid these

  1. Don't let the registrant count stand in for health. Track monthly active posters and how much of the activity is not incentivised. Here about 1% are active, and growth has stalled.
  2. Don't pay points for receiving reactions without quality controls. That invites tap-farming that inflates activity metrics.
  3. Don't let the vendor own your platform and your story. A separate login, separate coupons and vendor-run PR make integration and honest assessment harder. Plan for the app and the shop to be the community's front door.
