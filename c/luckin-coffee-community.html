TL;DR

  • What it is. After Luckin's April 2020 fraud scandal, it added a WeCom (Enterprise WeChat) persona, "Chief Welfare Officer Lucky", and put customers into location-based groups for each store, named like "Luckin Hangzhou Haichuang Tech store group 4". It took three months to reach 1.8M WeCom contacts and 1.1M members in 9,100+ groups (Jianshi/Qianzhan interview with Luckin's CMO team, 20 Jul 2020). By 2022 it had about 20M WeCom contacts (co-founder and CGO Yang Fei, May 2022), and in Dec 2024 the CEO said "100 million+" were reachable through private traffic (Guo Jinyi).
  • Why people come back. Money, at predictable times. The groups post on a fixed schedule built around the office worker's day: a breakfast push around 8:30, a limited flash coupon at noon (10 coupons of ¥15 per group, redeemed at 40%+), an afternoon-tea push, and an evening e-commerce push. They also announce new products and run weekly lotteries. There's almost no member-to-member conversation, and that's by design.
  • What the company feeds it. Not content. It feeds in store traffic (QR codes at pickup counters, scripts for baristas, adding members counted toward store KPIs), app and mini-program entry points, an official account with 29M+ followers, and a data platform that automates more than 90% of marketing rules, such as weather-based and store-specific coupons (Yang Fei, 2022).
  • Biggest mistake. It promised personalization it didn't deliver. Luckin described "thousand stores, thousand coupons" targeting by inventory and weather, but a month-long undercover look at 15 groups in 2023 found identical broadcasts in every group, no conversation, and a Lucky account that answered questions with a customer-service phone number (Song Xing / news.qq, 29 Aug 2023). For people who don't want coupons, the groups have no value.
  • The one lesson. For a cheap, frequent purchase, a "community" that is really a well-timed, opt-in reminder channel tied to transaction data can measurably raise purchase frequency. Company figures: +30% monthly purchase frequency and +28% weekly repeat buyers. Be honest that this is a channel, and don't expect it to produce belonging.

What the community is

The official spaces are:

  1. WeCom 1:1 contact. Customers add "首席福利官 Lucky" (Chief Welfare Officer Lucky), a cartoon deer-antlered persona that matches the brand logo. It sends a welcome message with the group invite and posts to WeCom Moments (the customer-contact feed) a few times a week. In March 2025 it was re-skinned as "首席幸运官 Lucky" (Chief Lucky Officer) to match a brand IP refresh (Digitaling, 17 Jul 2025).
  2. Store-based WeCom customer groups (福利群, "welfare groups"). A WeCom "live code" reads the user's location and offers the group for the nearest store. Users can also choose a store they visit often. Groups are capped at about 200 people, and in 2020 the top stores each had more than 10 groups (Qianzhan, 2020; Morketing, Feb 2022).
  3. The wider WeChat ecosystem around the groups: an official account with 29M+ followers in 2022 and "over 30M" by 2023, the ordering mini-program, Channels (视频号) livestreams, and the app. Luckin only sells through app or mini-program orders, so every group member already has an identity tied to their purchase history (news.qq, 2023).

Unofficial spaces matter little. r/LuckinCoffee is an investor sub with about 1,984 subscribers and 979 posts since 2019 (Arctic Shift). Third-party "deal" (羊毛) groups and resellers on Xianyu and Taobao trade Luckin coupons and "proxy ordering" (代下单) links (Hangzhou News, Dec 2023; Taobao guide, Sep 2025). Luckin doesn't back them, and customer service says some third-party coupons aren't supported.

Status today: active. The persona was rebranded in 2025, and 2025–2026 consumer guides still tell readers to "add the store's WeCom; the group gives out exclusive discount coupons every day" (Zhihu ordering guide, 2026). Luckin hasn't published a group-specific number since 2022.

Origin and strategy

  • Before 2020: paid acquisition and referrals. Under CMO Yang Fei, author of 流量池 ("Traffic Pool"), Luckin grew through "fission" referrals ("invite a friend, you both get a free cup"). It treated ad spend as referral rewards (Baidu Baike EN), alongside heavy subsidies and elevator ads. The referral module was removed from the app on 30 May 2020 (Meihua, 16 Jul 2020).
  • The trigger. On 2 Apr 2020 Luckin admitted fabricated sales, its marketing budget was slashed, and COVID-19 closed stores. Management framed the choice as livestreaming versus private traffic. Yang Fei picked private traffic because livestreaming "still depends on buying outside traffic", which is public-domain spending (WeSCRM/WeCom-sourced retrospective, 2022; Sina/新莓daybreak, 18 Oct 2023). WeCom 3.0 (early 2020) had just added customer contact, customer groups and customer Moments, which made it workable.
  • Pilot and rollout. In April 2020 Luckin piloted in 50 of about 4,000 stores, then from May added 50–100 stores a day (WeSCRM; Tech Buzz China, Dec 2022). A later account says 200 test stores (Sina 2023). By July 2020 the groups covered 90% of stores.
  • Stated goal. "Retention and frequency of existing users, not acquisition": "the groups' main job is waking up and reminding, reactivating users, like sending an SMS" (Luckin CMO team, Qianzhan 2020).
  • Champion: Yang Fei, co-founder and CMO who became Chief Growth Officer. He merged brand marketing and user operations ("品运合一"). His framework treats WeCom, groups and Moments as "small private domain", which on its own "easily becomes low-efficiency harassment". The real asset is the "big private domain": the app plus a CDP (customer data platform) that sets the rules (Yang Fei, May 2022). Internally Luckin reportedly replaced the word "private domain" with "traffic pool system" and uses "private domain" only for WeCom (Sina 2023). Context: Yang Fei served prison time for a 2013 paid-post-deletion case, which press raised around the 2019 IPO (PingWest, Jan 2019). He was still on the management team in Dec 2024 (Guo Jinyi talk).

Trajectory: very fast growth in 2020–2022, then the company stopped breaking out group numbers. The 2024 "100M+" figure probably counts the official account, app push and mini-program subscribers as well as WeCom, so it can't be compared to the 2020–22 group counts. The coordinator's "8–9M" figure is accurate for WeCom contacts in early 2022. The groups themselves held about 4.5M members at that point.

What keeps people coming back

  1. A fixed daily schedule of rewards. In 2020 there were four drops a day: an 8:30 reminder poster, a noon flash sale (10 × ¥15 coupons per group, 40%+ redemption), afternoon tea, and evening e-commerce (Qianzhan). By 2023 the schedule fit the office week: Monday mornings carry only official-account posts, nothing after 13:30 on Fridays, nothing on weekend mornings, and breakfast moved half an hour earlier to 8:00–8:30 (news.qq 2023). A weekly single-item flash sale and a weekly free-order lottery continue (Zhihu, ~2025).
  2. Scarcity and grabbing coupons fast. Limited coupons per group make people check at noon: "even camping out every day I don't always get one" (CBNData, Mar 2021).
  3. An instant reward on joining. A 4.8×-off coupon (i.e. 52% off) arrives automatically when you join and expires in 3 days, which forces a first order (CBNData).
  4. New-product launches as events. Luckin ships new drinks roughly every two weeks and runs 30+ co-brands a year. Members watch the group on launch days, e.g. a member who set a phone reminder for the Tom & Jerry co-brand launch (Sina 2023).
  5. Local identity, used thinly. Groups are named after stores, and campus groups got group-buy deals as low as 72% off plus student-only drinks (CBNData). There's no evidence of peer relationships forming.
  6. A mascot persona instead of a host. Lucky is a brand character and has no relationship with members. In 2023 it answered questions with a customer-service phone number (news.qq 2023).
  7. Low friction to stay. Muting a group costs nothing, and Luckin's model assumes members mute it and look only when they want coffee (LinkFlow/Sina, Nov 2020).

There are no streaks, badges, levels, member recognition or peer support. Switching costs come from the loyalty app and coupon wallet, not from the groups.

The content engine

  • Formats: promo posters, product photos, mini-program cards (one tap to order), Channels video clips, and twice-weekly mini-program livestreams in 2020. Eight livestreams were held by July 2020; the best peaked at 100k+ viewers, about 70% of them from the groups (Qianzhan). Livestreams were later dropped from the groups (news.qq 2023).
  • Automation: Yang Fei says more than 90% of marketing rules are automated: automatic pushes, coupon loading and creative selection. A weather bot flags unusual weather by city each morning to trigger delivery coupons or "share coupons" on rainy days (Yang Fei 2022; Sina 2023). Group bots handle welcomes, keyword replies, ad warnings and kicking spammers (Zhihu 2021).
  • Member-made content: essentially none inside the groups. UGC happens on Xiaohongshu and Weibo, and Luckin sometimes routes it through the groups, for example "post on Xiaohongshu to claim a ¥29 voucher" in 2024. That campaign drew a complaint when people received ¥9.9 instead (Heimao, 20 Aug 2024).
  • Cadence: several posts a day in groups, and 1–3 1:1 broadcasts a month (Qingua).

People, programs and governance

  • Central growth team. User operations and marketing were merged under the CGO in 2020 (Yang Fei 2022). The team size isn't disclosed. The groups are run by bots and a uniform persona, not community managers.
  • Store staff as recruiters. Pickup counters show "scan to join the welfare club, get a 4.8×-off coupon". Baristas ask at checkout whether you're in the group, and according to staff many stores count group sign-ups in their performance reviews (CBNData). Under the original model, store staff joined groups for 1:1 service-style contact (Sina 2023), but by 2023 observers saw no human replies.
  • Vendors. The SCRM vendor 小裂变 (Xiaoliebian) ran referral campaigns, adding 100k+ WeCom friends in a short burst and peaking at 19k new friends a day (Xiaoliebian case, Zhihu). The Jianshi media/consultancy says it "helped a little" with the transition (Qianzhan).
  • Rules: group announcements carry coupon instructions and rules, and bots warn or remove advertisers (Qingua).
  • Feedback loop: feedback flows through transaction data (CDP, a monthly "favorites list", demand-driven R&D), not conversation. There is no ideas portal or ambassador program.

Growth loops

  • Within the business: store → group → app order → store. Every channel (the app's "我的" page, mini-program banners, official account menu and 2nd/3rd articles, Moments LBS ads within 1.5 km of a store) points to "add Lucky, get a coupon" (Morketing; news.qq 2023).
  • Referral inside groups: bots post a referral mini-program where inviting 2 friends earns a 62%-off coupon (CBNData), and there's an official-account "invite a friend, get ¥20" offer (GrowthHK, 2022). In July 2020 Luckin said it had not yet done in-group referral, so these came later.
  • Indirect: Luckin's private-traffic case became the most-copied lesson in Chinese marketing media (dozens of teardowns on Zhihu, Woshipm, Qingua and others). That's free brand PR but doesn't bring in customers.

Evidence of business impact

Company-stated (2020, Luckin CMO team via Jianshi) (Qianzhan):

  • Group joiners: monthly purchase frequency +30% (about 5–6 → 7–8 orders a month), weekly repeat purchasers +28%, MAU +10%.
  • 35k+ cups a day ordered directly with group coupons (about 4 cups per group per day, roughly 8–10 extra cups per store per day), plus 100k+ cups a day "prompted" by reminders.
  • Private traffic was the #3 order channel, after the app and the mini-program and ahead of third-party delivery.
  • A later retrospective says direct group orders were about 4% of daily cups, about 10% counting purchases within 48 hours of a push (WeSCRM).

Caveat: these are before/after comparisons on self-selected, already-loyal users (60%+ of members were "loyal" by Luckin's own definition). There's no control group, and incremental lift isn't separated from coupon cannibalization.

Estimates (not company): 200–300k group cups a day in 2023, about ¥1B a year and about 1/20–1/30 of revenue (Qingua, Feb 2025); or 400k–1M cups a day against about 3.5M total (news.qq 2023). These vary by 2–4×, so treat them as rough.

Hard evidence: none in filings. SEC full-text search of Luckin's filings finds no "WeChat groups" or "private traffic" (EDGAR FTS). The CEO himself treats private traffic as one of several reasons for the turnaround and calls these "the tip of the iceberg", next to the Coconut Latte (2M cups a day), franchising in lower-tier cities and organizational change (Guo Jinyi, Dec 2024). Marketing spend fell from 88% of revenue in 2018 to 4.68% in Q4 2022 (news.qq 2023). That fits the cheap-retention story but can't be attributed to the groups.

What they did right

  1. They were honest about what the groups are for. "Welfare group" in the name, coupons on arrival, a fixed schedule. Members learn the rhythm and come back when they want coffee, with no forced chatter (Qingua 2021).
  2. The groups sit on top of the transaction graph. All orders are app/mini-program orders, so every push can be attributed (48-hour window) and targeted. Yang Fei's warning that "small private domain without big-private-domain data becomes harassment" is the core insight (Yang Fei 2022).
  3. The physical stores drive recruitment. Pickup-counter QR codes plus barista scripts and store KPIs got 1.8M contacts in 3 months at almost no media cost (CBNData).
  4. Pilot, measure, then roll out by store (50 stores → 50–100 a day), with explicit metrics (frequency, weekly repeat buyers, MAU, coupon redemption) (WeSCRM).
  5. Restraint and tuning over time. Rationed coupons (10 per group), quieter Fridays and weekends, and dropping livestreams that didn't pay off. They report a very low block rate of 0.2–0.3% (unverified) (news.qq 2023; Sina 2023).
  6. Durability. The same mechanism has run from 2020 to 2026 through growth from about 4,000 to 36,000+ stores, and Luckin keeps it fresh with persona updates rather than replacing it (Digitaling 2025; TMTPost, Aug 2026).

What they did wrong

  1. "Community" with no community. Group chats are silent, and members treat them as coupon dispensers (news.qq 2023). Critics from 2020 to 2025 call the groups "basically ads" with "too little sense of value" beyond money (Meihua 2020; Zhihu ~2025). Consequence: no loyalty survives if the coupons stop, and the groups add nothing for full-price customers.
  2. Personalization promised but not seen. The "thousand stores, thousand coupons" and inventory-clearance pushes described in 2020 weren't visible by 2023, when every group got the same content (news.qq 2023).
  3. A persona that can't help. Lucky deflects questions to a hotline, so the obvious service use of a 1:1 brand contact is lost (news.qq 2023).
  4. Discount dependence. Academic reviewers warn that long-running deep-discount coupons risk "adverse" long-term effects on price perception (BCP case study). The weekly ¥9.9 offer was narrowed to selected drinks in Feb 2024, which drew public grumbling (Dutenews, Feb 2024). Coupons also attract arbitrage: resellers and proxy-ordering on Xianyu and Taobao, and fraud complaints (Hangzhou News 2023; Heimao, Nov 2024).
  5. Campaign mismatches erode trust. A group-promoted ¥29 voucher paid out ¥9.9, leading to a formal complaint (Heimao, Aug 2024).
  6. The numbers stopped after 2022. There's no public group count, and the 2024 "100M+" claim blends channels. That makes the community's current scale impossible to verify (a transparency gap, not necessarily a failure).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Tie the space to your transaction data. Without attribution and segmentation, a group is spam. Luckin measured frequency, repeat buyers and 48-hour conversions per push.
  2. Recruit where the customer already is, at the moment of use, such as the pickup counter or the post-checkout screen, with an immediate reward that expires quickly.
  3. Build a predictable schedule around the user's day (Luckin: breakfast, lunch, afternoon tea) and ration rewards so checking in is worth it.
  4. Pilot in a small slice, define success metrics, then roll out fast (50 stores → national in about 3 months).
  5. Name it honestly. If it's a deals channel, call it one. Clear expectations kept block rates low and muted members still convert.

Avoid these

  1. Don't confuse a broadcast channel with a community. Coupons build habit, not belonging, so plan for what happens when the incentive shrinks.
  2. Don't promise personalization or service you won't staff. A persona that can't answer questions wastes the 1:1 channel.
  3. Don't let incentives become arbitrage. Deep, transferable discounts attract resellers and fraud and teach customers your list price isn't real.

Score justifications: retention_mechanics 2: a strong daily schedule and scarcity, but only economic hooks, with no status, peers or identity. content_engine 2: promotional posts and early livestreams, with no education or UGC. member_ownership 1: members can't create or lead anything. staff_investment 3: a whole store network and a central growth team plus a CDP and automation, but almost no human community staff. business_impact_evidence 3: specific company-stated metrics from 2020, but self-selected, without a control group and absent from filings. durability 4: continuous from 2020 to 2026 and refreshed in 2025.
