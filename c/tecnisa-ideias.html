TL;DR

  • What it was: an open-innovation website (tecnisaideias.com.br, beta mid-2010, official launch 13 Sep 2010 [2][4]) where anyone could post, vote on and comment on ideas for future apartments, condominiums and neighbourhoods built by São Paulo developer Tecnisa. It is cited with Fiat Mio as Brazil's other early corporate crowdsourcing case.
  • Why people came: prizes and one-off pushes, not habit. The biggest burst came from a November 2010 challenge on four popular Brazilian blogs (Jovem Nerd, Gizmodo Brasil and others) with an iPad as the prize. After it, participant counts stayed flat at about 2,100 in Dec 2010 and about 2,300 in Nov 2013.
  • What Tecnisa fed it: "Inspire-se" posts by a trend-hunting team, heavy cross-promotion on its pioneering corporate blog and in the trade press, and visible proof that ideas got built. It reported 30 to 35 ideas approved, including a real-time electricity monitor shipped in the Flex Paulicéia development.
  • Biggest mistake: it was run as a marketing campaign. After a 2012 relaunch (badges, company sponsorship, Facebook login) the challenges page said "Nenhum desafio no momento" ("no challenge right now"), no member ever earned a badge, and spam and unmoderated personal data piled up. Tecnisa's 2015 to 2021 losses then removed any reason to keep it.
  • The lesson: an ideas portal for a product people buy once or twice in a lifetime has no natural reason for repeat visits. Either attach it to an ongoing relationship (current owners and residents) with a steady cadence of challenges and visible status updates on ideas, or treat it honestly as a time-boxed research sprint.

What the community is

Tecnisa Ideias was a branded idea-management portal run by Tecnisa S.A., a listed São Paulo homebuilder. Its home page called it "uma plataforma de colaboração aberta entre você e a Tecnisa" ("an open collaboration platform between you and Tecnisa") [5]. It answered one framing question, "how should your home, apartment, condominium or neighbourhood be five years from now?" [2]. Members could post ideas, post and answer questions, vote ideas up or down ("Gostei / Não gostei"), comment, and share to Twitter, Facebook, LinkedIn and Orkut [5][6].

It had two versions:

  • v1 (2010 to early 2012). Sections for ideas, questions and "referências" (inspiration posts about architecture and design from around the world), browsable by most recent, most commented or most voted, plus a challenge area ("/desafio") [5][6][7]. The Wayback CDX shows a custom PHP back office ("___manager") with screens for ideas, questions, references and users, and AJAX endpoints for "reputação" on ideas and comments: a reputation score, not just vote counts [8].
  • v2 ("/web", from about April 2012). Sections were Sobre o site, Ideias, Desafios, Participantes, Inspire-se, Empresas, with Facebook Connect, 1 to 5 star voting, per-user stats ("Ideias enviadas / Mais aprovadas / Ideias comentadas"), badges ("Colecione badges"), and a sponsorship route for companies ("Patrocine") [9][10][11].

The audience was the general internet public, not specifically Tecnisa buyers. The Terms limited it to adults and gave Tecnisa a free, royalty-free, perpetual licence to submitted content [12]. Tecnisa itself staffed and moderated it. No unofficial fan spaces existed: there is no subreddit, and Reddit's archive has no relevant Tecnisa threads (Arctic Shift search, no evidence found).

Status: defunct. The last Wayback capture of the portal is from July 2016, and the newest idea visible then was dated 4 Jun 2015 [13]. On 13 Feb 2017 Tecnisa's blog said: "O TECNISA IDEIAS foi descontinuado" ("Tecnisa Ideias was discontinued"), presenting the Fast Dating startup program as its successor [1]. The domain later lapsed. In 2025 it hosts an unrelated gadget-review site called "Tecnisa Ideias Tech" [14]. Tecnisa still uses "Tecnisa Ideias" as a category name on its corporate blog [15].

Origin and strategy

  • Champion. Romeo Busarello, Tecnisa's marketing / internet / "digital environments and innovation" director and an ESPM MBA professor. He was known for Tecnisa's online sales channel (2002), for what Tecnisa called the first apartment sold via Twitter (2009), and for a Second Life presence (2007) [16][17][18]. Day-to-day work sat in the e-business team under manager Denilson Novelli [17][19].
  • Lineage of experiments. In 2006 came the corporate blog, which Novelli called the brand's "first social network" and which was open to criticism [17]. The May 2009 "Consciência Gerontológica" campaign used the blog and an Orkut community to gather ideas for apartments suited to older people: 50 participants, 200+ ideas, and two winners who received R$1,500 in vouchers. The winning ideas were a pool ramp and rubberised grab bars [20]. A February 2010 challenge on the Dutch student platform Battle of Concepts followed [21], then the Zooppa crowd-advertising contest in August 2010, which promoted the portal itself [22]. Busarello framed the portal as the next step: "Já realizamos alguns testes nesse sentido e os resultados foram ótimos" ("we have already run some tests along these lines and the results were great") [2].
  • Strategic logic. The goals were (a) product R&D, gathering features that differentiate otherwise similar apartments ("mais construtora por m²", "more builder per square metre" [23]), and (b) branding. Busarello said in 2011: "Esse projeto reflete-se também em nosso trabalho de branding, em nossa imagem de empresa inovadora" ("this project also shows in our branding work, in our image as an innovative company") [24]. On the decline of the suggestion box: "hoje vivemos o tempo de sabedoria das multidões, da inovação em massa" ("today we live in the age of the wisdom of crowds, of mass innovation") [25]. Tecnisa said it spent 0.6% of revenue on innovation projects [26].
  • Business model fit. It was weak by design. Tecnisa sells apartments, a purchase made rarely, so the portal could not support subscription-style retention. The intended value was better products and an "innovative" brand, which helped win A.T. Kearney / Época Negócios "Best Innovator" awards four years running (2010 to 2013), where Tecnisa was the only construction company [27].

Trajectory: the curve was a spike followed by a flat line. Most of the participants arrived in the first three or four months, during the paid promotions and the blog challenge. The participant count rose only about 10% in the following three years (2,091 → ~2,300). The company's idea counts contradict each other (1,161 in April 2012 vs 1,927 in May 2012), so treat all of them as PR figures. The Wayback CDX shows many more v1 idea pages crawled in 2010 than in 2011 or 2012, which fits the picture of an early burst, though the crawl itself is uneven [8].

What keeps people coming back

Very little, and that is the central finding.

  • Prize challenges as the main cadence. The one well-documented challenge was the Desafio de Blogs Tecnisa Ideias (Oct to Dec 2010). Four blogs each picked the best idea from their readers. Tecnisa chose an overall winner (an iPad), and a popular vote picked a second (a GPS unit). Both winners came from Jovem Nerd readers: Valter Muchenski Junior's household water-and-power dashboard and John Eric Ramalho's "Moderna Construção" [7][31]. The blogs' fan loyalty did the recruiting: Gizmodo Brasil ran posts like "Desafio Tecnisa Ideias: pra frente, Gizmodo!" ("Go Gizmodo!") asking its readers to "se unir e votar" ("band together and vote") [32]. No later challenge is documented. By March 2014 the v2 challenges page was empty [11].
  • Status systems that never activated. v1 had reputation scoring and most-voted / most-commented rankings, and a 2012 case deck mentions a "ranking geral de pontuação" (overall points ranking) [8][33]. v2 promoted collecting badges, but every archived participant card we checked from 2013 to 2016 reads "Este usuário ainda não possui badges" ("this user has no badges yet") [9][10][13].
  • Proof that ideas ship. This was the strongest pull. Tecnisa repeatedly published which ideas it would build: shared garage bicycles, live construction-site cameras, electric-car charging points, energy capture from gym bikes, and "VIP" garages modelled on shopping-mall parking [3][19][24]. The household electricity dashboard went into the Flex Paulicéia development as a monitor that splits consumption into five circuits and estimates the monthly bill. Tecnisa said the development's list of 18 differentiators was chosen "a partir do TECNISA Ideias e brainstorm da equipe" ("from Tecnisa Ideias and the team's brainstorm") [34][17].
  • No identity, rituals, peer relationships or staff personalities visible in the archive. Comments are mostly one-line praise, vote requests or vendor pitches (see Member voice).

The content engine

  • "Referências" / "Inspire-se" posts. These were editorial posts on global design and architecture (e.g. "Cyber-decoração: texturas de ambientes inspiradas na tecnologia" ("cyber-decor: room textures inspired by technology"), Nov 2010) [5]. They were produced by "RADAR TECNISA", a trend-hunting function of "profissionais especializados" (specialist staff) that posted ideas and trends inside the platform [33]. Tecnisa later described the portal as "destinado à inspiração de forma geral" ("intended for inspiration in general") [1].
  • Amplification by the corporate blog. Blog Tecnisa, billed as the first corporate blog in Brazilian real estate, ran a steady stream of posts about launches, challenge finalists, implemented ideas and press coverage (Exame, Proxxima, Você S/A, Brasil Econômico) [2][7][25][30][35].
  • Crowdsourced marketing. Members of Zooppa (not of the portal) made the portal's launch ad, and the winning ad ran in Propaganda and Consumidor Moderno magazines [28]. Later contests with Zooppa and WeDoLogos (2012) covered collaborative-consumption ads and artistic hoardings for construction sites [30].
  • Member-made content was simply the ideas themselves. There is no evidence of curation into collections, member spotlights or co-development with idea authors after selection. Winners got prizes. Authors of ideas built later got no documented credit.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. Busarello sponsored it from the board ("tenho autonomia para tocar os projetos, sou membro do conselho", "I have autonomy to run projects, I'm a board member") [19]. Novelli's e-business / digital team ran it. Ideas went to an internal R&D committee ("comitê de P&D") for feasibility, as with the energy meter [17]. Team size: no evidence found.
  • Feedback loop. Selected ideas went to R&D, then to product, then to a specific development. In 2012 Novelli claimed "cerca de 60% das inovações da empresa partem destas duas plataformas" ("about 60% of the company's innovations come from these two platforms", meaning Ideias and Fast Dating together) [29]. That is a company claim.
  • Rules. Adults only. Content had to be lawful and inoffensive. Members kept authorship but granted Tecnisa a free, perpetual, worldwide licence for any purpose [12]. In the v1 Q&A, members asked whether they could patent an idea ("patentiar ideia") and how to edit an idea after posting [8]. IP anxiety was present from the start.
  • No superuser, ambassador or moderator-volunteer program existed.
  • Moderation lapsed visibly over time. v2 shows an idea titled "Excluir conta" ("delete account"): "Eu quero apenas excluir meu cadastro" ("I just want to delete my registration", Apr 2012), because there was apparently no self-service way to do it [36]. A 2015 idea included the author's full phone number and email and was still published in 2016 [13]. Vendor pitches ran unchecked [6][37].

Growth loops

  • Borrowed audiences were the main source of growth: partner-blog challenges (Jovem Nerd's huge audience in particular) and the Zooppa creative community [7][22][32].
  • Earned media and awards. Exame, Proxxima, Época Negócios / A.T. Kearney Best Innovator 2010 to 2013, conference talks (the 1st Brazilian Crowdsourcing, Co-Creation & Communities Conference, where Fiat Mio also presented) [23][27][30].
  • Share buttons on every idea, for Twitter, Facebook, LinkedIn and Orkut [5].
  • SEO and customer acquisition: no evidence the portal drove home sales. Tecnisa reported that 35% of its sales started online (R$87m in Q3 2011) and planned more than R$10m of internet spend in 2012, but never tied any of it to Ideias [19][30].

Evidence of business impact

Hard-ish evidence (product shipped): the Flex Paulicéia development (São Bernardo do Campo) launched with per-circuit real-time electricity monitoring, an idea sourced from the community [34][17]. Tecnisa projected "até 20%" (up to 20%) savings for residents, which is a company estimate [34]. Novelli said the meter "se tornou um diferencial importante na decisão de compra" ("became an important factor in the purchase decision") [17]. That too is a company claim with no data.

Company claims: 30 to 35 ideas approved or in implementation [3][25][29][30]; "~60% of innovations" from Ideias plus Fast Dating [29]; branding value [24]; awards [27].

Missing: no figures on cost, sales lift, NPS, buyer participation or customer retention. Tecnisa's investor materials and filings were not searched in depth (Brazilian CVM filer, not on SEC EDGAR).

Context for the shutdown: Brazil's 2014 to 2016 property slump and a wave of cancelled purchases ("distratos") forced Tecnisa to refund close to R$3bn [38]. The company posted losses from 2015 (e.g. –R$91.8m in Q2 2016) and did not return to annual profit until 2022 [39][40]. A new CEO later cut 30% of staff [38]. Busarello left after 20 years at Tecnisa and gave up executive life in 2021. His 2021 retrospective interview highlights Fast Dating and does not mention Ideias [41]. The portal's decline (no challenges by 2014, last idea in 2015) lines up with the start of that crisis. That connection is inference: no source states the reason for the shutdown.

What they did right

  1. Showed members that ideas were built. Specific, named implementations (the energy monitor, EV charging, shared bikes, VIP garages) were announced publicly within a year of launch [3][19][34]. That is the most credible thing an ideas portal can offer.
  2. A sharp, imaginative framing question. "What should your home be like in five years?" invited anyone to "dar uma de engenheiro, arquiteto, urbanista ou designer" ("play engineer, architect, urban planner or designer"), with no technical knowledge needed [3][2].
  3. Iterative, cheap pilots before the build. Blog + Orkut (2009), then Battle of Concepts (Feb 2010), then Zooppa (Aug 2010), then its own platform (Sep 2010) [2][20][21][22].
  4. Partner-blog challenges recruited fast. Using fan communities' tribal loyalty (Jovem Nerd vs Gizmodo) produced most of the portal's lifetime participants within weeks [7][32].
  5. A clear internal path for ideas. Every idea had a defined route to the R&D committee and then to a specific development [17].
  6. Evolved into something durable. The same team used the learning to build Fast Dating (from Aug 2011), a pitch program for startups and suppliers. It had 210 companies registered and 10 deals closed by April 2012, and was still running in 2018 [29][42][43]. Tecnisa itself presents Fast Dating as Ideias' successor [1].

What they did wrong

  1. Campaign, not community. Participation was bought with prizes and borrowed audiences, then left to decay. Participants grew roughly 10% in three years after the first burst [7][27]. Consequence: no core of regulars ever formed.
  2. Gamification with nothing behind it. Badges and reputation were built, but no badge was ever awarded to any participant we found in 2013 to 2016 snapshots [9][10][13]. Empty status systems tell visitors that nobody is home.
  3. Silent abandonment. The challenges page said "no challenge right now, a new one soon" for years. Ideas kept arriving unanswered until 2015, and the closure was announced only later, in a 2017 blog post [11][13][1]. Consequence: goodwill left hanging and a lapsed domain now used by a third party [14].
  4. Weak moderation and data hygiene. Members had no account-deletion option (the "Excluir conta" idea), personal phone numbers and emails stayed published, and vendor spam appeared in comments [36][13][6].
  5. Wrong audience for a one-off purchase. The portal targeted the general public (blog readers, ad creatives) rather than Tecnisa's own buyers and residents, the only group with a lasting reason to return and stakes in the outcome. No evidence it was ever pushed to residents.
  6. One-sided IP terms with no credit loop. A perpetual free licence [12], members asking about patents [8], and no documented credit or reward for authors of implemented ideas beyond the challenge prizes.
  7. Inconsistent metrics (1,161 vs 1,927 ideas a month apart [29][25]) suggest that nobody owned measurement beyond PR.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Close the loop loudly and specifically. Name the idea, the author and the place it shipped. Tecnisa's best content was "these 30 ideas will be built" [3].
  2. Ask one vivid, open question that lets non-experts imagine the product ("your home in five years") instead of a generic feedback form [2].
  3. Pilot on borrowed platforms before building your own (Orkut, Battle of Concepts, Zooppa), and keep the build cheap until demand is proven [2][20][21][22].
  4. Use partner communities' rivalries for launch bursts (blog-vs-blog challenges), but plan the next ten challenges before the first one ends [7][32].
  5. Route ideas into a named internal decision body (R&D committee) so the portal has a customer inside the company [17].

Avoid these

  1. Gamification nobody can earn. Launch badges only if someone owns awarding them and they reflect real outcomes [9][13].
  2. Silent sunsets. Announce pauses, archive gracefully, keep or redirect the domain, and give members a way to delete accounts and data [11][14][36].
  3. Building a community for a once-a-decade purchase around strangers. Anchor it on existing customers and users (for an app: active users and power users) who will be around to see their ideas ship.

  • content_engine 2: staff inspiration and trend posts plus strong blog and press amplification, but no regular cadence, education or curated member content.
  • member_ownership 1: members only submitted ideas; no moderators, ambassadors, credit or co-development, and a one-sided IP licence.
  • staff_investment 2: a board-level sponsor and a 2012 rebuild, then visible neglect (empty challenges, unmoderated personal data) from about 2013.
  • business_impact_evidence 2: concrete shipped features (the energy monitor in Flex Paulicéia) but only company claims on value; no sales or retention data.
  • durability 1: a burst in 2010, flat by 2011, dormant by 2014, dead by 2016, and the domain lost.
