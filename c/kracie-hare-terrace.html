TL;DR

  • What it is: a free, members-only diary-style forum opened on 18 Jun 2025 by Kracie (the shampoo, kampo-medicine and confectionery maker formerly part of Kanebo), together with community vendor QON. Members post the "hare" (bright moments) and "ke" (ordinary days) of daily life, and staff add themes on health, food and rest. It runs on QON's Beach platform. It had 17,687 members and 44,642 posts on 8 Nov 2026 (Kracie press release; community homepage).
  • Why people come back: low-effort daily rituals and points. More than half of all posts sit in about 17 never-ending check-in threads ("What did you drink today?" has 2,068 comments, "Today's snapshot" 6,828). Every comment earns 30 points, and points enter a monthly lottery for ¥500 Amazon gift cards. About one member topic in five is a "I won!" report.
  • What the company feeds it: one staff persona, "Haruka", who posts a talk theme about twice a month with worked examples and "sentence starters"; a prize campaign every one to three months, each requiring comments on several themes; and, since December 2025, two AI features from QON: a turtle that recommends topics, and a robot, "Musubo", that now posts more topics than the human staff do.
  • Biggest mistake: the community has almost no visible link to Kracie. None of the roughly 240 topic titles in its monthly archive names a Kracie product, the prizes are gift cards and other companies' goods, and QON rather than Kracie runs the campaigns and the help desk. Two years in, Kracie has published nothing it learned or sold through it. Activity is broad but shallow and largely bought.
  • The lesson: sentence-starter prompts and "write your day" rituals reliably get ordinary people posting. But if the community never connects back to the product (sampling, co-creation, research the company acts on), you have paid to rent a pleasant diary, not built a brand community.

What the community is

Hare Tokidoki Terrace ("Sunny, with occasional Terrace", a play on a Japanese weather forecast) lives at beach.jp/community/KRACIE. It is one official "circle", the おしゃべりサロン (Chat Salon), plus a "membership circles" area where members can start their own sub-groups; none were listed in Nov 2026. Reading is public; posting requires free registration. The page header carries the Kracie logo (topic page).

It is one of the 60+ branded communities inside QON's "“絆”のコミュニティ" (Kizuna Community) network, which showed 2,339,536 members and 4.36M posts in Nov 2026 and promotes Hare Terrace from its top page with a 'utm_source=KIZUNA' link (Kizuna homepage; Wayback capture of that link, Sep 2025). Hare Terrace's 17.7k members are about 0.8% of that network.

Unofficial spaces: none found. There is no Reddit presence (an Arctic Shift search for "Kracie" returned nothing), a full-archive X search for the community's name returned zero posts, and web searches find no blogs or reviews about it, only unrelated cafés and gelato shops called "Hare Terrace".

Status: active and growing slowly, with a campaign running from 30 Oct to 7 Dec 2026 (campaign topic).

Origin and strategy

Stated purpose. The launch release (18 Jun 2025) frames the space as responding to rising interest in well-being. Kracie wants to "listen not only to consumers' surface needs but to the 'voice of the heart' underneath", to "search for a Kracie-like form of well-being" from those voices, and to "connect it to value creation as a company". The release also promises themes on "mind and body health", "food and nutrition" and "rest and leisure" (Kracie release; QON release). The release says it chose QON's network so it could talk with "people of many lifestyles and values" and make "new discoveries" in everyday life. Joining the community and its whole tone are product-free by design.

How it fits Kracie's strategy (context, partly inference). Kracie merged its three operating companies (home products, pharmaceuticals, foods) into Kracie Co., Ltd. on 1 Oct 2023. Its first Integrated Report (2026) lists "from 'LIKE' to 'LOVE': a shift to fan marketing" and "a shift to lifetime-value management" among its three strategic shifts. It targets ¥110bn group sales by 2030 and set up a Well-being Research Centre in Oct 2024 (Integrated Report 2026, PDF). Before Hare Terrace, Kracie's fan work was deliberately tiny. Its "共想いカンパニー" (Tomo-omoi Company) screened about 50 loyal web members from a 25,853-person survey and ran two-month online "meetups" of about 10 people, complete with fan "employee IDs" and an "entrance ceremony". It sat alongside a consumer-research unit, くらしの夢中観測所, under a fan co-creation platform called くらしえ港 (MarkeZine, 23 Oct 2023; MarkeZine on the research unit, Dec 2024). Hare Terrace looks like the scale complement to that deep-but-tiny model: a large, low-touch listening space. Kracie has not said so publicly; that is my inference.

Champion. The launch topic carries a message signed "Kracie Co., Ltd. staff, all of us"; no named executive or team has spoken about Hare Terrace in any interview, report or event I could find. The 2023 fan-community leads were 津田聖一郎 (Tsuda Seiichirō, Corporate Planning and the "CRAZY創造部") and 北原 (Kitahara) (MarkeZine). Whether they own Hare Terrace is unknown.

Corporate parent. Hoyu bought 60% of Kracie in 2009 and the rest on 30 Mar 2012 (Nikkei). That explains the March 2026 collaboration with Hoyu's own QON-built community (below).

Trajectory (my arithmetic from the table). Posting ran at about 4,500 a month during the opening campaign, fell to about 1,700 a month between Sep 2025 and Feb 2026, and recovered to about 3,350 a month during the Feb–Apr 2026 campaigns. It has held at about 2,600 a month since May 2026. Member growth came in campaign-shaped bursts: +3,600 in seven weeks (opening), +3,200 in six weeks (Feb–Mar 2026), and about 500 a month otherwise. Posts per member are about 2.5. The daily threads alone (about 24,600 comments) are about 55% of all posts, so the active core is small and posts daily. The same handful of names (ぴよこ, shiona, たんぼマスター, たか) recur across the daily threads.

Thread depth by type. Staff discussion themes typically get 250 to 570 comments (the January 2026 "dish on a freezing night" campaign theme got 566). The opening campaign topic got 836. AI-bot (Musubo) topics get about 20.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Points and lotteries (the main engine). QON's points scheme pays 150 points for registering, 30 per comment, 15 per new topic, 20 per survey, 5 when someone replies to you and 1 per stamp given or received, up to 1,000 a day. Points enter monthly draws for Amazon gift cards and catalogue gifts, and they expire after a year of inactivity (points page, shared Kizuna template). Because a comment pays double a new topic, the design rewards piling into existing threads. That is exactly what the daily check-in threads show: answers like "白湯" (hot water) or "コーヒー、緑茶" repeated every day (drink thread). About 43 of the roughly 225 member-created topics in the archive are prize-win reports ("6月のポイントくじ、当選!!", "Amazonギフト500円当選❗🎁") (Jul 2025 archive; Oct 2025 archive).
  2. Comment-gated campaigns every one to three months. Opening (18 Jun–3 Sep 2025): one comment = entry; 300 prizes. "Everyday talk is treasure" series (Dec 2025–Apr 2026): (1) comment on 5 of 13 themes, (2) write about 100 characters on 1 of 2 themes, (3) post 3 comments, encouraging replies to other members; 40 prizes each. First anniversary (27 May–8 Jul 2026): comment on both themes for one of 100 Henri Charpentier sablés in a custom anniversary design. "いまのわたし、メモしとこ。" (30 Oct–7 Dec 2026): comment on 3 of 6 themes; 50 prizes (opening; C1; C2; C3; anniversary; Oct 2026). The campaign rules are well designed: each step pushes for a different behaviour (breadth, then depth, then replies).
  3. Daily rituals members own. Members, not staff, created most check-in threads: "今日の夕食のメニューは?" (1,539 comments), "何を飲みますか?" (2,068), "今日、食べたオヤツは何ですか?" (1,985), "今日の体調は？いい、まあまあ、悪い?" (673). Several date from the first week (Jun 2025 archive). They work like a daily diary and attendance board.
  4. A gentle host persona. Every official post is signed by "Haruka", in the soft register of a friendly café owner, with emoticons and concrete sample answers.
  5. Light recognition. Twelve reaction stamps ("みてるよ", "ひとやすみ", "拍手"…) give every post some acknowledgment. In campaign 2, Haruka quoted three members' real posts as models of a good 100-character comment (C2).
  6. No status layer. No visible levels, badges, streaks or leaderboards; points are private.
  7. Switching costs: points (lost after a year idle or on leaving) and a personal diary history. Both are weak.

The content engine

  • Staff themes about twice a month. Counting the monthly archives, the staff account posted about 28 topics between Dec 2025 and Nov 2026, and none in Sep 2026. Themes stay on the stated well-being brief: sleep worries, "what do you do when you feel unwell", scents that calm you, a "on/off switch for rest mode", habits you'd like to copy (Oct 2025 archive; Feb 2026 archive).
  • Writing scaffolds: the standout tactic. Each prompt includes two or three sample answers. The Oct 2026 "ことばカード" (word cards) topic offers six cards ("the closet is full", "scrolling my phone for no reason", "I always put myself last") plus "つづきことば" sentence starters ("そういえば…", "でも…", "ほんとは…") for people who don't know how to begin (topic). It drew 287 comments in a month.
  • AI content from QON (Dec 2025–). A turtle-shaped AI pops up in the corner and recommends topics based on what you've done. "Musubo", an AI robot, posts topics with a "predicted post count" ("予想投稿数：97件"). Its first topic got 21 comments (announcement; Musubo topic). Musubo posted about 39 topics in Dec 2025–Nov 2026, more than the human staff's 28, at about a tenth of the engagement.
  • Collaboration topic. In March 2026 Haruka ran a shared theme with Hoyu's QON community 聞ける、話せる ヘアカラーの部屋 about getting your hair ready for "hare no hi" (special days). It drew 295 comments and pointed members to the sister community (topic; Hoyu community launch, Jan 2024).
  • What's absent: no product education, how-to content, kampo or nutrition expertise, staff or developer interviews, samples, events, newsletter or video. Compare Attenir's community on the same platform, which runs developer interviews, product trials and votes (see the attenir-fan-community report).

People, programs and governance

  • Team: one public persona ("「ハレ ときどき テラス」コミュニティスタッフ" / Haruka). QON co-runs it: QON "provides" or "hosts" the campaigns, and the Beach help desk handles enquiries (opening; anniversary). Whether Haruka is Kracie or QON staff is not disclosed; from the campaign wording, QON likely operates day to day.
  • No ambassador, superuser or moderator program. Member circles are possible but none are visible.
  • Rules: Beach's general terms and campaign rules, plus point penalties for abuse (Beach event rules).
  • Feedback into product: no evidence found. There are no surveys about Kracie products, no co-creation, and no "you said, we did" posts.

Growth loops

  • Rented traffic from QON's network. Promotion from the Kizuna top page and QON's 2.3M-member Beach base is the main inflow (Kizuna homepage). QON has used affiliate point sites to recruit Kizuna members (docomo's ポイント広場 paid d-points for registering and posting once) (listing, now ended). Sweepstakes bloggers also report Beach campaign wins (example, 2020), so part of the audience is prize hunters rather than Kracie customers.
  • Campaign bursts line up with the steepest member growth (see the table).
  • Cross-community collabs (Hoyu) move members between QON communities.
  • No organic loop found: no referral scheme, no social sharing, and no outside chatter. Kracie's own site and brands do not appear to promote it beyond the launch release (Kracie news).

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: none. Kracie is private (owned by Hoyu); its Integrated Report 2026 does not mention Hare Terrace. It does mention a "community site" for one hair-care brand and a general shift to fan marketing (PDF).
  • Vendor claims (not about Kracie): QON says Attenir's community members grew annual spend 5.6× faster than non-members, and cites other wins (SIXPAD, Kashima City) (QON "ikura system" page). These are QON's marketing and say nothing about Hare Terrace.
  • What the company could plausibly get: a stream of unprompted well-being talk (sleep, cold-sensitivity, snacking, stress) relevant to its kampo, toiletries and confectionery lines. The sleep theme alone yielded 343 comments, many detailed ("夜中に何度か目が覚めてしまうのが悩みです") (topic). Whether anyone at Kracie mines it is unknown.

What they did right

  • Lowered the bar to posting. Sample answers, word cards and sentence starters produced 250 to 570 comments per staff theme, with many thoughtful replies (ことばカード).
  • Let members own the rituals. The member-made daily threads carry most of the volume and keep running between campaigns.
  • Designed campaigns as a curriculum. Breadth (5 of 13 themes), then depth (about 100 characters), then replies (3 comments). The anniversary campaign looked back on "365 words" from members with a custom-design gift (C1; anniversary).
  • Kept the tone non-commercial and well-being-centred, in keeping with its "listen to the heart" purpose.
  • Reached 17.7k members in 17 months with modest prizes (mostly ¥500 gift cards).

What they did wrong

  • No bridge to the brand. None of about 240 archived topic titles names a Kracie product. Prizes are Amazon cards, fruit catalogues, Imabari towels, sablés and another company's soup. The space would work the same with any sponsor's logo (archives). Consequence: little reason to believe it builds brand love or LTV, the two goals in Kracie's own strategy.
  • Engagement is extrinsic and shallow. Pay-per-comment points, comment-gated lotteries and win-report threads make up much of the culture. Campaign posts as thin as "参加します" ("I'm in") and "ヘアカット" ("haircut") appear (anniversary; hair collab). Activity drops when campaigns end (about 1,700 posts a month in late 2025, versus 3,350 during campaigns).
  • Thin staffing, backfilled by AI. About two human themes a month, none in Sep 2026. QON's bot "Musubo" now posts more topics than staff, at about a tenth of the response, and publicly misses its own "predicted post counts" (97 predicted, 21 actual) (Musubo).
  • Outsourced identity. QON hosts the campaigns, runs the help desk and owns the member base and platform. Kracie has no public champion or voice inside the space.
  • No visible closing of the loop. No posts telling members what Kracie learned or changed.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Scaffold every prompt. Give two or three sample answers and sentence starters ("そういえば…", "ほんとは…"). Ordinary users post far more when they're shown what a good post looks like.
  2. Seed one daily ritual thread (today's snapshot, today's meal or workout) and let members create more. It becomes the community's heartbeat between events.
  3. Sequence campaigns to teach behaviours: breadth, then depth, then replying to others, each with its own entry rule.
  4. Quote members' real posts as examples. It is cheap recognition and models quality.
  5. Mark anniversaries with a look back at members' own words and a custom keepsake.

Avoid these

  1. Don't pay per comment unless you want volume over value. Pay-per-comment points plus lotteries attract prize hunters and turn threads into attendance logs. Reward replies, depth or contributions the company actually uses instead.
  2. Don't leave the product out entirely. Wellness talk is a fine way in, but without sampling, co-creation or research the company acts on, the community can't show business value and is easy to cut.
  3. Don't let a vendor's bot or persona replace a visible company voice. AI topics that miss their own predictions and an anonymous host make the space feel generic; members can't build ties to the people behind the brand.

Scores explained

  • Retention mechanics 3: points, lotteries, campaigns and daily rituals work, but they are extrinsic, and there is no status layer or product hook.
  • Content engine 2: well-crafted prompts, but only about two human themes a month plus AI filler; no education or product content.
  • Member ownership 3: members created most topics and the busiest daily threads, but there are no member leaders, circles or member-made resources.
  • Staff investment 2: one persona, outsourced campaign operations, and a month (Sep 2026) with no staff topics.
  • Business impact evidence 1: nothing public from Kracie; only vendor claims about other clients.
  • Durability 3: 17 months old, still growing and posting about 2,600 times a month, but it depends on QON's contract, traffic and prizes.
