TL;DR

  • What it is: KPN's Dutch-language customer forum (community.kpn.com, long called "KPN Forum"), opened in November 2010. Customers answer each other's questions about internet, TV, mobile and billing, and a paid team of about 15 KPN moderators steps in on account-specific issues. It has run on three platforms: unknown software (2010), Lithium (2011) and inSided, now Gainsight (2016 to today).
  • Why people come back: two different groups. Most visitors arrive once from Google with a problem and leave. A small core (about 10 invited "superusers" plus a few dozen regulars) comes back daily because helping is "almost addictive". They also get status (a title ladder, badges, the Superuser honour), insider access (a private board, direct lines to moderators, early product information, beta panels) and real-life days out at KPN.
  • What KPN feeds it: a dedicated team (two people in 2010, a team of its own from August 2014, 14 to 18 moderators since 2022), a moderator-written Kennisbank, firmware "Product Updates" that appear nowhere else, test panels, yearly member events, Christmas parcels and cards, and anniversary games.
  • Biggest mistake: letting a small, invisible inner circle become the community's public voice. Superusers who correct critics, best-answer marks that go to "less critical heavy posters", locked threads, and a private board ordinary customers can't see now draw press criticism (Totaal TV, 2026). Meanwhile moderator response times stretch to days and topic volume has fallen by roughly half since 2021.
  • The one lesson: a support community can pay for itself for 15 years (KPN claimed €4.5M saved in 2014 alone). But if your top volunteers are hand-picked, rewarded in private and seen to defend the company, newcomers stop trusting the answers. Make the criteria public and keep the volunteers clearly independent.

What the community is

KPN Community is the official peer-support forum of KPN, the Dutch incumbent telecom operator. It lives at community.kpn.com; forum.kpn.com still resolves to it. It is organised into product boards (Internet, KPN TV, Mobiel, Vast bellen, E-mail en cloud, Modems, Wifi-versterkers, Online veiligheid, Facturen), a Kennisbank (knowledge base), a Product Updates section, a "Testen met jou" area for surveys and test panels, and an off-topic living room, "De huiskamer" (KPN Community home; Kennisbank; Testen met jou). Sign-in uses KPN ID or Facebook. The software has said "Powered by Gainsight" since Gainsight bought inSided.

Official layers:

  • Open forum: questions, answers, likes, "best answer" marks.
  • Superusers: an invite-only honour (formerly called "Expert") with a private board (Dit zijn wij: KPN Superusers; profile titles thread, Sep 2025).
  • KPN staff: moderators ("Erik van KPN", "Marcia van KPN"…), plus KPN field technicians and employees who post under their own titles ("KPN Monteur", "KPN Medewerker").
  • Test panels and betas: the TV+ app beta and the Modem & Wifi Testpanel (Test mee, Sep 2025).
  • IRL member days (see below).

Unofficial spaces: KPN threads on Tweakers' Gathering of Tweakers forum, such as "[KPN Glasvezel] Ervaringen" (Tweakers KPN forum); the independent "KPN Gebruikers Groep" at kpn.gebruikers.eu, whose home page poll shows 62% of 1,122 voters not satisfied with KPN (KPN Gebruikers Groep); Providerforum.nl; and ad-hoc Reddit threads in r/Netherlands that point people to the KPN forum (example, Feb 2024). There is no official or large unofficial KPN subreddit: Arctic Shift has no r/KPN. Status: active, investing (events in 2024 and 2025, a 15th-anniversary campaign in November 2025), but quieter than in 2020-2021.

Origin and strategy

KPN's own retrospective says the community began in November 2010 as "a small experiment to be 'visible online'". It had two staff, topics were routed by hand, and an answer could take a week (15 jaar KPN Community, Nov 3 2025). It grew out of KPN Webcare, the Twitter and Facebook service team (@KPNwebcare, from 2009). Then-CMO Coen Olde Olthof described the logic in a Salesforce case study from about 2013: "Webcare started with three people and now has 80. The next step is to let customers help each other… We now have 30,000 active users… Transparency is the magic word" (Salesforce: KPN Social Care).

The strategy was explicit. KPN's 2015 Lithium-awards entry, by Lourens Reijgersberg, Hugo de Vries and Thomas Pel (Social Innovations Manager), says that in 2010 KPN set out to become "the best Dutch service provider in 2015" while making "a shift left" to "online first". The Lithium community, introduced in 2011, was "one of the key strategies to fast-track both ambitions" (Lithys 2015: KPN – Support Savings MVP). The business model was call deflection first, then product feedback and co-creation. In August 2014 KPN gave the forum its own team instead of ad-hoc Webcare moderators (15 jaar in cijfers, Nov 2025).

Over time the purpose shifted. As kpn.com's self-service tools improved, "fewer standard questions came in", and KPN now pitches the community as "a real co-creation platform" (15 jaar, Nov 2025). KPN folded in its sub-brands: the Telfort Forum closed on Nov 2, 2020 and sent its users to the KPN Forum (Telfort Forum closure notice), and XS4ALL customers were welcomed in March 2022 (XS4ALL klanten welkom, Mar 2022).

Platform history: unknown software (2010) → Lithium (July 2011; pre-2011 posts were lost) → inSided (2016; the new forum went live around June 2016 (Feedback nieuw forum, Jun 2016)) → Gainsight CC after the acquisition. A further behind-the-scenes rebuild was under way in April 2024 (Besloten boards zijn ineens zichtbaar, Apr 15 2024). Pre-2019 topics now sit in an "Archief" board that returns HTTP 403 to the public (observed Nov 2026).

Trajectory. Growth ran from 2011 to 2014, with posts per month up 60% in a year after badges and a redesign. Traffic peaked from the late 2010s to 2021: the Telfort merger in November 2020 and COVID pushed topic creation to roughly 30,000+ a year. The 2023 figure of "2,000 topics a month" fits the ID-based estimate of about 24,000 a year. Since then volume has drifted down to about 16,000-18,000 topics a year. That is about 45-50% fewer than in 2021 (an estimate: IDs may include deleted or private topics). This matches Totaal TV's July 2026 claim that "the number of topics on the community is falling", as service moves to chat, chatbot and phone (Totaal TV, Jul 18 2026).

What keeps people coming back

1. The helper's loop (the real engine). The community is a classic 90-9-1 shape. KPN itself says most registered users post once and leave, or only read (15 jaar in cijfers). The core returns for the satisfaction of fixing things. Superuser Joep1965, a ferry deckhand, says: "It is almost addictive, you check in every day… mostly on my phone, on the couch… It gives a kick when you calm them down and solve their problem… And then you read: 'It works, thanks!'" (KPN blog "Klanten helpen klanten", c. Dec 2024). Ten members account for a huge share of output: the top four posted about 220,000 replies between them.

2. Status ladder. Automatic rank titles (Nieuwkomer → Deelnemer → Helper → Slimmerik → Wijsgeer → Topper…) come from activity. The thresholds are deliberately secret: "the exact limits… are not (and will not be) made public", as a superuser put it in Sep 2025 (profile titles thread). Milestone badges ("5.000 berichten", "2.500 antwoorden") and event badges ("15 Jaar KPN Community") show on posts. KPN was the first customer worldwide to ship Lithium Badges (beta, 2014). It reported a 10% rise in activity among low- and mid-ranked members (Lithys 2015), and Lithium cited a 26% increase in onboarding activities (Lithium PR, 2014).

3. Superuser honour and insider access. Members are invited for "exceptional knowledge, activity and a positive-critical attitude". The perks: a private board, direct contact with moderators, first access to information on new products and changes, priority for pilots, and invitations to webinars, parties and drinks. Status is reviewed and can be withdrawn (Kom jij ons superuserteam versterken?, Jul 2022). Bind reported that superusers also get a Christmas parcel, a birthday card and a budget for outings (Bind, May 2022).

4. Being listened to: betas and test panels. Members tested the MyKPN portal beta in 2014 on a co-creation space called "OurKPN" (Lithys 2015). Later they tested the KPN TV+ Box, the TV+ app beta, the MijnKPN wifi scan and the Modem & Wifi Testpanel (15 jaar; Internet board). Member ikheetjeff: "Since recently I test a new beta of the TV app with a closed group. Then I check several times a day… KPN feels almost like the bakery around the corner" (KPN blog). Members also put panel membership in their signatures ("Lid Modem & Wifi Testpanel").

5. Real-life days. These go back to "the very first customer days with our most active members" (ex-community manager Allart), and include online coffee mornings and an escape-room game during COVID (oud-collega's, Nov 2025). Recent events: a day at KPN HQ "De Link" in Rotterdam with a roof terrace, water taxi and the IoT Innovation Playground (May 2024) (recap); a fibre meet-up (2024); a security day (Mar 2025); a SAIL Amsterdam boat trip on KPN's media boat "De Louis" for the 15th anniversary (Aug 2025) (recap); and a visit to a telecom museum (2026). Member Hoopvol: "Recently a CEO of KPN came to give a presentation. That is also a bit of appreciation" (KPN blog).

6. A living room. "De huiskamer" runs fun topics (pets, series, "what's your wifi name?"), an F1 prediction competition (5th edition in 2026), World Cup 2026 prediction threads, photo contests and the 15-year "Kopkrakers" puzzle game. Since December 2025 it also runs a "Community Spotlight" where each moderator names one member to praise (Spotlight #1). Members thank the team for mailed cards and gifts (Bedankt voor het kaartje).

7. Parasocial ties to named moderators. Moderators post as "Firstname van KPN" with personal bios (Star Trek fan, cats, padel), and several have stayed 10+ years. Marcia has 31,190 replies and Erik 29,733 (team intro; cijfers).

Switching costs are weak: the community doesn't lock anyone into KPN. A superuser described moving to Ziggo, being a superuser on Ziggo's forum, then returning to KPN (superuser intros).

The content engine

  • Kennisbank (KB): written and maintained by KPN moderators "and sometimes by active members". Topics include modem settings, low fibre speeds, SuperWifi and TV+ Box installation. Feedback on articles is invited publicly and summarised in quarterly KB updates (the latest is Q3 2026) (Kennisbank; Feedback op de Kennisbank, Sep 2024). The top KB article has 344,865 views.
  • Product Updates: release notes for modems, TV boxes and apps. KPN says the forum is "the only place" to find them (KPN blog, Nov 2023). That is a strong reason for enthusiasts to come back. A separate feedback thread on update policy opened in June 2026 (topic).
  • Blogs and "KPN Voor Jou": articles on perks, security (with Seniorweb and KPN's Mooiste Contact Fonds), streaming services and product launches, often with campaign tracking links (e.g. "campaignid=so:c=merk-community"). The community doubles as a marketing channel (KPN TV+ launch post).
  • Member content folded back in: in 2014 KPN found members "better at explaining our products in a user-friendly way than we were". It worked their content into internal and external FAQs and piped forum and KB answers into Salesforce Knowledge, so phone agents could reuse them (Lithys 2015).
  • No video, podcast or academy layer was found. The content is text, made by staff, and close to support.

People, programs and governance

  • Team: 2 people in 2010; ad-hoc Webcare moderators (the first was "Daan", July 2011) until a dedicated team in Aug 2014; about 16 named moderators plus a team lead and community manager in Jan 2022; 18 in Nov 2023; a team lead, community manager (Shyrin), coach and 14-15 moderators in Nov 2025, with 3 Webcare staff as backup. Moderators come from customer service, and many bios place them in Groningen, KPN's Webcare hub (CustomerFirst on KPN Webcare Groningen, Jan 2014). Some work part-time shifts ("my shifts are usually no longer than 4 hours a day").
  • What moderators do: they handle account-specific cases (customers put their customer number in a private profile field), escalate to technical teams, move and merge topics, mark best answers and enforce house rules. Ex-moderator Joran: "We stay with the problem from beginning to end… and know how to find the right people inside the organisation." One case ended with a digging crew rerouting a rural customer's copper line (oud-collega's).
  • Employee bridge: in 2014 forum questions could be flagged into TeamKPN, the internal social network, to pull in niche experts (Lithys 2015). KPN technicians still post as "KPN Monteur".
  • Superuser program: invitation only, by the team or by current superusers. Criteria are listed qualitatively (expertise, "positive (critical) attitude", reusable answers, friendly style, "offline contacts with other superusers and the moderators"). Status is reviewed regularly and can be withdrawn (program page). Superusers are explicitly not KPN representatives.
  • Rules: house rules ban "KPN" in usernames and multiple accounts. Moderator decisions are final, and public discussion of moderation is not allowed: "Respect the decision of Team KPN Community and do not question it in public" (Ik word betutteld, Sep 2026).
  • Feedback loop: the forum works as an outage early-warning system ("If there are general outages… we see that on the forum very quickly"). A forum report about a broken series recording led KPN to proactively message every affected customer (KPN blog, Nov 2023). Ex-moderator Paul credits "short lines with technical colleagues" for fast firmware fixes during TV-box update crises (oud-collega's).

Growth loops

  • SEO: the main loop. Answers are optimised for search engines (Bind, 2022), and the top topics have 600,000-840,000 views each. Most superusers' origin stories start with "I googled a problem and landed on the forum" (Klanten helpen klanten).
  • Brand consolidation: the Telfort (2020) and XS4ALL (2022) customer bases were funnelled in.
  • Service-channel routing: KPN Webcare's X bio and kpn.com help pages point to community.kpn.com (@KPNwebcare), and Reddit users cite the forum as the place to find answers.
  • Pre-sales showroom: prospective switchers read provider forums before choosing ("Before I switch provider, these forums are very handy to read up on", a reader comment at Totaal TV, Jul 2026). This loop also cuts the other way (see below).
  • No referral program or member-get-member loop tied to the community was found.

Evidence of business impact

All the figures below are company or vendor claims; none are audited or appear in KPN's annual reports (no evidence found).

  • €1.7M in call-deflection savings by end-2013; €4.5M in 2014, from 1M "answers found" (KPN's Lithys 2015 entry).
  • ~$4M per year saved, 45% of questions answered by customers, 600,000 answers found per year, 5M visits per year, and one third of community ideas implemented (inSided case study; summarised by CX Inspiration Hub).
  • Product outcomes: the MyKPN portal (2014) and the KPN TV+ Box were "tested, discussed and improved with the help of community members" (KPN, Nov 2025).
  • KPN keeps paying 15+ moderators, events and gifts 16 years in, which suggests it still sees a return. No retention, churn or NPS link was ever published.

What they did right

  1. A clear business case from day one. The forum was framed as call deflection, measured as "answers found", and turned into euros. That won budget and headcount in 2014 and has kept the community funded for 16 years (Lithys 2015).
  2. A dedicated, stable, human-named staff. Moderators sign as "Name van KPN", stay for a decade and handle account-level cases in public threads. That mix of peer help and the power to actually fix things is why members bother.
  3. Making the forum the source of truth for enthusiasts. Product Updates appear only there, and test panels recruit there. Power users have a reason to visit that has nothing to do with having a problem.
  4. Early, measurable gamification. The first Lithium badge rollout worldwide gave +10% activity among lower and mid-ranked members.
  5. Recycling member knowledge into the company. Community content went into FAQs and into Salesforce Knowledge for phone agents. Forum questions were routed to employee experts through TeamKPN.
  6. Low-key but real recognition: cards, Christmas parcels, IRL days at HQ, a CEO talk, anniversary campaigns and the new moderator-nominated Spotlight.

What they did wrong

  1. An opaque inner circle. Superuser status is invitation only, the criteria are qualitative, the rank thresholds are secret, and the superusers have a private board. Totaal TV (Oct 2026) groups KPN with Ziggo and Odido: providers where "a small group of loyal members gets a special status", where "what is discussed there is not visible to ordinary members", and where superclients "play moderator", pointing newcomers to the house rules (Totaal TV, Oct 18 2026). Consequence: a perception that answers are pro-KPN, which hurts the forum's value as a showroom.
  2. Volunteers seen as defending the company. "Why do I only get answers that defend KPN?" (Nov 2022) was closed by the community manager (thread). "All superusers against one defenceless customer" (Oct 2022) ended with a warning and a lock (thread). A Tweakers user in 2024 said "most (not all) moderators or KPN employees play dumb" about TV-box downgrades (GoT, Feb 2024).
  3. Recognition systems that demotivate. Best-answer marks go to moderators' "best-fitting" replies or to superusers rather than to whoever solved the problem. One helper said this makes him "stop helping for a while" (Aug 2026 thread).
  4. Heavy-handed, closed moderation of criticism. Moderators lock and delete threads about moderation and require any challenge to go through private messages. Bans are permanent with no appeal email, and one banned member's case surfaced during a two-day outage (Ban ongedaan maken, Jun 2024; Ik word betutteld, Sep 2026).
  5. Slow staff response as volume shifts. Members told others "the waiting time is currently about five days" for a moderator (Jul 2023) (thread). Totaal TV reports fewer moderators, longer waits and a shift to private resolution, partly so that one customer's compensation doesn't become everyone's. "The desired success seems to have been reached: the number of topics is falling" (Totaal TV, Jul 2026).
  6. History lost in migrations. All pre-July-2011 content was dropped in the Lithium move, and pre-2019 topics now sit in a 403 "Archief" board, which removes long-tail SEO and community memory (15 jaar in cijfers).
  7. Under-built ambassador program (Bind's 2022 critique): criteria not public, no path for the "9%" to grow into superusers, no direct line from superusers to technicians (Bind). The 2022 call for new superusers was a step, but the core was still "about ten" people in 2023.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Price the community in support savings from the start. Count "answers found" (views of solved threads), convert them into deflected contacts, and report the figure. KPN's €4.5M number bought it a dedicated team for a decade.
  2. Give power users an information edge they can't get elsewhere. Put release notes and changelogs, beta sign-ups and test panels inside the community. Enthusiasts will come back without needing a problem.
  3. Staff with named, long-tenured humans who can actually fix accounts. "Firstname from Company" moderators who escalate inside the company make peer support credible.
  4. Recycle community answers into your docs and agent tooling. Moderator-curated KB articles, FAQs co-written with members, and community answers available to phone and chat agents.
  5. Small, physical recognition for the core. Cards, holiday gifts, a yearly day at HQ with an executive, anniversary games. It costs little next to the deflection value of a few people writing 20,000-75,000 replies each.

Avoid these

  1. Don't let the inner circle become invisible and unaccountable. Publish superuser criteria and rank thresholds, keep the private board for logistics rather than for discussing members, and label volunteers clearly as independent. Otherwise newcomers will read every defence of the company as astroturf.
  2. Don't take credit away from the people who solved the problem. Best-answer marks, kudos and spotlights must go to the person who solved it, not to staff or favourites, or your best helpers will quietly stop.
  3. Don't silence meta-criticism or let response times drift while volume falls. Locking "why do you defend the company?" threads and moving fixes into private channels saves money in the short run, but the community loses its value as a public knowledge base and showroom, and critics move to Tweakers and Reddit, where you have no voice at all.
