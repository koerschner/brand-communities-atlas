TL;DR

  • What it is. スバ学 ("Suba-gaku", Subaru School) is SUBARU Corporation's official Japanese fan community at community.subaru.jp. It opened on 20 Nov 2023 on Asobica's coorum platform and replaced the 2000s–2023 members site #スバコミ. Everything is styled as a school: members are "students", the CTO Tetsuo Fujinuki is the "principal" (学長), hobby boards are "clubs" (部活), the safety-story board is the "nurse's office" (保健室), and offline events are "extracurricular lessons" and a "school festival" (press release).
  • Why people come back. Mostly friendship and hobby chat among older, long-time Subaru owners ("Subarists"), not product help. The site reached ≈2,750 members and a 74% reply rate within about three months (事務局, Mar 2024), then 11,500 members by March 2026 (事務局). Free talk, travel, photos and "other" boards hold about two-thirds of the ≈30,000 posts. The annual スバ学祭 at the Gunma plant turns online acquaintances into friends you meet in person.
  • What the company feeds it. Themed posting prompts with limited badges, points and five ranks, polls that engineers want answered ("where do you put your foot on cruise control?"), a printed student-ID sticker, factory festivals with CTO talk shows, and promotion through dealers and the owner magazine.
  • Biggest mistake. Two related ones. Subaru shut its long-running #スバコミ with ~5 weeks' notice and migrated no member data, so the new school started from zero. Then in 2026 it turned the free スバ学祭 into a paid (¥2,200), first-come-first-served SUBARU FAN MEETING whose weekday ticket launch crashed and double-charged buyers. The community's own boards filled with complaints.
  • The one lesson. A playful metaphor plus hobby sub-clubs gets older owners posting and meeting. But if the strategic content you want (here, safety testimonials) is only ~1% of posts, you need a deliberate pipeline for it. Membership growth alone won't produce it.

What the community is

スバ学 is a login-gated web community built on coorum, a white-label "loyal customer platform" from the Tokyo startup Asobica. Reading is mostly public, but posting needs a free SUBARU ID, the same ID used by the owner app マイスバル (My SUBARU) and the drive app SUBAROAD. You don't need to own a Subaru to join (Shizuoka Subaru dealer guide).

The site is run by スバ学事務局 (the community office). SUBARU's domestic sales & marketing division drives it: the champion is Atsushi Amuro, manager of the advertising section in marketing promotion, who also sits in the customer-experience group (coorum PR). The company's brand promoters SUBARU STARS blog about it and staff the events (Ameblo, Oct 2024).

Unofficial and adjacent spaces. Japanese Subaru owners also gather on みんカラ (Minkara, Carview's car SNS), where many スバ学 members cross-post event reports (example). Regional dealers promote スバ学 on their own sites (Ibaraki, Hiroshima). The US-side communities (Subaru of America's ambassadors, r/subaru) are a separate entry, subaru.md. Status today: active and growing.

Origin and strategy

Lineage. Subaru owner clubs go back to the Tokyo SUBARU Club of January 1959. By April 1961 there were 24 clubs with over 1,200 members, according to Subaru's own trivia page (#スバコミ trivia vol. 62). The owner magazine カートピア (Cartopia) has run since 1972 (J-CAST, Nov 2023). Online, members.subaru.jp ran as "スバルWEBコミュニティ SUBARU Plus" by 2012 (Wayback, Dec 2012) and later as "SUBARU WEB COMMUNITY #スバコミ" (its X account dates from July 2016: @subakomi). It had boards, model-specific car communities, a golf circle and a mail magazine (members.subaru.jp/with).

Why kill #スバコミ and start over. On 21 Aug 2023 Subaru announced that #スバコミ would close on 29 Sep 2023 (X). Amuro gave the reasons in Asobica's case study (coorum case, Aug 2024): a core of long-time fans had become "somewhat radical" (先鋭的) and new members found it hard to join, and the site had become one-way company broadcasting. The My SUBARU app connected owners only to their salesperson, not to the maker. Asobica's announcement put it the same way: "new customers found it hard to enter, and one-way communication from the company dominated" (coorum PR, Nov 2023).

The business case comes from Amuro's interviews (THE GOLD ONLINE book excerpt, Aug 2024; JBpress, Aug 2025):

  1. Acquisition through safety UGC. Subaru's safety image has slipped since about 2018. Safety is hard to feel until you crash, but Subaru receives "thank-you letters" from crash survivors almost every month, and those can't be published. The nurse's office exists to turn such stories into public UGC that prospects will read. That supports the stated goal of "a Subaru that third parties recommend" (第3者が勧めるSUBARU).
  2. Loyalty. Subaru's CDP data showed that owners who drive long distances and visit dealers often are the ones who buy again. Talking about the car is meant to raise "mind share" over a ~10-year replacement cycle (the average car age is 9.7 years).
  3. Engineers meet customers. The longer-term goal is to put "tongue-tied" engineers in direct contact with owners. That is why the CTO is principal, and it fits the "monozukuri reform" that came with the 2023 president change (coorum case).
  4. Measurement. Subaru has had a CDP since July 2016 and linked it to My SUBARU in Feb 2017. It planned to join community behaviour data to sales data and "look at correlation with LTV" (coorum PR).

Context: SUBARU says its share is about 1% globally and about 4% in Japan (GOLD ONLINE p.2). Domestic sales were 104,408 units in FY2025, down 4.1% (SUBARU, Apr 2026). So a community of ~11,500 is small next to the owner base, but it is aimed at the most vocal owners.

  • Free talk: ~160 posts/month around launch, ~125/month through 2024–25, ~230–250/month in 2026.
  • Travel: ~90/month (2024) rising to ~150/month (autumn 2026).

These four hold about 65% of all posts, so the site probably runs at roughly 1,000+ top-level posts a month, against a lifetime average of ~830. Growth is real but steady, not explosive.

Caveats. The office's March 2024 count (1,140 posts) is lower than what the board listings imply for that date, so the two counts probably define a "post" differently. Polls and likes suggest a much smaller active core: staff polls draw ~100–125 votes, and the code-of-conduct post has 392 likes. So probably a few hundred regulars out of 11,500 accounts (estimate).

What keeps people coming back

  1. A role-play identity that lowers the bar. Members are "スバ学生" (students), newcomers are "入学生" (entrants), and the office says "congratulations on your enrollment" (学長 welcome). The school code (校訓) is explicitly about onboarding: "respect your classmates", "be kind to new students: avoid jargon, actively send likes and comments", "just have fun" (校訓, Oct 2023). It is a direct answer to the "radical core fans" problem of #スバコミ.
  2. A welcome ritual. The self-introduction board (1,251 posts) reliably gets replies from veterans. In March 2024 the office reported a 74% reply rate, and the long reply chains on old intro threads show regulars still greeting new members in 2026 (学長 welcome thread).
  3. Hobby clubs, not product threads. Most activity is about life with the car: travel, photos, food, pets, car washing. That gives owners a reason to post weekly even when nothing is wrong with the car. The hobby framing also gets past the "Subaru owners are outdoorsy" stereotype that Amuro's dealer visits had already disproved (coorum case).
  4. Points, ranks and badges. Members earn 100 points for joining, 5 for a daily login, 3 per post and 1 per comment or like (capped at five a day per type), plus 3 for a best answer. Ranks run from 入学生 (entrant) through 申し子, 上級生 and 神童 (prodigy) to 生徒会幹部 (student-council officer) at 20,000 points (point page). Limited badges reward the content Subaru wants: hashtagged nurse's-office stories, dealer stories, new-car ride reports and poll answers (dealer stories; ride reports).
  5. A physical token. A "student ID" sticker was handed out at Tokyo Auto Salon 2024 and at the Ebisu HQ showroom to anyone who showed the logged-in screen (clicccar, Jan 2024; SUBARU STARS blog). Members still ask for it in Nov 2026 (FAN MEETING thread).
  6. The festival pilgrimage. スバ学祭 is held inside the ~90-year-old Gunma main plant, the "holy land" (聖地). It offers EyeSight pre-crash-brake rides, a tour of the old Nakajima Aircraft guest room, kids' mechanic sessions, an AED class, a stamp rally that requires posting with #スバ学祭, and a CTO talk show (outdoor-kaz, Oct 2024; Takano recap). Members say the site made meeting in person feel like "finally meeting" friends (free talk thread).
  7. Belonging for lonely owners. Some members say plainly that スバ学 is their main social outlet (see Member voice). That is a strong, if unglamorous, retention driver.

The content engine

  • Staff content is light by design. The principal's office holds just two posts, both from October 2023. The official notices board showed five items for 2026 (お知らせ). The office mostly sets prompts: rotating hashtag campaigns (dealer stories, MT love, the boxer engine's 60th anniversary in May 2026, BEV life), and "教えてコーナー" polls framed as "something being quietly debated inside SUBARU", such as where drivers rest their foot with EyeSight cruise control on (foot poll, Oct 2025).
  • Co-creation polls. At the 2025 festival, visitors designed Foresters with generative AI alongside Subaru's technical research staff. Members then voted for the best ten online (AI Forester poll, Dec 2025). Amuro says engineers use coorum research to test about five product ideas at a time with スバ学 members. They also ran a "tell us your Subaru debut" survey grouped by the decade people switched brands (JBpress). These are company claims; the results aren't public.
  • Member-made content does the heavy lifting. That includes travel logs, photo galleries, customizing builds and festival recaps by members such as the motoring writer and Ota city councillor Mario Takano (recap).
  • Rights to reuse. Campaign boards carry a clause granting SUBARU free use of posts, including edits, for promotion (dealer-stories terms). That is the plumbing for turning UGC into marketing. No evidence was found of a published ad or page built from スバ学 posts yet.
  • Promotion. The community is featured in the owner magazine, salespeople mention it in dealerships, and it is promoted to employees (GOLD ONLINE p.4).

People, programs and governance

  • スバ学事務局 (office staff) moderates, writes campaigns and replies to members. The office described itself as "the unpopular discipline committee" after a moderation dispute in its first month (Dec 2023). Ground rules ban politics, religion, criticism of specific countries, leaking work secrets, and soliciting money or gambling contacts (校訓).
  • The principal, CTO Tetsuo Fujinuki (still CTO and director as of June 2026, SUBARU officers), appears mostly offline, in festival talk shows in 2024 and 2025 (outdoor-kaz 2025). I found no evidence of him posting on the site after the 2023 welcome.
  • Launch ambassadors. Before opening, Subaru recruited enthusiastic "ambassadors" through the My SUBARU app and held an in-person event in Tokyo (one came from Shikoku). They seeded the site during pre-open (GOLD ONLINE p.4). No ongoing ambassador program with perks was found.
  • Vendor. Asobica provides "accompanying" support with regular strategy discussions (coorum PR). Its CCO uses SUBARU as a showcase in his book Community-Driven Management (Gentosha), and Amuro spoke at Asobica's CX+ Summit 2025 (PR TIMES).
  • Feedback into product. Engineer polls, the AI design vote and ride-feel reports are in place. One member asked Subaru to fit a factory right-foot rest after the foot poll (thread). No evidence was found of a closed-loop "you asked, we built" report back to members.

Growth loops

  • Owned channels and dealers: the SUBARU ID shared with My SUBARU, the owner magazine, dealer websites and salespeople, and the corporate X account promoting the festival (X, Sep 2025).
  • Events as acquisition: visitors at Auto Salon and the showroom got the student ID for signing up, and festival stamp rallies required a #スバ学祭 post. The 2024 festival drew a noticeable number of non-Subaru cars, so it doubled as a first EyeSight demo for outsiders (Takano).
  • Search and UGC: posts are publicly readable and indexed (Brave returns individual threads), so the safety and ride-feel reports can in principle reach prospects. No traffic data exists to show whether they do.
  • Weak point: the stated acquisition loop (prospects reading safety stories) depends on the nurse's office. It holds 311 posts in three years, about 1% of the total.

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: none published. I found no community-linked retention, LTV, conversion or NPS figure. The LTV-correlation analysis was announced in 2023 as a plan (coorum PR). Two years later Amuro's interview talks about loyalty-change analysis, not outcomes (JBpress). The only quantified wins he cites (cost per search halved) come from the CDP and advertising work, not the community.
  • Company and vendor claims: the site is "very well received" internally and executives read it (GOLD ONLINE p.4); engineers use it for rapid concept polls (JBpress).
  • Activity evidence: the membership target was beaten (11,500 vs 10,000), posting rates are rising, and the festival drew ~1,600 people in 2024. Subaru then scaled the event up to a 4,000-capacity paid FAN MEETING, which suggests management sees value.
  • Academic research: no evidence found.

What they did right

  1. Diagnosed the old community's failure and designed against it. The "kind to newcomers" code, a jargon ban and a welcome ritual tackled the cliquish core directly. The site went from zero to ≈2,750 members with a 74% reply rate in three months (事務局).
  2. A metaphor everyone understands. In Japan, school clubs, a nurse's office, student councils and school festivals are shared cultural scripts. The metaphor tells members how to behave, gives ranks a meaning (student-council officer) and makes an annual festival feel natural.
  3. Hobby-first structure. Travel, photo, food and pets boards generate most posts and give people a weekly reason to visit. Product-only boards like BEV life (10 posts) stay quiet.
  4. Seeded with ambassadors before launch. Recruiting superfans from the app for a pre-open and an in-person event meant the first public visitors found a lively room (GOLD ONLINE p.4).
  5. Offline events at a meaningful place. Holding the festival inside the historic plant, with the CTO on stage, gave online ties an in-person payoff. Members still talk about it a year later (2026 wish list).
  6. Incentives aimed at strategic content. Badges target the posts Subaru values (safety, dealer stories, ride-feel reports), and campaign terms secure reuse rights.

What they did wrong

  1. Killed #スバコミ abruptly and dropped its data. The closure came with about five weeks' notice ("突然のお知らせ", X, Aug 2023), and "member information was erased; nothing can be carried over" (outdoor-kaz, Nov 2023). Years of history and social graph were lost. Consequence: a reset to zero, though many veterans did re-enroll.
  2. The flagship purpose barely shows up. The nurse's office, the core of the safety-UGC acquisition thesis, has 311 posts against ~30,000 overall. The principal's office has been static since October 2023, so the "engineers meet customers" promise rests on occasional polls and two festival talks.
  3. Paid, first-come-first-served FAN MEETING (2026). The free school festival became a ¥2,200 ticketed event sold on a weekday at 10:00. The shop overloaded, buyers were double-charged or bought twice, and many loyal members got nothing. The members-only community had no priority or lottery (FAN MEETING thread, Nov 2026; みんカラ). Consequence: visible frustration in the community's most-read announcement thread a month before the event.
  4. Early moderation friction. In December 2023 a member was told their health post "deviated" from the site's purpose and complained the site had "made an ambiguous start". The office apologised for the "power word" (thread). A 2024 driving-etiquette dispute ended with a member quitting in anger (thread). Both were handled, but they show the tension between "brand site" and "friends' café".
  5. Small operational misses. The student ID promised as a festival handout is "still delayed" by members' account. A planned SUBARU 感謝祭 (thanksgiving festival) was announced in Feb 2024 and cancelled ten days later "for various reasons" (告知). An October 2026 security notice restricting access to Japanese connections (worded as "Japan VPN only") confused members (notice).
  6. Regional reach. Every flagship event is in Gunma (except the 2024 Osaka meetup), and members outside Kanto ask for a touring festival (wish list).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Pick a metaphor with built-in social rules. "School" told members how to greet newcomers, what ranks mean and why there's a yearly festival, without a rulebook.
  2. Make newcomer welcome a norm, and measure it. A written "be kind to new students" code plus an intro board pushed the reply rate to ~74% early on.
  3. Organise around members' lives, not your features. Hobby clubs produce most of the posting; product boards are quiet.
  4. Seed with recruited superfans before the doors open, and give them an in-person kickoff.
  5. Give online ties an annual in-person payoff at a place that means something, with a senior executive present.

Avoid these

  1. Don't shut down a long-running community without migrating people and history. You lose the social graph you're trying to rebuild.
  2. Don't let your strategic content depend on organic posting. If the business case needs testimonials, build a dedicated pipeline (outreach to letter writers, interviews, featured stories) and track it.
  3. Don't monetise or ration your community's flagship event without member priority. A members' lottery or presale would have protected the people the community exists for.

Score justifications

  • Retention mechanics 4: a full kit of identity, welcome ritual, hobby clubs, points and ranks, targeted badges, a physical ID and an annual pilgrimage. There's no streak, push or in-app hook beyond the shared SUBARU ID.
  • Content engine 3: clever prompts, polls and campaigns, but very little first-party content (the principal posted twice) and no visible editorial pipeline for the safety stories.
  • Member ownership 3: members produce nearly all content and the festival recaps, and organise meetups in a members-only board. Structure, campaigns and events stay firmly with the office.
  • Staff investment 3: a dedicated office, vendor support, SUBARU STARS staff and the CTO at festivals and plant events. Little visible day-to-day engineer participation online.
  • Business impact evidence 1: goals and planned LTV analysis are documented; no outcome numbers have been published.
  • Durability 3: three years old and growing, with executive sponsorship. But Subaru has already killed one owner community, and this one depends on a single SaaS vendor and one marketing champion.
