TL;DR

  • What it is: OPPO's China community has three layers. The first is an owned forum that ran from 2008 or earlier (bbs.oppo.com, later bbs.myoppo.com) and reached 9.1M members and 36M posts by Sept 2014, before it was closed in Mar 2016 and folded into the OPPO社区 app (briefly called OPPO+). The second is offline fan clubs: O粉汇 (2013–15, 70–75 cities), relaunched in May 2024 as O粉会, with "fan-led", officially certified city presidents (30 cities) and campus leads (62 campuses). The third, since Jan 2020, is ColorOS 共创者星球, a beta and co-creation program. It claimed 200K participants and 3K core volunteers in 2021, and more than 120 offline sessions with 20K+ adopted suggestions for ColorOS 15.
  • Why people come back: mostly material reasons. Daily check-in points, purchase-linked membership tiers that unlock real service perks (free screen protectors, discounted repairs, airport lounges), a points mall, and referral points. Status and access come on top: beta slots, honour titles such as "ColorOS 守护者", creator verification, trips to launches, and meeting executives in person.
  • How OPPO feeds it: with money and staff. It held hundreds of offline events a year in 2014, plus annual galas, the 88 O粉节 "fan festival" (from 2020), a 500+ member creator program (达人计划), engineers who sometimes visit beta testers at home, and public reports on which co-creation suggestions were adopted.
  • Biggest mistake: loyalty built on points is rented. OPPO cut check-in rewards to roughly a half or a quarter in Sept 2021 and devalued the points mall again in 2023, and members said so in public. The fan clubs themselves were allowed to fade after 2015 and had to be "restarted" (重启) by executives in 2024.
  • The lesson: when members can see feedback land in the product (feature lists, beta cohorts, engineers on call), their loyalty outlasts a perk cut. When they come for perks alone, they leave once the perks are cut.

What the community is

The China community is a different animal from OPPO's global forum, which is covered in oppo-community. It is older, much larger, wired into OPPO's account and points system, and it has an offline layer that OPPO certifies but hands to volunteers.

Origin and strategy

The forum (2008–2016) was a support and enthusiast hub that OPPO ran as part of its brand. The Dec 2008 snapshot already shows the machinery OPPO still uses: a staffed after-sales board, a moderator hierarchy with its own meeting room and complaints process, and a virtual currency with a shop (Wayback, 2008). When OPPO moved into smartphones (Find 5, 2012) and launched ColorOS (2013), the forum became where ROM builds were released each week. It also became the hub for O粉汇, OPPO's large offline fan program, which ran alongside OPPO's youth- and celebrity-driven marketing.

O粉汇 (2013–15) was event marketing at national scale. In 2014 OPPO ran a "7遇时光" event that took 50 fans to the Find 7 launch "with VIP treatment", a 70-city CityWalk on May 10, 2014 (驱动中国, snippet), a 75-city "梦想之旅，至美一拍" series (锋潮科技) and a fan-only micro-film whose signup ran through the forum (数英). It closed in Jan 2015 with an O粉汇年度盛典 (annual gala) in Shenzhen. About 300 fans attended, a vice-president sang with them on stage, and prizes went to fan archetypes such as "最牛斑竹" (best moderator) and "真爱粉" (true fan) (Leiphone via Aliyun, Jan 14 2015). The article says the program "着重聚合全国O粉" (focused on rallying fans nationwide) to strengthen closeness to the brand.

The move to an app (2016) tied the community to the account and loyalty system. After the forum closed, the community lived inside an app that also handles membership: you claim purchase points "in the OPPO社区 home-page card", check in there, and redeem points there (积分成长值说明; 会员权益与规则). For most users the community became the front end of a loyalty program.

ColorOS 共创者星球 (Jan 2020) turned feedback into a structured program. It grew out of informal user participation dating to about 2015 (ifanr, Dec 2021). It has work groups and annual titles, and it reports its own throughput (see below). Its purpose is plain: pre-release QA and prioritisation for an OS that, by OPPO's account, gained 200M active users in two years.

O粉会 (2024) is a deliberate restart. A Tencent News piece is headlined "OPPO重启O粉活动" ("OPPO restarts fan activities"). It describes 50+ fans invited to an O粉夏夜漫谈 (summer-night fan chat) in Hangzhou after the Reno12 launch on May 23 2024, with an OPPO executive (周意保) promising to keep using "热爱、连接、开放的O粉会" (an O粉会 built on passion, connection and openness) as the platform (Tencent News, May 24 2024). The Wayback Machine's first capture of the O粉会 page is Aug 4 2024 (CDX). Internal champion: Li Jie (李杰) headed OPPO China user operations from Sept 2021 until he became OnePlus China president in Jan 2022 (Baidu Baike; China News, Jan 2022). No named owner of the 2024 relaunch was found beyond the executive above.

Reading the numbers: the forum peaked around 2014. Posting doubled roughly every year from 2012 to 2014, and the forum outgrew any Western brand forum of its time. The 110.8M followers of an official account and the "2亿O粉" (200M fans) copy count accounts, not community members: following is probably automatic for anyone who signs in, which is my inference. The 30M MAU claim fits an app that is preinstalled and pays points for daily check-ins, so it measures loyalty-app usage, not conversation. Since 2016 no thread or post counts have been published, so the community's real conversational size is unknown. Tieba's oppo吧 grew only about 4% in posts from 2018 to c.2025, so independent forum-style talk has flattened too.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Points that are worth real money, earned daily. You earn points from purchases, repairs (¥1 = 1 point), check-ins (5–10 points), and OPPO社区, app-store and game-centre tasks (5–20 points). You spend them on gifts, raffles and in-store services, and points expire after two years. On the 16th of every month there is a 会员日 (member day) (rules). In 2021 bargain hunters described checking in across 12 OPPO apps for about 120 points a day and a guaranteed 10,000 points a month (smzdm/Zhihu, Aug 2021). The habit is real, but it is tied to the points, not to other members.
  2. Tiers tied to ownership and service. Growth value comes from the phone you buy (1,500–5,000 depending on model), referrals and check-ins. Gold and Diamond tiers unlock up to 8 free screen protectors, a 20% repair discount, free home repair visits, two free cracked-screen repairs, new-phone trial rights, official events and two airport-lounge visits. Gold and Diamond expire after a year and lose 2,000–3,000 growth points (积分成长值说明; 2019 upgrade notice). This creates a switching cost: you lose your tier if you switch brands.
  3. Access and recognition for the enthusiast core. Beta recruitment is announced in the community (ColorOS 14 recruitment, Sept 2023), and applicants are screened on good conduct "在ColorOS社区及官方公测QQ群" (in the ColorOS community and official beta QQ groups) (2020 form). 共创者星球 sorts its 3K core members into work groups: 星际先锋组 (early hardware), 内测组 (beta), 版务组 (moderation), 创作社 (content) and 解答组 (answers). It awards yearly titles, "ColorOS 先行者" (pioneer) and "ColorOS 守护者" (guardian) (ifanr). Creators who are verified as 达人 get early phones, priority for launches and music festivals, a "growth fund" and promotion on the OPPO社区 banner, splash screen and push notifications (达人计划).
  4. Meeting people in person. In 2014 there were 100+ events across 70+ cities. Since 2024, city clubs and campus leads have run local events such as campus football tournaments, MSI esports watch parties, launch-event trips and co-creation camps (oppo.com/cn/oppo-fans). The annual galas hand out fan-archetype awards.
  5. Access to staff. Fans have dinner with executives (2024), and engineers sit in beta groups. The best-known case: a 19-year-old tester's battery-drain report led OPPO to "直接派工程师上门抓取日志" (send an engineer to his home to pull the logs). He then helped run 18 beta groups for 10,000+ users and passed on thousands of logs (ifanr, Dec 2021). For years the parasocial hub was VP Shen Yiren's (沈义人) Weibo, where he teased products daily and talked with fans until he stepped down in Apr 2020 (Baidu TA说, 2019; Tencent, Apr 2020). This happened on Weibo, not in OPPO社区.

Score: retention_mechanics 4/5. The habit and status loops are deep and well wired. The economics behind them are fragile, and much of the loyalty rests on perks rather than on peers.

The content engine

  • Official content: in the forum era, weekly ColorOS builds were released first in OPPO社区, with tutorials ("实用教程") and O粉活动 on the home page (Wayback 2017–21). The community team now runs "OPPO营业中" livestreams with raffles, for example during ODC22 (oppo.cn thread, snippet), and a playful "谁是真的O社长" (who is the real O-chief) stream in which the community team posed as the forum's mascot leader (oppo.cn thread, 2021, snippet).
  • Creator program: 达人计划 recruits photography, tech, art, fashion and gaming creators. To be verified they must post ≥5 times in OPPO社区 with 3 posts featured officially, then produce ≥3 pieces a month (one on a set theme), with the OPPO camera watermark on, and sync them to OPPO社区. "500多位达人" (500+ creators) had joined by the time the page was archived (达人计划). Brand-level co-creation campaigns run on Xiaohongshu (ROI Festival award, 2025).
  • Closing the loop: OPPO publishes which co-creation suggestions it adopted. In Apr 2026 it listed ten high-vote 小布 (Breeno assistant) requests, such as bank-app auto-bookkeeping and syncing with OPPO Health, and said "部分反馈已有初步结论" (some feedback already has preliminary conclusions) (IT之家). ColorOS 15 shipped a separate notification centre and control centre, which users had asked for loudly (IT之家, Oct 2024).
  • Fan festival: the "88 O粉节" (Aug 8) began in 2020. Mostly a sales event (a 闪充 trade-in for ¥8.8, coupons), it was framed as "专属于OPPO粉丝的节日" (a festival just for OPPO fans) (Sina, Aug 2020; China Daily, 2021). By 2023 it had become a service promotion: free cases, repair vouchers (IT之家 via ashea, Aug 2023).

Score: content_engine 3/5. It has strong feedback-loop content and a big creator funnel, but I found little evidence of a steady, member-facing editorial calendar in the app today, and much of the "content" is promotion.

People, programs and governance

  • Moderation and volunteers: since 2008 the forum has had a moderator corps (斑竹/版主) with a public office for recruitment, appointments and complaints (2008). Today a "O粉-大版主" (head moderator) account still replies in threads (oppo.cn, 2021, snippet). In 共创者星球, the 版务组 (moderation) and 解答组 (answers) groups do moderation and peer support, and the best of them get the 守护者 (guardian) title.
  • City presidents and campus leads (O粉会): volunteers apply by form and are "入选" (selected). In return they get honours, perks and project experience; the obligations are not published. The relaunch page also says events are "官方组织" (organised by OPPO), so "fan-led" really means fan-fronted with OPPO staff behind them (oppo.com/cn).
  • Staffing: no headcount was found. OPPO had a China "用户运营" (user operations) head (Li Jie, 2021), community-team livestreams, ColorOS product managers at camps and galas, and executives at fan dinners.
  • Feedback flow: in-app "提交产品建议" (submit a product suggestion) in the ColorOS circle (added 2023, APKPure changelog), beta QQ groups, "攻城狮面对面" (engineers face-to-face) chat groups, numbered offline camps, and published adoption lists.

Score: member_ownership 3/5. Volunteers hold real titles and run groups, but OPPO picks the leaders, runs the events and decides which ideas count. staff_investment 4/5: visible executives, engineers, galas, and 100+ events a year at the 2014 peak.

Growth loops

  1. Purchase → app → referral. Buyers claim points in OPPO社区 within three months of purchase. If they enter a referrer's phone number, the referrer gets half the buyer's points plus 1,000 growth points, for up to 8 referrals a year (积分成长值说明). The perks even pulled in people who don't own OPPO phones: an Honor user wrote that he farmed OPPO points from a rival phone and would "probably buy OPPO next" (smzdm, Aug 31 2021).
  2. Creators push content outward. 达人 post first on Weibo, Xiaohongshu, Douyin, Bilibili and Zhihu, with the OPPO watermark, and OPPO promotes them back through the app (达人计划).
  3. Campus and city clubs. 62 campus leads and 30 city clubs bring OPPO into student life (football, esports). This matters because OPPO's target buyer is young.
  4. Beta waiting lists. Each ColorOS beta call (6万+ testers for ColorOS 15, Sina, snippet) sends enthusiasts into the community.
  5. Absorbing sister brands. On Apr 1 2026 realme's China community was shut and moved into "realme圈子" inside the OPPO商城 (OPPO store) app. Posts, medals and follower graphs stay exportable for a year, until Apr 25 2027 (IT之家, Mar 2026). That consolidates communities under OPPO's commerce stack.

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: none on retention or revenue. OPPO is private: there are no SEC filings, and EDGAR does not apply. I found no repurchase, churn or LTV figures tied to the community or loyalty program.
  • Company-reported product impact (the strongest evidence): 120+ offline sessions, 40K+ pieces of feedback and 20K+ adopted suggestions over the ColorOS 15 cycle (IT之家, Oct 2024); 60K+ beta users, 9 camps and 20K+ pieces of feedback for ColorOS 15 (Sina, snippet); 100K+ pieces of feedback a year and 100K+ public-beta users of ColorOS 12 by Oct 2021 (ZOL; ifanr). These are self-reported, and "adopted" is not defined.
  • Company claims on reach: 100M+ users and 30M+ MAU (c.2019), "2亿O粉" (2025 store copy). These are reach claims for a preinstalled loyalty app, not evidence of engagement.
  • Indirect: OPPO cut points twice (2021, 2023), which suggests the program cost more than it returned. That is my inference.

Score: business_impact_evidence 2/5 (credible, specific feedback-throughput numbers, nothing on money).

What they did right

  1. Built it into ownership. Every buyer is sent to OPPO社区 to claim points and tier status, and the tier unlocks after-sales benefits people use (screen protectors, repairs, home visits). The community gets a reason to open it that has nothing to do with posting (rules).
  2. A structured co-creation program with visible follow-through. Work groups, annual honours, numbered themed camps in many cities, and published "adopted" lists (ifanr; IT之家 2026). Members can see their requests ship (the separate notification centre in ColorOS 15).
  3. Treated superusers like colleagues. Engineers made home visits and active testers were promoted to beta-group admins. One became a co-manager of 18 groups and went on to study software engineering (ifanr).
  4. Offline at massive scale with real ritual. 70–75-city programs, a fan micro-film, and an annual gala with awards named after fan types and an executive singing with fans (Aliyun/Leiphone 2015).
  5. Grew the roster after the relaunch, from 27 to 30 city clubs and 47 to 62 campuses (2024 → 2026), and made the local leaders public by name (Wayback 2024; 2026).
  6. Gave migrating users a data off-ramp. When realme's community was folded in, members could keep and export their posts, medals and follower relationships for a year (IT之家). That is better than OPPO did for OnePlus users overseas.

What they did wrong

  1. Devalued the currency. In Sept 2021 OPPO replaced streak check-ins (10 points a day after 7 days) with a 1-3-5 cycle across most of its apps: "签到积分直接打了对折" (check-in points were cut in half outright) (smzdm, Sept 25 2021). By about 2022 a user estimated earnings were down to about a quarter, regional raffles had ended and the points mall had gone stale (Zhihu). In 2023 rewards moved to points plus cash (smzdm, Mar 2023). Consequence: members said they would stop buying OPPO, or that their points had become worthless (see Member voice).
  2. Let the offline network lapse, then had to restart it. O粉汇 peaked in 2014–15. By 2024 OPPO itself called the Hangzhou dinner a "重启" (restart) (Tencent, May 2024), and the city network went from 70–75 cities to 27–30. Clubs rebuilt from scratch lose their old leaders and traditions.
  3. Replaced the forum with a loyalty app, and the open web record with nothing. Closing bbs.myoppo.com (2016) ended a forum that was posting 75K times a day. Today oppo.cn is only a download page and old threads mostly fail to load or say "作者设置，您无权查看" (the author has restricted this) (oppo.cn). There is no searchable public archive and little SEO, and OPPO stopped publishing activity metrics after 2014.
  4. Inflated, static vanity numbers. The "在线人数 300万" (3M online) banner stayed the same from 2017 to 2021 (Wayback; 2021). "2亿O粉" counts accounts. Neither tells members or the company whether the community is healthy.
  5. Silence and opaque gatekeeping on high-stakes questions. Members complained that staff stopped engaging ("社区的人怎么一直闭口不谈", why does everyone at the community keep quiet, 2021), and that beta recruitment for their model was quietly cut (2022; see Member voice).
  6. Raffle trust problems in the commerce layer. In the OPPO商城 618 "red-envelope rain" (May–Jun 2026), winners of several prizes were told they would get only one, and the rest were voided as "违规" (rule violations) with no explanation (DoNews, Jul 2026). Now that communities are being merged into the store app, a store promotion that goes wrong damages trust in the community as well.

Score: durability 3/5. The community has been continuous since 2008 or earlier, and 共创者星球 has been steady since 2020. But the forum died in 2016, the fan clubs lapsed and had to be restarted, and the points economy has been cut twice.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Route every new customer through the community for something they want (warranty, perks, points on purchase), and attach service benefits to tiers so the account is worth keeping.
  2. Turn feedback into a program with named roles and annual honours. Use beta, moderation, answers and creator tracks with titles like "守护者" (guardian), and publish what you adopted, cycle by cycle.
  3. Put engineers within reach of power users. A single home visit turned a tester into the manager of 18 beta groups. Promote your most active testers to admins.
  4. Use numbered, themed offline camps ("2025 第 4 期 … 西安站 影像专场", the 4th camp of 2025, Xi'an, imaging session) so in-person co-creation becomes a predictable series, not a one-off.
  5. Name local leaders publicly and offer recognition plus experience (city presidents, campus leads) to reach young users where they already gather.

Avoid these

  1. Don't build retention on a currency you will later devalue. Points cuts read as broken promises, and points-driven members leave with the points.
  2. Don't let the offline network lapse. Restarting from zero costs more than keeping 30 city leaders supported every year.
  3. Don't swap a public, searchable forum for a closed app without keeping the archive and real metrics. You lose SEO and history, and you end up showing vanity numbers instead of health indicators.
