TL;DR

  • What it is: a free, application-only Slack workspace for HR and People Ops professionals that the Berlin HR-software company Leapsome launched in Q3 2020, under the same "People Over Perks" (POPs) brand as its newsletter. Since 2025 it has been rebranded as an umbrella "Leapsome Community" (Slack + events + newsletter + partner network + knowledge hub) and claims 10,000+ members ([1], [2]).
  • Why people come back: peer answers to hard HR questions ("a people team for the people team"), a curated member base, topic and city channels, and a promise that joining won't land you on a sales list ([2], [3]). There is no visible gamification or member-status system.
  • What the company feeds it: a newsletter every second and fourth Friday since 2020, a podcast whose guests then did AMAs in the Slack (2021–2023), webinars, a first virtual conference in October 2025, IRL "get-togethers" in Berlin and New York, a DACH "People Leaders to Watch" list in 2026, and paid or partnered HR influencers who recruit members ([4], [5], [6], [7], [8]).
  • Biggest mistake: letting the content that gave the Slack its rituals lapse. The podcast (the source of the AMA guests) stopped after 11 episodes in January 2023, and member growth slowed from about 2,000 a half-year (2021–22) to a plateau at "8,000+" from December 2024 to October 2025 ([9], [10]). Meanwhile the community page runs Leapsome product demos beside "sales-free" claims.
  • The one lesson: a vendor can build a durable B2B audience community cheaply by naming it after a belief rather than the product, screening members and keeping sales out, but it will only hear "it's growing" unless it measures and publishes engagement and keeps a steady drumbeat of rituals.

What the community is

People Over Perks is Leapsome's community for HR and People Operations professionals. The core is a private Slack workspace; around it sit a newsletter, events and a resource library. Leapsome (founded in Berlin in 2016 by Jenny von Podewils and Kajetan von Armansperg; ~2,000 customers; one $60M Series A from Insight Partners in March 2022) sells performance, engagement and HRIS software to the same HR leaders the community serves ([11]).

Official spaces, as of November 2026:

  • POPs Slack — application form on leapsome.com/community, "Free forever." Channels listed on the page: #compensation-and-benefits, #learning-and-development, #candidate-profiles, #ama-sessions, #events, #diversity-equity-inclusion, #performance-management, #hiring-advice, #dear-pops, #general, #employee-engagement, #tools-and-resources, #hr-jobs-listings, #introduce-yourself, #random, "+ many location-based channels" (January 2026 snapshot, [10]).
  • People Over Perks newsletter — twice a month since October 2020; by November 2026 the landing page describes a monthly column "from inside the work" written by Jessica Zwaan, Leapsome's VP People Strategy & Operations ([4], [12]).
  • Events — webinars, the People Over Perks Live virtual conference (16 Oct 2025), and IRL meetups ([6], [13], [14]).
  • Partner network and Knowledge Hub — templates and outside HR consultants, folded into the "Leapsome Community" umbrella page ([1]).
  • Podcast — 11 episodes, January 2021 to January 2023, hosted by Leapsome's Head of Marketing Andy Parker; dormant since ([5], [15]).

Unofficial spaces: no fan-run space. In r/humanresources, People Over Perks is recommended alongside rival vendor Slacks: "Slack groups: Leapsome has People over perks. Lattice has Resources for Humans. Culture Amp has People Geeks. All are wonderful." ([16]). Leapsome is not visible taking part in Reddit (no evidence found; the Arctic Shift archive mostly timed out for keyword searches).

Origin and strategy

  • Launch: the October 2020 Wayback snapshot of leapsome.com/en/slack-community says the community was "preparing for launch toward the end of Q3 2020" and was taking applications ([9]). The newsletter launched alongside it: "For a full run-down of why we've launched the People Over Perks newsletter and Slack community, see here" ([4]).
  • Champion: Andy Parker, Leapsome's employee #3 and head of marketing, who built marketing "from 2 to 16 marketers" with a "$9M annual budget" and says he "Founded People over Perks, Leapsome's global community for HR leaders" (10,000+ members) ([17]). The newsletter was curated by Isabela (Alzuguir-Woidtke) of the marketing team ([4], [18]).
  • Why it was built: Leapsome's stated reason is about the brand: "we believe that a company should invest in its people above all… With People Over Perks, we want to provide a space for HR and People Operations professionals to discover valuable resources and exchange best practices" ([9]). The commercial logic is plain from the context. Leapsome was bootstrapped until 2022 and marketing generated "~98% of pipeline" ([17]), and HR buyers buy rarely, so a free peer space keeps the brand in front of them between buying cycles. The community explicitly is not support: "this community is not for technical support requests relating to the Leapsome platform" ([9]).
  • Internal priority: at the spring 2022 company offsite, one of the company-wide hackathon topics was "Becoming the largest, most useful, and most engaged community for HR professionals (with our People Over Perks Slack community)" ([19]). That ambition puts it in direct competition with Lattice's Resources for Humans (23,000+) ([20]).
  • 2025–2026 repositioning: the page moved from "/slack-community" to "/community" (301 redirect in March 2025, [21]). In January 2026 it read "The HR community you'll actually use… 9,000+ people-first leaders" ([10]). By November 2026 it is "The Leapsome Community — For HR leaders who want more," a multi-touchpoint "HR support system" ([1]). In the same period Leapsome's About page says "we build People over Perks, a community that brings HR leaders together across multiple touchpoints" ([22]).

Trajectory: fast early growth (0 → 4,000 in about 20 months, helped by the pandemic and remote HR work), then a steady ~1,000–1,500 a year from 2023, with a plateau in 2025 and a pickup in 2026 when influencer partnerships, the conference and IRL meetups started. What keeps people coming back

The evidence is thin: there are no published engagement metrics, and the Slack is private. What can be seen:

  1. Peer Q&A utility. The pitch since 2020 is "Get answers in minutes from people who've been there," with topic channels for the recurring questions of HR work (comp, performance, hiring, DEI, L&D) ([1], [9]). Member testimonials on the page stress emotional support as much as answers: "here, you have a people team for the people team" ([10]).
  2. Curation as status. It has always been "manually curated": you apply and the moderation team admits you, with a "Did someone refer you?" field on the form from day one ([9]). Membership is open to "specialists, managers, executives, and consultants" in people roles ([10]).
  3. The sales-free promise. Steve Browne (a well-known US HR voice who partners with the community) writes: "It's completely sales-free. Leapsome hosts the community but never uses it to promote their platform, and joining won't land you on a sales call list" ([3]). This is the main trust device for a vendor-run space, though see "What they did wrong."
  4. AMA ritual tied to the podcast (2021–2023). "We'll first interview the guests on the People Over Perks podcast so that you can get to know them, and will then meet at the #ask-me-anything channel to answer your specific questions." Members also got episodes first ([5], [9]). The channel survives as #ama-sessions, but the podcast that supplied guests stopped in January 2023 ([15]).
  5. Seasonal prompts. An example: on Halloween 2023 Leapsome posted that "Your HR peers are sharing their HR Horror Stories over in our People Over Perks Slack community today, complete with candidate ghosting, terminations gone wrong" ([26]). No evidence was found of a regular weekly cadence comparable to Resources for Humans' weekly prompts.
  6. Location channels and IRL meetups. "Many location-based channels" ([10]); in 2025–2026 there were Berlin "get-togethers," a New York event on 15 July 2026 announced by Jessica Zwaan, and a wellbeing session with letters to future selves and a guided meditation ("Full house, full hearts") ([7], [14], [27]).
  7. Staff and expert presence. Jessica Zwaan, a recognisable HR author and LinkedIn voice, tells podcast audiences: "I strongly suggest everybody joins the Leapsome People Over Perks community. I'm present there, it's on Slack" ([7]). Members praise "warm yet punctual moderation" ([10]).
  8. Recognition (new in 2026). "People Leaders to Watch 2026": 25 HR leaders in Germany, Austria and Switzerland, chosen by a panel (Selma Sadiković, Jessica Zwaan, the two founders and Manjuri Sinha), published 27 October 2026 with a book and "a graphic that's yours to post" ([8]). Honorees posted about it widely on LinkedIn, e.g. "Being awarded one of People Leaders to Watch 2026 by Leapsome and People Over Perks means a lot to me" ([28]).
  9. Career utility. #hr-jobs-listings and #candidate-profiles make it a job market for HR people ([10]).

Not present (no evidence found): badges, levels, streaks, leaderboards, a member ambassador/MVP program, member-run chapters, or any link between the community and the Leapsome product. Switching costs are low: a member's history and network sit in a Slack workspace that rival vendor Slacks copy almost exactly.

The team size is not disclosed. The community sits inside marketing, not customer success.

Growth loops

  1. Content → Slack → newsletter cross-promotion. Every podcast description, the LinkedIn company page and blog posts carry "Join the People Over Perks Slack community" calls to action ([5], [31]). Blog posts on unrelated SEO topics (e.g. "Employee development program," "23 HR influencers to follow") end with a POPs plug ([32]).
  2. Third-party listicles. Being listed in "best HR Slack communities" roundups (Worknice, RecruitCRM, Joveo, HackerEarth, AttendanceBot, Medium) gives a steady stream of applicants at no cost ([23], [24], [25], [33]).
  3. Referral field and peer recommendations (form field since 2020; Reddit recommendations) ([9], [16]).
  4. Influencers and speakers. Browne's blog, Zwaan's podcast appearances and conference speakers each bring their own audience ([3], [7]).
  5. Recognition loop (2026). The People Leaders to Watch nomination drive and honorees' own LinkedIn posts spread the POPs name across the DACH HR network ([8], [28]).
  6. IRL events in Leapsome's two hubs (Berlin, New York), promoted on staff LinkedIn with tracked join links ([14], [27]).

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: none found. Leapsome is private, files nothing with the SEC, and has not published figures on community-sourced pipeline, customer overlap, churn or NPS for members.
  • Company and insider claims: Andy Parker credits marketing (which includes POPs) with "~98% of pipeline" while Leapsome grew "from 70 to 2,000+ customers" and "into the tens of millions in ARR" ([17]). The community's share is not broken out. Third-party estimates put ARR around $30M in 2024 (Latka, an estimate) ([34]).
  • Revealed preference: Leapsome has kept investing for six years. It made the community a company-wide offsite topic in 2022, rebranded it as the umbrella "Leapsome Community" in 2025–26, launched a conference, IRL meetups and an award in 2025–26, and put its VP of People Strategy on the newsletter ([1], [8], [12], [19]). That suggests leadership sees value, but that is inference, not evidence.

What they did right

  1. Named it after a belief, not the product. "People Over Perks" is an HR slogan any practitioner can sign up to, and it doubles as Leapsome's positioning. It lets the brand sit on the newsletter, podcast, conference and award without sounding like a vendor ([9]).
  2. Screened membership and banned support. Manual review keeps vendors and spammers out. Another Redditor warned off rival Slacks "filled with vendors spamming every channel and no moderation" ([16]), and POPs members praise its moderation ([10]).
  3. A clear "no sales" promise, voiced by outside HR figures (Browne) instead of Leapsome itself ([3]).
  4. Bundled the brand across formats (newsletter + Slack + podcast + AMAs) from the start. The podcast launch post says the "great engagement we've had with the POPs newsletter and Slack community from the very start" inspired it ([35]).
  5. Six years of continuity with a single name and steady growth to 10,000+ without paid membership ([1]).
  6. Moved offline and into recognition in 2025–26 (conference with SHRM credits, Berlin and NYC meetups, People Leaders to Watch), which gives members status and a reason to post about the brand ([6], [8], [14]).
  7. Localisation: location channels, German-language webinars and a DACH-specific award match Leapsome's European base; a 2022 observer called it "more EU-focused and… a great space to keep up with what is happening in the HR community in Europe" ([1], [36]).

What they did wrong

  1. The podcast-to-AMA ritual died quietly. The best-designed hook (interview a leader, release early to members, host an AMA in Slack) stopped after 11 episodes. The cadence went from monthly in early 2021 to one episode in 2022 and a last one in January 2023 ([15]). The community page kept advertising "exclusive AMA sessions with top People Ops leaders" through 2025 ([10]). Consequence: growth slowed to roughly 1,000 a year and the headline count sat at "8,000+" for about ten months of 2025 (Wayback series).
  2. Mixed messages on selling. Browne's "never uses it to promote their platform" sits beside a community page whose "What's happening" feed leads with "Leapsome Recruiting is live" and "What's new at Leapsome" product demos, and a conference that included a customer case study ([1], [3], [6]). Consequence: no member backlash was found, but this is exactly the tension that erodes trust in vendor-run spaces.
  3. No member ownership. There is no ambassador/MVP programme, no member-led chapters or member-written content programme, and no rewards for top helpers. Almost every visible activity is staff-run. Consequence: the community depends on marketing staff, and its founder has now left Leapsome ([17]).
  4. Nothing measured or published but the headline count. There are no activity metrics, and the count is cumulative joins. Lattice's Resources for Humans at least shows conference sizes and process ([20]). Consequence: outsiders, and possibly Leapsome's own leadership, can't tell a 10,000-member community from a 10,000-account graveyard.
  5. Company instability spills into a "people-first" brand. Glassdoor reviews (undated in snippets, roughly 2023–2024) describe "mass layoffs (50% of the NY office)" and "Multiple layoff rounds" at a company whose community is literally named "People Over Perks" ([37]). There is no evidence the community team was cut, but the brand is exposed to that irony.
  6. Shifting format promises. The newsletter has been "twice a month," "every week" and "monthly" on different versions of the same page ([4], [12]).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Brand the community after the belief your product stands for, then reuse that one name across newsletter, podcast, events and awards so each feeds the others.
  2. Make it application-only, ban product support and sales pitches in writing, and have credible outsiders vouch for the "no selling" promise.
  3. Chain your content into rituals: publish an interview, give members early access, then host the guest live in the community. Keep the chain running on a fixed schedule.
  4. Give members status that they will share: an annual list of peers to watch, judged by a credible panel, with a ready-made graphic, drives both recognition inside and reach outside.
  5. Add city channels and small IRL meetups in the cities where your staff already live; they turn lurkers into regulars cheaply.

Avoid these

  1. Don't let the flagship ritual lapse while the landing page keeps promising it; members notice before you do, and growth stalls.
  2. Don't run product demos in the same feed where you promise "sales-free." Keep commercial events on a separate surface.
  3. Don't make the community a one-person marketing project without member leaders or engagement metrics. When the champion leaves, there is nothing (and no data) to sustain the case.

Score notes: retention mechanics 2 (peer utility and curation but no visible weekly rituals, status systems or product hooks); content engine 3 (consistent newsletter, decent webinars and a 2025 conference, but the podcast died); member ownership 2 (staff-run; no ambassadors or member content programme); staff investment 3 (marketing-owned, an executive-level voice and growing events, team size unknown); business impact evidence 1 (no published numbers specific to the community); durability 4 (six years, same name, still growing, re-invested in 2025–26).
