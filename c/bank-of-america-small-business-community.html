TL;DR

  • What it was: a free, public peer forum and member network for small-business owners. Bank of America launched it in Oct 2007 on Jive Software, open to anyone and without ads. It had Forums, Experts, Stories, Members and Events tabs, and it ran until Dec 2020, when the bank turned it into a content-only "Small Business Resources" site (Inc., Oct 17 2007; SBC Team, Oct 26 2020).
  • Why people came back: fast, free answers to practical questions (loans, merchant services, starting a business), plus a small core of volunteer answerers. They were ranked on a points leaderboard and answered for years: "LUCKIEST" had 26,617 points by Dec 2008 and was still posting in 2016. For many vendors, the forum was also a place to be seen by prospects.
  • What BofA put in: a mid-size launch push (banner ads on Yahoo!, Tribal Fusion, Advertising.com and LinkedIn Answers in 2007–08, and a pitch on the online-banking logout page in 2009), weekly live expert Q&As in 2008, a moderator team, and a steady flow of expert articles (Steve Strauss, Rieva Lesonsky, Mari Smith, the agency byline "Touchpoint"). Later came Google Hangout panels and a podcast.
  • Biggest mistake: after about 2009 the bank fed the content side and let the peer side wither. New threads fell from about 12,000 a year (2008–09) to about 600 a year (2018–19). Much of what remained was vendor promotion and spam, and the site stayed on Jive 8.0.2 from 2015 to 2020. Complaints about the bank itself were auto-moderated, so the one topic members most needed help with (BofA products, PPP in 2020) was off limits.
  • The one lesson: an open network with no product tie keeps a long tail of SEO traffic, but nobody comes back for it. Without a reason tied to the product, steady staff hosting and new rituals, the peer layer turns into a help desk for strangers and a billboard for vendors, and the company finally keeps only the articles.

What the community is

The Small Business Online Community (SBOC; renamed "Small Business Community", SBC, around 2014–15, and "Business Advantage Small Business Community" by 2020) lived at smallbusinessonlinecommunity.bankofamerica.com, with vanity URLs bankofamerica.com/sbcommunity and later /sbc. It ran on Jive Software from the start: the footer read "© Jive Software", and the URLs used Jive's index.jspa and /thread/<id> patterns (Wayback, Dec 2008). At launch the navigation was Home, Forums, Experts, Stories, Members, and Events was added in 2008. Member profiles were built like LinkedIn and showed location, website and annual revenue (Mashable, Oct 10 2007).

Anyone could read it or join, Bank of America customer or not. PBN noted that "there are no advertisements, only a small logo in a top corner of the site that links to another bank Web site pitching products" (Providence Business News, Nov 17 2007).

Status today: defunct. The bank announced on Oct 26 2020 that the community would "become Small Business Resources" in early December and that "the forums will be retired when the new site launches" (SBC Team). A 2021 press release confirmed the Small Business Resources site "launched in December 2020" as an "educational hub" (PR Newswire, Oct 21 2021). On Nov 8 2026 the old hostname returned NXDOMAIN from Google Public DNS, and bankofamerica.com/sbc redirected through /smallbusiness/resources/ to business.bankofamerica.com/en/resources, the "Center for Business Empowerment", which has no member features (checked by curl, Nov 8 2026).

Unofficial spaces: no evidence found. Arctic Shift searches of r/smallbusiness, r/Entrepreneur and r/BankOfAmerica found no discussion of the community. The one Hacker News trace is a 2013 article link that came from the SBC newsletter (HN, Nov 12 2013).

Origin and strategy

  • Champion: Sophia Tolsma, SVP of small business e-commerce: "The goal of the community is to help small businesses overcome their challenges – that's our focus... Small businesses can network, share, learn and dialogue." She declined to give member or visit numbers (PBN, 2007).
  • Business logic: acquisition and brand positioning in a market where big banks were moving onto community-bank turf. Aite Group's Christine Barry said it "illustrates Bank of America's desire to be more than simply a provider of banking transactions, but instead a trusted partner and adviser," and warned that "banks not willing or able to follow a similar approach will find it increasingly difficult to retain existing customers" (same source). BofA already served about 4.5M small businesses, more than 20% of the US market (Aite, via PBN).
  • Stated mission (2007): "to create a thriving online community that empowers people in building a successful business." It promised that "contributions and members that are rated by the community as the most helpful may be prominently featured across the site", and pitched members on gaining "exposure for your business by being an active contributor" (About page, Oct 2007).
  • Original product vision: member profiles would "eventually become a resource for small businesses to find new customers, partners, suppliers and investors", and the site would be "entirely user-driven" (Inc., 2007). By 2012 that idea was branded "InterConnect" ("Search the Small Business Online Community to connect with small business suppliers, partners and consultants") (Wayback, Jan 2012).
  • Contrast with Amex: it launched the same year as American Express's OPEN Forum. Black Enterprise compared the two in Nov 2009 and scored BofA higher on user-generated content and openness ("anyone can sign up") but lower on navigation and expert content (Black Enterprise, Nov 2009).

Trajectory: fast growth in 2007–09, a peak in late 2008 and 2009 (the financial crisis, when owners needed credit advice), then a steady decline of roughly 95% in new-thread volume by 2019. Many sampled threads from 2010 on have zero replies and are plainly promotional ("Dataservices offering BPO services @ cheapest rate", Feb 2010; "trade lines to help you build credit faster", Mar 2009). That points to engagement falling faster than the raw counts show.

What keeps people coming back

  • Status and leaderboards (early). Jive points and a "Top Contributors" widget on the homepage, with points shown publicly (Dec 2008). By 2020 the site displayed Jive's stock status levels ("achieved a new status level: Scout / Adventurer / Wayfarer") in its activity stream (Dec 2020). The same handful of names (LUCKIEST, Lighthouse24, DomainDiva, intechspecial, Iwrite, amspcs, CorpCons08) top the list in 2008 and again in Dec 2010 (Dec 2010). So the leaderboard held a few superusers but didn't draw in new ones.
  • Rituals: scheduled live expert Q&As. In May–June 2008 the Events tab listed sessions held as live forum threads: a Jive CEO on "Bootstrapping to VC Funding", an iCrossing SEO expert, Digitas on paid search, Google's financial-services industry director, plus earlier "Live Q&A with Barbara Weltman – Tax Expert" and "Small Business Online Community Trivia" (Events, May 2008). Asking a question required registration, an explicit conversion hook. No evidence was found of this cadence surviving past about 2012. It was later replaced by off-site Google Hangouts.
  • Peer help and etiquette norms. The core value was getting an answer quickly. PBN's 2007 example: a Fall River designer asked where to advertise for free and had answers "within minutes" (PBN). Superusers set norms themselves, as in Iwrite's 2009 thread scolding members who asked questions with empty profiles (thread 13032).
  • Self-interest and visibility. BofA explicitly pitched "exposure for your business" (About, 2007). Many of the most active answerers were service vendors (merchant-services brokers, funding brokers, web designers) who signed posts with their URLs, e.g. amspcs, "Barry G, Boynton Beach, FL, www.MerchantServices-help.com" (thread 5585, Jun 2008). This kept answer supply high, at the cost of trust.
  • Recognition. "Best of" stories and articles and a "Recent Member Story" on the homepage, e.g. Solid Cactus's "Yahoo Store Changed My Life", Dec 2008 (homepage).
  • Staff presence. Named moderators replied to threads: Moderator_PaulR (2012), Moderator Cath (2012), Moderator Melinda (2014), moderator_lee (2016). Sometimes they were the only reply, as on LUCKIEST's 2016 post (thread 38193).
  • In-product hooks: weak. No evidence that the forum was integrated into online banking, beyond a logout-page promotion (2009) and an email newsletter (2013). There was no account-linked identity and no switching cost: anyone could participate without being a customer, so the community did nothing to bind people to the bank.

The content engine

  • Expert articles from day one. The 2007 site had articles by industry experts (PBN). The archive has articles dated Dec 2007 (e.g. "Thinking about Inc-ing") and continuing to 2020, across Cash Management, Credit & Lending, Legal & Insurance, Merchant Services, Startups, Employees, Sales & Marketing and Technology (archive URL structure in the CDX listing).
  • Bylines: "SBOCTeam"/"SBC Team" staff; an agency byline "Touchpoint" (Q&As with businesses such as Susquehanna Brewing Co., pricing and Google algorithm explainers, Jan 2014); syndicated Inc. pieces; and named "Small Business Community influencers": USA Today columnist Steve Strauss, Rieva Lesonsky, Mari Smith and Shama Hyder (Jan 2014 homepage; 2019 author list). Contributors syndicated their own pieces back to their sites, e.g. Marsha Egan's 2016 quote and NAWBO's 2018 cash-flow repost (Egan; NAWBO).
  • Proprietary research: the twice-yearly Small Business Owner Report (from 2012; surveys of 1,000 owners) was published on the community (Medialogic, Oct 2014; Nov 2015 homepage).
  • Video and live: the "Bank of America Small Business Social Series", monthly Google Hangout panels co-sponsored with Mastercard and moderated by people like Carol Roth and Susan Solovic, 2014 to at least 2017 (PYMNTS, May 2014; YouTube, Aug 2015; YouTube, Oct 2017).
  • Podcast: "The Heartbeat of Main Street" with ForbesBooks, hosted by Gregg Stebben, featuring Strauss and BofA executives such as Head of Small Business Sharon Miller (about 2019–2020) (SBC author page; BofA on X, Jan 2020).
  • Member-made content: "Stories" (a Share Your Story template) and forum answers. No evidence was found of templates, rewards or curation programs beyond the "Best of" featuring.
  • The arc: content gradually became the product. By 2020 the homepage hero was influencer articles, and the forum was what the bank cut (Dec 2020 homepage).

People, programs and governance

  • Team: "The Small Business Community Team" plus named moderators (listed above). The team's size was never disclosed. In 2007 the site was being tuned actively: after seeing members announce their businesses at random, the team added a "business announcements" folder (PBN).
  • Rules: the 2007 guidelines asked members to keep it professional and stay on topic, banned unsolicited advertising and "content you've been paid or otherwise rewarded by a third party to write," and relied on member "Report inappropriate" flags (Guidelines, Oct 2007). By 2020: "any posts to this community regarding a Bank of America customer service issue or complaint will automatically be moderated and may not appear on the site" (Dec 2020).
  • Superuser program: no formal one (no MVP or champion title or perks found), only points, status levels and homepage featuring. The "Experts" tab featured invited outside experts rather than promoted members.
  • Feedback loop: a "Community Feedback" space and staff posts such as "We need feedback" (Sep 2010). No evidence that the forum fed BofA product development.
  • Platform: Jive from 2007 to 2020. URLs moved from /blogs/ to /community/… around 2011–12, which looks like a Jive upgrade. A 2012 maintenance page suggests the same. The final footer read "Jive Software Version: 8.0.2.0, revision: 20150911", so the platform went unchanged for five years before shutdown (2020 page).
  • Score: staff_investment 2. There was a real moderator presence and a funded content budget, but no visible community leadership, superuser program or platform investment after 2015.

Growth loops

  • Paid media at launch: Wayback captures of the About page carry campaign codes from 2007–08 banner and text ads on Yahoo!, Tribal Fusion's small-business channel, Advertising.com (a "BAC_Pre-Approved_Subnet" placement) and LinkedIn's Answers section, targeted at "SMB with 2-50 employees" (e.g. Dec 2007 capture).
  • Owned channels: a "Join Today" pitch on the online-banking logout screen (Finovate, Sep 23 2009), an SBC email newsletter (utm tags, Nov 2013), and bylines in local newspapers pointing readers to the community (Las Vegas Review-Journal, Feb 2017).
  • SEO: thousands of indexed Q&A threads and articles. Brave still indexes community URLs in 2026, five years after shutdown. Search traffic was probably the main way in after 2010 (an estimate; no traffic data was found).
  • Word of mouth / third parties: recommended in small-business advice articles as "a free and open forum available to all small business owners" (North Central PA, 2017 repost).
  • What was missing: no referral mechanism and no member-to-member invitations, and the directory and "InterConnect" networking vision never produced visible deal flow.

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: none found. BofA declined to disclose usage in 2007 (PBN). No mention was found in filings, earnings calls or case studies, and EDGAR and the academic literature turned up nothing specific. Score business_impact_evidence 1.
  • Company claims: "We've gotten tremendous response" and "bank officials have deemed it a success" (Tolsma, 2007). In 2020 the bank framed the shutdown as progress ("Big improvements coming soon") and promised that Small Business Resources would be "a tool you reach for again and again", which implies the bank valued content reach over peer interaction.
  • Analyst view: Aite Group's 2007 praise (above) is the only outside business assessment found.
  • Inference: keeping it running 13 years at modest cost suggests it cleared an internal bar as brand/SEO marketing. Replacing it with a content site suggests the forum itself wasn't seen as driving customer value.

What they did right

  1. Open to everyone, no ads. Letting non-customers in and keeping marketing "very low-key" earned press goodwill and differentiated it from Amex's cardmember-gated features (PBN; Black Enterprise).
  2. Real launch investment. Paid acquisition across four ad networks plus a logout-screen pitch roughly doubled traffic year over year by 2009 (40k → 70k UV, Compete) (Finovate).
  3. Live expert events as a conversion hook. Weekly Q&As with recognizable names (Google, Digitas, Jive's CEO) where asking required registration (Events, 2008).
  4. Fast iteration early. Adding a business-announcements folder to absorb self-promotion was a sensible design response (PBN).
  5. Durable content program. Thirteen years of articles, a proprietary survey report, video panels and a podcast gave the space a steady editorial pulse that other sites reposted.
  6. Graceful sunset. A two-month notice with a thank-you to members ("your stories have inspired us... for thirteen years") and a redirect, not a sudden 404 (SBC Team).

What they did wrong

  1. No product tie, no switching cost. Openness drove signups, but nothing linked participation to being a BofA customer: no verified-customer badge, no banker answers and no integration in online banking. The community never had a reason to exist inside the bank's relationship. Consequence: at the end it was easy to replace with articles.
  2. Let vendors and spam fill the forum. The guidelines banned marketing, yet top answerers routinely signed posts with business URLs, and sampled threads from 2009–2010 are often pure solicitations with zero replies. Even in 2008 a member was pleading "Somebody write something interesting. Quick!!!!!" in Merchant Services (thread 5585). Consequence: less trust and fewer serious askers.
  3. Banned the most relevant topic. Auto-moderating any post about BofA customer service meant the forum couldn't double as a support community. In 2020, members still asked BofA PPP questions (e.g. how Intralinks handled PPP applications) and got answers only from each other (thread 41223). A support-deflection use case went unused.
  4. Retired the rituals. Live in-forum expert events (2008) gave way to off-site Google Hangouts on YouTube (2014–17) and then a podcast, so the bank's events no longer brought anyone back to the forum.
  5. Neglected the platform and the superusers. The site stayed on Jive 8.0.2 (a 2015 build) until the 2020 shutdown. No superuser program was ever created, even though a few volunteers carried the forum for 8+ years. New-thread volume fell about 95% from the 2009 peak.
  6. Name churn. Small Business Online Community → Small Business Community → Business Advantage Small Business Community → Small Business Resources → Center for Business Empowerment weakened any member identity.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Launch with real distribution. Pay for the first wave and use your owned surfaces (BofA's logout screen) to send traffic. An empty forum doesn't promote itself.
  2. Run scheduled live expert events inside the community and gate questions behind sign-up. It's the cleanest registration hook BofA had.
  3. Use public points and featuring to recognize the few people who answer most questions. A handful of volunteers produced a large share of the answers for years.
  4. Adjust the structure to how people actually behave. Give self-promotion its own lane, as BofA's announcements folder did, rather than fighting it everywhere.
  5. Sunset honestly. Announce in advance, thank members by name or by contribution, and redirect rather than 404.

Avoid these

  1. Don't build a community that's unrelated to your product. If members can get the full value without being customers and you never answer product questions, the community can't drive retention, and it'll eventually be cut.
  2. Don't ban talk about your own product. Route complaints to staff answers instead of auto-moderating them. That's where support deflection and trust come from.
  3. Don't let the editorial budget replace community management. Articles, panels and podcasts kept the site alive but moved the bank's events off the forum. Spend part of that budget on hosts, superuser programs and the platform.

Related communities worth adding

  • American Express OPEN Forum — American Express — direct 2007 rival on the same Jive-era model (already in the catalogue as american-express-open-forum).
  • Bank of America / Mastercard Small Business Social Series — Bank of America and Mastercard — a co-branded live panel series (2014–2017) that took the place of in-forum events; it may matter for co-sponsored event formats.
  • Small Business Owners Community (SBOC) — independent, with bank-sponsored conferences (Horicon Bank, 2025) — a paid, ritual-heavy small-business community (weekly "Idea Slam" on Zoom, monthly Coffee Club) that could be compared with the bank-run forum model (smallbusinesscommunity.com).
  • LinkedIn Answers — LinkedIn — the Q&A product (shut down in 2013) where BofA bought ads to recruit members; it shows the platforms that competed with brand forums.
  • Jive Community — Jive Software — the vendor's own customer community, which also powered the BofA, Amex and many other 2007–2015 brand forums.
