TL;DR

  • What it is: Telefónica Deutschland's official help forum at hilfe.o2online.de. It went live on 4 February 2010 as the "o2 Forum", moved through three platforms (Lithium, then Jive, then inSided/Gainsight since August 2018), absorbed the E-Plus/BASE customer base after the 2014 merger, and passed 800,000 registered members in September 2024.
  • Why people come back: askers return because a team of about 30 named O₂ moderators can open a verified customer's account and fix things, which the hotline often fails to do. Regulars return for status and belonging: a green "R" (Routinier) or blue "E" (Community-Expert:in) after their name, closed boards, test devices, bi-monthly video calls with O₂ staff, and two all-expenses weekends a year (Rostock, Munich, Düsseldorf, Berlin) with workshops, cooking classes and graffiti sessions.
  • What O₂ feeds it: a staff news desk, a device-loan programme (Angetestet) open to every customer with a "review of the month" and a "review of the year", a Betawerkstatt for beta tests, a "Mein o2" ideas board with public status updates, annual badges, an advent giveaway and milestone parties.
  • Biggest weakness: it depends on one in-house team. Account cases now wait days ("from 1–3 days it's now at least 3–7 days", April 2021). New topics fell by roughly 60% from 2019 to 2025, and in October 2026 Telefónica announced the sale of its Bremen customer-service site, where "the complete O₂ social media team" sits, to the outsourcer TP. The community lead could only say there is "no final decision".
  • The lesson: the German O₂ forum, unlike O₂ UK and Vodafone Germany, never cut the staff route to accounts, and its members say outright that this is why they stay customers. A volunteer tier works best on top of staff who can actually fix things, and it costs real money (people, devices, travel) to keep.

What the community is

hilfe.o2online.de is a German-language Gainsight (formerly inSided) community. It has a news section (O₂ News), a "Digitales Wohnzimmer" (digital living room) for off-topic chat, a section about the community itself, Testgeräte and Testberichte (device loans and reviews), help boards for mobile contracts, billing, SIM, prepaid, devices, O₂ Home (DSL, cable, fibre, HomeSpot) and services (O₂ TV, Priority, Mein O₂), two English-language boards, the Betawerkstatt, a knowledge base ("O₂ Hilfe-Center") and large archives, including a 4,338-topic archive for the BASE→O₂ migration (homepage and board index, Nov 2026).

Every community account sits on a Mein O₂ login. Customers who log in with a contract are "legitimised", so moderators (usernames starting "o2_") can look into and change their contract by private message. Prospects' accounts cannot get that help (Legitimation, updated Jan 2026). "The moderators are the only people in the community who can see data. Everyone else is here privately" (Moderator page, May 2026). This staffed account route is the defining difference from O₂ UK, whose escalation route was removed in 2023 before the forum went dark in 2026, and from Vodafone Germany, which turned its forum into a pure customers-help-customers forum in August 2022 (see the dataroom's o2-community and vodafone-germany-community reports).

The forum is also linked from o2online.de and from the Mein O₂ app (Telefónica interview, 18 Dec 2020). The same Bremen team also answers on Instagram, TikTok, Threads, Facebook, Reddit, the independent Telefon-Treff forum, Trustpilot and the app stores (Community Management bei O₂, 18 Jun 2026).

Status today: active and staffed, but under review after the planned sale of the Bremen site (see "What they did wrong").

Origin and strategy

  • 2010 launch as a help forum. "On 4 February 2010 we flipped the switch, and the help forum of the time saw the light of day... as social networks like Facebook and Twitter were gaining relevance in Germany... it was only logical for us to offer our customer service online and in public, too" (15th birthday, 4 Feb 2025). It started as the "o2 Forum: you ask questions, we answer them, done" (14 Jahre, 4 Feb 2024). Wayback shows it on Lithium in Nov 2012 and May 2014 ("o2 Hilfe"), on Jive from Feb 2015 to June 2018, and on inSided from Aug 2018 (Wayback 2012, 2015, 2018, 2020; platform inferred from page markup).
  • 2014: the expert programme. "The Community Experten programme has existed since October 2014. Since the term is 12 months each, the first programme year ends on 30 September" (nominations, 2 Sep 2015). A veteran remembers "the expert round in Berlin 2014 and the kickoff for the E-Plus/O₂ merger 2015 in Berlin" (15th birthday). The forum absorbed BASE/E-Plus migration questions in 2016 (archive).
  • Nov 2016 relaunch: from forum to "community". The Jive rebuild added a help area with FAQs in every topic, a news section ("Lesenswert") and a big test area ("Angetestet"), and kept a points/missions "Rewards" system (announcement, Nov 2016; welcome post). Staff described "a not only mental shift from a forum to a real community" (14 Jahre).
  • Aug 2018: inSided, mobile first. "A thorough core renovation", driven by the fact that "many of you visit our community on a smartphone", with a live video walkthrough for members on 21 Aug 2018 (Die neue o2 Community ist live, Aug 2018).
  • Stated strategy. Social media head Florian Jedlitschka: "We want to meet the service and information needs of our customers and increase their satisfaction... the direct contact at eye level helps us understand our customers' concerns... and in the Betawerkstatt they give us important impulses for product development" (Telefónica, Dec 2020). Gainsight's 2023 award text calls it a repositioning: "With the rise of real-time customer service solutions such as chatbots and AI, the O2 Community had to ensure relevancy... the community evolved into a 360 degree Customer Community... by expanding its scope to product, innovation, and brand" (Gainsight, Nov 2023).
  • Champions. Florian Eichinger (Senior Social Media & Content Manager, community steering, 2019–20s) and Jedlitschka (head of social media) (Telefónica, 2020). Björn Schulze (o2_Bjoern) was answering platform questions in 2016, accepted the Gainsight award in 2023, and in Oct 2026 described himself as the person who "is responsible for the o2 Community... the platform, the strategy, our community programmes" (Gainsight community; Bremen thread, Oct 2026). That is ten years of continuity in one owner, which is rare.

What keeps people coming back

  1. A fix, not just advice. The forum works as O₂'s main asynchronous service channel. Moderators take verified cases by private message and hand them to specialist departments. Members say so bluntly: "If the o2 Community didn't exist I would have thrown all my contracts at o2's feet long ago, because I'm not willing to deal with an outsourced hotline... The o2 mods are the only ones who really have the knowledge" (Bremen thread, Oct–Nov 2026).
  2. A visible status ladder run by invitation. Members start as ordinary users. Moderators invite steady contributors to become Routiniers (a green "R", a closed board, exclusive test devices, beta tests) (Routiniers, updated Jan 2026). The top tier is the Community-Expert:innen (a blue "E"). Its perks are: rights to anonymise personal data in posts and to manage content, a closed board with moderators, another closed board with Routiniers, "access to advance and internal information", "direct contacts at O₂", exclusive test devices, invitations to Germany-wide events, and regular video calls about products, tariffs and processes. Experts get no access to customer data (experts page, Jun 2026).
  3. Twice-yearly cohort rituals. Appointments are re-made every six months, on 1 April and 1 October until 2025 and in June and December from late 2025. Each round opens with a public nomination thread where members can nominate others or themselves, and closes with a news post naming new and "re-appointed" experts (Oct 2020; Apr 2025; nominations for Jun 2026). Re-appointment is a recurring public thank-you. The fixed term (12 months at launch) also lets the team quietly drop people who have gone inactive.
  4. IRL weekends. "The Community expert meetings take place twice a year at o2's invitation" (Rostock Apr 2019, Munich Nov 2019 with a Friday evening in the O₂ Tower sky lounge, talks on the network, devices like the Galaxy Fold handed out, a cooking class, and a Sunday "o2 Community First" workshop on the forum itself) (Munich report, Nov 2019). During COVID they ran virtual meetings, the fourth on 7 May 2022 (report), then went back to Munich in 2022 (member report), Düsseldorf in Nov 2024 (Apr 2025 post) and Berlin in May 2025, where they painted an "o2 Community" graffiti (Berlin report, Jun 2025). Experts write long trip reports that become content and show newcomers what the tier offers.
  5. Insider calls. The "Experten-Telko" on MS Teams was monthly and has been every two months since 2024, alternating with a "Community Talk" that also includes Routiniers. In one session experts were told about a free tariff upgrade in advance and helped rewrite the customer email and SMS, and they were invited into a pilot of a new service offering (Experten-Telko, updated Feb 2024).
  6. Annual badges and rituals for everyone. Each year the top 10 by accepted solutions become "Community Helfer:in", the top 10 by likes become "Community Liebling", the top 3 reviewers become "Produkttester:in", and there are a "review of the year" vote and an "Adventswichtel" badge for taking part in all three stages of the advent giveaway (Abzeichen 2023, Mar 2024). There are also birthday posts every 4 February, milestone parties naming the member who wrote the 1,000,000th or 2,000,000th comment, World Cup prediction games in the living room, and member self-introduction threads.
  7. Belonging. Members call it their "Wohnzimmer" (living room). Some came from rivals' forums: one joined in April 2021 after more than ten years in "another network operator's community", driven out by "the unrest and the infighting there". Another writes, "I've been in the magenta colleagues', the red ones' and congstar's communities, and I feel nowhere as at home as here" (Bremen thread).

Retention mechanics score 4/5: a full ladder of status, rituals and IRL weekends, but almost all of it is aimed at a top group of a few dozen people. The ordinary asker's hook is the service.

The content engine

  • Staff news desk. A small named team (Anna, Rebecca, Michi in 2024) writes O₂ News on tariffs, hardware, maintenance, outages and digital life (14 Jahre). There are 382 live news topics and 960 archived. The team publishes a yearly review written jointly by the whole news team (Jahresrückblick 2024).
  • Angetestet (device loans and reviews). Any O₂ customer, whether on prepaid, contract, DSL or My Handy, can apply in a thread to borrow a device for a few weeks, return it, and post a review (how it works, 2025; devices must be returned per members, 2019). Staff (Ines, Kurt, Dennis) "hunt hardware" year-round. There is a monthly review of the month, and members vote on which devices to add to the pool. The section had 1,460 device-call topics and 2,058 review topics in Nov 2026, and the top three testers wrote 107 reviews in 2023 (Abzeichen 2023). One YouTuber made a video telling viewers that O₂'s community is his "secret source" of test devices (Nov 2020).
  • Member FAQs and tutorials. Expert candidates are told to "write guides (tutorials) or FAQs". Staff add their own FAQs and a structured knowledge base (the Hilfe-Center).
  • Betawerkstatt and ideas. Examples: private beta boards for the DSL Service Suite (Jan 2019), the Homebox 3 router (experts only, 2023), and a nationwide test lifting O₂ Unlimited Max to 1,000 Mbit/s 5G (from July 2022) (DSL beta; Betawerkstatt; award post). The Mein O₂ ideas board, opened in May 2020, drew more than 75 ideas, 424 votes and 458 comments in two months. Staff published a first summary listing 2 ideas implemented, 2 partly implemented and 13 "in progress", including an English (and Turkish) hotline trial and a better process for bereaved relatives (Fazit, Jul 2020).

Content engine score 4/5: staff news, a steady flow of device reviews, beta tests and visible responses to ideas. There is no video or podcast arm on the scale of O₂ UK's Guru TV.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. The May 2026 moderator page names 30 people, all with the "o2_" prefix. They sit in the Bremen social media team, which runs every O₂ Germany social channel (Jun 2026). Staff also introduce themselves personally ("so that we aren't just a black hole called 'o2 moderator'") (members and staff introduce themselves, Jan 2019). Some came from the hotline, e.g. o2_Saskia, "for years at the hotline and now in the social media team" (moderators introduce themselves, Mar 2017). Staff investment 5/5.
  • Volunteer tiers. Experts (25 in Jun 2026, with new additions of 1–2 per round, e.g. two in Oct 2020 and one in Apr 2025) and Routiniers. Membership is long-tenured. Many experts named in 2019 (tommiFFM, EikeS., Joe Doe) are still listed in 2026. There is no cash or bill credit, unlike O₂ UK's monthly reward. The rewards are access, devices and trips.
  • Rules. There is a netiquette page. Moderators move posts between boards and note it in a visible edit line. Users must not post account data, and experts may anonymise data. Moderators "never ask in writing for your personal customer code" (Legitimation).
  • Feedback loop. Feedback reaches O₂ through the Experten-Telko (guests from product and strategy teams), the Betawerkstatt, the ideas board with status labels, the expert weekends (Sunday workshop on the community itself) and a member-run bug thread for the Gainsight platform (Austausch board). Member ownership 3/5: members produce most answers and reviews and help shape the forum, but every tier is invitation-only and run by staff.

Growth loops

  • SEO and the long tail. About 425,000 topics answer German telco questions, and they rank for long-tail searches. No traffic split was found, beyond 340,000 monthly visitors in 2020.
  • Product entry points. There are links from o2online.de and the Mein O₂ app (Telefónica, 2020), and every Community account is a Mein O₂ account, so prospects can sign up too.
  • Acquisition testimony. "The o2 Community and the o2 moderators were back then a central reason for me to switch from Telekom to o2." Another member: "I only became a contract customer through the social media team (via Facebook back then)... and this community is also the reason why I'm still an o2 customer" (Bremen thread).
  • Device reviews as pre-purchase content, which O₂ links from campaigns and members cross-post to YouTube.
  • Open events. The first open live event, "Zukunft gemeinsam erleben" at BASECAMP Berlin on 9 May 2025, was an experiment in reaching beyond the core (BASECAMP listing).

Evidence of business impact

  • Company and vendor claims (not audited): Gainsight's 2023 Pulse Europe "Architect" award credits the repositioning with improving "NPS (by 29%), call deflection, and comments per topic (by 26%)", with no baseline, period or deflection figure (Gainsight, 17 Nov 2023). Telefónica's 2020 interview gives reach (650,000 members, 340,000 monthly visitors) but no savings figure.
  • Member testimony on churn and acquisition is plentiful and consistent (above), though anecdotal.
  • Product effects: documented small changes (Mein O₂ speed test, chat entry point, a bereavement process, an English/Turkish hotline trial, co-written tariff emails).
  • No hard numbers found for deflection value, retention or the programme's cost, and no academic study. Telefónica's annual and CR reports did not surface community metrics in my searches. Business impact evidence 2/5.

What they did right

  1. Kept the staff route to accounts. Legitimation through Mein O₂ plus about 30 moderators means the forum can resolve problems, not just discuss them. This is the single biggest contrast with O₂ UK (escalation removed in 2023, site dark in 2026) and Vodafone Germany (staff 1:1 help ended in Aug 2022). Members name it as their reason to stay.
  2. A two-tier volunteer ladder with clear, non-cash perks. The badges (R/E), closed boards, insider information and devices cost little per head, while the IRL weekends cost more but build very strong loyalty. The expert roster stayed stable for over seven years.
  3. Regular, public recognition rituals. Half-yearly appointment posts with nominations from the crowd, annual badges in several categories (solutions, likes, reviews, review of the year, advent), milestone posts naming members, and expert trip reports.
  4. Device loans open to everyone, not just the elite. This created a second identity (the reviewer) for people who don't answer billing questions, produced more than 2,000 reviews, and gave the forum a reason to visit beyond complaints.
  5. Closing the feedback loop in public. The ideas board summary with statuses, and experts co-writing a tariff email, show members that their input lands.
  6. Ten years of continuity in leadership and three platform migrations without data loss. Posts, points and profiles carried over in 2016 and 2018, with live walkthroughs for members.

What they did wrong

  1. Slow account handling as staff time gets stretched. "From 1–3 days it has now become at least 3–7 days of waiting" (Apr 2021). "An answer here can take up to 10 days" (Feb 2021). "Requests about contract renewals here in the community unfortunately come with a wait of several days" (Jul 2026). The same team also covers TikTok, Instagram, Reddit and the app stores. Consequence: complaint threads and askers sent to the hotline, which is what they came to avoid.
  2. Expert tone and gatekeeping. In a "customers help customers forum... why do you experts answer the askers verbally arrogantly with a sharp tongue?" (2017 thread). On teltarif's forum, a former customer called O₂'s "experts" people who "think they've eaten knowledge with spoons... arrogance beyond words" (teltarif forum, undated). This is the classic super-user problem and was never solved publicly.
  3. Pushing a "customer must help customer" model. A long-time critic writes, "I said back then that rebuilding the forum into a portal where the customer has to help the customer was a pretty bad idea... if only O₂ had listened to the community", citing the DSL problems after the Alice takeover (Bremen thread, Nov 2026). The 2016 points system was also tied to email addresses and lost some members' points in the migration (Nov 2016).
  4. One point of failure: the Bremen team. Telefónica announced the sale of its TGCS Bremen and Rostock service subsidiaries (about 430 staff) to TP. An internal paper reported by teltarif spoke of moving the work "fully" abroad within three years (teltarif, updated 30 Oct 2026). An expert asked: "Who among the very active users (Routiniers and experts) will still sacrifice their free time for o2 if there are no direct contacts any more? Will there still be test devices and giveaways?" The community lead replied: "There is currently no final decision about the long-term organisational setup of community management and moderation" (thread opened 8 Oct 2026). Consequence so far: uncertainty for the experts. If the team is outsourced, the O₂ UK pattern suggests the volunteer core will leave.
  5. Falling new-question volume. Topic creation fell roughly 60% from 2019 to 2025 (my estimate). This reflects better self-service and chatbots, but it also thins the stream of easy questions that brings new helpers into the ladder.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Give the forum real power over accounts. A verified login plus staff who can act turns a forum from a deflection page into the channel customers prefer, and members credit it with retention.
  2. Build a two-step volunteer ladder with visible badges (Routinier, then Expert), re-confirmed every six months through public nominations. Short terms keep the tier fresh, and re-appointment is itself recognition.
  3. Pay volunteers in access, not cash: advance information, closed boards, video calls with product teams, a hand in customer emails, and devices. Add a twice-yearly IRL weekend; the trip reports become content and a recruitment ad.
  4. Run an open device-loan and review programme with a monthly and yearly "best review". It gives non-support people a role and fills the site with pre-purchase content.
  5. Close idea loops in public, with counts, statuses and named contributors, as in the 2020 Mein O₂ summary.

Avoid these

  1. Don't let one in-house team carry everything. When the same team handles every social channel and the forum, any cost cut or outsourcing threatens the whole community. Name a budget owner and protect the moderators' time for account cases.
  2. Don't let service waits slip silently. Multi-day waits for account help push askers back to the hotline. Publish a target and staff to it.
  3. Watch your super-users' tone. An elite badge without coaching produces "arrogant expert" complaints that put off the newcomers you need.
