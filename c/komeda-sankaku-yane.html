Komeda's Coffee fan community (さんかく屋根の下)

Komeda's Coffee fan community: Komeda-bu (コメダ部) and さんかく屋根の下 ("Under the Triangle Roof") · Komeda Co., Ltd. (Komeda Holdings, TSE 3543)

A cheap, durable fan club that two staff keep warm with lottery-only events. Its sign-up count runs on giveaways, and its business value has never been measured.

TL;DR

  • What it is. Komeda (a ~1,150-store, 95%-franchised Japanese full-service coffee-shop chain) began with in-person "Komeda-bu" (コメダ部, "Komeda club") tasting panels in December 2017. On 31 March 2020 it moved them online as さんかく屋根の下 ("Under the Triangle Roof," named after the stores' roofs), a free, standalone fan site. Registered members are called コメダ部員 (club members) and member friends コメ友. The site had about 57,000 registered members in April 2026.
  • Why people come back. Daily "today's Komeda" photo and chat posts with a small set of regulars; a few lottery-only in-person events a year (tastings, Shiro-Noir-making, factory and sustainability workshops); and an annual October point campaign with ranks, badges and prizes. Prizes arrive with handwritten notes, which members post about publicly.
  • What the company does. Two in-house staff plan the content and run the site. They write a monthly staff blog ("屋根裏部屋より," "From the attic"), event reports, behind-the-scenes serials, polls, quizzes and contests. An agency (amana) built v1 and consulted; the SaaS vendor coorum (Asobica) has run v2 since July 2022.
  • Biggest mistake. The 2022 replatforming made every member re-register and broke the old content. Reported membership went from 30,000+ (January 2022) to about 10,000 (February 2023). Recovery came mostly from giveaway-driven sign-ups (30,000+ entries for a sofa-set prize in July 2023), not from organic growth.
  • The one lesson. Real-world events that members must win a seat at, plus a calm online "living room," make a tiny team go a long way. Without measuring visits or LTV, though, the community stays a marketing ornament, not a business asset.

What the community is

  • Official spaces. The site komeda-sankaku.com is readable by anyone; posting, likes, comments and votes need a free account. It has: the chat board コメ友の部屋 (themed rooms such as 今日のコメダ場 "today's Komeda spot" and 当選報告 "prize-win reports"); the photo feed コメダ写真館 and フォトコンテスト; polls (みんなの声); イベント・キャンペーン; the staff column 屋根裏部屋より; 地域の活動紹介, which reports franchise stores' local activities by prefecture; and announcements and reading pieces (site guide). Events are "コメダ部イベント," and entering any of them requires site membership (2025 press release).
  • Adjacent programs. The Komeda official app (launched 31 Aug 2020, renewed July 2023) has its own gamified loyalty layer, くつログ ("kutsu-log"), with ranks from スタンダード up to どえりゃあジョーレンさん ("seriously big regular" in Nagoya dialect). Some events are app-rank-only (app launch, member blog on ranks). The community and the KOMECA prepaid card use separate accounts (1000-store campaign page).
  • Unofficial spaces. Fan blogs such as "コメダ珈琲だいすきクラブ" (example) and personal Ameblo and note posts. No Reddit presence worth noting. The brand's X account has about 900,000 followers (X API, 7 Nov 2026), roughly 16 times the community's registrations.
  • Status. Active as of November 2026: new chat threads every few hours, and a June 2026 sustainability workshop (release) and an August 2026 factory event (report).

Origin and strategy

Komeda's founder made repeat customers ("fans") the top priority in Nagoya's crowded café market. Komeda sells dwell time and visit frequency rather than table turnover (販促会議, Dec 2020; Yahoo/大竹, Apr 2020). Because 95% of stores are franchised, each store had its own regulars but the brand had no direct relationship with them. Ayako Itō (corporate communications) said the idea was to connect regulars "as Komeda, not as one store," and to "take time to listen" to customers (coorum case study, Feb 2023).

The sequence ran from offline to print to online:

  1. Komeda-bu, December 2017. Members were recruited on social media. Events ran about monthly in Nagoya, Tokyo and Osaka, at 10 to 20 seats each: pre-launch tastings, "design your ideal Shiro-Noir," and office and factory tours. Peatix was used for sign-ups (Peatix group, 6th event, Osaka).
  2. Magazine 『くつろぎの時間』, February 2018 to May 2019. Seven bimonthly issues reported on Komeda-bu. The team dropped it because print was "one-way" (Yahoo, 2020; AdverTimes, Mar 2024).
  3. さんかく屋根の下, 31 March 2020. The site was built to remove the seat and distance limits of in-person events. It opened just as Covid shut stores, and Komeda pitched it as "Komeda at home" (launch release). PR head Daiki Shimizu said it was built "not from a marketing angle, but so customers feel close to Komeda" (Yahoo, 2020).

Official business framing: Komeda's FY2020 securities report (有価証券報告書) lists Komeda-bu and さんかく屋根の下 under "improving brand loyalty," aimed at "raising customers' motivation to visit and promoting regular-customer formation" (securities report, May 2020, p.12). The 2025 integrated report lists "strengthening Komeda-bu activities" as a mitigation for the risk of dependence on franchisees (Integrated Report 2025, p.26). The stated mission evolved from "maximize digital LTV" in 2023 (coorum) to "brand strengthening; for now we prioritize UGC over LTV" in 2024 (AdverTimes).

Champions. Daiki Shimizu (head of corporate communications, 2020); Ayako Itō (corporate communications and digital communication); and Tatsuya Matsuoka, who has run the community since September 2021, led the 2022 renewal, and writes the staff blog (宣伝会議, May 2023).

What keeps people coming back

  • Scarce, lottery-only in-person events. Seats are small (12 for the June 2026 sack-upcycling workshop; 12 parent-child pairs for "First Coffee" in September 2025) and free, and only members can apply (2026, 2025). Matsuoka's key finding: people who meet staff and other members in person "rise a level in enthusiasm" and become more active online afterward. After the 2022 head-store reopening tour, participants organized their own offline meetup at a Komeda and posted about it (AdverTimes 2024). Winning is itself content: a whole room, 当選報告, is for posting wins (room).
  • A daily ritual tied to the real visit. Rooms like 今日のコメダ場 turn each store visit into a post. The 2021 archive shows morning check-ins ("コメモー," member slang for Komeda morning set) with members greeting each other by name (Wayback, Aug 2021). One member marked their 3,000th post 18 months after joining in July 2022 (board page, early 2024, search snippet).
  • Time-boxed gamification. Since 2023, an October "さんかくポイントキャンペーン" (point campaign) awards points for logins and actions. Ranks are named after menu items (クリームソーダ 200 → シロノワール 2,000 → コメダブレンド 4,000 → モーニング 10,000), badges stay on profiles, and prizes are gated by rank (2025 rules; 2023; 2024). Komeda deliberately keeps it to one month a year because the app already runs a permanent points and rank program. The month-long campaign lifted views and comments more than sign-up campaigns did (AdverTimes 2024).
  • Identity and inside language. The names コメダ部員, コメ友 and "the attic" (the staff room), Nagoya-dialect ranks in the app, and the red-sofa aesthetic all come from store design, so belonging maps onto a place members already use weekly.
  • Personal touches. Winners repeatedly post about handwritten notes in prize envelopes, for example a quiz-prize winner on 19 June 2026 who "was touched" and made her first post because of it (room).
  • Polls and co-creation. Polls on menu and goods favorites; goods-design meetups (a metal charm in Dec 2021, "relaxation goods" in Jan 2021); pre-launch tastings where members' feedback set details such as spaghetti noodle thickness (event report #7, Wayback; past events).
  • Switching costs. Weak. No exclusive discounts; the value is relationships and access to lotteries.

The content engine

  • Staff-made. A monthly first-person staff blog by Matsuoka (~190 posts since July 2022 across the column, about 4 a month; column). Event reports, behind-the-scenes serials (a six-part "new head store construction diary" in Sept to Oct 2022, vol.1), quizzes about the mascot コメダンディ, app how-tos, and "コメダ部員さん教えて！" prompts. Answers to these get reused in press releases, such as childhood coffee memories in 2025 (release).
  • Events as content. About 6 in-person events a year after Covid, plus online formats in 2020 to 2022: remote jam tasting with samples mailed home, staff talk streams for up to 500 people, puzzle events written by fans (past events). Since 2025, themes have leaned toward sustainability (the ステナイ "don't throw away" program) and family.
  • Member content. Photo contests with prizes (a bread-arrangement contest in 2021, release); a stream of roughly 1,000 photos a month; and regional store-activity posts that showcase what franchisees do locally (~190 posts; section). Production is in-house: two people handle planning and even simple graphics, and other departments help with large events (AdverTimes 2024). amana's coffee creator and the food studio hue produced early contest and recipe content (amana).

People, programs and governance

  • Team. Two people in Customer Relations / Digital Communication. Other departments help with big events. amana provided launch and operations consulting; coorum provides the platform, analytics and roadmap input (coorum).
  • Operating philosophy. Hands-off on purpose. Members asked for more conversation with staff, but the team "does not step in too far" and limits where staff write. The goal is a space that runs on members' own posts (coorum). The site offers spaces for different fan intensities (read, like, vote, post, attend) (宣伝会議 2023).
  • No ambassador, MVP or moderator program; no evidence found of member moderators or formal super-user perks. The terms state the program exists for Komeda's marketing and sustainability activities (terms).
  • Feedback loop. Tasting panels and goods-design sessions feed product teams directly. An internal survey found ~80% of employees knew the community but only ~30% knew what it does. Those who read it said it raised their morale, and the team set itself the goal of spreading it internally (AdverTimes 2024).

Growth loops

  • Giveaways that require membership are the main engine: 100 limited-edition KOMECA cards at launch (launch); the 2023 1000-store sofa-and-table set (30,000+ entries; staff said many people "learned of the community for the first time" through it, staff blog); ¥500 e-gift sign-up lotteries in early 2026 (win reports, Feb 2026).
  • Owned channels. The corporate site, the app and the 900k-follower X account promote campaigns (example tweet). Press releases for every event reach local media.
  • Word of mouth from event-goers and from the open-to-read site. No referral program; no SEO strategy evident. Matsuoka himself says the standalone site "spreads poorly" (AdverTimes 2024).

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: none published. No LTV, visit-frequency, retention or revenue data tied to members. In 2023 the team said it wanted to "verify the correlation between LTV and community members" (coorum). By 2024 it said LTV was "an area to watch in future" and that it was measuring member count and action rate while prioritizing UGC (AdverTimes).
  • Company claims. The community "contributes to visit motivation and brand strengthening" (AdverTimes 2023 headline). It is named in statutory filings as a loyalty lever (securities report 2020) and as a franchise-risk mitigation (IR 2025).
  • Context, not causation. Komeda placed second in Nikkei Business's 2022 coffee-chain NPS ranking, after Starbucks (Nikkei). Store count grew from 896 (Feb 2020) to 1,154 (Apr 2026) (launch release; 2026 release). The community is too small to explain either.

What they did right

  1. Built offline first, then online to scale it. Two and a half years of monthly panels created a core who arrived on day one (7,000 sign-ups in a week, amana). The online site answered a known limit: seats and distance.
  2. Treats in-person events as the retention engine for the online space. Measured qualitatively: attendees post more afterward and start their own meetups (AdverTimes 2024).
  3. Kept gamification scarce and short. Running points for one month a year avoids clashing with the app's permanent program and avoids point-chasing fatigue. Komeda reports it beat sign-up campaigns on engagement (same).
  4. Cheap and durable. Two staff, nine years from Komeda-bu, six-plus years online, still posting daily. Being named in IR documents gives it standing.
  5. Brand-native identity. Store architecture, sofas and menu items become the community's vocabulary (site name, rank names, prize "home Komeda set").
  6. Real co-creation at small scale. Tastings and goods sessions where developers sit with fans (event #7).

What they did wrong

  1. A forced re-registration on replatforming (July 2022). Reported membership fell from 30,000+ to ~10,000 within seven months (event page, coorum). Members found out late ("I only learned after it ended that I had to re-register," 2022-06-28). Old threads, contest entries and event reports now return 404 and survive only in the Wayback Machine. The site lost about two years of member history.
  2. Membership counts inflated by giveaways. Most growth since 2023 follows sweepstakes that require membership, so the "57,000" headline mixes fans with prize-seekers. The active core is roughly 70 to 90 posters in any three-week window (my sample).
  3. No measurement of business value after six years. The LTV correlation was promised in 2023 and deferred in 2024. Without it, the community depends on goodwill and cannot argue for a bigger budget.
  4. Fragmented identity. Separate logins and point systems for the community, the app (くつログ ranks) and KOMECA. Some fan events are app-rank-only, which splits the most loyal customers across two programs.
  5. Low visibility, inside and outside. A standalone site that "spreads poorly"; only ~30% of employees know what it does (AdverTimes 2024). Chat activity has drifted down since 2024.
  6. Thin staffing. Two people cap the event count (6 in 2024) and leave room for few member-facing replies, despite members asking to talk with staff (coorum).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Start with a small in-person panel and move it online only when seats and distance become the limit. Your first online members already know each other and the staff.
  2. Use rare, free, members-only events as the lever that wakes people up. Then give winners a room to post about it, so each event produces weeks of posts.
  3. Run gamification as a short annual season, not an always-on economy. Use ranks named after your product and badges that persist after the season ends.
  4. Make the daily ritual the product moment. A "today's visit/use" thread turns ordinary use into a post.
  5. Small human gestures scale: handwritten notes in prize envelopes get photographed and praised publicly.

Avoid these

  1. Never replatform with a forced re-signup and broken URLs. Migrate accounts and content, or expect to lose about two-thirds of your base and its history.
  2. Don't let sweepstakes define your membership number. Track and report active posters, returning members and event-to-post conversion instead.
  3. Measure the business link from day one (member vs. non-member visit frequency or retention). Otherwise, years in, you still can't justify more than two staff.
