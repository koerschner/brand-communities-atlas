TL;DR

  • What it is. with Glico is Ezaki Glico's free, login-only membership site. It launched on 24 June 2020 with a "town" theme, in which members are "residents", and replaced Glico Club, a points site that dated back to at least 2002. On 9 April 2026 it was rebuilt as with Glico Park, with seven themed zones, public profiles and likes (PR Times 2020, PR Times 2026).
  • Why people come back. Most come for "hearts", points earned by daily logins, streaks, comments (capped at 5 a day), surveys, a daily digital toy capsule game and referrals. Hearts are spent on lottery entries for Glico snack boxes, fan meetings and factory events (heart rules). The hearts sit on top of pre-launch product trials, staff-posted discussion prompts and IRL fan meetings.
  • What Glico feeds it. Glico produces a steady stream of in-house content: "Glico Employee Ekiden", a relay of staff self-introductions that turned out to be a surprise hit; product-development stories; online "Wellness Campus" seminars; a Recipe Club with 400+ recipes; and themed fan meetings. The site is run by an in-house Digital Promotion Department of 50+ people, formed in December 2022 (advertimes 2025, Fungry interview 2026).
  • The biggest mistake. Glico built the community around extrinsic rewards, which drew point hunters (ポイ活) and low-effort engagement. Glico paid point-reward sites to buy sign-ups, and its own measurements found that fewer than 1% of members were loyal customers or evangelists. Its head of digital now calls the old model "one-way" and incentive-led, and the 2026 rebuild is an attempt to move past it (advertimes 2025, Fungry 2026).
  • The one lesson. Measure your engaged core honestly; Glico found it was under 1%. Event participation, not points, was what predicted buying, so put the budget into experiences, staff contact and co-creation for that small core rather than more prize lotteries.

What the community is

with Glico (ウィズ グリコ) is Ezaki Glico's owned fan community website, at with.glico.com. Joining is free but requires a Glico Members account, the single ID that also covers the Glico Direct Shop, SmileBisco (custom-printed Bisco biscuits) and factory-tour bookings (FAQ, member terms). Signing up asks for your real name, address, phone number and date of birth, plus a public nickname (member walkthrough, Nov 2024).

The site has gone through three versions:

Era Name Defining features
about 2002 to 24 June 2020 Glico Club (glico-club.net) A points site ("Glico coins") with prize draws, "lessons" on products and health, and small fan meetings of 11–15 people. It ran a mixi "official community" until mixi closed those in March 2011. Glico coin issuance stopped on 28 Feb 2020 (Wayback 2020, Wayback 2002, closure notice, mixi, fan meeting report)
24 June 2020 to April 2026 with Glico ("everyone's fan town") A health-themed "town" where members are residents. It had the Janken Glico sugoroku board game, hearts, the Ochaberi Café board, the Idea Camp, a "Try it first" pre-launch chat room, the Employee Ekiden, fan meetings, the Recipe Club (Aug 2022) and the Sports Park (Nov 2021) (PR Times 2020, Recipe Club release, Sports Bull)
9 April 2026 to now with Glico Park Seven theme-park "zones": Main Square; Co-parenting & Learning; Food & Living; Health & Sports; Experiences & Factory Tours; Brands & Campaigns; Shop & Amusement. New features are public profiles, likes on profiles, real-time polls and "Digital Asobi Glico", a daily toy-capsule collection game. Hearts were revised, and the relaunch had a launch event at Sunshine City in Ikebukuro on 17 April 2026 (PR Times 2026, Nissyoku)

Unofficial spaces. There is no fan-run forum, subreddit or Discord. Arctic Shift returns no r/glico subreddit, and no Reddit posts were found. Outside the site, the community's life is on X and Ameba blogs, where prize-hunting (懸賞) accounts post "win reports" (当選報告), and on point-reward (ポイ活) blogs that explain how to farm hearts and referral bonuses (Ameba tag, ke-ma.net). Glico does not engage with these spaces beyond retweet-style prize campaigns.

Origin and strategy

  • Founding rationale. Glico framed the community as a return to its founding idea: the original 1922 "Glico" caramel was a nutritional sweet sold with a toy, for "body and mind". with Glico was launched under the slogan "create together with customers, from the same viewpoint", and it was positioned as health-specific, built around exercise, nutrition and rest (PR Times 2020).
  • Business case. Glico's digital chief, Koji Takeshi, said the company built it company-wide ahead of its centenary because "to keep a lifelong, next-generation relationship, valuing and nurturing the fans who support us is indispensable for sustainable growth" (advertimes, Jan 2025).
  • Strategic slot. The community appears in Glico's July 2022 digital strategy as one of the customer touchpoints that feed a "customer data / analytics platform", which in turn feeds product planning, promotions and PR (careerconnection summary of the Glico DX 2030 deck).
  • Glico Members as the hub. Glico sorts its customers into three layers: Glico Members, SNS followers, and offline retail customers. The goal is to convert SNS followers into Glico Members. Takeshi says membership has "increased substantially in the last few years", and he credits with Glico as a main driver (advertimes, Mar 2025).
  • Champion. Koji Takeshi spent about 26 years at Kao, where he ran a community for parents of children with allergies on mixi-era social networks in 2005. He joined Glico in 2019 and became head of the Digital Promotion Department when it was formed in December 2022. The department has five groups: D2C, which runs the fan site and the online shop; Digital Business Development; Customer Relations; C-commerce; and Digital Planning (Fungry 2026, advertimes 2025).

Trajectory. Steady, with a renewal every few years (2020 and 2026). Comment counts in the low hundreds per topic suggest a few hundred to a few thousand regular commenters (an estimate). That is a small active core inside what is likely a much larger registered base. The 2026 rebuild is an attempt to grow member-to-member activity, not just member-to-brand activity.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Points (hearts) and lotteries: the main driver. Under the 2026 rules, members earn at least 10 hearts a day for logging in, with bonuses for login streaks and their birthday month. Comments and replies earn hearts too, capped at 5 a day, and "too short or repeated-character comments" don't count. Surveys pay hearts, with a 100-heart bonus for the first one. Collecting Digital Asobi Glico toys earns hearts, and referrals earn 500 hearts each for both sides. Members spend hearts on prize lotteries at the "Heart Exchange", on applications for fan meetings and events, on extra gacha pulls and on members-only articles (about hearts, notice, referral). Glico even hides hearts inside its physical factory tour at Glicopia Kobe (Glicopia Kobe). Member posts show that this is the loop people experience: "I'll keep saving points and applying" (X, Jul 2020).
  2. A daily casual game. In 2020 this was the Janken Glico sugoroku, a looping board game whose squares carry Glico history and health trivia, with hearts paid out on rock-paper-scissors wins (PR Times 2020). Since 2026 it has been Digital Asobi Glico: one free pull a day of a digital version of the toys packed with Glico caramels. Extra pulls cost hearts, and the collection is meant as "a shared topic of conversation" (PR Times 2026). This plays directly on the brand's toy-in-the-box heritage.
  3. Staff-posted discussion prompts ("Minasan oshiete", "tell us"). About two or three new prompts a week, mixing light lifestyle questions ("your autumn pleasures", "your game-day meal") with product questions ("Pocky & Pretz Day plans") and polls for real products ("which Pocky/Pretz assortment should the Direct Shop make?") (Main Square). One member describes the board as "mostly commenting on simple themes and liking comments" (note, Nov 2024).
  4. Insider access. Members get lotteries for pre-launch product trials (the "Try it first" chat room, where staff join the conversation), factory-tour events and themed fan meetings. Fan meetings have included a 3rd-anniversary meeting with small-group chats between staff and members, a hybrid Glicopia Kobe tour, a low-carb meeting where members tasted an in-development SUNAO pasta, and a joint Kirin Hyoketsu × Ice no Mi meeting (3rd anniversary, Glicopia, SUNAO, Hyoketsu).
  5. Identity through the "town" theme. Members are "residents" of a town with a café, a news stand, a post office and the Heart Exchange. The 5th-anniversary meeting was run as a "Town Tour" that turned those web locations into physical stations (report). The theme already existed in 2002, when Glico Club's home page was a "Glico street" shopping arcade (Wayback 2002).
  6. Social features (new in 2026). Public profiles, likes on profiles and a weekly "everyone's favourite articles" ranking aim to make members visible to each other (PR Times 2026, oshikiji topic).

What is missing. There are no levels, badges or member tiers on the community side (the Direct Shop has purchase-based ranks), no ambassador programme, no member-led groups or chapters, and almost no member-to-member threads outside staff prompts. Leaving costs members little beyond unspent hearts.

The content engine

  • Glico Employee Ekiden (社員駅伝). A relay in which an employee from a different department introduces themselves each week: their job, hobbies and dreams. Takeshi said that "surprisingly, the employee interviews are well received", and Glico uses them to warm up light users (advertimes 2025, Ekiden explainer, example: Pocky developer, Feb 2025).
  • Product news and development stories. Every renewal or launch gets a page, such as "the development story behind SUNAO's renewal" or "the secret of the 50-year-old Coméco", and members comment on them.
  • Wellness Campus seminars. Online seminars on exercise, nutrition and rest led by experts. For example, session 6 (30 Aug 2021) was a disaster-food seminar with dietitian Mayuko Imaizumi, and it also presented the Idea Camp's results (report).
  • Recipe Club and recipe contests. Glico ran the "Gochitan Grand Prix" for quick, balanced recipes in 2020 and a recipe club with 400+ recipes from 2022, alongside online cooking classes with dietitians. It also runs member recipe contests judged partly on likes and comments, and winners become "standard recipes" on the site (PR Times 2020, Recipe Club, food-loss contest, Koubo listing 2023). This is the main channel for member-made content.
  • Recaps and IRL tie-ins. Fan-meeting reports are published back onto the site and onto glico.com. In 2026 Glico added parenting seminars, workshops (turning snack boxes into robots), a co-creation app built with Ritsumeikan University students, and calligraphy collaborations (PR Times 2026, X, Oct 2026).
  • Audience segmentation. Glico steers heavy users toward participatory content such as factory tours, and light users toward recipes and the Employee Ekiden, which can be read without logging in, "to rank customers up" (advertimes 2025).

People, programs and governance

  • Team. with Glico sits in the D2C group of the Digital Promotion Department, which has 50+ in-house staff: business designers, digital marketers, engineers and data scientists. Glico brought development in-house after "a system trouble in August 2020, right after launch". In Takeshi's words: "without people inside who understand the whole system, we can't protect the fan experience." Engineers now ship UX fixes "the next day" after reading fan reactions (Fungry 2026).
  • Idea Camp. A limited cohort of keen fans worked alongside employees on a single theme. The first cohort started in October 2020 on "rolling-stock disaster supplies built around Glico products". After about six months it produced a folding carry-container box with expiry-date stickers. No later cohorts could be confirmed (Wellness Campus 6, Ameba summary). A 2026 "Tech Koshien" student-contest theme asks for ideas to make with Glico Park more fun (X, Aug 2026).
  • Moderation. A formal community guideline, dated 30 April 2026, bans posting personal or medical information, off-topic posts, spam, harassment, impersonation and sock puppets, links out to other sites or social accounts, and proselytizing or protest posts. Glico can delete posts or suspend commenting (guideline).
  • Feedback into the product. Feedback reaches the product through pre-launch trial chats, in-site polls on actual SKUs (the Pocky/Pretz assortment vote), meta-topics that ask members what they think of new features, and fan meetings. In 2026 Takeshi also described using AI to triage which member posts staff should answer, and having factory directors reply to tour reviews ("a challenge how far we can go") (Fungry 2026).
  • No ambassador or MVP programme was found.

Growth loops

  • Inherited base. Glico Club members were migrated into with Glico in 2020 and given goodwill "thank-you" prize campaigns at the switch (X, Jun 2020).
  • Referral hearts. Both sides get 500 hearts per referral, and the figure was 1,000 for the new member in 2024–25. Some prize-hunting accounts posted the same referral link dozens of times between 2024 and 2025 (referral page, example account's posts, 2024–25).
  • Paid point-reward-site acquisition. Glico ran listings on point-reward sites (Powl, Point Income, Hapitas and others) paying about ¥140–210 for "register and enter a 500-heart prize draw". The listings were active between 2024 and 2025 and had stopped by November 2026 (Powl offer via X, Oct 2024, yurui-okozukai tracker, hatena blog, Nov 2024).
  • Cross-channel funnel. Brand X accounts such as ICREO promote members-only articles, and factory tours and KidZania bookings require Glico Members IDs (advertimes 2025, ICREO on X).
  • Win reports as free advertising. Winners post "#当選報告" photos that tag @GlicoPRJP (example).

Evidence of business impact

All of it is company-reported, given in a December 2024 conference talk by Takeshi (advertimes 2025):

  • In its first year after the relaunch, members rose 25%, page views roughly doubled and actions tripled compared with Glico Club.
  • Members in the top 20% by event applications showed "site coverage" of 215% and an online-shop purchase rate of 155% compared with the rest. "Many event participants became subscription [repeat] customers", so Glico is putting more weight on events.
  • Honest negative finding. Fan-site satisfaction scores are inflated because "people who dislike Glico don't visit". Measured by actual logins and actions, "loyal customers and evangelists are less than 1%."
  • There are no revenue, churn or LTV figures, and nothing appears in the IR or CSR reports. The 2026 CSR report has no mention of the community, based on a text search of the PDF. The figures are correlations from a self-selected group, not causal results. Score for business-impact evidence: 2.

What they did right

  1. They measured honestly and said so in public. Admitting that fewer than 1% of members were loyal, and that satisfaction scores were biased, is rare. It led to a sensible pivot toward events and toward ranking light users up (advertimes 2025).
  2. They linked the community to commerce data. A single Glico Members ID across the community, the shop, factory tours and SmileBisco let Glico show that event-engaged members buy more online.
  3. They gave the brand a human face. The Employee Ekiden and staff in trial chats and fan meetings provide parasocial ties to real employees. The Ekiden was a surprise hit (staff investment score 4).
  4. They built on brand heritage. The town theme, the Janken Glico game and the digital Asobi Glico toys turn Glico's toy-in-the-box history into daily habits. The lineage, from a points club around 2002 through renewals in 2020 and 2026, shows staying power (durability 4).
  5. They brought development in-house after an early failure. The August 2020 outage pushed Glico to build an in-house team that iterates daily (Fungry 2026).
  6. They ran a broad, steady content calendar. Seminars, recipes, development stories, workshops and about two to three prompts a week, all tied to the health purpose (content engine 4).

What they did wrong

  1. Hearts became the product. Glico's own interviewer described the old site as mostly "answer surveys, earn points, get prizes — a fan-site format", and Takeshi calls the 2026 rebuild "graduation from a one-way model centred on point incentives" (Fungry 2026). The consequence is visible on X: from 2022 onward, many "#withGlico" shares are one-line comments such as "Looks tasty" or "I want to eat it", posted by the same accounts across many products (example, another). The 2026 rules still pay for logins and comments, now with anti-spam caps. That sits uneasily beside the stated aim that the design should not "demand action predicated on reward" (Sendenkaigi caption, Jun 2026).
  2. Glico bought an audience of point hunters. Paying point-reward sites per sign-up, plus generous referral hearts, inflated registrations with people whose goal was cashback. That is consistent with the under-1% loyalty finding.
  3. Members had little ownership (score 2). Staff set every topic. There are no member-run groups, no member roles, and only one confirmed Idea Camp cohort. A guideline bans links to outside social accounts, which keeps members from building ties beyond the site.
  4. Reliability problems at each relaunch. There was a system failure in August 2020 right after launch. After the April 2026 rebuild, the "stay logged in" feature broke and had no fix date as of May 2026. Glico then suspended all logins from 21 to 23 October 2026 "for system checks", with no cause given (login notice May 2026, suspension 21 Oct 2026, resumed 23 Oct 2026). On a site whose loop depends on daily login streaks, outages directly hurt habits. (Separately, Glico's 2024 ERP failure halted chilled-product shipments for months; that event is unrelated to the community but shows the company's wider systems risk (Toyo Keizai).)
  5. Repeated rebuilds broke continuity. The old site's pages, including fan-meeting reports and Idea Camp write-ups, now return 404 or redirect after the 2026 renewal (FAQ), which erases the community's own history.
  6. Little transparency. No member numbers have ever been published, so outside observers, including Community Zukan, cannot judge whether it is working.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Use one customer ID across community, store and events, so you can test whether engagement predicts purchase. Glico's 155% purchase-rate finding came from that link.
  2. Put real employees on show. A weekly staff relay is cheap, and Glico found it surprisingly popular. It also gives members people to feel connected to.
  3. Turn brand heritage into a daily ritual. Digital Asobi Glico and the janken game are short daily hooks that are unmistakably Glico.
  4. Run events as the top of the engagement ladder. Hybrid online and in-person meetings, factory tours and pre-launch tastings are where Glico's most valuable members appear.
  5. Audit your "loyal" share against behaviour, not surveys. Fan-site satisfaction is biased by self-selection.

Avoid these

  1. Making points and lotteries the core value. You get point-hunters, scripted comments and a membership that leaves when the prizes stop.
  2. Paying point-reward sites or rewarding referrals heavily if you want a community rather than a mailing list.
  3. Relaunching without stability and archives. Login outages break streak-based habits, and deleting old pages erases shared history.
