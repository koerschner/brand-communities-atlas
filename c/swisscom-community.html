TL;DR

  • What it is: Swisscom's official, multilingual (DE/FR/IT/EN) peer-support forum at community.swisscom.ch, live since 2010. Customers answer one another's questions about internet, TV, mobile, email and billing. It ran on Lithium/Khoros until 3 Dec 2024 and has run on a custom Flarum build by the Bern agency Glowing Blue since 4 Dec 2024 (welcome thread).
  • Why people come back: the answerers return for status, insider access and each other. Swisscom picks prolific helpers as "Superusers" (renamed "Mentors" in 2024) and gives them a private lounge, moderation rights, regular meetups with Swisscom specialists and early product tests (Mentor privileges). Askers come back because answers are fast: a 27-minute median first reply and 655 "helps" a week (home page, Nov 2026).
  • What Swisscom feeds it: a small community-management team, an agency "Community Squad", single sign-on with the Swisscom customer login, levels and badges, and since 2025 a pipe that sends buyers' questions from swisscom.ch product pages into the forum. Swisscom's own staff barely post: the terms of use call the moderation "passive", and staff wrote under 2% of posts in a 3,000-post sample.
  • Biggest mistake: the December 2024 replatforming. Members met weeks of bugs (double posts, broken editing and notifications, missing Mentor rights, garbled DeepL translations) and lost all their private messages. Only about 9,000 discussions from before the migration are publicly visible today. New threads fell from about 610 a month in 2025 to about 550 in 2026, and post volume looks 35-50% below the Khoros years (estimate).
  • The one lesson: a support community can run for 16 years on a tiny core of expert volunteers if you give them status, access and a back channel. That same core is the single point of failure: three members who joined in 2010 wrote 28% of all posts in Oct 2026.

What the community is

community.swisscom.ch is a classic telco "customers help customers" (Kunden helfen Kunden) forum. Its sections are Internet, Telephony (fixed and mobile/roaming), TV, Email, Security, Apps & Services (myCloud, myAI), Business, All About the Community and Off-Topic. It also has a "Swisscom Knowledge" knowledge base, Polls, and a "Product Questions" area fed from swisscom.ch (home page; tag list via API). Anyone can read it. Posting requires the Swisscom Login: the Flarum config shows ssoOnly: true, allowSignUp: false and OAuth providers "swisscom" and "swisscom-ad" (forum API). Swisscom's help pages send customers there: "Get a quick answer from experienced Swisscom customers" (Swisscom help). So do the My Service pages: "Customers help customers: In the forum, you can search for knowledge articles and discussions" (My Service).

The forum has five visible roles. Members earn levels and badges. Mentors (formerly Superusers) are volunteers with extra rights. Moderation staff carry a Swisscom badge. "Product experts" are Swisscom employees. Community managers mostly work in the background (Who's who; groups from the API). Glowing Blue AG runs the platform technically and takes part in strategy as part of Swisscom's agile "Community Squad" (Glowing Blue case).

Related Swisscom spaces:

  • Swisscom (Mobile) Labs, 2007 to about 2015: a co-creation site where users tried and commented on prototype apps and developers pitched their own apps. It was a 2007 Forrester Groundswell finalist in the "Embracing" category, built with Optaros (Social Media Today). In 2011 it added an "Ideas" area, and its first ideas competition drew over 300 proposals (Swisscom press release, Mar 2011, via search snippet; Labs home, Wayback 2011). Labs was later folded into the Khoros community (Glowing Blue).
  • Swisscom Friends, from 2013 (end date not found; later run through Mila): a paid, offline extension of the forum built with the startup Mila. Customers booked tech-savvy neighbours for home installs. It had 1,500+ active "Friends" and handled about 1,000 requests a month by Nov 2015, when Swisscom bought a majority of Mila (Mila/LinkedIn, 2015; Südostschweiz, Nov 2013).
  • Unofficial spaces are marginal. r/swisscom has 109 subscribers and 23 posts in its lifetime (Arctic Shift). The older fan forum Digi-TV.ch was the main independent rival in 2010 (Digi-TV thread, Dec 2010).

Origin and strategy

Former Swisscom community manager Jan Biller (now CEO of Glowing Blue) told the story in an interview from about 2014-15. Swisscom opened Facebook and Twitter accounts for marketing in 2009, saw customers using them for support, and in 2010 "professionalised customer service over social media with a dedicated team. At the same time we established our 'Support Community' as a customers-help-customers platform." A 14-person social-care team in Customer Care answered about 80% of questions on social channels, "with the exception of the community, in which customers help themselves 96% of the time" (futurebiz interview). The strategy was support deflection from day one, with product feedback as a second goal.

The earliest accounts date from March 2010: WalterB joined on 17 Mar 2010 and POGO 1104 on 6 Mar 2010 (API user 138, user 65). In Dec 2010 an independent forum sneered that the "highly praised forum built in Silicon Valley" (Lithium) would fail because customers would just vent at the operator (Digi-TV). Internal champions on record include Jan Biller, community manager and head of social media support; Armin Ledergerber, "Social Media Service Designer", who entered the community in Khoros's 2016 Lithy awards for "Total Community All Star" (HP won that year) (Khoros entry; PR Newswire winners); and since 2024, community manager Tamara (TamaraA).

The strategy changed in Aug 2024: "We no longer want to be just a 'customers helping customers' community. In future, we want to 'know more together'." Swisscom announced a new platform, swisscom.ch product questions answered by the community, DeepL auto-translation to erase language barriers, new badges ("Rising Star, Terence Tao and Marie Curie"), and Superusers renamed Mentors, a name Superusers picked in a lounge survey (announcement, 29 Aug 2024).

What keeps people coming back

For the core helpers (the real engine):

  • Status by invitation. "Superuser" was invite-only: "one day the phone rings and a friendly voice asks whether you'd like to join this exclusive little group" (Swisscom Magazin, 2015). The rule still holds for Mentors: no minimum level, "engage and stand out positively. We regularly review candidates... maybe we'll suddenly knock on your door" (Mentor privileges). Old Khoros profiles carried an annual badge: "You were Super User in 2015 and remain in our Hall of Fame" (profile snippet).
  • Insider access. This is what members value most. "They're often allowed to test prototypes and get access to services the public doesn't know about yet... That is the best thing, it makes all the effort worthwhile" (2015 article). The 2024 program codifies it as #CoCreation (testing products with product managers), #MentorsLounge (insider information from community managers and specialists), #MentorsHappenings (regular meetups with Swisscom specialists) and #Superkräfte (co-moderation rights) (Mentor privileges).
  • IRL bonding. Swisscom took Superusers to dinner, and they socialise and help one another outside the forum (2015 article).
  • Mastery and identity. WalterB, a retired electrical engineer, spent 4-5 hours a day on the forum in 2015 and is "active in about 20 forums". By 2026 he had 41,946 posts and 4,100 best answers, and POGO 1104 had 24,060 posts and 4,125 best answers (API). Members describe the work as learning ("I learn an incredible amount", snowmanch) and as critical independence ("Not uncritical fans!").

For ordinary members:

  • Fast, reliable answers (27-minute median first reply, 93-99% of threads answered) and the lack of a better free channel at night.
  • Gamification. Flarum's "gamilytics" extension gives experience points, levels (WalterB is level 10), 32 achievements and a leaderboard. Points come from starting discussions, replying, solutions, likes given and received, tenure, time online and followed threads (Who's who; API config). A Feb 2020 overhaul replaced a Khoros system that "linked ranks and badges in a non-transparent way" (reward-system post, search snippet). Gamification matters little to the core helpers: after the 2024 relaunch user levels were "no longer displayed prominently", and one veteran called that "quite okay" (post 74).
  • Account lock-in. The Swisscom Login creates an account automatically on the first visit while logged in, which surprises some users (thread 840153). This lowers the barrier to a first post, but it creates no real switching cost.

No rituals or cadence turned up: no weekly events, seasons or challenges. Occasional competitions and seasonal greetings from the team are the only recurring touches (Festtage post, Dec 2025).

The content engine

Swisscom's own content here is thin.

  • Knowledge base. Khoros had per-language "Wissensbox" and knowledge bases. From 2018 Superusers could propose and write Wissensbox articles (Wissensbox page, search snippet, 2018), and Glowing Blue built a filterable KB with "helpful" ratings. On Flarum the publicly tagged knowledge base now holds a handful of articles (56 tagged, 7 visible to guests). In May 2025 the community manager wrote that "an expansion of the knowledge base is not currently planned" after a Mentor asked for curated how-tos to stop answering the same questions (thread 836305).
  • "Swisscom informs" posts on the home page announce changes (TV channel line-ups, 3G shutdown maps). They provoke rather than retain: the Dec 2022 notice that the Internet-Box NAS feature was ending drew 349 replies, mostly angry (thread 738419).
  • Crowdsourced data threads. The evergreen "Report wrong channel logos and names" thread has 253 replies and 284k views (API, evergreen tag).
  • Product questions (Jan 2025). Shoppers ask on swisscom.ch product pages and the community answers, "to make potential buyers' purchase decisions easier with the community's expertise" (launch post, 75k views).
  • Polls, competitions and loyalty-voucher posts are occasional.

Member-made content (step-by-step guides inside answers, screenshots, device setups) is the real content engine. Its reward is best-answer credit and Mentor status, not curation.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. Named community managers include JoelV/IvanH (2020), TamaraA (2024-26) and StefanP (2025), plus moderators such as TS_ and MirkoP and a "Product expert" group of Swisscom employees. Swisscom moderates "passively; it generally does not take part in discussions itself". Employees may post but must disclose their role and leave official statements to moderation (terms). In the Oct 2026 sample, staff groups wrote under 2% of posts.
  • Agency. Glowing Blue AG (Bern) has run strategy consulting, design, technical operations and the 2017 and 2024 rebuilds (Glowing Blue). Its CEO, Jan Biller, used to lead Swisscom's community and social support (b-h.ch interview). Glowing Blue also runs the rival Sunrise community on Flarum (Glowing Blue Sunrise), and it is Flarum's biggest sponsor (flarum.org).
  • Superuser/Mentor program. See above. About 20 Mentors were active in Oct 2026, and Mentors wrote at least 46% of all posts. Several have been active since 2010-2011 (WalterB, POGO 1104, GuidoL, marcus, kaetho, Prelude, Lapinou, Herby, Dani CH, KMDD).
  • Feedback loop. Standing threads ask for feedback on the community itself (since 2019), and Mentors have a private lounge with community managers. In 2017 Glowing Blue answered Superuser feedback "until 2 a.m." and shipped fixes several times a day (Medium). Product feedback runs through Mentor co-creation tests. No public evidence shows specific product changes credited to the community.

Growth loops

  • SEO and help-page routing bring in askers. The default homepage plugin shows "how often you helped" figures, and Glowing Blue says about 80-90% of users enter through topic pages rather than the front page (most likely from search, an inference) (Glowing Blue). The 2024 migration probably cost much of this long-tail SEO: older threads that Google indexed now return 404 to guests.
  • SSO auto-accounts turn every logged-in visitor into a potential poster.
  • Product questions on swisscom.ch turn the community into a pre-sales asset (since Jan 2025).
  • Asker-to-helper conversion is slow. In Oct 2026, 17 of the top 80 authors had joined in 2026, but almost all heavy helpers joined before 2017.
  • No referral or ambassador-acquisition loop was found. Swisscom Friends was the closest thing to monetising peer helpers, and it later moved into Mila.

Evidence of business impact

Hard numbers are scarce, and all of them are company or agency claims:

  • "Customers help themselves 96%" in the community, versus 80% agent-answered on social media (Swisscom community manager, c.2014-15, futurebiz).
  • "Significant savings in customer support" and "product improvement through active feedback" (agency, unquantified, Glowing Blue). "Swisscom saves support costs and develops better products" (Jan Biller, b-h.ch).
  • "We take a lot of work off Swisscom's hands" (Superuser POGO 1104, 2015).
  • Swisscom Friends handled about 1,000 field-service requests a month (2015).
  • Swisscom's own scale gives a rough sense: 655 helps a week is about 34,000 resolved contacts a year. That is an estimate of deflected contacts, not a measured saving.

No filings, earnings-call mentions or academic studies of this community turned up. Swisscom is listed on SIX, not with the SEC. Evidence score 2/5: plausible, long-standing claims with no published retention, churn or cost data.

What they did right

  1. Recruited and kept a core of expert volunteers for 16 years. Eleven of the top 80 posters in Oct 2026 joined in 2010. The levers were invite-only status, insider access, meetups and a back channel, not money (2015 article; Mentor privileges).
  2. Kept the purpose narrow and honest. "The community is not a platform to contact Swisscom" (terms). Peers police that norm themselves: "Here is not Swisscom Support!!!" (thread 606317).
  3. Gave members a hand in design. The Mentor name came from a lounge survey. Agency staff answered relaunch feedback at night in 2017 and shipped daily fixes (Medium).
  4. Solved Switzerland's language problem. DeepL auto-translation lets DE/FR/IT speakers share one answer pool instead of four thin forums. Members praise it despite the glitches: "The automatic translation... works correctly. I'm delighted" (Stéphane Mauris, post 97).
  5. Integrated the forum into the funnel. SSO, help-page links and product-page questions make the forum part of the customer journey, not a separate site.
  6. Tested adjacent ideas early: Labs (2007) for co-creation and Friends (2013) for paid peer help.

What they did wrong

  1. A painful 2024 migration. Swisscom chose a big-bang rebuild over continuity. Private messages were not migrated, and many members weren't told: "I was not informed" (post 42). Bugs lasted for months; in April 2025 the agency had "a task force for weeks working only on fixing bugs" (post 102). New features went live on an unstable base: "first get the new platform running stably, BEFORE adding new features" (POGO 1104, 856439). Some longtime users quit: "For me the community is dead" (Smurf, post 68). Afterwards new threads dropped about 10% a year, and posts appear well below Khoros levels.
  2. Lost content. Only about 3,400 discussions from 2014-2023 and 5,400 from 2024 are publicly visible today, out of what was likely tens of thousands of threads (estimate). Older indexed URLs 404 for guests. That erodes the SEO and answer archive a support community exists to build.
  3. Under-investment in curation. The knowledge base was not expanded even when Mentors asked (836305). Small wishes like dark mode waited on approval from Swisscom ("Swisscom hasn't given us the OK yet") and were still being asked for in Sept 2025 (post 116).
  4. Dependency on a shrinking, ageing core. Three 2010-era members wrote 27.7% of Oct 2026 posts and the top ten wrote 42.7%. Superusers were almost all men ("there are practically no women among the superusers", 2015).
  5. The forum absorbs anger from corporate decisions it can't change. Volunteers report constant rudeness from users who think they are staff (2015 article). Product cuts (NAS removal, 738419) and service automation complaints land on volunteers.
  6. Removing the forum from the app (2022) hid the community from mobile users and pushed them to the chatbot Sam: "Swisscom now tries by all means to get customers used to Sam" (thread 708729).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Recruit superusers by invitation and pay them in access, not cash: early product tests with PMs, a private lounge with staff, meetups, co-moderation rights and a permanent "hall of fame". This kept Swisscom's core active for 16 years.
  2. Let peers answer and keep staff in the background. Passive moderation plus clear "this is not official support" rules made the forum cheap to run, and members enforce the norm themselves.
  3. Wire the community into the product journey: SSO, links from help pages, and pre-sales questions from product pages answered by experienced users.
  4. Pool small language markets with machine translation so one answer serves every language.
  5. Run redesigns with your power users, and respond in public, fast, while it's live (the 2017 playbook).

Avoid these

  1. Big-bang replatforming that drops private messages, archives and stability. For a support forum, the archive and SEO are the asset. Migrate everything, keep URLs working, and freeze new features until it is stable.
  2. Letting volume concentrate in a few ageing veterans without a pipeline from askers to helpers or curated content to lighten their load. Plan for succession.
  3. Using the community as a shock absorber for unpopular product decisions while starving it of curated knowledge content and small UX wins.

Scores explained

  • Retention mechanics 3/5: strong superuser levers (invitation, lounge, meetups, beta tests), basic levels and badges, but no rituals or cadence for ordinary members.
  • Content engine 2/5: Swisscom produces little beyond notices; the knowledge base shrank after 2024 and expanding it is "not planned".
  • Member ownership 4/5: volunteers write about half of all posts, co-moderate, and named the Mentor program; staff write under 2%.
  • Staff investment 3/5: a standing community team plus an agency squad and two full rebuilds, but deliberately passive staff presence and slow fixes.
  • Business impact evidence 2/5: only the c.2014 "96% self-help" claim and unquantified agency claims of savings.
  • Durability 4/5: 16 years and two platform changes so far, with activity drifting down since 2024.
