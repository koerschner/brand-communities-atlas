TL;DR

  • What it is: one board for every listed Japanese stock (plus indices, US stocks, funds and FX), sitting under the quote page of Yahoo! Finance, Japan's largest finance site, along with user-created chat threads. They date from Yahoo!掲示板 (July 1998). Yahoo Japan moved them to textream in 2012–13, then to Yahoo! Finance itself on Jan 28, 2019, when it closed every other board category (ja.wikipedia: Yahoo!掲示板; ITmedia, 2018). It is the opposite of the US boards, which Yahoo turned into a comment widget in 2016.
  • Why people come back: money on the line plus the trading day. Users post next to a price they own, tag each post with a feeling (強く買いたい "strongly want to buy" … 強く売りたい "strongly want to sell"), and those tags roll up into a weekly bullish/bearish bar on every board. Volume is rising, not falling: posts went from 25.7M in FY2022 to 27.4M (FY2023), 29.4M (FY2024) and 31.0M (FY2025) (LY transparency reports).
  • What the company feeds it: mostly policing, not programming. A 24/7 human team (76 people shared with Chiebukuro and Yahoo! News comments) works alongside ML that deletes spam and images automatically. Bans are tied to phone numbers. A 2025 rulebook gives concrete examples ("売り豚" ["sell-pigs"] is banned). The community-building layer is thin and recent: favorites with a "popular poster" label (Jan 2025), a "was this useful for investing?" vote (Feb 2025), AI summaries of each busy board that show both sides (2025), and a pilot paying selected users to write analysis (May 2025).
  • Biggest mistake: for two decades the product rewarded volume and emotion over usefulness. Voting was "agree/disagree" until 2025, and identity is a masked ID or a disposable nickname. The board became Japan's byword for 民度が低い (a low-class crowd), a venue for 風説の流布 (spreading false rumours to move a stock) that drew arrests and regulator action in 2011, 2014 and 2024, and since 2024–25 a channel for LINE investment-scam recruiting.
  • The one lesson: put the community on the page people already check every day and it will outlive every redesign, migration and parent-brand change. But you have to design for signal (what counts as a good post, who gets reputation) from day one. Bolting it on 25 years later, as LY is now doing, is slow and fights an entrenched culture.

What the community is

  • Structure. Each stock has exactly one thread; users cannot create stock threads. In the 株式雑談 and FX・為替雑談 categories users create their own threads (PC and app only). Delisted stocks move to a 上場廃止 category and may be deleted later (LY transparency report FY2025). The live boards cover Japanese stocks, indices (the Nikkei 225 board), US stocks and investment trusts (Nikkei 225 board; Rakuten US index fund board).
  • Posting. Posting requires a Yahoo! JAPAN ID. Posters appear under a nickname or a masked ID such as "577*****". Posts are capped at 2,000 full-width characters (help: 投稿できない). Each post carries "useful for investing? yes/no" buttons, links to favorite or ignore the poster, a report link and a "report to the Securities and Exchange Surveillance Commission" link (Metaplanet board).
  • Sentiment. When posting, users pick a 感情 (feeling). Each board shows the share of each feeling over the last week, e.g. Toyota on Nov 7, 2026: strongly buy 52.63%, buy 13.82%, wait-and-see 18.42%, sell 1.32%, strongly sell 13.82% (Toyota board).
  • Status (Nov 2026). Active and growing. A redesigned PC board went live on Oct 7, 2025, with the old view still reachable (notice, Sep 2025). A daily "boards by post volume" ranking runs across Japanese stocks (ranking).
  • Unofficial spaces. Japanese retail investors also gather in 5ch's stock boards, on X (株クラ, "stock cluster"), at Minkabu and on aggregator blogs such as 市況かぶ全力２階建, which often quotes Yahoo board drama (example). No fan-run "Yahoo board" community exists. A wiki of famous Yahoo board posters (house.tonkotsu.jp) shows that regulars become characters, though.

Origin and strategy

  • 1997–2001: boards as part of the portal. Yahoo! Finance launched on Mar 4, 1997 as Japan's first free quote service for all listed stocks. Yahoo!掲示板 launched in July 1998 (ja.wikipedia: Yahoo!ファイナンス; ja.wikipedia: Yahoo!掲示板). By 2001 Yahoo Japan described "銘柄別掲示板" (per-stock boards) as part of Finance. It added per-fund boards on Mar 23, 2001 and called them a "community service" for "timely information exchange" (Yahoo Japan release, Apr 3, 2001). That release reported 10M daily page views for Finance, excluding the boards and chat. The boards were seen as companion traffic to the quote pages. No founding champion is on record (no evidence found).
  • 2005 renewal. Yahoo Japan added batch reading, one-click violation reports, an ignore list and a word filter (ja.wikipedia).
  • 2012–2013: textream, "anonymous but with a persona." Yahoo!掲示板's screens had not changed since 1998. The team also wanted something Facebook and Twitter couldn't do (CNET Japan, Dec 21, 2012), so it rebuilt the boards as textream. The pitch was 人格を持った匿名, anonymity with a persistent persona built from a nickname and photo, and the launch brought push notifications, good/bad votes that enlarged or shrank posts, and a planned "visualisation of feelings" with agree/disagree polls (Yahoo Japan press release, Dec 21, 2012). That planned feature is the ancestor of today's sentiment bar. The feature-phone version was dropped, and the old boards closed on Mar 26, 2013.
  • 2018–19: keep finance, kill the rest. On Oct 2, 2018 Yahoo Japan announced that textream would end. Only the 株式 and FX・為替 categories moved into Yahoo! Finance, on Jan 28, 2019, and every other category closed (ITmedia, Nov 12, 2018; 財経新聞). Press coverage pointed to abuse by malicious users. No official reason was given (SHARE info). Members of other categories scattered, some posting "emergency evacuation" threads pointing to Teacup boards (textream feedback board).
  • Strategy in management's words. In 2018 the Finance service manager said the top KPI was "total Finance users". The service then had about 19M monthly unique browsers (14–15M people), mostly men in their 40s–50s with above-average income, run by a 32-person team. He did not mention the boards (Yahoo Japan corporate blog, Feb 15, 2018). By 2025 the stated purpose had shifted: the boards exist so investors "encounter diverse opinions and analysis," and every 2025 change is justified as making useful comments win (rating change notice, Jan 31, 2025).
  • Regulatory driver. On Apr 30, 2025 the boards, Chiebukuro, OpenChat and VOOM were designated "large platforms" under Japan's 情報流通プラットフォーム対処法 (Information Distribution Platform Act). That forces deletion notices, response deadlines and public reporting (LY, saferinternet page; Impress Watch, Jun 1, 2025). Much of the 2025 rule-tightening follows from this.

What keeps people coming back

  1. The stock is the clubhouse. Every board sits one tab away from a price the member owns. Market hours set the rhythm: LY's own audience data shows Finance traffic peaking at the 9:00 open and around the close (media guide). Earnings, PTS night trading and disclosures all prompt new posts. Members return because their money moved, not because of any program.
  2. Self-declared sentiment, aggregated. Picking 強く買いたい … 強く売りたい when posting is a tiny act of identity ("I'm a holder"). The weekly bar turns the crowd into a scoreboard that people watch and argue about. An outside analyst treats it as a contrarian indicator: "I personally turn cautious when the buy rating reaches 100%" (Asian Century Stocks, Dec 2025). Academic work finds pre-IPO board sentiment predicts higher first-day returns and later underperformance (Tsukioka et al., 2018).
  3. Votes as status, and as weapons. For years "そう思う/そう思わない" (agree/disagree) counts were the status currency. Thread creators could even choose agree-only threads. In Feb 2025 LY replaced them with "投資の参考になりましたか？ はい/いいえ" ("Was this useful for investing? yes/no"), applied retroactively, and removed the agree-only option (notice). Members still use the buttons tribally. One regular describes a stalker who pressed "disagree" within seconds of every post "for five years" (a_toshio2, Dec 10, 2024).
  4. Following people. Users can favorite up to 15 posters or threads and get new-post notifications on PC. From Jan 7, 2025 a beta "みんなのお気に入り" ("everyone's favorite") label marks heavily followed posters (notice). That is the first explicit reputation signal in 27 years. textream had planned favorite-user push notifications back in 2012 (press release).
  5. Rankings as a daily ritual. The "most-posted stocks" ranking, which the official @yahoojpfinance X account posts at noon or daily (e.g. Aug 26, 2026), makes the boards themselves market news. Members ride the hot board of the day.
  6. Salons inside the chat category. Long-running user threads ("株について語る会", "the stock-talk society", with 22,437 posts; "テクニカルテクニカルテクニカル", 11,919; "たまたま＆N氏と楽しい仲間の掲示板", "Tamatama & Mr N's fun friends' board") and self-styled "investment salons" post their own rules about mutual respect and keeping motivation up (株式全般 category; みんなで学ぶ株式投資サロン). This is where peer support and belonging live, and the threads are member-made.
  7. Shared slang as identity. Words such as イナゴ ("locusts", momentum swarms), 養分 ("fodder", the losers who feed winners) and 売り豚 ("sell-pigs") are board culture. When LY banned animal and insect insults in March 2025, members protested "イナゴ禁止は厳しい" ("banning 'locust' is harsh") (Iza, Mar 13, 2025).
  8. Comfort tools. The ignore list, plus auto-mute: posts by users whom many others have ignored, or who are suspended, are hidden board-wide (rules page via search).

Missing: no levels, badges, streaks, verified holdings or onboarding beyond a beginners' practice thread (textream era). Retention is driven by the market, not designed. Score 3/5: strong natural hooks plus a sentiment mechanic, but few deliberate progression or recognition systems until 2025.

The content engine

  • Company-made content is around the board, not for it. Yahoo! Finance supplies quotes, 35 news providers, timely-disclosure alerts (Sep 2024), AI summaries of earnings reports (Feb 2025) and PTS prices (media guide; LY release, Sep 30, 2024; feature list). Each item is fuel for posts.
  • Board-specific content (2025–):
    • AI board summaries. On busy boards, generative AI summarises "the main opinion" and "the opposing view," refreshed at about 10:00 and 16:00 on weekdays from selected good comments (smartphone web only, beta) (notice). This is a deliberate design choice that counters the herding the sentiment bar encourages.
    • 投資ウォッチャー ("Investment Watchers"). A pilot from May 16, 2025 in which selected Yahoo! Finance users write company analyses (DeNA, Metaplanet, MUFG, Nintendo, Seven-Eleven) published on stock pages, with comment threads. Recruitment is to recur (notice). This is LY's first creator program on the boards; incentives are not disclosed (no evidence found).
    • Discussion prompts. Banners such as "Anthropic is going to IPO! Share your views on the board" (YHOO board).
    • Social amplification. Daily post-volume rankings on X.
  • Education about the board is mainly rules: a long page of concrete banned examples (2023 and 2025 revisions), warnings about 風説の流布 and scam invitations (notice, Nov 27, 2025; LY story, Feb 24, 2026).
  • Member content is the product: about 104,000 posts a day. Member-created threads exist only in the chat categories. There is no curation of "best posts" beyond votes and the AI summary.

Score 2/5: rich surrounding data and news, but almost no content programming aimed at members until 2025, and those efforts are still betas.

People, programs and governance

  • Moderation (heavy). A 24/7 team patrols the board. 76 people cover Chiebukuro, the Finance boards and Yahoo! News comments; 145 overall moderate in Japanese. A 13-person escalation group (including lawyers) handles hard cases, and every new moderator gets at least a month of training (FY2025 report). Of FY2025's self-detected deletions (436,845), 76% were commercial or advertising posts. AI auto-deleted 153,314, but AI never suspends accounts. Of deletions after user reports, 84.7% happened within 2 hours. Appeals were tiny (33 against deletions). Since 2024 generative AI removes obscene or violent images, and ML scores and removes off-site advertising. In FY2025 those models were extended to commercial spam and to copyright and portrait-rights images (same report).
  • Ban evasion. Suspended users' phone numbers are matched, and new accounts with the same number cannot post (same report). Profile resets (forced default name and image) are a lighter penalty.
  • Rules. An April 2023 revision added concrete examples (multi-posting, faces) (FY2022 report). The Mar 12, 2025 rules ban comparing people to animals or insects, mocking looks ("ハゲ社長", "bald CEO"), doxxing executives, disaster hoaxes ("man-made earthquake to crash stocks") and off-topic chatter ("I'm hungry" on the Nikkei thread) (ITmedia, Mar 13, 2025). The Jul 24, 2025 revision bans manga and anime images regardless of copyright status and makes the page the legal deletion standard under the new law (notice).
  • Legal pipeline. In FY2025, 1,053 out-of-court requests to disclose posters' identities produced 0 disclosures. Courts ordered 24 (FY2025 report). Companies sue: Tokyo Koki obtained a court disclosure of a poster's phone number and sued for ¥2M in Feb 2024 (ja.wikipedia; Tokyo Koki notice). Rosetta filed a criminal complaint in 2020 (Rosetta).
  • Champions and ambassadors. No formal moderator, ambassador or MVP program (no evidence found). The closest are the ~2,000-member Yahoo! Finance supporters' club of 2018, which held its first in-person meetup at Yahoo HQ and published a newsletter (2018 blog; newsletter vol. 9 via search, page now offline), and the 2025 投資ウォッチャー pilot.
  • Feedback loops. An official feedback thread in the textream era ("we read every comment"), a voice-of-customer form for the 2025 redesign (notice), and Yahoo Japan researchers using board text in published research (e.g. JSAI 2016; Sasaki et al. 2021, co-authored with Yahoo Japan staff).

Staff investment score 3/5: heavy and well documented on trust and safety, light on hosting, programming or recognition. Member ownership 3/5: members create salon threads and set their own norms in them, but they have no role in governance and stock boards are fixed.

Growth loops

  • Portal distribution. Every quote page links to its board, and Yahoo! JAPAN reaches about 107M daily browsers (LY media guide, Oct 2026). Searching for "<company> 掲示板" lands on the board, so 28 years of posts act as SEO.
  • Market events as acquisition. Hot IPOs and meme stocks (Kioxia, Metaplanet, Terra Drone in 2025–26) send newcomers to the board. The company's X rankings amplify this.
  • Data as product. Sentiment and attention data feed the paid VIP倶楽部 ("today's notable stocks" ranking from browsing data, Feb 2026) (LY release). Advertisers are sold "the voice of the market" (media guide).
  • Negative loop. The same reach attracts scammers and pumpers, and their posts erode trust (see below).

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: posting volume (31M a year, still rising), 104K posts a day, and the boards listed as a selling point in Yahoo! Finance's 2026 advertiser media guide. Finance as a whole delivers 2.4B page views a month to an audience where 21–22% earn ¥8M+ (media guide; San-An blog, Jan 2026). That Yahoo Japan kept only the finance boards in 2019 is a revealed preference: they earned their keep.
  • Not published: the boards' share of Finance page views, board users' retention or VIP conversion compared with non-posters, and moderation cost. LY's financial filings don't break out Finance (no evidence found).
  • Costs: a large standing moderation operation, legal exposure under the 2025 platform law, and reputational spillover for listed companies. An industry of IR monitoring and takedown vendors now exists (Kannon, Jul 2026; Luminage, May 2026).

Score 2/5: durable volume and the advertiser pitch are real, but there are no retention or revenue figures.

What they did right

  1. Kept the community where the habit lives. Moving finance boards into Yahoo! Finance in 2019, instead of keeping them in a standalone community product, put them next to the quote. They grew afterwards while textream's other categories died.
  2. Made sentiment a lightweight, aggregate signal. One tap per post produces a per-stock bull/bear bar that outsiders and academics use (Tsukioka 2018; Asian Century Stocks).
  3. Invested in moderation and published it. Annual transparency reports with quarterly volumes, deletion reasons, response times and appeals are rare for any brand community. Deletions fell from 4.0% to 1.7% after FY2022 anti-abuse measures (FY2023 report).
  4. Phone-number-tied bans raise the cost of coming back as a sock puppet without forcing real names (FY2025 report).
  5. Rules written as examples. "Don't post 'I'm hungry' in the Nikkei thread" is clearer than abstract policy, and the press coverage itself spread the norms (ITmedia).
  6. Redefined the vote around the community's purpose. Moving from "agree" to "useful for investing" (2025) and adding two-sided AI summaries both aim the incentive at information instead of tribal agreement (notice; notice).
  7. Migrations preserved history. textream synced posts from Yahoo!掲示板 during the overlap, and the 2025 redesign kept every post and vote and left the old view available (press release 2012; notice 2025).

What they did wrong

  1. Two decades of designing for volume, not quality. Agree/disagree votes, disposable nicknames and no reputation produced a board that even its users call 民度が低い. A 2024 Chiebukuro question ("why is the crowd so low-class… just position-talk and flame wars") drew 14 agreements (Chiebukuro, Apr 9, 2024). A top analyst advises inexperienced investors not to read it at all (Zai Online, 2023). The consequence: high-quality posters leave, and the board's value as information shrinks.
  2. A manipulation venue. A man was arrested in 2011 for fake tie-up posts (Nikkei, Dec 2, 2011). The SESC (Securities and Exchange Surveillance Commission) filed a criminal referral over board-based rumour spreading in 2014 (SESC). In 2024 it recommended its first-ever administrative fine for 風説の流布, ¥2.09M, over a 2021 Yahoo board post about HMT (SESC, Jul 26, 2024).
  3. Spam and scams outran moderation. "Commercial/advertising" posts are three-quarters of self-detected deletions, and in 2025 LY had to warn users about LINE scam invitations (notice). Users built ad-blocker filters themselves because "reported posts disappear after a while, but no posting limits or ID bans happen" (Qiita, Feb 2026).
  4. Platform churn that cost whole communities. Feature-phone users were dropped in 2013, and textream lasted six years before all non-finance categories were deleted with three months' notice (ITmedia).
  5. Late, top-down norm changes. Retroactively relabelling ten years of "agree" votes as "useful", and banning ingrained slang, provoked pushback ("ask 'Are you bullish?' instead… sound arguments don't get through here", S&P 500 board, Nov 2026; "banning 'locust' is harsh", Iza).
  6. Little for good members. Until 2025 there was no recognition, curation or creator program. The supporters' club (2018) left no visible legacy (its page is now a "service ended" notice).
  7. New risk: AI-written posts. Long, polished, persona-driven analyses (e.g. a "諸君！" lecture on Toyota after an earthquake) now sit among human posts, with no labelling policy found (Toyota board, Nov 7, 2026).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Anchor the community to the object users check every day (here, the ticker). It survived three platforms and a parent-company merger because the habit belonged to the quote page, not to the forum product.
  2. Ask for a one-tap stance with every post and show the aggregate. It costs nothing, gives lurkers a reason to visit, and creates proprietary data you can monetize (sentiment, attention rankings).
  3. Publish your moderation numbers. Transparency reports with volumes, response times and appeals build trust with regulators and advertisers. They also give you a baseline for measuring whether interventions work (deletions down 4.0% → 1.7%).
  4. Write rules as concrete, slightly funny examples, and tie bans to a scarce identifier (a phone number) instead of real names.
  5. Define a "good post" in terms of the community's purpose ("useful for investing", two-sided AI summaries), then let reputation follow it (follower labels, invited writers).

Avoid these

  1. Don't let raw agreement be the only status currency. It rewards tribal cheerleading and harassment and pushes experts out. Retrofitting usefulness 25 years later meets resistance.
  2. Don't rely on reactive deletion when money is at stake. Pumpers, scammers and spam follow the attention; plan for them before scale (rate limits, link policies, new-account friction).
  3. Don't migrate communities more often than they can absorb. Each replatforming (2013, 2019, 2025) shed users or whole categories. Keep history, keep the old view, and announce early.

  • Naver Finance 종목토론실 (Naver): Korea's per-ticker stock boards, the closest analogue.
  • Toss Securities community (Viva Republica): Korean broker community with holdings-verified posts.
  • Xueqiu / Snowball (Xueqiu): Chinese investor social network (East Money Guba is already listed).
  • 5ch 株式板: the unofficial anonymous rival. Not company-sponsored, but where much of the slang (イナゴ) began.
