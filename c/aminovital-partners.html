TL;DR

  • What it is: aminoVITAL Partners is Ajinomoto's free, members-only club for users of its aminoVITAL sports-supplement brand. It opened on 8 Feb 2024 as a WordPress member site, with a talk room hosted on Quon's "Beach" platform. In Oct 2025 it was rebuilt as a Commune community and app, with about 23,000 members (PR Times; MarkeZine). Correction to the coordinator's note: the "27,000+ members by Feb 2024" figure belongs to Ajinomoto's other community, the open "Sports Life Community" run by Quon on Beach (launched Mar 2022), not to Partners.
  • Why people come back: for perks and real-world access more than for conversation. Members get lottery seats at practice sessions, seminars with Olympians and pro coaches, walking and running events, prize gifts and product monitors, plus (since Oct 2025) a points-based rank system whose rank names members voted on.
  • What Ajinomoto feeds it: a named CRM owner (Chihiro Okuda) who posts as "ちひろ", event-report and nutrition-tip posts, sponsored mass-participation races, 30th-anniversary gifts, and partnerships with media and sports organizations (Triathlon LUMINA, Cycle Sports, FC Tokyo, YAMAP).
  • Biggest mistake: fragmentation and churn of platforms. The community moved from Beach to WordPress plus Beach to Commune, alongside two Facebook groups, and members had to re-register each time. The whole bet ended up on one outsourced platform: since the Commune breach of 5–6 Oct 2026, Partners has been offline for over a month with no visible notice to members.
  • The lesson: Ajinomoto's strongest results came from the narrowest, most IRL-heavy group: the 1,400–1,500-member triathlon Athlete Club, with Olympian "club captains", monthly practices and quarterly camps. That club co-created the brand's first fan-designed product, aminoVITAL ONE (Apr 2026). The 23,000-member general club produced reach, not depth.

Origin and strategy

  • COVID shock. Sports nutrition head Tomoyasu Ogino said Japan's marathon calendar fell from over 1,200 races to about 200. aminoVITAL sales fell roughly 30% to about ¥7bn, and the brand "lost 2 million users". Ajinomoto wanted to thank the "hundreds of thousands" who kept buying and to give sports lovers a safe place to talk (Sankei, sponsored by Quon, Sep 2023).
  • Step 1, an open community (2022). The Sports Life Community deliberately did not require aminoVITAL use; 40% of early members had little or no experience with the product. Ajinomoto held back from product talk for a year on Quon's advice. Ogino admits he asked Quon "several times" after six months whether he could start mentioning products, and now says "we absolutely shouldn't have" (Sankei).
  • Step 2, a user club (2024). Partners was billed as "the second phase", a co-created community for actual users. It offered member-only practice sessions, races and sports events, limited goods, and product-monitor programs. The stated business goal was explicit: link it to the group-wide AJINOMOTO ID to enable "optimal one-to-one marketing… customer-experience optimization and higher LTV" (PR Times, 8 Feb 2024).
  • Step 3, fan marketing (2025–26). For the brand's 30th anniversary (Mar 2025) the slogan became "the challenge partner for everyone who exercises". The work was organised around "thanks" rather than short-term sales (MarkeZine). By 2026 the department head, Tatsuo Warita, framed it as fan marketing. Ajinomoto's usual playbook is "gather information, develop, then blast it on TV". Sports fans, in contrast, are "fervent… with a strong sense of 'our product', and spread it through the community" (Shokuhin Shimbun, Apr 2026).
  • Champions: Tomoyasu Ogino (sports nutrition head, 2023), Tatsuo Warita (Sports & Health Nutrition head, 2026), Yosuke Ran (marketing manager), and Chihiro Okuda (CRM and fan-building, the Partners owner) (MarkeZine).

Read-through. The open Sports Life Community grew fast: from 0 to 50,000 members in 3.5 years, with about 900 new posts a week in the Dec 2024–May 2025 window. Then it stalled. Between May and Sep 2025 it added only about 280 posts a week, as Ajinomoto steered members toward Partners. In Jul 2025 it shut the Marathon and Mountain sub-clubs (notice), and then it closed. My inference is that Ajinomoto consolidated onto Partners v2 around Oct 2025, but I found no closure notice. Partners itself reached about 23,000 members in 20 months. That is less than half of the open community's headcount; it is gated by registration but not by product ownership. Ajinomoto has published no activity metrics for Partners (posts, DAU, MAU). Commune post IDs are global across tenants, so they can't be used as a proxy.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Scarce real-world access (the core). Nearly every Partners call to action is "apply for X; Partners registration required". Examples: a soccer seminar with the coach who supports Kaoru Mitoma (30 seats by lottery) (X, Jun 2025); hill-climb seminars at ¥5,500 (Cycle Sports); 100 km Extreme Walk entries; Haneda Sky Walk entries in 2025 and 2026 (post); a basketball event, FC Tokyo lottery booths (FC Tokyo); and the first Partners New Year party (X, Dec 2025). This is a perk and lottery loop, close to a loyalty program.
  2. Status that members helped name. Partners v2 has ranks earned through logins and posts. Members voted on the rank names in Oct–Nov 2025 (vote; result, 5 Nov 2025), and ranking up unlocks a custom icon ("念願のアイコンGET", member post). This is Commune's stock gamification with a co-naming twist.
  3. Goal-based programs. The "2024 Runner All-Out Support Program" ran one year with one goal: a personal best. It had sub-3.5, sub-4 and sub-5 divisions. Winners got a year of free product and appeared in ads "like a top athlete", and participants got lectures from Olympian Naoko Chiba and pro runner Yuki Yagi (Beach post; runner2024). It gave runners a season-long reason to stay.
  4. Seasonal cadence in the Athlete Club. FY2026 runs on quarterly "strengthen and focus" themes matched to the triathlon season. There is a monthly practice (MONTHLY GRIND), a quarterly training camp, and a year-end "Fun Night" party that "sells out instantly every year" (av-triathlonclub.com). It is the only space with a true ritual calendar.
  5. Parasocial ties to coaches. Olympians Yuichi Hosoda and Ai Ueda, Ironman legend Cameron Brown and coach Magosaki lead the camps. One member describes about 10 participants on an open-water swim with 5 coaches and lifeguards watching over them, a "VIP swim" (note).
  6. Light-touch belonging in the open community. Sports Life ran playful prompts: a sports senryū contest drew 750+ entries, and "your sport in one kanji" drew 400+ (Sankei). There were also aminoVITAL karuta cards, quizzes, mixed staff-and-member relay-marathon teams (19 people at the third edition, May 2023), and Beach's stock points and stamp card.
  7. Gifts as recognition. The 30th-anniversary gift was a serial-numbered (1–100) lantern bottle designed from a pre-survey of the community. Winners posted it to social media and wore it at events (MarkeZine p.3).

What's missing: in Partners itself I found no peer-led rituals, member hosts or local chapters, and nothing that creates switching costs. Retention there rests on perks the company has to keep funding. (Retention mechanics 3/5: a strong IRL and seasonal design in the Athlete Club, a thin perk loop in Partners.)

The content engine

  • Staff posts in a personal voice. Okuda posts as "ちひろ" with event reports (Haneda Sky Walk 2026, 31 Mar 2026), sports-nutrition how-tos such as "breakfast for runners" (22 Oct 2025), and campaign news (AJINOMOTO ID monthly prizes, a tie-in with the band Sukima Switch, a YAMAP "Mountain Community Night" report in Aug 2026). On Beach, brand staff posted under their own names (e.g. "aminoVITAL staffer Toyoizumi explains summer hydration"), and a Quon "community staff" facilitator ran the daily prompts.
  • Education. Product usage ("how to take the granules: パッ！サッ！グッ！"), product-matching quizzes, and Q&A threads. There is also a separate aminoVITAL Academy on the brand site.
  • Borrowed media. Partner publishers produce much of the content: Triathlon LUMINA (event pages, magazine features, a YouTube playlist of online events), Cycle Sports (monthly cycling-club articles), Sankei (a sponsored feature by Quon) and MarkeZine (a sponsored feature by Giftee).
  • Member-made content is mostly reactive: answers to prompts, event photos, self-introductions. The one big co-creation output is a product, not content (see below).
  • (Content engine 3/5: steady, competent and staff-driven, but there is no member content flywheel and no owned library that compounds.)

People, programs and governance

  • Team: marketing in Ajinomoto's Sports & Health Nutrition department, with a CRM owner (Okuda) and a brand manager (Ran). Day-to-day facilitation and analytics were outsourced: Quon ran operations support and data analysis for Sports Life (Sankei), LUMINA and Cycle Sports ran the sport clubs, and Commune supplies Partners v2. In 2023 Ajinomoto also created a company-wide Marketing Design Center, which Sankei said had high hopes for the community.
  • Ambassadors: Olympians and coaches act as paid "community ambassadors" or club captains for the Athlete Club (PR Times). I found no member-ambassador or superuser program in Partners.
  • Feedback loop: the standout is aminoVITAL ONE (launched 1 Apr 2026). Ajinomoto calls it the first fan-co-created product in its 100-plus-year history. It went through 100+ prototypes with the 1,400-member Athlete Club, using interviews, surveys, flavor votes, tasting sessions, members sitting in on development meetings, and input on the package design (PR Times; Runtrip). Partners was used for lighter feedback, such as the pre-survey for the anniversary gift and a Jul 2025 "election" on which perks members value most (Beach).
  • Moderation: standard terms that ban commercial self-promotion, impersonation and "competitor-focused" posts. Commune and Beach both provide reporting tools. I found no public moderation controversies.
  • (Member ownership 2/5 for Partners: the company sets the agenda, ranks are cosmetic, and co-creation happened in the Athlete Club, not Partners. Staff investment 3/5: a real budget for events, gifts and Olympians, but core operations sit with vendors.)

Growth loops

  1. Gated perks as a registration magnet. Every campaign posted on X, Instagram, Beach and partner media requires Partners sign-up, which feeds AJINOMOTO ID. This is acquisition of first-party data more than community growth.
  2. Feeder community to user club. Sports Life members got Partners "first" with an opening campaign (Beach, Feb 2024), and the open community kept pointing people to Partners until it closed.
  3. Event sponsorships as top of funnel. These include title sponsorship of races ("aminoVITAL presents" the Arakawa and Tamagawa half marathons and the FC Tokyo relay marathon), product sampling at the Osaka 30K, Haneda Sky Walk (run with IZANAMI Sports), and onboarding events for beginner triathletes in Tokyo, Osaka and Nagoya (LUMINA).
  4. Co-creation as launch PR. The ONE launch drew national trade and consumer press (Shokuhin Shimbun, Mainichi via PR Times, TV Tokyo Plus, Cycle Sports, Runtrip). Ajinomoto said the launch would rely on influencers telling "real stories" of how the product helped them over 100 km rather than "I tried it, it was tasty" (Nichiryu).

Evidence of business impact

Company and vendor claims (mostly sponsored content):

  • In Quon's analysis of Sports Life, about 20% of members started exercising because of others' posts, and about 20% increased frequency or intensity; "some" started using the product (Sankei, sponsored).
  • Community analysis showed Ajinomoto it had misread "light" exercisers. That insight led to a podcast giveaway aimed at middle-aged women, which "raised sales" and kept some of them as users. aminoVITAL was then on track to top ¥10bn in annual sales for the first time in FY2023 (same source). The community's causal share of that is not separated.
  • 1,500+ applications for 100 anniversary gifts, a record for the community (MarkeZine).
  • B2B gifts reportedly opened doors with retail buyers (same source; not a community effect).

Hard-ish outcome: a shipped product, aminoVITAL ONE (10,000 mg of amino acids, launched Apr 2026), defined by community input. No sales figures have been published.

Missing: no retention, repeat-purchase, LTV or churn data linking members to non-members, and nothing in Ajinomoto's IR materials. (Business impact evidence 2/5.)

What they did right

  1. Patience in the open community. Ajinomoto gave the space a year without product talk, and members began raising products on their own (Sankei). Ogino's later regret about wanting to rush it is a rare candid admission.
  2. Used the community as a research instrument. Reading about 40,000 posts changed the brand's target model from "turn light users into hardcore athletes" to "light users are fine as they are". That shift fed directly into the 30th-anniversary repositioning ("partner for everyone who exercises") (MarkeZine).
  3. Went narrow and IRL for depth. The Athlete Club combines a single sport, a seasonal calendar, Olympian coaches and a trusted media partner. Ajinomoto itself calls it its "most active, highest-engagement community", and it yielded the first co-created product (PR Times).
  4. Let members name the status system. The rank-name vote is a cheap way to create ownership (post).
  5. Designed gifts with the community. The pre-survey, serial numbers and a distinct fan gift (rather than reusing the cheaper B2B gift) produced organic posting (MarkeZine).

What they did wrong

  1. Too many spaces, too many migrations. In four years members were asked to join Beach, then register on a WordPress member site and a separate Beach talk room, then re-onboard to Commune and its app. Meanwhile cyclists were moved from Facebook into Partners (Cycle Sports), triathletes stayed on Facebook, and the 50,000-member open community was wound down without a public closure notice I could find. Each move resets habits. Partners at ~23,000 never matched the open community's ~49,500.
  2. All eggs in one vendor basket, now dark. On 5–6 Oct 2026 attackers abused Commune's invite links and admin privileges. Commune took all tenant communities offline and later confirmed leaks of a cumulative 166,505 member IDs across tenants (Security Lab tracker). Ajinomoto posted a notice only for its other Commune community, 味のもト～ク on AJINOMOTO PARK, promising individual contact with affected customers (park.ajinomoto.co.jp; piyolog). On 8 Nov 2026 Partners still shows Commune's maintenance page. I found no Partners-specific statement on the brand site or from the @aminovital_jp account. Members who joined for event lotteries have nowhere to go.
  3. A loyalty program dressed as a community. Partners' value proposition is perks: lotteries, gifts, monitors, early product news. The July 2025 member "election" asked which perk people valued most (Beach). Perk-driven membership tends to go dormant between campaigns. That is my inference; no activity data is published.
  4. Co-creation claimed by the umbrella brand but done elsewhere. The launch PR said Partners would be "co-created with users". The actual co-creation ran through the Facebook-based Athlete Club, while the Partners terms formally decline user product ideas (Art. 8).
  5. Evidence is vendor marketing. Almost every metric comes from sponsored articles (Quon/Sankei, Giftee/MarkeZine) or press releases. That leaves no defensible ROI case if budgets tighten.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Start open and don't sell for a year. A topic-led space ("people who exercise", not "people who own our product") grew to ~50,000 and taught the brand who its light users really were.
  2. Find your Athlete Club. A single-sport subgroup with a seasonal calendar (monthly sessions, quarterly camps, an annual party) and expert hosts gave the deepest engagement and the only co-created product.
  3. Make co-creation concrete and visible. Flavor votes, tasting sessions, members in development meetings and a launch party where co-creators speak: members called it "our product".
  4. Let members name the status layer. Voting on rank names costs little and creates ownership.
  5. Design rewards with members. Survey first, make the reward scarce and personal (serial numbers), and expect members to post about it unprompted.

Avoid these

  1. Don't keep re-platforming. Every migration (Beach → WordPress + Beach → Commune, Facebook → Partners) resets habits and splits the audience. Pick a home and stay.
  2. Don't run your whole community on a single SaaS without a fallback channel and an incident-communication plan. A month of silence after a vendor breach wastes years of goodwill.
  3. Don't mistake a perk lottery for a community. Gated giveaways grow sign-ups and ID data, but members leave when the perks stop. Peer rituals and member leadership are what last.
