TL;DR

  • What it is: vivo's official Chinese fan community. It began as a Discuz forum at bbs.vivo.com.cn that already had 4.89M members by June 2012 and 6.25M by June 2013, while vivo was still branded "步步高vivo" (BBK vivo). It now lives as "vivo官网社区" inside the vivo官网 app that comes preinstalled on vivo phones, plus a new standalone vivo社区 app. Around it sit fan-run V粉同城会 city clubs (since Dec 2013), an annual V粉嘉年华 carnival at vivo's Dongguan headquarters, the V-Voice / V粉顾问 co-creation program (2022–), and the sign-up threads for every Funtouch OS / OriginOS beta.
  • Why people come back: a material loop and a status-and-access loop. The material loop is daily check-in streaks (1 to 14 points a day, plus raffle draws), points that work across all of vivo's apps, a points mall with Monday flash redemptions, and member tiers that unlock free screen protectors and repair perks. The access loop is beta seats, early devices for "新品体验官" testers, yellow-V verification for fan work groups, seats at launches, and talking directly with product managers.
  • How vivo feeds it: with staff time and executive presence. Product managers run weekly Q&A sessions and "V粉茶话会" (tea-party) threads; 12 business units ran 31 of those in seven months. A weekly OS digest goes up on the forum. City-club captains get a monthly "salary" in points (V值). CEO Shen Wei spoke at the 2023 and 2024 carnivals.
  • Biggest mistake: vivo paid for posting, then took the pay away. Posts earned 10 points and replies 2 points, up to 60 rewarded actions a day. That bred spam (灌水), so vivo cut the post and reply rewards in late Aug 2023. Its own team admitted the cut "打击了大家认真发帖、回帖的积极性" (discouraged people who posted carefully), and dated thread IDs suggest new-thread volume fell by about two-thirds afterwards.
  • The lesson: the parts that last are the ones where members can see their effort change the product: beta logs, adviser workshops, product changes credited to fan feedback (such as the X Fold's tent mode). Raw activity bought with points goes away when the points do.

What the community is

vivo (founded 2009–2010 out of BBK's phone division, a sibling of OPPO) runs one of China's big three OEM fan ecosystems, alongside OPPO's and Huawei's. Its main spaces:

Origin and strategy

No founding statement survives, but the archive shows the forum was already huge before vivo became a smartphone brand in its own right. The first Wayback capture of the forum front page (June 2012) is titled "步步高vivo智能手机互动社区" and reports 4,887,181 members and 8,318,240 posts (Wayback, 4 Jun 2012). vivo's first smartphone shipped in November 2011, so many early members probably came over from BBK's feature-phone and music-phone era (an inference). In 2012 the forum's pitch was downloads and support: "提供vivo智能手机安卓软件、安卓游戏下载以及手机玩家交流" (Android apps, games and a place for phone enthusiasts) (2014 meta description).

The strategy has moved in three stages:

  1. 2011–2015: download hub, support and offline buzz. Fans set up V粉会 in Dec 2013, and it ran city meetups, charity visits and photo walks. On 13 Dec 2015 it held X6 launch experiences in 100 cities at once (Baidu Baike: V粉会, via r.jina.ai).
  2. 2014–2020: beta pipeline for the in-house OS. Funtouch OS 2.0 recruited 100 internal testers in Sept 2014 (驱动中国). Funtouch OS 4.5 recruited 5,000 through the forum in Dec 2018 (IT之家). OriginOS (Nov 2020) opened with 200 seats per model and 12–31 screening questions (CSDN, Jan 2021).
  3. 2022–: "user-oriented" co-creation. In May 2022 vivo launched V-Voice, "以用户为原点，与用户共创" (start from the user, create with the user), with weekly online tea parties and offline meetups. Wu Jianrui called fans "不仅仅是朋友，更是vivo的家人" (not just friends but family) (Tencent News, 2 Jun 2022). This matches vivo's corporate value of "用户导向" (user orientation), which runs through its press releases.

The business ties are QA (thousands of log-sharing beta testers), product insight (product managers in the room), ecosystem engagement (the points system nudges people to open vivo's app store, themes, games and browser), and repurchase (member tiers earn experience from phone purchases; vivo会员).

Trajectory: posting peaked in the Discuz era (2012–13, 20–30K posts a day). The modern forum is large but far less dense, against a base of more than 500M monthly active vivo phones worldwide (IT之家, Oct 2024). The thread-ID estimate assumes IDs count new threads only. If comments share the counter, absolute activity is lower still, but the relative drop after Aug 2023 holds either way. The bright spots are the parts that are rationed: beta pools have grown from 200 seats per model to 3,000, and offline events sell out.

What keeps people coming back

1. A daily points habit wired into the whole vivo ecosystem. Check-in streaks pay 1, 3, 7, 10 and 14 points on days 1, 3, 7, 10 and 14, with 3 to 7 raffle draws a day. Extra draws cost 20 "gold beans" or 4 points (newcomer guide). Before Aug 2023 a post earned 10 points (up to 10 a day), a reply 2 (up to 50 a day), and reading three threads 5. Essence ("精华") posts earn 50 points with no cap (福利篇). The points are vivo's cross-app currency, also earned by checking in at the app store, themes, music, video, games and browser. They expire after a year, which forces people to spend them (积分百科). The points mall runs raffles for phones and a Monday flash-redemption drop ("周一抢先兑"), and points can pay for part of an order at vivo's store (FAQ).

2. Purchase-linked member tiers. Members move through Ordinary, Silver, Gold and Diamond on experience earned by buying devices and checking in. Higher tiers get free screen protectors, free mail-in repair shipping and photo prints (member FAQ; vivo会员). This is a real switching cost: the perks reset if you leave the brand.

3. Access as status. The forum's own pitch leads with "与产品经理0距离沟通" (zero distance to product managers), first news, priority for beta seats and free trials of new products (guide). Beta seats are rationed and screened by a quiz, and the 100 testers who file the most valid bugs win JD gift cards worth ¥10–100 (OriginOS 7 recruitment). "新品体验官" testers keep the device if their review is good enough (福利篇).

4. Badges and work groups. Blue V marks staff and yellow V marks verified fans. Yellow-V fans join groups: management, content (影像先锋 photo pioneers, 美拍达人 selfie and fashion, 玩机达人 tips, 资源组 resources, 畅聊组 chat), the answer team, the adviser group and vivo创作者 (creators). Perks include beta priority, new-device trials, launch seats, being exempt from spam filters ("社区白名单") and direct contact with staff. Applicants must own a vivo phone (some groups ask for three), log in daily and be over 16 or 18 (certifications guide, Jan 2025; 创作篇, Apr 2023).

5. Belonging offline. The V粉同城会 clubs run photo walks timed to launches (e.g. X300 shoots in Nanjing and Haikou, Oct 2025), meals and factory tours (江苏 circle; 海南 circle). Fans call the August carnival "回家" (coming home). It brought people back after a three-year COVID gap in 2023, and 350 attended in 2024, with CEO Shen Wei, a fan workshop and a fan-staff variety show (PingWest).

6. Identity. "V粉" (V-fans) and vivo's nickname "蓝厂" (the blue factory) are shared tags. In 2024 fans painted a blue mural that stays at headquarters ("刷蓝行动").

The content engine

  • Official cadence: a weekly system-update digest ("本周系统升级资讯", numbered past Vol.120; Vol.120) and monthly "OriginOS 体验优化一览" changelogs (Nov 2024). PingWest reported vivo posts "OS周报" each week in response to feedback (PingWest, Nov 2022).
  • Product-manager Q&A as published content. "产品经理答疑" runs on Mondays in a fan chat group, and the transcript is posted. In Vol.18 the points PM answered 10 questions, including whether post points would return (Oct 2023). The numbered "V粉茶话会" threads take feedback on one topic at a time and reward good replies with points (Vol.5, Sep 2023). A points weekly answers questions about the points system (Vol.1, Mar 2023).
  • Education: curated "影像学院" (imaging academy) and "玩机学院" (tips academy), the "玩转社区" onboarding series, and bug-reporting guides that teach log capture (feedback guide).
  • Member content: fan groups produce most of the tips, resources and photography. The 2023 creator program ("vivo创作者… 提供创作资金和流量扶持", funding and traffic support) pays for original work (guide). Contests carry big prizes, such as Nicholas Tse concert tickets in a 2025 concert-photo challenge (Baidu Baike). Every circle states that "所有内容默认授权V粉社区使用，官方有权对内容进行2次修改": all content is licensed to vivo by default, and vivo may edit it (e.g. 玩机技巧).

People, programs and governance

  • Staff: a community operations team signs announcements as "vivo社区运营团队" (circle reorg, Oct 2023). vivo has a named "粉丝负责人" (head of fans), 吴坚锐 (imobile, Sep 2022), and a community product manager, 邹尚君, who presented at a 2023 fan meetup (PingWest, Jan 2023). Product managers post under their own names; for example the X-series PM 赵创 recruited advisers himself (thread). Team size: no evidence found.
  • V粉顾问计划 (user advisers): one-year terms, selected by application, interview and confirmation; 50 X-series advisers in 2024 and 30 S-series advisers in 2025, plus Vision headset advisers. Advisers run regular workshops with PMs and get an honorary letter of appointment, early access to products, priority launch seats and, from 2025, a fast track to internships. They are reviewed during the term (X series; S series).
  • City-club captains are paid in points. Captains and organisers "对接代理和总部" (liaise with regional distributors and HQ), keep chat groups active and "完成总部下发的其他任务" (complete other tasks HQ assigns). In return they get "月度工资V值" (a monthly salary in V-points, exchangeable for phones, tablets and watches) plus launch seats (Oct 2023).
  • Rules: the rulebook sets escalating bans: a 7-day ban for one political or porn post, and a permanent ban after three in a year. Banned topics include "VPN、定位找人、偷渡包、手机解锁内容" (VPNs, people-tracking, smuggling, phone unlocking), which covers bootloader unlocking (rules, 2023). New posts are held for review (FAQ).
  • How feedback reaches the product: 问吧 reports carry a log extraction code for engineers. Beta testers must join the user-experience program. Workshops feed PMs directly. vivo credits fan input for the X Fold's "帐篷模式" (tent mode) (PingWest); that is a company claim.

Growth loops

  • The default channel: the community sits inside the vivo官网 app preinstalled on vivo phones, so every buyer is a potential member. That is the main acquisition loop; the community is a retention tool more than an acquisition engine.
  • Beta and launch hype: OriginOS beta sign-ups are covered by tech media (e.g. 快科技, Sohu), which sends traffic back to the forum.
  • City clubs and social posting: clubs ran monthly contests rewarding Weibo vivo超话 posts tagged with the club (湖北 circle), and new clubs pay vouchers to the first 50 people who join their groups (Oct 2023).
  • SEO: a long tail of how-to and support threads ranks in Chinese search (an inference from search results).

Evidence of business impact

vivo is private and files no public reports, so there are no audited retention or NPS figures tied to the community. What exists:

  • Company-reported process numbers: V-Voice gathered 18.5K feedback items from 518.4K views across 31 sessions in seven months (GeekPark). Beta pools grew to about 42K testers for OriginOS 7 (smzdm).
  • Company-claimed product changes: the X Fold tent mode (PingWest); points-system fixes answered in the points weekly (thread).
  • Context, not causation: vivo cites three straight years as China's best-selling brand (2024 sustainability report coverage), but nothing ties that to the community. No academic study of vivo社区 found.

Score justifications: retention mechanics 4 (a dense streak, points, tier and access stack, but heavily material); content engine 3 (steady official changelogs and PM Q&A, with education mostly made by fans); member ownership 3 (fans run the clubs and work groups, but vivo pays them, tasks them and owns their content); staff investment 4 (named PMs, a head of fans, the CEO at the carnival, budgets for prizes and events); business impact evidence 2 (only process metrics claimed by the company); durability 4 (about 15 years without a shutdown or rebrand away from "V粉", though activity has fallen).

What they did right

  1. Made the product team visible. PMs post under their names, hold weekly Q&A and publish the transcripts, and sit in adviser workshops. Evidence: PM Q&A Vol.18, X-series adviser recruitment. Members say face time is the high point (see Member voice).
  2. Turned beta access into a structured, scaled ritual. Seats are screened by quiz, split into log-sharing and non-log tracks, timed, and rewarded with leaderboard gift cards. The pool grew from 200 seats per model in 2021 to 3,000 in 2026 (2021; 2026).
  3. Kept the offline layer going for more than a decade. V粉会 began in Dec 2013, reached 100 cities in 2015, and still opened new city clubs in 2025, including Yunnan's first meetup on 22 Feb 2025 (thread). Unlike OPPO, which let its clubs lapse and relaunched them in 2024 (see oppo-china-community), vivo kept its clubs running.
  4. Admitted mistakes and asked members. After the Aug 2023 points cut, vivo opened a tea-party thread to ask whether it had worked, and later held a 2025 "社区共创大会" (community co-creation conference) in Nanjing, asking fans for their complaints in advance (tea party; 集结令).
  5. Linked forum points to the whole ecosystem and to purchases, so the community is part of vivo's loyalty system rather than a separate site.

What they did wrong

  1. Paid for volume, then pulled the pay. Rewarding up to 60 posts and replies a day bred spam. Removing the rewards in late Aug 2023 cleaned things up, but in vivo's own words it hurt careful posters. The PM promised "新任务" (new tasks) in Oct 2023 (Q&A). By 2025 fans were still asking whether "回复取消积分" had hurt activity (集结令). Thread-ID growth fell from about 3M a year to about 1M (estimate).
  2. A points economy that feels like a lottery. Members suspect the phone raffles are rigged ("黑幕"), people run auto-clickers on the redemption pages, and gifts can't be returned (points weekly). vivo also adjusted member benefits in 2024 (notice); details are only in an image.
  3. Shut out the modder core. The rules ban unlocking content, and vivo/iQOO "官方从不开放BL解锁" (never officially allow bootloader unlocking), so in 2026 root guides spread on Bilibili rather than on the forum (smzdm, Aug 2026). The hobbyists who drive forums elsewhere (compare Xiaomi) have no place here.
  4. Feedback channels absorb anger without settling it. OriginOS 7's support list changed four times without explanation, and a tablet line was added only after protest (smzdm, Sep 2026). Users describe the forum as a wall of complaints (Zhihu quote below). Review queues make new complaint posts vanish for a while, which vivo had to explain in a FAQ (FAQ).
  5. Volunteers on a payroll, and content claimed by default. Paying captains a monthly salary in points and setting them "HQ tasks" blurs fan and contractor. The default content licence lets vivo re-edit members' work (club recruitment; circle rules).
  6. Fragmentation: separate vivo and iQOO forums, the forum inside the vivo官网 app, a new standalone app and Tieba split one audience. Beta posts have to be cross-posted to three URLs (OriginOS 7 FAQ).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Put named product managers on a fixed weekly slot (Q&A, tea parties, workshops) and publish the transcript, so lurkers see that answers come back.
  2. Run betas as a rationed, recurring ritual: an application quiz, a log-sharing track, a published roster and a bug leaderboard with small prizes. Scarcity creates status, and the logs pay for the program.
  3. Give superusers bounded terms and named roles (one-year adviser terms, work groups with clear criteria). That keeps the circle fresh and lets you retire people gracefully.
  4. Build a yearly homecoming at HQ with executives present, plus local clubs that hold events around launches.
  5. Tie community rewards to the wider account (points usable across your apps and stores, tiers linked to purchases) so leaving the community costs something real.

Avoid these

  1. Don't pay per post. Reward quality (essence picks, likes received, accepted bugs) from day one. Removing volume pay later looks like a cut and empties the room.
  2. Don't ban your most technical users' hobby (unlocking, modding) if you want a forum with depth. They will gather elsewhere and take the expertise with them.
  3. Don't let feedback channels become complaint sinks. Close the loop in public (changelogs that credit threads, explanations when lists change), or the forum becomes evidence against you.
