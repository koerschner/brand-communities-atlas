Now

Still SGMW's single owner app (renamed 五菱汽车 App, Sep 2023; v8.2.25 Android, 20 Oct 2026). The listing claims '2700万+ users', which looks like the brand-wide owner base, not app actives. How it pays off for the company

Sales and service funnel more than community: online orders and deposits (2020 ¥9.9-deposit campaign), referral 'partner' (合伙人) programme paid in Ling值 points, accessory and lifestyle mall, maintenance bookings and free-maintenance vouchers issued through the app, remote car control (which forces installs). The social layer was meant to raise engagement and feed product ideas; no retention or LTV figure has been published.

TL;DR

  • What it is. SAIC-GM-Wuling's single owner app for the Wuling and Baojun brands. It launched in 2015 as 菱菱邦 (Linglingbang), a "vertical social app" for 13M van and minivan owners, built and run by an outside startup, Yunyang (云漾科技). It was rebranded as the LING Club "user brand" in April 2021 and renamed 五菱汽车 App in September 2023. Today it combines car shopping, remote control, service booking, a points mall and a community feed (App Store; China Auto Trends, Apr 2021).
  • Why people came back. Early on: a plain-spoken social space where working-class owners posted their jobs and lives, plus city 车友会 boards where owners helped each other (Zhihu, ~2019). Later the reasons were utilitarian: remote unlock and charging status, free-maintenance vouchers, daily sign-in points (Ling值) and Covid-era mask giveaways (Gasgoo, Mar 2020).
  • What the company does to feed it. A points-and-badges member system (four badge types and five tiers, up to a 10%-off car voucher), a referral "partner" scheme, staff mascots (小五/小菱), WeChat Work groups run by "LING號玩家" personas, a KOC programme (菱感体验官), offline parties and LING HOUSE centres. About 2,000 user listening sessions a year were run by company staff, not dealers (Woshipm, Oct 2022; Hualong, Apr 2021).
  • Biggest mistake. Wuling turned the app into a monetization and data funnel. MIIT publicly named it in July 2023 for illegal and excessive collection of personal data and frequent self-launching (Autohome, Jul 2023). It shows splash ads to owners who just want to unlock their car. Users report that dealers phone them the day after they register. The car-control features, which are the reason most people install it, fail often enough that one-star reviews dominate (App Store reviews feed).
  • The one lesson. A must-have utility (the car key, maintenance vouchers) can force tens of millions of installs. But a community only lasts if the utility works and the social space isn't treated as ad inventory. Wuling's real community energy moved to WeChat groups, Xiaohongshu and Douyin, and to agency-run events.

What the community is

The app is the owned hub of a much larger set of Wuling "user operations" (用户运营), so the community layer has to be separated from the service app around it.

Origin and strategy

  • 2015–16: a social network for working drivers. Yunyang launched 菱菱邦 in March 2015 as a single-purpose car Q&A app. In January 2016 it rebuilt it into a social product so that "13 million Wuling and Baojun owners" could form groups by region, interest and business (snippet of a Feb 2016 Mandian EV article; site now unreachable). The pitch on its website still reads "最靠谱、最赚钱、最热闹、最有趣" (most reliable, most profitable, liveliest, most fun) and includes a 生意圈 (business circle) for "supply and demand" posts, because most Hongguang vans were work vehicles (00bang.cn; ddooo listing). The first App Store version is dated 1 Dec 2015 (Analysys).
  • 2016–2020: from social to sales and service channel. SGMW used the app for "contactless" online sales, maintenance bookings, annual-inspection errands and traffic-fine payment from 2016 (CQCB, Feb 2020). In February 2020, during Covid, it ran a ¥1bn subsidy campaign through the app: a ¥9.9 deposit gave ¥1,000 off, and app orders also got four free services. Registered users were then "nearly 10 million" (Sina, Feb 2020). In March 2020 the "五菱牌口罩" mask giveaway ran only through 菱菱邦: 10,000 winners a day, plus masks bought with in-app gold coins (Gasgoo). Analysys called the mask section a cheap way to build stickiness (Analysys).
  • April 2021: the "LING Club user brand". At the Shanghai auto show Wuling announced LING Club "standing on 23 million users". It had a new mini-program, the upgraded app, launch partners (Wanda, Ping An, Ant Chain) and mascots 小五 and 小菱 (China Auto Trends). User-ops director Ding Xiaobin (丁晓斌) explained the logic: 13M registrations and 200k+ daily actives meant "200,000 people talking to me every day", so Wuling would layer cross-industry perks (food, travel, shopping, education) on top of car control (Hualong). In February 2022 the plan became the "用户之翼" (user wing) of the "China Wuling" strategy: LING Club online, LING HOUSE offline, plus dealers. Zhou Xing (周钘) reported LING Club at 14.5M+ registered users (Sohu, Feb 2022).
  • 2023: renamed and folded into the brand. The app was renamed "五菱汽车" in v5.2.0 on 18 Sep 2023, ten weeks after the MIIT notice (App Store version history). Its marketing lead-in became "与2700万+用户一起" (together with 27M+ users).
  • Who championed it. Marketing VP Xue Haitao and Zhou Xing (brand) set direction. Ding Xiaobin ran user operations. Delivery was outsourced: Yunyang (later Souche) built and operated the app, and 知家 DTC has run the KOC and events programme since 2021 (Dasouche, Jun 2020; Zoomedia, Aug 2024).

Reading the numbers. The "27M+" figure is almost certainly brand-wide. Wuling quoted 23M "users" in April 2021 and 25.5M in February 2022 as total owners, while app registrations were 13–14.6M (Sohu). Baidu Baike repeats 27M as "registered users"; treat that as unverified. Activity peaked around 2020–21, when the app was the only route to masks, Covid deals and Mini EV remote charging status. Monthly actives of ~0.8M against ~13M registrations means roughly 6% active monthly (an estimate). Wuling's absence from the 2025 QuestMobile leaders, despite the largest fleet of any Chinese brand, suggests the app never became the daily habit that BYD's or NIO's did. No post-volume or community-specific figures have been published at any point: no evidence found.

What keeps people coming back

  1. The utility hook (strongest). The app is the phone key and status screen for connected Wuling EVs (Mini EV, Bingo, Starlight). App Store feedback in 2025–26 is almost entirely about unlocking, Bluetooth keys, charging status and widgets (reviews feed). Free-maintenance entitlements are issued through the app after identity verification. Customer service told a reporter the app is not strictly required, since an invoice also works (bqiche).
  2. Points as a currency (Ling值). Members earn points by spending (¥10 = 1 Ling值), signing in, completing tasks, playing mini-games and inviting others ("invite 10 friends → 100 Ling值 ≈ ¥10 gift"). Points buy drinks vouchers, coupons and mall goods; car accessories are reserved for verified owners (Woshipm). The listing says "Ling值也当钱花" (Ling值 spends like money). The app has kept the daily sign-in ritual: one release note reads "恢复自动签到啦" (auto sign-in is back) (2265 changelog). Wuling also uses points as goodwill currency: in one complaint, compensation was paid in Ling值 (Heimao).
  3. Status ladder. Four badges (activity, influence, reach, spending) add up to a star total that maps to five tiers, from 普通 to 钻石 (Diamond). Perks escalate from launch-event rewards and beta access through birthday gifts to a 10%-off car-purchase voucher and free roadside assistance at Diamond (Woshipm). Tying the top tier to a discount on the next car is a direct repurchase lever, but no data on its uptake was found.
  4. Belonging among working-class owners (the early magic). A 2019 Zhihu essay describes 菱菱邦 (classified as a "social" app and rated 12+) as an unpretentious space where owners posted their work. A bricklayer shaped cement into a heart; a farmer, selling his van, wrote "老伙计，希望新主人好好对你" (old friend, I hope your new owner treats you well). In a city 车友会 board a fruit vendor asked for help, and 10+ local owners replied "发定位，马上到" (send your location, on my way) (Zhihu). Wuling called owners "奋斗者" (strivers), and that identity stuck.
  5. Expert help. "Golden technicians" and engineers answer questions in the app. A 2020 MyApp reviewer said this saved a repair-shop visit (MyApp).
  6. Events and status offline. Yearly 菱感派对 parties ("for three years running"), creator salons and owner-first test drives give KOCs early access and titles (Zoomedia). Milestone galas continue, such as a 3M-unit Mini EV/Bingo owner event in August 2026 (Sohu, Aug 2026).

Score 3. The full textbook set is here (sign-in, points, badges, tiers, referrals, events), and the car key forces installs. But the mechanics reward transactions more than relationships, and the social layer has thinned.

The content engine

  • Official feed. Brand news, activities and "modding tips" were in the app from early on. A 2017 reviewer praised "文章 活动 还有改装小技巧 车友的分享 疑问解答" (articles, activities, mod tips, owner sharing, Q&A) and asked for more news (App Store). Later versions added a 潮创大V (trend-mod influencer) zone, @-mentions, community search by 车友会, a block list for "chat messages and posts" and, in Nov 2023, a "星光专区" (Starlight zone) (2265; APKPure).
  • Staff personas. Mascots 小五 and 小菱 front the user brand and also live in the car's Ling OS voice assistant. WeChat Work accounts named "LING號玩家-xx" post 2–3 videos a day. Woshipm's teardown found them "mechanical" and product-only (Woshipm).
  • Member-made content, mostly off-app. The 菱感体验官 KOC tier gets test drives and traffic support. A middle tier earns points for 菱感 posts. Mass campaigns run as hashtags on Xiaohongshu/Douyin/Weibo (#我的缤果车生活, #缤果姐姐的日常). The agency claims 1M+ UGC pieces in five years and Xiaohongshu No. 1 (Zhihu/知家, Nov 2025), but that is an agency self-report.
  • Commerce as content. Drops sold only in the app: a ¥3,999 "car-grade first-class" office chair in April 2022 (Yiche), 菱小茶 tea from Sanjiang county, co-branded merchandise.
  • Score 3: steady official and agency output and real creator support, but the best member content is produced for third-party platforms, not the owned app.

People, programs and governance

  • Team. A user-operations department under the sales company (director Ding Xiaobin in 2021). Yunyang/Souche ran the app operationally; its terms of service are signed with 云漾科技, not SGMW (00bang.cn ToS). The 知家 DTC agency handles KOCs and events. Headcount is not disclosed.
  • Listening. "Close to 2,000" user listening sessions a year, invited by after-sales staff, with department directors attending (Hualong). Woshipm credits model groups with the brief that became the Mini EV, a car for "a 10–20 km radius that keeps the rain off and isn't expensive" (Woshipm), a widely repeated, unverified origin story.
  • Ambassadors. 菱感体验官 (150+ by Aug 2025, per Xinhua, cited in the [wuling-mini-ev-modding] report). The referral 合伙人 (partner) scheme paid ¥500 fuel cards in 2020 (Sina) and "large Ling值 rewards" later.
  • Co-creation editions. The 2024 "星光共创版" (Starlight co-creation edition) was framed as built with 52,000 Starlight owners (Gasgoo). App orders got launch perks (Xinhua, Apr 2024).
  • Moderation. No published community rules were found beyond the ToS. The 2026 build added a user block feature; the 2019 Zhihu author noticed a help-request post had been removed.

Growth loops

  1. Exclusive access through the app: Covid masks (2020), app-only deposits and launch perks (2020, 2024), app-only merchandise. Each drove installs from non-owners. The app was "the only channel" for masks (Sohu).
  2. Referral partners pay owners (fuel cards, then Ling值) for successful car referrals, and "invite friends" tasks spread points-based virality (Woshipm).
  3. Public → private funnel: Douyin, Weibo, Bilibili and Xiaohongshu profiles link to app downloads and WeChat Work groups; dealers livestream and hand leads back (Tencent eSchool).
  4. Dealer integration: the dealer edition passes leads from app activity to sales staff. The downside is that users report sales calls right after signing up (see Member voice).

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard-ish: No. 1 owner-service app by MAU in Q1 2021, more than double BYD's at the time (Analysys). Souche called 菱菱邦 the automaker platform "with the most users, most dealers covered and highest activity" when it bought Yunyang (Dasouche); that is a buyer's claim.
  • Company claims: 23M users and 13M registrations (2021); 14.5M (2022); "27M+" (2026).
  • Not found: any figure on repurchase, retention, churn, NPS, referral volume or mall revenue tied to the app. SGMW is not separately listed; SAIC Motor's filings do not break it out. Score 2.

What they did right

  1. Built an owned owner network early (2015), years before most legacy Chinese automakers, and made it social, not just a service portal (Mandian EV snippet).
  2. Designed for their real users. A "business circle" for van owners who drive for a living, city 车友会 boards and the 奋斗者 identity produced genuine peer help (Zhihu ~2019).
  3. Used the app as crisis infrastructure. Masks and contactless buying in 2020 coincided with the app's MAU leadership (Analysys).
  4. A complete, legible loyalty ladder: points that are real money, four badge types, five tiers and a top-tier car-discount voucher (Woshipm).
  5. Close listening. About 2,000 user sessions a year, run by staff rather than dealers, plus model-specific groups (Hualong).
  6. Linked online and offline: LING HOUSE spaces, parties, galas.

What they did wrong

  1. Privacy violation, publicly named. MIIT's 2023 batch 4 (30th overall) notice of 31 apps listed LING Club for 违规收集个人信息, 超范围收集个人信息 and 频繁自启动和关联启动 (illegal collection, excessive collection, frequent self-launch and linked launch) (China Peace, 8 Jul 2023; Autohome). Customer service "didn't know about it". The app was renamed ten weeks later. The 2026 App Store privacy label still declares identifiers used for cross-app tracking (App Store).
  2. Ads inside a car key. Complaints about splash ads go back to April 2021 ("must watch your ad screens before reaching login") and continue in 2025–26 ("I bought your car, why must I watch an ad to unlock it?"). One user "couldn't find the car-control screen, thought I was on Douyin" (reviews feed). Feed content now competes with the core job.
  3. Unreliable core utility. A mass outage on 28 Jul 2025 produced a burst of one-star reviews. Bluetooth-key, location and charging-status failures recur release after release, and iOS lags Android (5.2.x vs 8.2.x). 31% of App Store ratings are one-star.
  4. Community as sales lead pipe. "Downloaded the app yesterday, the local salesperson called today"; "calls every day like a death notice". Mall after-sales was weak too: accessory refunds stuck in loops, and the ¥3,999 chair was mocked as "harvesting" fans when similar seats cost ¥300 at mod shops (bqiche).
  5. Co-creation, then a price cut. The Starlight was marketed as co-created with owners, then cut by ¥6,000 two months after deliveries, with only a one-year warranty as compensation. It became a top complaint of 2024 (Jiemian; 12365auto). This is the kind of betrayal that drains an owner community.
  6. Outsourced and rebranded too often. It went 菱菱邦 → LING Club (2021) → 五菱汽车 (2023) in eight years. A vendor ran it and was then sold to Souche. The grassroots identity of the 2016–19 app has no visible successor.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Put the community inside the tool people must open. A car key, service vouchers or a status screen guarantees installs. Wuling reached No. 1 MAU in its category this way.
  2. Design for your actual users' lives, not an aspirational persona: business circles, city boards and a "striver" identity produced real peer help.
  3. Make points real money with a visible ladder. A clear earn rate, owner-only redemptions and a top tier that discounts the next purchase.
  4. Have senior staff listen in person (2,000 sessions a year) and give super-users early access and titles.
  5. Use moments of scarcity or crisis (masks, Covid buying) to bring people into the owned channel, then give them reasons to stay.

Avoid these

  1. Don't monetize the utility path. Ads before unlocking and dealers phoning new sign-ups turn a forced install into resentment (3.3★, 31% one-star).
  2. Don't let data collection outrun consent. A public regulator notice costs trust that a community can't buy back, and a rename doesn't erase it.
  3. Don't brand owners as co-creators and then cut the price on them, and don't let the reliability of the core utility slide while you ship feed features.
