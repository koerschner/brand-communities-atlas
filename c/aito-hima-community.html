TL;DR

  • What it is. AITO (问界) owners, and from late 2023 owners of all of Huawei's HIMA (鸿蒙智行) car brands, gather mainly through two Huawei-published apps: the AITO App (2022) and the HIMA App (December 2023). Both combine car control, charging, shopping and a post-and-comment feed. Around them sit about 950 user service centers that run monthly owner events, owner-run city clubs and WeChat groups, and the big third-party car forums (Baidu Baike, HIMA App; Baidu Baike, 鸿蒙智行). There is almost no community outside China; no AITO subreddit exists.
  • Why people come back. Points that work almost like cash. Ten points equal RMB 1, and they buy Huawei phones, watches and charging, so they resell on Xianyu at about 90% of face value. Owners earn them through referrals (10,000 points a car), sponsored posts and compensation. Add very generous service ("鸿蒙智行大饭店", the "HIMA Grand Restaurant": free meals, car washes and charging at user centers) and the pride of driving "a Huawei car." AITO has topped the Landroads NPS survey of Chinese EV brands several times (83.7 in H1 2024, 82.5 in H2 2025) (21jingji; Autohome thread; Sina).
  • What the company does to feed it. Launch events and constant posting by Richard Yu (余承东; 10M Douyin followers). Monthly "owner usage reports." OTA "early-taste" recruiting. Holiday service drives at hundreds of stores. Model-tier clubs: the M9 "Starlight Club" has capped Gold, Platinum and Black-Gold tiers. Celebrity owner galas. Points paid for posts on Dongchedi, a third-party car platform.
  • Biggest mistake. The community's identity was borrowed from Huawei, and Huawei then took it back. On 15 Sep 2026 AITO's front-end operations moved to Seres. Within ten days the HIMA App had moved AITO's brand entry off its home page, AITO was dropped from HIMA's referral bounty, and owners found themselves with two point currencies that don't mix. Owners joked that overnight they had gone from "Huawei's own son" to "Dongfeng Sokon owners" (Seres's old name). Earlier, in December 2025, HIMA had abruptly banned the free meals and washes its fans loved.
  • The lesson. Points tied to an ecosystem people already value (Huawei devices) and a bounty on every referral can turn owners into a sales force fast. But if belonging rests on a partner's brand, the partner can end it overnight. Owners need an identity, a currency and perks that survive a change of corporate structure.

What the community is

Official digital spaces. Huawei Device Co. publishes both owner apps. The AITO App (iOS id1611515160, live since early 2022) promises "a user sharing platform with posts, private and group chat… the home of official events." It also offers car control, charging and a points mall (App Store). The HIMA App went live on AppGallery on 8 Dec 2023 and launched officially on 12 Dec 2023 with a "user community" among its features (Baidu Baike; IT之家). It now covers all five brands (AITO, Luxeed, Stelato, Maextro, Shangjie). Its feature list includes topics, Q&A answered by an AI assistant, author tags in comments, an owner-benefits page, and a 专属服务群 ("dedicated service group": an in-app group chat with each owner's sales, delivery and service staff) (App Store; China Daily). Since the September 2026 split, Seres runs a separate 问界服务 WeChat mini-program. Its "AITO points" buy only car accessories, and it hosts the M9 Starlight Club (CNMO).

Official physical spaces. HIMA's "user centers" combine sales, delivery and service. By March 2026 there were 1,835 sales stores and 946 service stores (Baidu Baike). They publish monthly event calendars: coffee salons, crafts, camping, horse riding, intangible-heritage study trips (Sina, Nov 2026).

Huawei's fan base as a feeder. Huawei Club (花粉俱乐部; see the huawei-club report) recruits "Huafen" (Huawei fans) to HIMA launch events, for example the Stelato G9 and HIMA launch recruiting list in August 2026 (Huawei Club). At the 2023 Huafen gala, 26 AITO M5 and M7 cars picked fans up from the airport (China Fund News); in 2024 the pickup fleet was M9s and S9s (雷科技).

Unofficial spaces. City owner clubs such as the Nanjing AITO 车友会, which held its second-anniversary event in November 2024 with Seres headquarters staff helping out (新华报业). Nationwide WeChat group networks, one of which advertises 3,000+ members (self-reported; Sohu). Model "circles" on Dongchedi (the 问界M9 circle has 48k members; Dongchedi) and forums on Autohome and xchuxing (新出行). xchuxing's 鸿蒙智行社区 runs weekly activity prizes of its own (xchuxing). On Weibo, the brand account @鸿蒙智行 has 4.84M followers, but the fan super-topic has only 14k posts and 16k followers (Weibo; super-topic). Outside China there is almost nothing: Arctic Shift finds no AITO or HIMA subreddit, and r/Huawei has only 26 AITO posts since 2022.

Origin and strategy

Huawei began selling Seres-built cars in its phone stores in 2021 under its "smart selection" (智选车) model. The AITO brand followed in December 2021, and deliveries began in March 2022 (Sina, Jan 2023). Jiemian describes the logic plainly: buyers learned about the cars at Huawei launches, test-drove and bought them in Huawei stores, and Huawei ran user operations, "so in users' eyes AITO was basically Huawei's car, and consumers bought it for the Huawei brand" (Jiemian). The community strategy therefore borrowed Huawei's 14-year fan apparatus (Huafen, galas, betas, Richard Yu's personal brand) rather than building a car-native identity.

HIMA (鸿蒙智行) replaced "smart selection" in November 2023 so that Huawei could add more partner brands (Chery's Luxeed, BAIC's Stelato, JAC's Maextro, SAIC's Shangjie). Separate HIMA stores and the HIMA App followed in December 2023 (Baidu Baike; 21jingji, 2023). In August 2024 Seres bought 919 AITO trademarks from Huawei for CNY 2.5bn (BYD Today). On 15 Sep 2026 AITO's product definition, marketing, retail and service moved to Seres, with Huawei "empowering" (Tencent News). From 1 Jan 2027 the stores split into AITO-only and four-brand formats (Sina). Seres now says AITO's focus is "whole-lifecycle user operations" for its 1.2M users (Sina, Nov 2026).

The business model is plain: a premium car (HIMA's average transaction price is above RMB 400k) sold by referral and reputation, with points that recycle into Huawei devices.

Reading the trend. Huawei and Seres have never disclosed community metrics such as posts, community DAU or club membership. We found no evidence of any. What the data does show: owner sentiment (NPS) has stayed at the top of the industry since 2024, and app usage was still growing in mid-2026. The fan-built layer, by contrast, is thin: a 16k-follower super-topic against 1.6M cars delivered. Owners consume the brand; they don't build their own spaces around it. The 2026 sales drop and the split have hit the community's identity before any metric captured it.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Points that are almost cash (the core loop). AITO points are worth 10:1 against the RMB and buy "everything in the mall," including Huawei phones and bands, plus charging (Autohome). An owners' guide lists the ways to earn them: 500 points for a "I got my car" or "car diary" post that reaches 60 likes, shares and saves (so owners swap likes in local groups); 100 points for referring a new owner to the charging network; 200 points in a weekly Friday guessing game; 500–3,000 points for featured posts on Dongchedi; and 10,000 points per referred purchase, with a year of free maintenance after two referrals. Because the points buy Huawei devices, owners say HIMA points resell at about 90% of face value, "the best-holding points of any carmaker" (21jingji).
  2. Compensation paid in points. When AITO cut prices in January 2023, it gave first owners of the M5 EV and M7 a warranty extension to 8 years / 160,000 km plus 120,000 points (RMB 12k). Owners of the unchanged M5 range-extender got two years of free maintenance. The press called it "generous," and owners called it "太良心" ("so decent") (Sina; Yiche). It defused the kind of price-cut backlash Tesla and XPeng suffered.
  3. Service as ritual. User centers became "鸿蒙智行大饭店" (the "HIMA Grand Restaurant"): free meals, washes and charging (IT之家). There are recurring campaigns: a monthly service day (first Friday to Sunday) with safety checks and discounts (Sina); holiday "安心行" ("travel with peace of mind") drives at May Day and Spring Festival; and "星夜服务" ("starry-night service"), night servicing with free pickup at 467 centers (Sina). Owners spread these through stories, such as staff driving hundreds of kilometres to change an M9 owner's tyre in Tibet (新华报业).
  4. Status tiers by model. The M9 Starlight Club (July 2026) is open only to first, non-commercial M9 owners. It awards points for purchase, repeat purchase, referral and quality content, with four tiers: Starlight ≥100, Gold ≥160 (capped at 9,000 members), Platinum ≥260 (500) and Black-Gold ≥360 (200). Points reset every 31 December (CNMO). Celebrity owner nights add to the sense of belonging to an elite, such as the Feb 2026 "M9 Starlight Night" with TV hosts and actors as "user representatives" (Autohome).
  5. Getting first. HIMA recruits owners for the first batch of each OTA update (IT之家; Sina). In January 2024, for example, the first 200 owners got mapless city driver-assist (Sohu). This mirrors the Huafen Beta scarcity loop described in the huawei-club report.
  6. Huawei identity. Many owners bought "a Huawei car" and act as Huawei loyalists. Some put Huawei and HarmonyOS badges on their tailgates (Sina). Some issue online challenges, like the M9 owner who promised to pay in full for any car under RMB 10M whose driver-assist beats Huawei's (Sina).
  7. The in-app service group. Each owner gets a group chat with their own staff, which makes the app the place to get help (China Daily). Leaving that group became a public gesture of anger in October 2026 (see Member voice).

The content engine

  • Executive-led launches and social media. Richard Yu is the community's main character. He fronts launch events (at the September 2023 M7 launch he said "遥遥领先", "far, far ahead", five times; BiaNews), posts delivery milestones, and holds 10M Douyin followers. By 2026 commentators saw his superlatives as self-contradicting across five brands (Sina).
  • Monthly owner usage reports. Each month HIMA publishes driver-assist kilometres, voice-assistant wake-ups and active-user shares (October 2026: 1.114bn km of assisted driving; IT之家). These double as collective-pride content.
  • OTA guides and release notes in the app and on Weibo; the summer 2026 OTA had 34 new features across about 2M cars (SMZDM).
  • Event calendars and owner storytelling. User centers post monthly calendars, and HIMA-placed stories frame events as "双向奔赴" ("meeting each other halfway"), for example a camping night with 30+ Henan owner families (Sohu).
  • Owner content, mostly paid and off-platform. Points reward check-in posts and featured posts on Dongchedi, a third-party platform (Autohome). The strategy is less about growing an owned forum than about seeding the public platforms where buyers research. We found no template, guide or creator-certification program comparable to XPeng's.
  • No education layer. Unlike YNAB, there are no structured how-to courses. The app's AI Q&A and service staff handle how-to questions.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. Huawei Device ran the apps and HIMA's user operations. Seres ran service and, since September 2026, all of AITO's front end, with a new "dedicated" joint team (Sina). Product directors hold verified accounts in the app (Baike notes Peng Lei's verification changing from AITO to Stelato in July 2025). We found no public community-team size.
  • Ambassador programs. Referral programs branded "推荐官" ("recommendation officer"), plus the M9 Starlight Club. There is no volunteer moderator or superuser program in evidence.
  • Owner clubs. These are owner-run and supported by Seres or local stores (Nanjing), not formally chartered. We found no national accreditation scheme.
  • Moderation and brand defence. HIMA is aggressive with critics. In March 2025 it reported "hundreds of thousands" of coordinated negative comments and offered RMB 100k–5M rewards for evidence (Baidu Baike). In May 2026 it publicised court wins against car bloggers (RMB 1.5M and 400k) and police detentions of account operators (Sina Finance). Fans see this as protection. It also chills ordinary criticism and keeps the Xiaomi-versus-Huawei fan war going (IT之家).
  • Feedback loop. This mostly runs through the service group, complaints and OTA recruitment. We found no public ideas portal or roadmap voting.

Growth loops

  • Referral bounties. Referral bounties are the main loop: 10,000 points (≈RMB 1,000) per car, capped at 100,000 a quarter in Q4 2026, open even to owners who have only placed a deposit (Sina). The Autohome guide openly advises posting on WeChat and Xiaohongshu, or waiting in stores to sign up buyers and split the points. An unsourced 2026 explainer claims more than 30% of new HIMA buyers came from owner referrals; treat that as an unverified claim (Weibo).
  • Huawei's retail traffic and fan base. From 2021 to 2026 cars sat in Huawei phone stores. Huafen meet-ups doubled as AITO test drives, for example a 2023 Huafen photo outing in AITO cars (Sohu).
  • Seeded third-party content. Points for posts on Dongchedi, plus NPS awards reposted as marketing.
  • Executive virality. Richard Yu's posts and his public back-and-forth with Lei Jun of Xiaomi.

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard third-party data. Landroads NPS (7,800+ owners surveyed across 67 brands in H2 2024) ranked AITO No. 1 in H1 2024, H2 2024 and H2 2025, and its M9 the top model four times running (Global China EV; Tencent). Analysys put AITO's app MAU at +67.8% YoY in June 2026 (36Kr). Deliveries reached 1M in 43 months (Zhihu).
  • Company claims. A HIMA-placed Autohome article credits NPS for "a user brings a batch of users" growth (Autohome). Seres says 1.2M users and lifecycle operations.
  • Counter-evidence. None of this isolates the community. NPS here measures the car, Huawei's driver-assist and the service; the community and points are one input among several. When Huawei's backing weakened in autumn 2026, AITO deliveries halved year on year despite the same points and clubs (product-cycle gaps also contributed; Sina). Score 3: strong third-party satisfaction data, but no retention, churn or referral-share disclosure.

What they did right

  1. A currency people value. Pegging points 10:1 to the RMB and letting them buy Huawei hardware made every activity, from posting to referring to charging, worth doing. The 90% resale value is a sign the currency is real (21jingji).
  2. Paying existing owners first when prices fell. The January 2023 package of points plus warranty turned a price cut into goodwill (Sina).
  3. Service as community glue. Hundreds of stores run the same rituals (service days, holiday drives, night service, hospitality), which gives owners a physical home. The NPS lead is consistent with this.
  4. Borrowing a ready-made fan base. Galas, Huafen recruitment and Huawei retail gave AITO an instant identity and launch audience no start-up could match.
  5. Tiered status for the top buyers. The Starlight Club's scarce tiers (200 Black-Gold seats) build aspiration among the highest-value owners at low cost (CNMO).

What they did wrong

  1. An identity that belonged to the partner. Owners bought "Huawei." After the Seres takeover of operations (15 Sep 2026), AITO's entry left the HIMA App home page (Baidu Baike). Richard Yu said HIMA would "focus on the other four brands" (Sina). Owners called themselves "Dongfeng Sokon owners" (Sina). Some quit service groups and declared themselves anti-fans. AITO's October deliveries fell 54.7% YoY.
  2. Splitting the currency. HIMA's Q4 2026 referral program excluded AITO cars, and AITO activity now pays "AITO points" that buy only car accessories, while HIMA points still buy Huawei devices. "Didn't you say our rights would be unaffected?" (21jingji). The promise covered existing benefits but not the loop that made them valuable.
  3. Taking away a beloved perk without warning. On 16 Dec 2025 HIMA ordered stores to stop advertising the "大饭店," scrub old promotional material and end free meals, washes and charging "to cut dealer costs." Stores were exempted from complaints that followed (IT之家). Nine months later Seres revived it as "问界大饭店," a sign that the cut had been a mistake (Weibo).
  4. Points that reward gaming. Like thresholds pushed owners into like-swapping groups, and referral bounties led to strangers being approached in stores to split points (Autohome). This makes engagement numbers noisy and content thin.
  5. Litigation as community management. Bounties of up to RMB 5M and lawsuits against bloggers protect the brand, but they deepen tribal fan wars. With five brands in one app, even OTA announcements set owners against each other: AITO owners complained when they were left off a push list, and the other four brands' owners complained when AITO was added back (Sina).
  6. Safety crises handled by lawyers first. After the April 2024 Shanxi M7 crash that killed three people, AITO gave a technical explanation and Seres announced legal action against "untrue information" (Guancha). The defence was factual, but its first instinct was to defend the brand rather than reach out to the community.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Make your points worth real money and spendable on things people want. Even better, make them spendable on your wider ecosystem, so the currency keeps customers inside it.
  2. Compensate existing customers before or at the same time as a price cut, and pay in your own currency so the goodwill keeps circulating.
  3. Give the community a physical or ritual home (monthly service days, holiday campaigns, hospitality) run the same way across every location.
  4. Use scarce, capped status tiers for your top customers (200 seats at the top). They cost little and are highly aspirational.
  5. Recruit early adopters for every major update and frame it as a privilege. Owners turn into testers and evangelists.

Avoid these

  1. Don't rent your community's identity from a partner or a celebrity executive. If the partnership changes, members lose who they are. Build a name, rituals and currency that belong to the product.
  2. Never split or devalue the currency while telling members "your rights are unaffected." Members judge the loop, not the legal wording.
  3. Don't give a famous perk and then ban it overnight to save costs. Phase it out with notice, or swap it for something of similar value.

Score notes: retention mechanics 3 (strong cash-like points, referral and service rituals, but a thin owned social layer and no published engagement data); content engine 3 (executive-driven launches, monthly reports and OTA content, but owner content is mostly paid posts on outside platforms and there is no education layer); member ownership 2 (owner clubs exist but are informal; the super-topic is tiny); staff investment 4 (about 950 service stores running coordinated rituals, Huawei's fan machinery, dedicated service groups); business impact evidence 3 (repeated No. 1 NPS from a third party and +67.8% app MAU, but no retention or referral-share disclosure); durability 2 (four years old, and its identity broke apart in the 2026 restructuring). Outcome mixed: a highly effective acquisition and satisfaction engine from 2022 to 2025, whose community identity proved fragile once Huawei stepped back.

  • Will Seres build its own app community, or migrate AITO owners to the 问界服务 mini-program? Will HIMA points remain usable for AITO owners after 2027?
  • Did the December 2025 hospitality cut measurably dent NPS? The H1 2026 Landroads results weren't found.
