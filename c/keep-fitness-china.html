How it pays off for the company

The free courses and social feed were the acquisition and habit engine; the company monetized the audience through a RMB 19/month membership (member-only live classes, plans), paid virtual medal races (booked as online paid content), ads, and own-brand gear and food. Community engagement was meant to cut paid acquisition and raise subscriber retention (avg monthly membership retention 70.8% 2019, 73.3% 2020, 65.3% 2022).

TL;DR

  • What it is. Keep is China's largest fitness app (launched Feb 2015). It runs as a social platform: free follow-along courses, a feed where members post workout check-ins ("Keepers"), friend leaderboards, badges, member-only live classes, paid virtual medal races, and, for a while, offline Keepland studios and city road races. Average MAU peaked at 36.4M in 2022 and fell to 18.6M in H1 2026 (prospectus; TMTPost).
  • Why people came back. Beginners got a free, structured "answer" (put the phone on the floor and follow along). Each workout produced a shareable stat card, a badge, or a leaderboard rank. Influencer courses and live classes built parasocial ties, and from late 2021 collectible IP medals (Sanrio, Crayon Shin-chan, Genshin Impact) gave people a reason to run 5 km for a physical reward they could show off on Xiaohongshu and WeChat Moments.
  • What Keep fed it. A pre-launch community-seeding campaign (the "Burying Mines Plan", 埋雷计划), 44 in-house instructors ("Koaches"), a PUGC creator program that grew from 140 to 17,800 courses, roughly 760 live classes a month, a Fitness Encyclopedia, celebrity courses (Li Xian, Jackson Yee), and a constant stream of themed races.
  • The biggest mistake. Keep treated the community as traffic to monetize rather than a place to protect. Free courses moved behind the paywall, ads appeared inside workouts, auto-renewal complaints piled up (22,500+ on Black Cat by Oct 2023), and the app filled up with shopping. Medals became collectibles you could pay someone to "run" for, so they stopped proving effort. When Liu Genghong's Douyin livestreams pulled 60M followers in a month (April 2022), Keep had no live, social, fun-first format to match.
  • The one lesson. Progress artifacts and a check-in feed drive sharing, but they don't create relationships. Keep never built strong member-to-member ties (it even removed its group check-in squads in 2019), so once it monetized aggressively and a more social format appeared elsewhere, there was little to hold people.

What the community is

Keep (Beijing Calorie Technology, 北京卡路里科技) was founded in 2014 by Wang Ning (王宁), a college student who lost weight by piecing together advice from the internet, and launched on 4 Feb 2015 (Wikipedia; Blake/Lillian Li deep-dive). It listed in Hong Kong on 11 July 2023, raising only HK$300M against a 2021 private valuation of US$2.4B (The China Project).

The company runs all of the community spaces itself, and most of them live inside the app:

  • The Keeper community feed and circles. Posts in text, image, or video, usually auto-generated workout cards (calories, time, the number of times you've done the course, your running route). Members follow friends, influencers, and instructors, DM them, and compete in a weekly ranking of exercise time. Since around 2022 an AI-ranked feed recommends posts and "fitness circles" (prospectus, pp. 156–157). The 2016 rule: you could only post after you'd completed a workout (Lillian Li).
  • Progress layer. KG levels, milestone and record-breaking badges, hidden badges, and friend and regional leaderboards (woshipm, 2023; Zhihu PRD).
  • Virtual sports events (线上赛). Launched in 2018 and taking off in late 2021. You pay an entry fee (usually RMB 19–139), log a run, ride, or rope-skipping session in the app within a time window, and get a physical medal plus a virtual badge (prospectus, p. 16; NBD).
  • Member-only live classes with bullet chat (弹幕), "high fives", real-time in-class rankings, virtual gifting, and product selling (prospectus, p. 160).
  • IRL spaces. Keepland studios (from 2018), a 2022 "selected gyms" partner program, city K-MARS road races (from 2023), and Good Ride cycling festivals (Keep about page; 163/体育大生意).
  • Unofficial spaces. The medal culture moved largely to Xiaohongshu (50,000+ "Keep medal" notes by Feb 2023), Douyin unboxing videos, and the Xianyu resale market (The Paper). Complaints concentrate on Sina's Black Cat (黑猫投诉) complaint platform and Zhihu. Keep's relationship with these spaces is passive. It benefits from the medal posts but doesn't run them. No Reddit presence of note was found (the Arctic Shift search turned up nothing usable).

Status today: still operating and profitable on an adjusted basis (first full-year adjusted profit in 2025). But MAU has roughly halved since 2022, paying members are falling, and the company now calls itself an "AI-driven fitness and health ecosystem" built around an AI coach, "Kaka" (2025 annual results).

Origin and strategy

Cold start through borrowed communities. Before launch, a five-person ops team ran the "Burying Mines Plan": for about a month they posted long, high-quality fitness guides in nearly 100 QQ groups, WeChat groups, BBSes, Baidu Tieba bars, and Douban groups until they became the most active voices there, with the founder lurking in QQ fitness groups too. At launch they revealed that all this advice came from a tool called Keep and posted the link. "One post in a 2,000-person QQ group brought 100+ downloads." iOS downloads reached 40,000+ a day (opp2; Zhihu, Wang Ning). A "Chief Test Officer" beta recruited about 4,000 seed users through Weibo and WeChat, and the official WeChat account had been publishing since Oct 2014 (opp2; woshipm). Keep reached 1M users in 105 days and 100M registered users in Aug 2017 (The Paper; Keep about).

Tool first, community second. An early investor told Wang that a Q&A community with coaches was "too heavy" and that the courses should be the answer (Zhihu). Wang's stated principle was "focused over scattered": be a fitness tool before a community (Lillian Li). The community grew as a by-product of workouts.

Then the "ecosystem" bet. After passing 100M users in 2018, Wang said Keep wanted to become a sports brand with Nike as its benchmark: Keepland for cities, KeepKit hardware for homes, with apparel, content, social features, and data all in one loop (TMTPost). The membership launched in Sept 2018 and reached 1M subscribers in July 2019 (Keep about). The prospectus frames the community as the glue of a "virtuous cycle": content brings in users, users become influencers, and content sells gear (prospectus, p. 155). Champion: Wang Ning throughout. No named community lead was found in public sources.

Trajectory: steep growth from 2015 to 2017. A plateau in engagement followed: usage time per user peaked in 2017 and was back at 2016 levels by mid-2019 (iheima/QuestMobile). COVID brought a second surge (2020–22), and the decline since 2023 has erased about half the MAU.

What keeps people coming back

  1. A free, structured on-ramp for beginners. The core hook was never the feed. It was "don't know how to squat? Put your phone on the floor and follow along" (TMTPost), plus multi-week curriculums and AI-adjusted plans. Member retention peaked in 2020, when lockdowns made the home-workout use case dominant (73.3%, prospectus).
  2. Check-ins as social currency. Every workout makes a ready-to-share card for the feed and WeChat Moments. Product managers describe the early formula as free structured training "with an instant-feedback check-in system and social-currency attributes" (China Youth Daily summary via search). One casual user described the cycle: run occasionally, screenshot it, post to Moments, collect likes: "Keep's task for the year is done" (Sina).
  3. Status and progress. KG levels, record-breaking badges that count each new personal best, weekly friend leaderboards for exercise time, and regional rankings (Zhihu; Zhihu). A 2019 analyst who used the system concluded its effect was "minimal". Motivation mostly came from within, or from friends (Zhihu).
  4. Collectible, limited-time medals. This was the strongest retention and monetization mechanic Keep found. IP themes rotate every 2–6 weeks, so a design is gone once its window closes, which made the races work like blind boxes (The Paper). Medals became gifts ("medals my boyfriend ran to get me" drew 120,000 likes) and couple rituals (The China Project). Keep says paid-race users pushed ARPPU up 30%+ in 2023 (CS.com.cn).
  5. Parasocial ties to instructors and influencers. The prospectus credits influencers with "a strong bond and ... deep emotional connections" that "help increase user retention" (prospectus, p. 189). Celebrity courses gave measurable bumps: while Li Xian's drama aired in 2019, new-user next-day retention rose 3 points. But his HIIT course had 2M followers and only 300–2,000 concurrent exercisers (iheima).
  6. Live-class co-presence. Bullet chat, high fives, real-time rankings, and group-challenge rewards. Interactions rose from 0.9M (Q4 2020) to only 1.3M (Q4 2022) (prospectus), modest next to Douyin livestreams.
  7. Identity. Members are "Keepers", and the slogan "自律给我自由 / Discipline Sets Me Free" greeted users on every launch and, per the prospectus, "unifies our Keeper community" (p. 156). It was refreshed in 2024 under Keep 8.0's "只要动起来" ("just move") positioning (smzdm; SocialBeta).
  8. Switching costs. Years of workout records (14B cumulative records by end-2025) and 22M connected devices (2025 and 2024 results). Users leaving are now told to export their data first: "years of check-ins are your only sunk cost in Keep, and its biggest retention lever" (smzdm).

What was missing was peer accountability. In Aug 2019 Keep removed the "打卡小分队" (group check-in squad) feature and replaced it with a solo "My Goals" (iheima). The same piece reports that Keep tried community-operations conversion with about 200 staff and then disbanded the team (rumor, unverified).

The content engine

  • PGC. Standardized recorded courses shot by 41–44 in-house "Koaches", who go through a formal onboarding program. Live classes are run with directors who can cut the stream, and bullet chat is AI-moderated (prospectus, p. 158). Recorded courses drove about 1.3B workout sessions in 2022.
  • PUGC creator program. Keep mines community posts to find potential influencers (experienced, prolific posters in one discipline), then helps them produce courses, grow a fan base, and monetize, including introductions to advertisers. PUGC courses grew from 140 (2019) to 17,800 (2022), and their sessions from 10M to 771M. Keep also grew in-house stars such as An An Xiaoyu and Zhao Meili and imported outside stars such as Pamela Reif and Zhou Liuye (prospectus, pp. 156, 172; PingWest). "Fat Burning Cardio Dance" sessions went from 5M to 165M in 2019–22.
  • Education. A Fitness Encyclopedia organized by topic (fat loss, posture, running...), mixing PGC articles, free courses, and member posts. Articles and short videos also go out on Weibo and WeChat (prospectus, p. 159).
  • Event content. A steady cadence of themed races: seasonal "时令赛" from 2018, official online editions of the Beijing and Guangzhou marathons from 2016, and IP collaborations from 2021 with Sanrio, Crayon Shin-chan, Conan, Genshin, A-SOUL, Pokémon, and Chinese web novels (Sina/TMTPost; smzdm).
  • AIGC, since 2024–25. Content iteration cycles went "from weekly to daily". Keep reports AI-generated plans complete at rates "comparable" to human-made ones, and says the new free-course pool in Keep 9.0 is AI-assembled from a motion library (2025 results; smzdm).

Member-made content in the feed is mostly check-ins. Critics in 2019 called it low-value "dirty messages", stale (posts weeks old surfacing on the home feed), and weaker than Xiaohongshu's "useful" notes (iheima).

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. 41–44 in-house instructors (2019–22). Total headcount fell from 827 (end-2024) to 632 (mid-2026) (TMTPost). A Nov 2024 layoff of 10–15% hit online, international, and marketing teams (The Paper). In Mar 2024 Keepland operations staff were cut (Sina).
  • Influencer and creator program (see above). There was no member ambassador or superuser program comparable to adidas Runners captains, and no evidence of one was found.
  • Keepland Academy (2023). A coach-training program that claimed 1,000+ coaches onboarded in total (Sina/TMTPost).
  • Rules. Published community norms ban reactionary, pornographic, or pirated content, with temporary or permanent bans and post wipes (Keep policy). Live-class gifting is capped at RMB 50,000 a day (prospectus).
  • Feedback loop. Mostly data-driven: Keep uses interaction data to decide where content goes. Users who complain get boilerplate replies (see the App Store developer response below). No public idea portal was found.

Growth loops

  1. Seeding in borrowed communities. Content first, the product link later (Burying Mines). This is the textbook cold-start story Chinese product managers still cite.
  2. Shareable artifacts. Workout cards and medal photos posted to WeChat Moments, Xiaohongshu, and Douyin. 100,000+ medal-hashtag posts and 10M+ views on Douyin's "Keep medal unboxing" (The China Project). The weakness: "Keep has not yet realized an 'internal loop' for the competitions." The buzz lived on other platforms.
  3. Creators and celebrities with outside fan bases (Pamela Reif, Li Xian, A-SOUL), plus App Store features (featured two weeks after launch).
  4. Paid acquisition. About RMB 1.8B on marketing in 2019–22 for 14.6M net new users, roughly RMB 319 each, versus Bilibili's RMB 148 (The China Project). In mid-2019 Keep spent 40M+ in two months, half of it on in-app activations with low retention (iheima).
  5. IRL races as advertiser products. K-MARS and Good Ride are sold to sponsors (JD Health, McCormick, Uni-President) with prizes, booths, and race-kit sampling (163; The Paper 2024).

Evidence of business impact

Hard evidence (from filings):

  • Average monthly membership retention was 70.8% in 2019, 73.3% in 2020, 71.7% in 2021, and 65.3% in 2022. Keep explicitly linked the 2020 high to lockdowns and the drop to its expanding user base (prospectus).
  • Virtual sports events drove growth in online membership and paid content. Q1 2023 revenue growth was "mainly attributable" to them (prospectus). Online membership and paid content reached RMB 996M in 2023, up 11.4%, "driven by the growth of virtual sports events" (PR Newswire; CS.com.cn). In 2024 revenue fell partly because of "a decrease in online sports events revenue due to the high base effect set by several top-grossing events in 2023" (2024 results).
  • By H1 2026 online membership and paid content had fallen 26.9% to RMB 247M (gross margin 73%), while lower-margin own-brand goods (40.1% margin) rose to 58.5% of revenue (TMTPost).
  • 2025 filings report engagement gains among the users who stayed: DAU workout rate 57.8% (+5.3 pts), DAU next-day retention 41.7% (+2.7 pts), and next-day retention of 69% for the AI data-analysis skill (2025 results).

Company claims and third-party numbers: the "Keep made RMB 500M selling medals" figure came from a Weibo post, and Keep denied it (The Paper). One 2022 cartoon-IP race reportedly drew 1.5M+ paying users and RMB 90M+ GMV (懒熊体育, cited by smzdm; unverified). No filing links the social feed itself causally to retention. Keep only asserts that the community is "highly complementary" (p. 154). Market value fell from about HK$22.2B (Aug 2023) to under HK$0.9B (Aug 2026) (TMTPost).

What they did right

  • Seeding with value before asking for anything. Burying Mines built trust in existing communities and turned staff into opinion leaders before launch, which made the launch spike possible (40,000+ daily iOS downloads, 1M users in 105 days) (opp2).
  • Making the answer the product. Free, standardized follow-along courses for beginners, rather than a Q&A forum. The investor's "too heavy" warning was right, and the tool brought the users (Zhihu).
  • Building sharing into every workout. Auto-generated stat cards and a post-after-workout rule kept the feed tied to real activity and produced 1.1B interactions in 2022.
  • A creator ladder sourced from the community. Keep spotted prolific posters, helped them make courses, and shared monetization. It scaled PUGC 127x in three years, and PUGC delivered 771M sessions (prospectus).
  • Turning effort into a physical, giftable artifact. Medal races converted an abstract habit into something people could hold, gift, and post. They ran with almost no marketing spend, and Keep's filings credit them as the main driver of high-margin revenue growth in 2022–23.
  • Moving IRL in a lighter way when studios failed. K-MARS (20 stops, 60,000+ entries, sponsor-funded) and partner gyms replaced owned studios.

What they did wrong

  • Monetizing the commons. Free courses, then even post-run stretches and goal runs, moved behind the membership. Separate cards came in (member, live "畅练卡", RMB 88/month "Super AI"), ads ran inside workouts and voice runs, and opening-screen ads appeared (The Paper 2026; smzdm; iheima). The cost: a stream of "8-year user, uninstalled" posts, and core runners leaving for Joyrun (悦跑圈) and other free apps. Keep's own claim that only "trend-driven, low-frequency" users left is contradicted by members falling 22% in H1 2026 even as penetration dropped (TMTPost).
  • Billing practices that damaged trust. First month at RMB 9, then auto-renewal at RMB 19; non-refundable race fees with consent pre-ticked; a RMB 49.9/month "Keep 好物" shopping membership charging people who had never opened Keep. That added up to 22,500+ Black Cat complaints by Oct 2023 and more since (Time Weekly; The Paper; smzdm).
  • Medals that stopped meaning effort. Thresholds fell to 1.031 km or 31 jumping jacks, and riding a bike counted as a run. Proxy running ("代跑") cost RMB 5 for 5 km, medals were resold on Xianyu for less than the entry fee, some races required a membership, and medals shipped late or damaged (163/BJ Youth Daily; smzdm). The craze cooled by mid-2023, and The Paper (2026) concluded that "the medal incentive has failed."
  • Bloat that buried the core. Ten tabs by 2019, a feed that "looks like Xiaohongshu" next to a store that "looks like Pinduoduo", and users who couldn't find their own run history (iheima; Sina/TMTPost; App Store review). Keep 9.0 (Apr 2026) folded the store into "Discover", an implicit admission.
  • Missing the live, social moment. When Liu Genghong drew 60M followers in a month (Apr 2022) with fun, interactive livestreams, Keep's founder reportedly didn't even discuss it internally. Keep briefly featured user uploads of his routines, then pulled them overnight, and the users who'd been checking in to them moved to Douyin (Sina). Its creators also multi-homed: Zhou Liuye had 9M followers on Bilibili and 5.3M on Keep (PingWest).
  • Weak peer ties. Keep removed group check-in squads in 2019 and never built captains, teams, or local chapters at scale. The community was a broadcast feed plus leaderboards, which is easy to replace.
  • Expensive offline and side bets. The Keepland Shanghai stores closed after about a year (Apr 2020) because of low traffic and high costs. Beijing stores went from 13 to 7 (2019–22), and in Mar 2024 Keep stopped renewing leases and paused its 9 Guangzhou partner stores (EqualOcean; prospectus; Sina). Other failures: a 2019 food delivery mini-program cited for lacking a physical kitchen (PingWest), and the overseas apps Keep Trainer and Keep Yoga, shut on 30 June 2022 (TMTPost).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Seed by giving, in the communities your users already live in. Have staff write the best answers in existing groups for weeks, then reveal the tool behind them (Burying Mines).
  2. Gate posting on doing the core action, and auto-generate the post. A stat card after every workout keeps the feed tied to real use and makes sharing effortless.
  3. Grow creators from inside the community. Spot prolific members, give them production help and a revenue share, and let their courses carry your content library (PUGC went from 140 to 17,800 courses).
  4. Give milestones a physical, time-boxed, giftable form. Limited-edition medals tied to a real effort window drove the highest-margin growth Keep ever had. Keep the effort requirement meaningful.
  5. Move IRL in a light, sponsor-funded way. Recurring city races or rides that partners pay for, not owned studios.

Avoid these

  1. Don't put a paywall around what your community already treats as its commons. Moving the free courses and basic run tools behind the membership a little at a time made long-time users feel "my Keep is disappearing piece by piece", and the users who left were committed ones, not only casual ones.
  2. Don't let your status artifacts become purchasable. Once a medal can be had for 31 jumping jacks or a RMB 5 proxy run, it no longer signals effort, and the ritual turns into merch.
  3. Don't confuse a feed and leaderboards with a community. Without peer ties (squads, teams, local groups, live co-presence), a more social, entertaining rival such as a Douyin livestream can take your members in a month.

Score notes. Retention mechanics 4: a deep gamification stack and medals that worked, but weak peer ties and 30-day retention below the category median. Content engine 4: very large PGC plus PUGC, live classes, education, and AIGC. Member ownership 2: members post check-ins, but the company owns every space and removed group features. Staff investment 3: strong seeding, instructors, and a creator program, but no community management or ambassador layer, plus repeated cuts. Business impact evidence 4: filing-grade MAU, member, and retention data, and revenue explicitly attributed to virtual events, though no causal link for the feed. Durability 2: half the MAU gone in four years, and a pivot to goods and AI.
