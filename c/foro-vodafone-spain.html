TL;DR

  • What it is: Vodafone España's official customer support forum at foro.vodafone.es. It ran on Lithium from September 2010 until 15 April 2020. Only Vodafone customers who logged in through Mi Vodafone could post; anyone could read. It covered phones, ADSL/fibre, TV, billing and contracts (Contact Center Hub 2012; bandaancha.eu 2020).
  • Why people came back: Mostly because it was a channel that worked. Named staff such as "Pedro", "Lucía" and "Alex_Vodafone" would take a stuck complaint into a private message and fix it, which customers said the phone line didn't do. A small core of volunteers stayed for Héroes Vodafone (from Oct/Nov 2013). It had four "power badges" (speed, wisdom, aim, creation), weekly winners, points redeemable for gifts and bill discounts, a private "Central de Héroes" lounge, and moderator powers for the top tier (Lithys 2014; forum launch post, Oct 2013).
  • What Vodafone fed it: Paid agents with a published service window (Mon–Fri 08:00–22:00, answers in 24–48 h). A community manager wrote about 386 engagement posts a year. There were tutorials, "Píldoras Geek" tech news, contests (smartwatches, concert tickets, PlayStation codes), a 2015 home-page and mobile redesign, and personalised re-engagement emails (Lithys 2016; Lithys 2017).
  • Biggest mistake: It was dismantled in steps. Customers of ONO, bought by Vodafone in 2014, were never allowed to post. From November 2018 staff stopped handling complaints by private message, which removed the forum's main use. Héroes vanished from the menu by December 2019. Then the site was closed with all its threads deleted, in the middle of Spain's first COVID lockdown (forum notice, Feb 2019; bandaancha.eu).
  • The lesson: A support forum that customers value as a backdoor to a human is fragile. When the backdoor closes, the community has nothing left. Build the peer layer, meaning superusers, accepted solutions and an archive, so that it would survive a cut in staff answers. And never delete a decade of indexed fixes.

Correction to the coordinator's first guess: Foro Vodafone was not a Lithy award winner in 2014 or 2017. Vodafone Spain entered the Lithys every year from 2014 to 2017, in about 10 category entries. The named winners in those years were others: Telefónica Spain, Telstra and Fox Sports in 2014; Fitbit, Comcast, Southwest and Post Office UK in 2017 (LiNC 2014 PR; 2017 PR; Post Office). The only related win was "Regional Star: Europe 2015" to Vodafone Group, which credited the group's Lithium communities as a whole (2015 PR). Also, the launch year is 2010, not "the 2010s" in general.

What the community is

Foro Vodafone was Vodafone Spain's owned, vendor-hosted (Lithium, later Khoros) support community. Its 2013 board structure shows what it was for. There were device boards (Android 15,446 posts, iPhone/iPad 8,897, BlackBerry 8,237) and fixed-line boards (ADSL 18,709). The heaviest were account-pain boards: "Contrato – Tarifas y servicios" (32,821), "Contrato – Facturación" (23,277) and "Mi Vodafone" self-service (14,528). The off-topic board had 9,929 posts, and a small "Héroes Vodafone" board had 51 (homepage, Wayback 31 Dec 2013). Later layouts added Vodafone One/TV (HBO, Netflix, TiVo), Fibra ONO, business boards, "Comunicados", "Píldoras Geek" and "Tu Espacio" (Wayback Dec 2018).

Rules: anyone could read, but posting required being a Vodafone customer registered in Mi Vodafone, plus a public nickname (forum post, edited Feb 2019). Staff read every post but did not promise to answer each one. The response target was 48 hours within an 08:00–22:00 window (roams.es summary of the rules).

Related official spaces. In July/August 2013 Vodafone also opened an official, staff-run support board inside ADSLZone, the biggest independent Spanish broadband forum. Initially it was staffed Mon–Thu 9–18 and Fri 9–15 (xuss.es / ADSLZone, Sep 2013). The same staff handle, Alex_Vodafone, pinned the index threads there and ran Héroes on the owned forum. That board still exists. In Nov 2026 its recent threads (2024–2025) had 0–3 replies and no visible staff answers, so it looks orphaned (ADSLZone board). ONO's own official forum closed in Feb 2016, and ADSLZone pointed ONO customers to the Vodafone board there (ADSLZone, Feb 2016).

Unofficial spaces. Spain's telecom conversation happened mostly on independent forums: bandaancha.eu, ADSLZone, HTCMania, GSMSpain and Neeo. It did not happen on Reddit. These forums outlived the official one and are where its closure was reported.

Origin and strategy

  • Launch: "Vodafone España lanzó en Septiembre de 2010 el servicio de Atención a Cliente a través de Foro Vodafone… que tuvo una gran acogida" (Contact Center Hub, 2012, via search snippet; the page itself blocks bots). bandaancha.eu framed it as following "la tendencia iniciada por Movistar" of being reachable through social channels (2020).
  • Business model: Pure cost-to-serve. The 2017 entry spells it out: "Our support community has three goals: call deflection, customer satisfaction and providing an appropriate SLA" (Lithys 2017 Digital CX). The 2015 entry: "Our challenge… was to reduce calls through social media support" (Lithys 2015 CSAT). It also had a listening role. The complaints department received a weekly list of the forum's top search and query terms so it could prepare for the complaints coming that week (Lithys 2015 Support Savings).
  • Organisation: In 2013 Vodafone Spain's community managers sat in External Communications and depended on "Social Support" for care. About 10 people across communications and support worked on social channels, and the forum was "el canal ideal para la gestión de incidencias" (TreceBits interview with Jonathan Jiménez, Mar 2013). Internal champions named in award entries: Zaira Pérez (Specialist, Self Management Channel, 2014), Gustavo Casado (Social Media Specialist, 2015) and Alejandro Moreno (Community Manager, then Social Media Manager, 2016–17). Forum-side handles included Alex_Vodafone, Rafa_Vodafone (Community Manager) and Chelo_Vodafone. At least one community manager, Josep Antoni "Xoxe", came from the agency and Lithium side (xoxe.es CV).

Scale in context. Vodafone Spain had 14.26M mobile and 2.96M fixed broadband customers (Lithys 2016). About 15K new registrations a year is roughly 0.1% of the base, so posters were a tiny minority and readers arriving from search were the real audience. The "over 100K total posts" in the 2016 entry conflicts with the ~258K board total for end-2014. It probably means posts created in 2015, and that would put yearly volume flat to down from 2014's ~80K increase. That is my inference. No member counts were published. No reliable peak-versus-trough series exists after 2015, because later homepages stopped showing counters.

What keeps people coming back

  1. A working escalation channel (the real hook). Customers used the forum to reach a named human who would take the case private and actually fix it. One GSMSpain member wrote that it had been "estupenda" four times (Feb 2015). When a Mi Vodafone login bug locked people out in July 2018, users complained that they had open cases "por privado con dos empleados (Pedro y Lucía)" (ADSLZone, 19 Jul 2018). This is a transactional bond, not a community one.
  2. Héroes Vodafone (gamified superusers), from Oct/Nov 2013. The design is the most transferable part:
    • Entry gate: pass an initial challenge to become Aspirante/Apprentice. Earn one power badge to become Héroe Vodafone, which grants thread-moving rights. Earn all four (speed, wisdom, aim, creation) to become Superhéroe (obsolete how-to, snippet).
    • Cadence: weekly winners per badge. A weekly private message reported each person's "Puntos de Poder". There was a public "Clasificación Héroes Vodafone" leaderboard in the sidebar.
    • Rewards: a points catalogue of goodies, temporary bill discounts (capped at three months), event access, devices, prize draws, priority service in the forum, and the chance to write for Vodafone blogs such as Smartblog and Cooking Ideas (launch post; relaunch post).
    • Belonging: the private Central de Héroes board for heroes to talk with each other and suggest improvements. Community managers followed up one to one every week (Lithys 2014).
    • Status and power: the top tier could moderate or ban users (Lithys 2014). Heroes carried a signature: "El ser Superhéroe Vodafone no implica ser Empleado del Foro."
  3. Personalised lifecycle emails. Vodafone said it sent "personalized emails, depending on the number of registrations, use, frequency, and proficiency of Lithium features" (Lithys 2015). No results were published.
  4. Contests and giveaways for all forum users, such as smartwatches, cinema and music tickets and PlayStation codes. The forum also helped spread a Mi Vodafone app raffle of two Fiat 500s (Lithys 2017).

What it lacked: no evidence of member-led rituals, local meetups, a member identity beyond "Héroe", or any product feedback loop visible to members. The off-topic board (~10K posts by 2013) was the only purely social space.

The content engine

  • Staff knowledge content: a "Knowledge Center with loads of Tutorials and tips" for phones and DSL, OS-update announcements by device (Android, iOS, Windows Phone and BlackBerry update boards), FAQs on TiVo TV and Secure Net, and phishing alerts (Lithys 2014; Wayback 2018 featured posts).
  • Community manager output: 386 posts in 2015 "to engage users" (Lithys 2016). From 2015 there was a deliberate two-track strategy: "support content and tech & geek content (to create interesting content which could go viral…)". This included content pegged to films, series and video games (Lithys 2017). The "Píldoras Geek" board held it.
  • Member content: accepted solutions and kudos made answers reusable. Heroes earned the "creation" badge for useful posts, and the 134 hero posts in the programme's first months were credited with 67K visits, about 500 each (Lithys 2014). The forum invited "techies" to publish articles and device-update news (forum post).
  • Drift: by Dec 2019 the "Post destacados" were promotions such as Vodafone OneNumber, #PlayFaster esports, back-to-school, the Game of Thrones exhibition and "MiercoYES" (Wayback). The forum had become a marketing surface as support was pulled out.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff: employee agents with "_Vodafone" handles (Alex, Rafa, Chelo, Pedro, Lucía and others) and a "Foro_Vodafone" admin account, registered 24 Oct 2011, with 2,339 posts. Service hours were Mon–Fri 08:00–22:00 with an estimated 48-hour reply (Wayback 2018). The 2017 entry claimed a 24-hour SLA, seven days a week (Lithys 2017). By late 2018 the published window was back to weekdays and 48 hours.
  • Superusers: Héroes. About 25 active at any time, more than 100 nominated over three years (Lithys 2016). The programme was relaunched as "nuevo Programa Héroes" around Dec 2014 and refreshed again in 2016–17. The rewards catalogue was marked "(En renovación)" in July 2017 (Wayback board).
  • Moderation: strict and brand-protective. Staff reserved the right to delete content "que puedan dañar la imagen de Vodafone" (roams.es). Members on outside forums repeatedly accused it of deleting complaints (see Member voice).
  • Feedback into the business: the weekly top-terms report to the complaints team (Lithys 2015). No evidence of an ideas portal or product co-creation.

Growth loops

  • SEO: the main loop. Indexed answers drew people searching for a fix. GSMSpain users pointed others to "el propio foro oficial de Vodafone accesible desde su web o haciendo búsqueda en Google" (Jan 2018). Héroes was credited with "+20% SEO" (Lithys 2014).
  • Owned-channel routing: the vodafone.es help menu linked "Consúltanos en Foro Vodafone" (Wayback 2013). Facebook posts pushed the forum (Vodafone España FB).
  • Going where users already were: the official board inside ADSLZone (2013). It was criticised on rival bandaancha.eu as paid placement (bandaancha forum, Sep 2013).
  • No acquisition loop: posting required being an existing customer, so the forum could not convert prospects.

Evidence of business impact

All figures below are self-reported in entries to the platform vendor's award programme, with no stated method:

  • "6,000 interactions on our Lithium eForum per month reduced 50,000 customer service calls"; "2% savings in customer service calls"; "+20 points NPS, +30 points resolution rate, +100 points CHI" (2015).
  • "€3.7 M potentially saved on incoming Customer Call Center Calls" in 2015; "15% increase on NPS and over 90% resolution rate on our Employees contacts" (2016).
  • "40% of visitors surveyed say that they got their problem resolved"; "Unassisted Resolution Rate" up 40%; "one of the biggest self-management customer service forums in Spain" (2017).

Hard evidence: none independent. Vodafone Group's filings don't break out Spain's forum. No academic study was found. The claimed savings (~€3.7M a year) were small against Vodafone Spain's revenue, which sat in the multi-billion-euro range (€3.6B in 2024 per Wikipedia). That likely made the forum easy to cut once cheaper automation arrived: help-centre articles, the TOBi chatbot and WhatsApp (Vodafone press, 2023). The reasoning is mine, an inference rather than a documented cause.

What they did right

  1. A superuser programme with real structure. Clear tiers, four skill-specific badges rather than one post count, weekly cadence, a points economy, a private lounge, real powers, and one-to-one coaching. It produced measurable output from a tiny group: 1,700 posts and 500 solutions in 2015 from about 24 people (Lithys 2016).
  2. Status rewards beyond swag. Bylines on Vodafone blogs and moderator powers cost little and signal trust (launch post).
  3. Turned forum data into an operational early-warning signal for the complaints team (Lithys 2015).
  4. Invested in usability: a click-heatmap-driven home redesign, a mobile version, and direct links to coverage and Mi Vodafone how-tos (Lithys 2016 Design).
  5. Published service hours and a reply target, which set honest expectations (Wayback 2018).

What they did wrong

  1. Built a staff help desk, not a peer community. Most value came from employees resolving cases by private message. When that stopped in Nov 2018, officially for data-protection reasons and "para que se promueva la autogestión y la ayuda entre usuarios", the peer layer was too thin to carry the load (notice; staff signature, 2019). Within 17 months the forum was gone.
  2. Excluded acquired customers. After the 2014 ONO acquisition, ONO subscribers on a Vodafone One bundle still couldn't post. ONO's own forum closed in Feb 2016, leaving them with no official forum at all (Neeo, Feb–May 2016).
  3. Tied forum identity to the billing login. A Mi Vodafone registration failure in July 2018 locked people out of the forum in the middle of open cases (ADSLZone, Jul 2018).
  4. Brand-protective moderation bred distrust. Members called the forum a place where complaints get "censored". Rivals positioned themselves as the independent alternative (bandaancha, Sep 2013; HTCMania thread below).
  5. Let Héroes decay quietly. The "nuevo programa" was relaunched, the catalogue was left "en renovación" in 2017, the claimed interactions fell from 10K to 5K, and the programme was gone from the menu by Dec 2019. No public send-off was found (Wayback; Wayback 2019).
  6. Deleted the archive. "Toda esta información ha dejado de estar accesible en la red", with only a farewell banner pointing to social channels (bandaancha.eu, Jun 2020). Ten years of indexed fixes vanished. The domain no longer even resolves (checked 2026-11-07). ADSLZone summed up the motive as centralising communication in official channels "y tener un mayor control sobre su servicio" (ADSLZone, Feb 2025). The closure came about 14 months after Vodafone Spain agreed restructuring layoffs of 1,000 people in Feb 2019 (El Mundo, Feb 2019), and during the COVID lockdown. That suggests cost-cutting context, not a documented cause.
  7. Left an orphan. The official ADSLZone board still carries Vodafone's "soporte oficial" label with unanswered threads in 2025 (ADSLZone).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Multi-dimensional superuser badges. Reward speed, accuracy, reach and content creation separately, so different kinds of helpers can win, and run a weekly cadence of winners and points statements.
  2. Status that costs little: guest bylines on company channels, moderation powers, a private lounge with staff, and priority support.
  3. Coach your top helpers one to one every week, and pipe "top query terms" to the operations team as an early-warning feed.
  4. Publish service hours and reply targets for staff answers, and keep them.
  5. Design for search arrivals. Most of the value lands on readers who never register, so invest in solved-thread visibility and mobile.

Avoid these

  1. Don't make "private message a staffer" the core value. Members will treat it as a help desk, and the community collapses when the policy, GDPR, or budget changes.
  2. Don't gate participation on a billing account or exclude acquired customers. Every merger and login outage becomes a lockout.
  3. Never delete the archive. Freeze it read-only. A decade of indexed answers is the asset; deleting it hands the audience to independent forums.
