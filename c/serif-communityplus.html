TL;DR

  • What it was: Serif's official support forum for its budget Windows "Plus" software (PagePlus, WebPlus, DrawPlus, PhotoPlus, MoviePlus, CraftArtist). It launched in spring 2013 as a Q&A site with points, replaced the older vBulletin forum (read-only by July 2014), and was shut down and removed around late June 2022.
  • Why people came back: a small core of volunteer experts (Bhikkhu Pesala, Alfred, alansp, RichardS, MJH and others) answered most questions, often "within minutes". Visible PlusPoints, "Good Post" votes and a Top Contributors leaderboard gave them status, and email notifications on followed threads kept them returning.
  • How Serif fed it: staff FAQ articles, video and written tutorials, product-launch and patch announcements, newsletter competitions and customer betas. One QA lead and moderator (Patrick Connor) was present throughout.
  • Biggest mistake: when Serif ended the product line it closed CommunityPlus and removed all its content (about 48k threads and roughly 387k posts, by our estimate), as it had with two earlier forums. That broke continuity for users who still ran the software. Affinity users named it as the precedent in 2025, when Serif made the Affinity forum read-only.
  • The lesson: the volunteers, not the platform, are the asset. When you sunset a community, keep the archive readable and hand people somewhere to go before you close. Serif's superusers had to build their own refuge.

What the community is

CommunityPlus (community.serif.com) was the official customer community of Serif (Europe) Ltd, a Nottingham software company founded in 1987. Serif made low-cost Windows creative software sold direct and at retail: PagePlus (DTP, 1990), DrawPlus (1994), PhotoPlus (1999), WebPlus (2000) and MoviePlus (2003) (Wikipedia: Serif Europe). It was Serif's third-generation community. The first was a custom-built forum. The second was the vBulletin Serif Software Forums (forums.serif.com), active from at least 2008 to 2014. CommunityPlus came third. Alfred, a longtime superuser, recalled in 2025 that each time Serif closed one of these "they took its contents with it" (Alfred's forum, 30 Sep 2025).

Its spaces, per Wayback captures from 2013 to 2022:

  • Forum, with boards per product (CraftArtist, DrawPlus, MoviePlus, PagePlus, PhotoPlus, PhotoStack, WebPlus, Serif Web Hosting, Serif Web Resources, Customer Services, CommunityPlus, Other Products) and thread types Question, Discussion, Suggestion, FAQ and Announcement (forum, Mar 2014).
  • Tutorials (video and written), Lounge (off-topic), People (member directory ranked by PlusPoints), and Articles and Announcements (homepage, Oct 2015).
  • From 2017 it also hosted CraftArtist support after Serif closed DaisyTrail, its separate scrapbooking community, on 11 Aug 2017. Patrick Connor moved DaisyTrail's YouTube tutorials into CommunityPlus (Just Art Community, Aug 2017).

Around 2019 the site was rebranded "Serif Legacy Product Support" and cut down to "Legacy Product Forum" and "Legacy Product Tutorials" (forum, Jun 2019). Status today: defunct. The domain redirects to Affinity's legacy-info page (affin.co/legacy) (Wayback CDX, Feb 2024).

Unofficial spaces. The main one is Alfred's Serif Users' Forums (punster.me/serif). Alfred opened it on 21 Jan 2022, after Serif announced the closure, and Serif itself now points legacy users there (welcome thread; Serif's legacy FAQ). Bhikkhu Pesala, the top contributor, runs softerviews.org, a long-running review and support site for the Plus range (softerviews WebPlus page). Many former DaisyTrail members moved to Just Art Scrapbooking, which gave them their own "DaisyTrailers" section (Just Art, 2019).

Origin and strategy

CommunityPlus went live in spring 2013. The launch article ("The new Community is here!") was "published 1 month ago" in a June 2013 capture. It pitched six benefits: "Immediate answers" (type a question and the site suggests an answer, or "our dedicated experts will be on hand"), "Help users like you", "Expert learning resources", "Earn your PlusPoints" ("other users can reward you with 'PlusPoints' helping build your reputation"), "Create your own identity", and "Worldwide customer services" (launch article, Jun 2013). The early site shared its front page with template-store promotions and PagePlus X7 launch news (homepage, Jun 2013).

The strategy, inferred from that pitch and the site's structure:

  1. Support deflection. Serif sold cheap perpetual licences direct and at retail, with phone support bundled into some SKUs (B&H listing for PagePlus X7 mentions "online support with CommunityPlus, as well as phone support"). Self-service Q&A was the cheapest support channel.
  2. One identity with the store. Unlike vBulletin, CommunityPlus used the Serif customer account: "If you are a Serif Customer... then you already have a CommunityPlus account" (CommunityPlus FAQ). The vBulletin forum, by contrast, warned "You cannot use your existing Serif login" (forums.serif.com, Jan 2014). This tied the community to purchase and registration flows.
  3. Product feedback and QA. It had a Suggestion thread type and per-product wishlists, and it ran customer betas (for example the "PagePlus X9 Customer Beta (19.0.2.22)" in 2017, homepage, Jun 2017). The community's staff champion, Patrick Connor, was listed as "Moderator and Head of QA" (People, Jul 2017).

The strategic pivot. Around March 2016, Executive Chairman Gary Bates posted "Affinity is coming to Windows!" on CommunityPlus. He wrote that "we will not be releasing another major version of any of our 'Plus' range applications" and asked users to "continue with us on this journey and transition to Affinity," with upgrade pricing (thread, captured Nov 2016). From then on, CommunityPlus served a sunset product line. Serif wound down the services around it: Serif Web Resources closed 31 Aug 2018, Serif web hosting on 31 Jul 2019, and the Plus range was withdrawn from sale in late 2019 (softerviews; Serif legacy FAQ).

Trajectory. New threads ran at about 13.5k a year in 2014–15, about 11.8k in 2015–16 and about 7.7k in 2016–17. After the Plus range was frozen they fell to about 2k a year (2017–19) and under 1k a year (2019–22). The peak was 2014–2016, which is also when the old vBulletin crowd migrated over. Registered "People" kept rising to 2017, probably because store customers were auto-provisioned, so that figure is not a measure of engagement.

What keeps people coming back

  • Reputation and leaderboards. PlusPoints came from "Good Post" votes and appeared next to every name. A "Top Contributors" leaderboard sat on every homepage. Its leaders were remarkably stable: Bhikkhu Pesala went from 6,470 points (Jul 2014) to 20,885 (Oct 2015), 36,230 (Jun 2017) and 40,625 (Aug 2018), and Alfred from 7,420 to 33,630 over the same span (2014, 2015, 2017, 2018). Points were the status currency for a group of about ten superusers who answered most questions. On a typical evening in Nov 2016, Bhikkhu Pesala posted the last reply on five of the six newest threads (forum, Nov 2016).
  • Identity carried over. Profiles had avatars, locations and taglines ("Grumpy", "Here just for fun! ;)"). Veterans kept their old names: alansp's tagline reads "Known as alspar on the Old Serif Forum" (People, Jul 2017).
  • Notification hooks. Any thread you posted in went into your "Interests", with email notifications and "continue where you left off". A "CommunityBot" sent status messages (profile FAQ).
  • Belonging off-topic. The Lounge, and early rituals such as "Let's play TAG!!!!" and celebrating the 1,000th member (homepage, Jun 2013), gave regulars a social life beyond tech support. That life later moved intact to Alfred's forum, whose biggest boards include Art & Literature (1,825 posts) and Mathematics & Science (punster.me).
  • Staff presence. Patrick Connor (QA head) ranked in the top ten by points, and answers were flagged "Serif Response". He also delivered the news of Affinity, patch betas and closures in person (Affinity is coming to Windows thread).
  • Switching costs came from knowledge, not the product. Long-lived reference threads did the work: "Registering Serif Legacy Software" had about 70K views and "Contacting Serif" about 136K (forum, Jun 2022). For users still running legacy software, the forum was the only place this knowledge lived, which is why its deletion hurt.

The content engine

  • Staff-written FAQs and how-tos. Locked FAQ threads by "Andy C" (2013) covered registration, product keys, Windows compatibility and unlocking MoviePlus features, and Patrick Connor kept them updated until June 2022 (forum, Nov 2021).
  • Articles and tutorials. Launch articles ("Quick Start (PagePlus X7)", "New Features", "Starting from scratch") and a tutorials section with video and written lessons (homepage, Jun 2013). DaisyTrail's YouTube tutorials were added in 2017.
  • Announcements as a channel. Product launches (PhotoPlus X8, Affinity Photo for Mac), "Community Release" patches (WebPlus X8 16.0.3) and customer betas were announced on the homepage (Oct 2015). So were service shutdowns (SWR closure, 2018).
  • Member showcases and competitions. In 2013 the site ran a "Newsletter Competition" (April 2013 winner: "Allan, from Cumbria... with his neat and minimalist website") and threads like "Please review my new company website" (homepage, Jun 2013). Gallery-style showcases such as the "DrawPlus Art Gallery on CommunityPlus" were reported by softerviews (DrawPlus page). No evidence found that member-made templates or guides were formally rewarded beyond PlusPoints.
  • Member-made reference content lived off-platform too. One example: a WebPlus contact-form guide by regular "Frank B" survived only because users re-shared it (Alfred's forum, 2024).

Cadence was highest around Plus releases (2013–2016) and stopped once the range was frozen. After 2017 almost all new staff content was about shutdowns.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. Visible staff were few: Andy C (FAQ author), "Claire" (launch article), Ashley, and Patrick Connor, "Moderator and Head of QA". Patrick was the long-running face of the community and later staff account #13 on the Affinity forum (People 2017; affinity-forums report). No evidence found of a dedicated community-manager role or team size.
  • Superusers. There was no formal MVP or ambassador programme (no evidence found). Recognition came only through PlusPoints and the leaderboard. Several superusers (Alfred, Ali, Patrick Connor) went on to help seed the Affinity forum.
  • Rules. There was a "CommunityPlus rules and guidelines" FAQ, community flagging, thread merging and a contact address for admins (FAQ list).
  • Feedback loop. Suggestion threads and wishlists, plus customer betas (PagePlus X9 patch betas in 2017, after "staff have returned to the PagePlus X9 codebase"). After 2016 the feedback had nowhere to go: users asked for PagePlus import into Affinity Publisher and were told to "export as PDF" (Affinity is coming to Windows thread). Six years later, one user still noted that "the official response was NO" (Alfred's forum, Oct 2022).

Growth loops

  • Built into the purchase. Every Serif customer account was a CommunityPlus account, and retail SKUs advertised CommunityPlus support (B&H). People grew from 47k (Jul 2014) to 170k (Jul 2017) mainly through this account link.
  • Migration from vBulletin. Serif closed the old forum to posting between late May and early July 2014 and pointed everyone to CommunityPlus (forums.serif.com, Jul 2014). Active vBulletin members had already fallen from 2,058 (Jan 2013) to 861 (Jan 2014).
  • Search and word of mouth. Third-party reviewers sent readers there ("Ask a question on Serif's CommunityPlus support forum and you will often get a reply within minutes", softerviews.org). No evidence found of referral programmes.
  • Bridge to Affinity. The community was used to recruit for Affinity: the Windows beta sign-up and the Affinity Mac launch were both announced there (Oct 2015; 2016 thread).

Evidence of business impact

Hard evidence: none found. Serif was private until Canva bought it in March 2024 (reported at US$380M, Wikipedia). It published no deflection, retention or NPS figures for CommunityPlus.

Indirect signals:

  • Volume: about 48k threads and an estimated 387k replies (2013–2022) were handled mostly by volunteers. The vBulletin predecessor had another ~946k posts (2017 capture).
  • Company claim: Gary Bates said Affinity Designer "has sold five times as many units so far as the last version of DrawPlus" (2016). This is evidence about the product pivot, not the community.
  • Talent transfer: CommunityPlus superusers became core helpers on the Affinity forum (Alfred, profile #8358, is among its most prolific answerers, per the affinity-forums report). That is a plausible but unquantified return on the community.

Business-impact score 1/5: there are no numbers, only volume and claims.

What they did right

  1. Peer-answer Q&A with visible reputation. PlusPoints and the leaderboard sustained a stable top ten for 5+ years, and response times were measured in minutes (softerviews; leaderboards above). Retention mechanics 3/5: simple, but they worked on the people who mattered.
  2. One login with the store. Removing the separate forum account reduced friction and grew registrations about 3.6x in three years (47k → 170k, 2014–2017).
  3. Staff stayed in the room for bad news. The chairman's end-of-range letter was posted in the community with a discussion thread (49 pages of replies), and the QA head answered questions directly (thread).
  4. Kept it running about six years after the last release. Serif kept CommunityPlus going from PagePlus X9 (Nov 2016) to mid-2022, updating FAQs as services closed (Serif legacy FAQ). It also folded DaisyTrail's CraftArtist support in rather than leaving those users with nothing.
  5. Pointed users to the fan-run successor. Serif's official legacy FAQ links to Alfred's forum: "a dedicated member of the Plus range community has created their own unofficial legacy forums" (Serif legacy FAQ). Serif also released a universal registration key (881887) so the software kept working.

What they did wrong

  1. Deleted the archive, three times. The custom forum, the vBulletin forums and then CommunityPlus each disappeared with their content (Alfred, 30 Sep 2025). Alfred set up his site because CommunityPlus would be "removed completely so that posts and attachments became unavailable" (Alfred's forum, 2025). Consequence: years of peer-written answers for software people still use were lost, and the community remembered. When the Affinity forum went read-only in 2025, the first reaction on Alfred's forum was "We've seen this before" and "I wonder how long before they disappear the now read-only forums" (tomH, 30 Sep 2025). Durability 1/5.
  2. Account and data teardown stranded users. Citing a "GDPR exposure review", Serif removed legacy account logins, so customers could no longer look up product keys (Serif legacy FAQ). Legacy questions posted on the Affinity forum are locked and redirected (same; 2025 example). DaisyTrail kits that users had not backed up were lost when that site closed.
  3. Serial platform migrations. Three forum generations in about ten years, each with a new account system, made users start over each time. Before the 2014 cutover, vBulletin's active members had already fallen 58% in a year (2,058 → 861).
  4. No migration path for the work. There was no PagePlus/DrawPlus import into Affinity, and the request was answered with "export as PDF". Some users stayed on the legacy apps (and in the refuge forum) rather than move.
  5. Little ownership for the people who did the work. Superusers earned points but had no formal programme, data export or say in the closure. They rebuilt the community themselves on a phpBB-style site that needs manual email registration (punster.me). Member ownership 2/5.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Make the community account your customer account. Single sign-on with the store removed friction and multiplied registrations.
  2. Give your top ten answerers visible, cumulative status. Points per helpful post plus a homepage leaderboard kept a handful of experts answering for nearly a decade, and a handful is all a support community needs.
  3. Auto-follow threads people post in, and email them updates. Interests and notifications brought helpers back to the next question.
  4. Put staff in the thread when news is bad. The chairman's own letter plus a QA lead answering in the replies kept the discussion civil through a product-line sunset.
  5. Keep a non-support room. The Lounge's social bonds were what survived. The refuge forum's busiest boards today are off-topic.

Avoid these

  1. Never delete the archive when you sunset. Make it read-only and indexable. Deleted knowledge becomes a grievance users cite years later, in this case against a different product.
  2. Don't migrate platforms repeatedly with new accounts. Each move cost Serif active members. Carry identities and reputation across.
  3. Don't leave superusers out of the end-of-life plan. Give them notice, data export and, ideally, an endorsed successor space before closing. Serif endorsed Alfred's forum only after he had built it himself.

Related communities worth adding

  • DaisyTrail — Serif — CraftArtist scrapbooking community with in-app gallery upload (about 2008–2017). Closed with its gallery and kit store, and members moved to Just Art Scrapbooking. A strong example of an in-product UGC community killed with its product.
  • Serif Software Forums (vBulletin) — Serif — predecessor forum: 41k members and 813k posts (2013), closed to posting in 2014, content later removed.
  • Alfred's Serif Users' Forums — fan-run — refuge since Jan 2022, now covering both legacy and Affinity users.
  • Just Art Scrapbooking — independent — absorbed DaisyTrail's community. Useful for studying how communities migrate.
