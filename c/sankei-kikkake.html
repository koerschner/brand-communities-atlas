TL;DR

  • What it is: a free online community that Sankei Shimbun (産経新聞社), Japan's fifth national daily, has run with the community vendor QON (クオン) on QON's Beach platform since 28 Aug 2020. It is built as a "shopping mall": a central plaza (the Kikkake center community) surrounded by "tenant" partner communities that brands and organisations sponsor. Members can move freely between them (launch release).
  • Why people come back: a constant run of prize campaigns (Amazon gift cards, QUO cards, hotel vouchers), points for posting that buy lottery tickets, easy daily questions posted by staff (and now an AI host), and the chance to see your comment printed in the newspaper's monthly "Kikkake page" or read out on a Nippon Broadcasting radio show.
  • What the company feeds it: the in-house "Kikkake reporting team" (きっかけ取材班) of reporters, QON's community managers (the mascot hosts "Kakeru" and "Kiku"), a monthly newspaper page in the national edition, a weekly radio corner, online events, and comment-mining reports sold to sponsors.
  • Biggest mistake: member growth is bought with incentives (sign-up prizes, cash-back from points sites, prize-hunting portals). Partner communities run on fixed sponsorship terms and close when the money stops: 5 closures between Dec 2025 and Jul 2026 alone, which also pulled the public post counter down by ~38,000.
  • The one lesson: "your words become news" (publishing member voices in a mass medium) is a distinctive, cheap and durable reason to post. But if every other reason to come back is a prize, you get a large, shallow audience that is easy to sell and hard to keep.

What the community is

Kikkake ("trigger" or "chance") runs on Beach, a shared platform owned by QON Inc. QON says Beach hosts 2–2.5 million users and 30+ corporate communities (launch release; beach.jp). Signing up requires a Sankei iD, after which members log in with a Beach ID (LP). The parts:

  • Center community (センターコミュニティ): general-interest threads ("Do you ask people things lately?", "How do you notice the seasons changing?"). Staff, the reporting team and an AI host called "Musubo" (ムスボ) open the topics, and members reply (live index, Nov 2026).
  • Partner communities (パートナーコミュニティ): themed spaces "supported by" a sponsor, grouped since Oct 2024 under social-issue headings: "A heart-rich life", "A society where women shine", "Discovering Japan's charm" and "Preparing for a better life". In Nov 2026 the 11 were: Ouchi-de Machi-Chuka (Eat&Foods), Sansupo Talk (Sankei Sports), "Food" Chat Marché (National Supermarket Association), Machi-iku Hiroba (Mori Building), Baseball Fans (Nippon Broadcasting "Show Up Nighter"), B.V.D. Suzu-bra (Fujibo Apparel), Fem Talk (Sankei's Fem Care Project), Gyunyu Sekken Bath Club, Curious-about-the-Countryside (Sankei with the relocation body JOIN), Disaster-prep Kikkake, and Life Plan Hiroba (partner list; 2024 sponsor deck).
  • Off-platform extensions: the monthly "Kikkake page" (きっかけ面) in the Sankei national edition and on sankei.com (tag page), the X account @sankei_kikkake, and the radio corner "Tell Us Your Kikkake" (あなたのきっかけ教えてもらいまショー) on Nippon Broadcasting (Sankei, Aug 2021).
  • Unofficial spaces: no evidence of any fan-run Kikkake space (no subreddit, Discord or fan blog). The only outside chatter is on prize-hunting ("kenshō") and points ("poi-katsu") sites, discussed below.

Origin and strategy

  • Why: it launched in August 2020 under the COVID "new normal" banner, to talk about remote work, online drinking parties, money and children's education. Sankei's media-sales division called it "a third interface after the newspaper's print and web editions" (launch release). The project sits in Sankei's media-sales / media-business division (メディア営業局, later メディアビジネス本部), not the newsroom: its pitch page lives on the ad-sales portal "Sankei Media Labo" (archived 2025).
  • Business model: QON normally builds a single-brand community for each client. Kikkake was billed as "the first fan-community service by a newspaper" and as a mall: Sankei supplies the traffic hub and the media reach, and brands rent a tenant community. QON CEO Takashi Takeda calls "malling" of communities his core innovation: "companies and municipalities sharing fans was unthinkable before" (Shachō Meikan interview, Jun 2024). What sponsors buy: a branded community, staff-run "talk themes", product-review drives (around 100 reviewers), co-occurrence ("共起") text-mining reports and a "community diagnosis", plus optional newspaper ads, sankei.com sponsored articles and events. Budgets range "from a few hundred thousand yen to over ¥10 million" (JOIN/countryside sponsorship deck, Jan 2023; Fem Talk deck, Oct 2024).
  • Positioning: "Social Mass Media No. 1": member conversations are mined by reporters and experts and published as news (Dec 2021 renewal). The Oct 2024 relaunch reframed it as a social-issues community with the slogan "Take Japan one step forward" (ニッポンを一歩先へ) (Sankei, Oct 2024).
  • Context: Sankei's morning circulation fell below 800,000 in June 2025 (798,252, down 51,539 in a year) (RTB Square; MEDIA KOKUSYO). Kikkake is one of several non-subscription revenue lines (events, crowdfunding via "White Canvas", sponsored content), not a subscriber-retention tool.

Readings. Sign-ups grew about 3,000 a month from May 2024 to Apr 2025 (63k to 96k), then slowed to about 1,000 a month in the second half of 2025 and about 750 a month in 2026. The "posts" counter has fallen by ~38,000 since Dec 2025. The drops line up with partner communities closing, whose posts appear to leave the total. Content per member is thin: about 5.5 lifetime posts per registered participant. Individual partner spaces: B.V.D. Suzu-bra had 33,000+ members in Mar 2024 and 34,000+ in May 2024 (Sankei release; Sankei sponsored article), Fem Talk had 20,468 in Jul 2024 (deck), and Countryside had about 13,000 in Jan 2023 (deck). The coordinator's note is right that the counter isn't clearly limited to Kikkake: participant figures can overlap across Beach's other malls.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Prizes, all the time. Almost every partner launch and every anniversary comes with a lottery tied to posting. Examples: QUO cards for posting in a new community (Taiyo Life, Nov 2021); Amazon cards for 100 people (Daiwa Connect, Jul 2023; Sansupo Talk, Jun 2025); ¥30,000 Grand Vista hotel vouchers in the Dec 2021 renewal campaign (thread); and 66 Amazon cards for the 6th anniversary (Sankei, Nov 2026). In Nov 2026 four campaigns were running at once (anniversary, Machi-iku, Countryside, a supermarket "stamp rally").
  2. Cross-community quests. The mall design is enforced through campaign rules: "post in two or more partner communities" to qualify for the bigger prizes (renewal 2021, 3rd anniversary 2023, New Year festival 2026, 6th anniversary 2026) (koubo.jp 3rd-anniversary listing; Sankei, Feb 2026). This is how a center plaza sends traffic to its paying tenants.
  3. Activity points. Members earn points for registering, answering surveys, starting topics, commenting, giving a "feeling stamp" (きもちスタンプ, the clap) and receiving comments or stamps, up to 1,000 a day. Every 100 points buys a lottery entry for web coupons (points page). Earning points when others react to you rewards attracting replies, not just posting.
  4. Recognition by a real newspaper. The reporting team picks comments for the "Kikkake no Tane" feature and the monthly Kikkake page, then announces in the community when they appear. On a Nov 2026 "published in the Sankei" thread, other members replied with congratulations (thread). The radio corner reads out selected posts and sends winners a show sticker (Sankei, Aug 2021).
  5. Low-effort daily prompts. Staff post easy, seasonal, personal questions. A typical Nov 2026 topic drew 43 short anecdotes. Since 2025–26, QON's patented AI host "Musubo" (described in the patent as a topic-auto-generation and engagement-boosting device) also posts topics (QON ikura LP; live index).
  6. Experiences for members. Online events built from member requests (1st anniversary: tuna-butchering lesson, a pâtissier's kitchen, behind-the-scenes at a radio baseball broadcast) (Sankei, Sep 2021); a Tohoku-recovery dinner event for 50 drawn users (deck); a 4-day relocation trial in Hitachinaka for 4 of 35 applicants; a 5th-anniversary disaster-prep event (Aug 2025, X); free talk events and seminars promoted on the tag page.
  7. Co-creation that ships. B.V.D. members voted for a new Suzu-bra colour (387 comments). "Mimosa Yellow" won with more than half the vote and went on sale in Mar 2024 (Sankei release). Members also voted on the Oct 2024 site design (Sankei).

What's missing: no evidence of streaks, levels, member badges, a superuser or ambassador programme, member-run sub-groups of note, or any switching cost. Members have no reason to stay except the next prize and the next prompt.

The content engine

  • Newspaper output: the monthly "Kikkake page" in the Sankei national edition. Reporters and experts analyse comments on that month's seasonal theme and turn them into stories, which are mirrored on sankei.com (Media Labo, archived). In 2026 the tag page shows roughly two items a month: one themed piece (convenience stores, the 30 years of World Cup football, disaster worries, relearning as an adult) plus one campaign or event notice (tag page).
  • Radio: the "Tell Us Your Kikkake" corner on Knights The Radio Show (Nippon Broadcasting, a Fuji Media/Sankei-group sibling). It has run since 2021, with a new "the moment that got me into X" theme every two weeks, and was still on air in Aug 2026 (X post).
  • In-community: staff topics from the hosts "Kakeru & Kiku", "Kikkake reporting team" topics (including a local-bureau team for the Countryside community), sponsor-written topics (e.g. Gyunyu Sekken's monthly "ask the bath expert" videos, thread), interviews with people who relocated, and AI-generated "Musubo" topics.
  • Social: the X account from May 2021, which picks out members' comments (Sankei); an explainer video from 2020 (YouTube).
  • Member-made content is the raw material (comments and review reports), and the reward for it is publication or prizes. There's no evidence of member-created guides, circles of lasting note, or a member-content programme.

People, programs and governance

  • Run by: Sankei (the media-business and event divisions; Kikkake is listed under Sankei's events business, sankei.jp/events) together with QON, whose staff moderate, run campaigns and produce analytics. Team size isn't disclosed. Named personas: the hosts "Kakeru" and "Kiku", the "Kikkake reporting team", and "Musubo" (AI).
  • Moderation: Beach's rules and QON's moderation ("safety, activation, expansion, analysis") (launch release). Points-site referral terms explicitly reject "unrelated, prank or nonsense posts" (Hapitas), a sign that low-quality incentivised posts are a known problem.
  • Feedback loop: feedback flows to sponsors, not to Sankei's own product. Comment mining feeds ad visuals, product colours, municipal pitches and speeches to industry (e.g. Gyunyu Sekken presenting "what gets people to go to public baths" to the bathhouse trade) (2024 deck). Members' wishes for Kikkake itself are collected through anniversary campaigns ("a community for sports other than baseball", "books", "home gardening") (Sankei, Nov 2026).
  • No ambassador, MVP or superuser programme found.

Growth loops

  • Paid and incentivised acquisition: sign-up prizes at launch and at each new partner; listings on prize-hunting portals (koubo.jp); cash-back offers on points sites (Hapitas paid 50–100 pt for "sign up + one comment"; Moppy also listed it) (Hapitas; Moppy).
  • Media cross-promotion: Sankei print and web articles at every launch and campaign, the radio corner, partner media (Halmek's LINE and web channels for the "Grown-up Challenge" community) (Halmek deck, Mar 2025).
  • Sponsor-brought audiences: each partner launch is announced by Sankei, QON and the sponsor through PR wires (PR Times, @Press, Nikkei Compass).
  • Mall cross-traffic: members recruited for one tenant are steered into others by campaign rules. QON's patent "Nearby circles" recommends other groups to members of a group (C&R release, Dec 2025).
  • No evidence of organic word of mouth, SEO from threads, or referrals by members.

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard numbers on Sankei's revenue: none found. No figures for revenue, renewal rate or profit.
  • Sponsor case claims (company and vendor claims, not audited): Fujibo Apparel changed its ad visuals after community text-mining and says "EC site access ×10" and "record monthly sales in June" (Halmek/Kikkake deck, p.8; 2024 deck). JA Zenchu's "Hitokuchi farmer" crowdfunding raised more than ¥1M (2024 deck; White Canvas). The Hitachinaka relocation trial produced 400+ comments on 4 trip reports and a newspaper feature (JOIN deck). Fem Talk began as a pilot under METI's "femtech support" programme (government-funded) (Fem Talk deck).
  • Revealed preference: sponsors renew or leave. The core of the tenant base (Fujibo, the supermarket association, Nippon Broadcasting, Gyunyu Sekken, Eat&Foods, Mori Building) has stayed for 2–5 years. But Dinos (closed 2024-03), Taiyo Life (2025-12), the New Normal community (2026-06), JA Zenchu (2026-07), Shimizu (2026-07, after one year), Halmek's two communities (gone by Nov 2026), "Hotel lovers" and Daiwa Connect (dropped from the list) all left. Closure dates come from the close notices and their siblings for TAIYO-SEIMEI, NEW-NORMAL, KOKUSHO-KOKUSAN and OUCHIGOHAN-DINOS. The tenant count has fallen from 18 to 11 in eight months.

What they did right

  1. "Your words become news" as the core loop. No pure-play community vendor can offer this. Members' comments go into the paper every month, onto the radio every week, and onto X. Congratulation threads show members care (thread). It is also the thing sold to sponsors as "Social Mass Media".
  2. The mall structure solves cold-start for tenants. A new sponsor community starts next to an existing audience instead of from zero, and cross-community campaign rules move traffic there. Takeda says sharing fans "makes both sides livelier" (interview).
  3. Co-creation with visible payoff. The Mimosa Yellow vote went on sale 10 months after the poll, with the community credited in the press (release). Members still mention it in reviews (thread).
  4. Steady rituals. Anniversary campaigns every August/September for six years, a New Year festival, a radio corner every two weeks for five years, and a monthly paper page for five years. This consistency is why the community is still alive six years in.
  5. Packaging community as a sellable media product. Clear price tiers, reports, case studies and options to bundle print and web (decks). Self-sponsored communities (Countryside, Fem Talk, Disaster-prep) let Sankei resell themes to municipalities and companies.

What they did wrong

  1. Extrinsic-motivation dependence. Prizes are the main call to action. Result: the 6th-anniversary thread collected 1,121 mostly one-line replies, many of them "よろしくお願いします" ("hi, nice to meet you"), which shows people are entering a lottery, not joining a conversation (thread, Nov 2026). An outside Beach user advised fellow prize-hunters that "active users may not be that many, so the win rate is far higher than ordinary sweepstakes — new corporate communities are the best target" (mineo forum, Jan 2020).
  2. Paying for sign-ups through points sites brings people whose goal is the cash-back, and inflates the headline "110k users" that is sold to sponsors.
  3. Sponsorship-term communities die on contract dates. Communities close at "13:00" on a set day, and members' posts disappear with them. Shimizu's community lasted exactly a year (2025-07-11 to 2026-07-13). This churn erases member content and has already shrunk the public post counter.
  4. Mission drift. The positioning went from the "new normal" (2020) to "Social Mass Media" (2021) to "social issues / Japan one step forward" (2024), while daily content stayed light lifestyle chat. Members' own wishes are hobby communities (books, gardening, other sports), not social causes (Sankei, Nov 2026).
  5. No link to the core newspaper business. Kikkake's audience (70% women, mostly in their 30s; deck) differs from Sankei's older, male, politically conservative readership. There's no evidence it converts members to subscriptions.
  6. Opaque metrics. The post counter jumped 5× in a year (2022–23) and has fallen since, with no explanation, and participant counts may overlap with Beach's other malls.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Turn members' words into published output. A monthly "best of the community" piece (newsletter, blog, release notes, podcast segment) that names members gives people a status reason to post that costs little and outlasts prizes.
  2. Ask easy, personal, seasonal questions on a fixed cadence. The Kikkake prompts ("Who did you ask something recently?") get dozens of real anecdotes from casual members.
  3. Run co-creation votes and actually ship the result. Credit the community publicly when it lands (Mimosa Yellow, 10 months from poll to shelf).
  4. Use quests that move people across spaces when you have several sub-communities (e.g. "post in two of these spaces this month").
  5. Reward receiving reactions, not just posting (Beach's points for "got a comment" or "got a stamp"). It pushes people toward posts others want to answer.

Avoid these

  1. Buying members with points-site cash-back and lotteries. The member count goes up, conversation quality goes down, and you end up with a metric you can't defend.
  2. Tying a community's life to a sponsor contract or a campaign budget. When it closes, the members' history goes with it and trust in every other space drops. Archive the content and fold the members into a neighbouring space instead.
  3. Changing the mission without changing the experience. Rebranding (new normal, then social mass media, then social issues) doesn't help when members say they want hobbies and peers.

Scores (justification)

  • Retention mechanics 3/5: many hooks (points, quests, anniversaries, publication, radio), but mostly extrinsic, with no status or identity layer.
  • Content engine 4/5: an unusually strong external output (monthly print page, weekly radio, X) plus daily staff and AI prompts.
  • Member ownership 2/5: staff and sponsors set almost all topics; members can't run anything of substance; their content disappears when a space closes.
  • Staff investment 4/5: newspaper reporters, local-bureau teams, QON community managers and analytics, events.
  • Business impact evidence 2/5: only vendor or sponsor case claims (Fujibo ×10 EC traffic); no Sankei financials.
  • Durability 3/5: six years of continuous operation, but member growth is slowing and tenants have shrunk from 18 to 11 in 2026.
