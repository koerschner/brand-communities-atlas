TL;DR

  • What it is: VodafoneZiggo's owned peer-support forum, launched as the Ziggo Community in 2014 (on inSided), moved to Khoros in January 2021, and merged with the Vodafone NL forum in August 2025. It ran on a few dozen to a few hundred very heavy volunteer posters plus a paid moderator and webcare team, and since June 2026 it has been open to customers only.
  • Why people come back: the core is a small group of power users (one has more than 86,000 posts) kept in place by status: levels, about 60 badges, an invite-only "Super Expert" / "T.E.A.M." tier with a private board, early product news, priority access to product tests, and free events at HQ, the Ziggo Dome and Ziggo Sport. Ordinary customers mostly arrive once, through Google or the chatbot, with an outage or billing problem.
  • What the company feeds it: a staffed community team (full- and part-time moderators drawn from webcare), a product owner and an "innovation team" (2018), a Kennisbank with staff- and member-written articles, quarterly webinars with internal specialists, an annual Community Experience Day, and a Testpanel that VodafoneZiggo says delivers about 90% of its Jira issue tickets and decided the go/no-go for Mediabox Next.
  • Biggest mistake: letting a visible, privileged inner circle act as unofficial enforcers. "Super Experts" lecturing complaining customers led to press coverage from 2022 to 2026 ("superklanten", a "headache dossier"), a long run of title changes and reversals, critics quietly disappearing after the 2025 merger, and finally closing the forum to non-customers, which throws away its value as a shop window for prospects.
  • The one lesson: a support community's superusers save real money (the company claims €5.3M in a year), but if they look like company defenders with a back room, every closed board and every badge becomes evidence against you. Make recognition transparent, keep enforcement with staff, and keep staff visibly present.

What the community is

The Vodafone & Ziggo Community (community.vodafoneziggo.nl; community.ziggo.nl redirects there) is the official customer forum of VodafoneZiggo, the Dutch cable, fixed-line and mobile operator owned 50/50 by Liberty Global and Vodafone. It has three main areas:

  • Support Forum. Boards for Internet, TV & Radio, Ziggo GO, Subscription, Fixed calling, Devices, Mobile services and Prepaid, plus "Producttips & Updates", "Over de Community", Offtopic, the Lounge and a "Weekoverzicht" (weekly overview) (Lounge page navigation, Mar 2026).
  • Testpanel. Customers and employees test modems, apps and firmware in closed groups, most recently Ziggo service over DELTA Fiber's fibre network (Testpanel).
  • Events. Webinars, offline meet-ups and the annual Community Experience Day (Events).

Staff appear as "Community Moderator" with the VodafoneZiggo logo. Some work on the community full time; others split their time with webcare, chat, mail or phone support. At least one moderator now covers each weekend day (moderators topic).

Unofficial spaces. Independent fan forums predate the official one: Ziggo-Gebruikers / ziggo.gebruikers.eu and Chelloo (UPC), which merged into Ziggoforum.nl in 2017. VodafoneZiggo webcare is active on them, and Totaal TV noted that Ziggoforum.nl "is independent, but Ziggo's webcare is active on it" (Totaal TV, Mar 31 2017). One of today's moderators says he had been a Ziggo webcare agent on Twitter, Facebook and those two fan forums since July 2009 (team intro, 2021, search snippet). Tweakers' Gathering of Tweakers and Providerforum.nl are the other independent venues. Reddit hardly matters: r/ziggo has 44 posts in total (Arctic Shift, accessed Nov 2026).

Origin and strategy

  • 2014 launch, customers only. Ziggo soft-launched the forum for several months and then announced it to all subscribers in a newsletter in September 2014. Login required a "Mijn Ziggo" account, "so the forum is only accessible to Ziggo customers", and "Ziggo employees are present on the forum daily" (Totaal TV, Sep 4 2014). The first Wayback capture (Feb 2015) shows 1,557 topics, 13,053 replies and 4,231 members on inSided (Wayback, Feb 5 2015).
  • Stated strategy. VodafoneZiggo's 2022 Khoros Kudos Awards entry (contact: Arakou Khader, "Product Owner | Community") is the only detailed primary account. It frames the community under the "Go Digital" strategic pillar (Khoros Atlas entry, Jun 2022, via Wayback):
    1. Self-service and deflection. The community took peer support for "topics that did not fall into our service level" (for example a Sonos speaker's Wi-Fi) and became an escalation route "when more traditional services failed". Its content is fed into the website and chatbot, and the entry claims "5.3 million euro in cost deflection in 2021". The inSided vendor described the same loop in 2019: Ziggo's website chat and Facebook Messenger served a 10-step Chromecast answer written by a customer with 235 accepted answers, using machine learning to pick community answers (Wieger Aarts, LinkedIn, Mar 18 2019).
    2. Product feedback. From 2016 active members were brought into go-to-market work, which brought market research in house. Mediabox Next was "tested since 2019 by more than 1,200 customers", which the entry ties to "the highest product NPS in our company"; community input is "crucial for the GO/NO GO decision"; and "the Ziggo Community delivers roughly 90% of the issue tickets that arise from Jira projects."
    3. Platform investment. An innovation team was formed in 2018. In 2021 Ziggo left its "previous vendor" (inSided), whose customization limits "forced us to create complex work-arounds", for Khoros.
    4. Employee knowledge. A private Wi-Fi "Community of Experts" (guild) for staff was piloted with 70 employees and launched in February 2022. The entry claims 20+ articles and 25+ ideas in four months and a 6% higher first-time-fix rate for the "WiFi crew".
  • Mergers shaped it. When UPC was folded into Ziggo in 2015, UPC Community users were invited to join Ziggo's forum with their Mijn UPC login from April 13 (UPC Community page, search snippet). The Vodafone NL forum (forum.vodafone.nl, archived since January 2010, Wayback CDX) was merged in August 2025, and accounts, topics and likes were combined into community.vodafoneziggo.nl (merger announcement, Jul 7 2025; post-merger tour, Aug 2025).

Trajectory: steep growth from 2014 to 2020, helped by the UPC merger and COVID ("since corona digital channels matter more than ever", Telecompaper, Jan 28 2021). A peak of about 200k+ posts a year ran from 2020 to 2022, then a drop of roughly 40% to about 125k a year from 2024 on, which even the Vodafone merger did not reverse. The message-ID method is an estimate: Khoros IDs may also count blog, Kennisbank and private test-group posts. The decline matches Ziggo's falling customer base (Totaal TV commenters cite a loss of about 90,000 internet customers in 2025; Totaal TV, Mar 25 2026) and less moderator presence.

What keeps people coming back

Two populations behave very differently.

1. Drive-by customers (most visitors). They arrive with a broken modem, a TV-box problem or a billing dispute, often from Google or because the chatbot pointed them there. Many treat the forum as the most reliable way to reach a human: "here you have a fixed point of contact with Ziggo," a level-21 member tells a newcomer (Oct 2026). A moderator often takes the case to private messages ("a moderator will come by" notices were public until 2025; Totaal TV, Sep 30 2025). Retention here is incidental: people come back when the service breaks again.

2. The regulars (dozens to low hundreds). What brings them back:

  • Status ladder. Members start as "Nieuwkomer" and level up through replies, likes given, accepted solutions and "community age". The thresholds are deliberately secret ("to keep the system a bit mysterious… so nobody can game their way up"), each level-up arrives as a private message, and level 8 automatically added "Expert" (levels explainer, Dec 2022). In December 2022 about 60 badges were added (logins, likes, birthday, 1/10/100 best answers) (ranks & badges, Dec 20 2022). The old guide called badges "digital flippos" for building "your own hall of fame" (Zo werkt de Community).
  • The inner circle. Super Experts wore an orange crown and later a different icon. Perks: "access to a private community area", "close contact with the moderators", "news about Ziggo's product developments", a say in community developments, "priority for product tests" and invitations to special user events. Selection followed the T.E.A.M. values (Toewijding, Enthousiasme, Aanwezigheid, Meewerken: dedication, enthusiasm, presence, cooperation) and required 3, later 6, months of activity; members applied through a form (Super Experts, Jan 2021 via Wayback; Alles over het T.E.A.M.). Totaal TV adds embargoed (NDA) product information plus dinners and outings (Totaal TV, Oct 11 2022).
  • The Lounge (Mar 2026). A closed group "for and by active community members" at level 6 and up. Members apply and are reviewed within two working days, a rejection always comes with a reason, and email invitations went out to higher-level members. It is pitched as an online version of "our offline events: informal, substantive" (Alles over de Lounge, Mar 16 2026).
  • IRL rituals. Events run every quarter in three formats: webinars with technical specialists (four a year), a "Spotlight" interview built from member questions, and offline meet-ups (a museum, a server room). The annual Community Experience Day in November is held at a VodafoneZiggo site, usually Utrecht HQ (Events 2025). Recent outings include Ziggo Sport (Mar 19 2026), a Ziggo Dome tour (autumn 2026), the technicians' training centre in Hoogeveen, and Experience Day on Nov 12 2026 (Events). One veteran recalls winning "Member of the Month" in 2017 and "a VIP outing to Guus Meeuwis for 4 people" (Dec 2022).
  • Insider product access. The Testpanel means trying a new modem or Mediabox before release, inside closed groups with product specialists.
  • Social belonging. Offtopic banter, nicknames, and a sense of guarding "our" forum against "Ziggo haters". One loyalist says Ziggo customers like "a corner of their own where they aren't bothered by the noise of Ziggo haters" (Totaal TV comments, Jun 2026).

Switching costs: low for customers. Some power users even stay after leaving Ziggo. One helper has not been a customer since 2009 but still answers questions, and another former customer says he joined Ziggo because of the community (Jun 2026 thread). The June 2026 customers-only rule cuts exactly these people off.

The content engine

  • Kennisbank / Kennisgids. Information, instruction and video topics "made by the Ziggo team and Ziggo users". Active members can write their own articles, and there is a support-video library (Telecompaper, Jan 2021; welcome post, 2021). Examples include tested how-tos such as "ESPN in Ultra HD" and "3 tips for the best picture on Mediabox Next" (2021).
  • Producttips & Updates. The staff blog, filterable by Vodafone or Ziggo, plus "Leestips van de redactie" (editor's picks) pinned on every board (merger tour, Aug 2025). The top staff "Nieuw op de Community" thread had 1,389 posts and 127K views (Mar 2026 homepage snapshot).
  • Webinars and Spotlight sessions. Quarterly sessions with internal experts on Priority loyalty perks, the DELTA Fiber project, crisis management, Customer Impact Management, data security, social media care and the product-test team (Events). They show the company to members, not product how-to to the mass market.
  • Weekoverzicht (weekly digest category) and a monthly community newsletter (the 2022 Super Experts page offered a monthly newsletter signup).
  • Member content. In practice this is mostly answers: one customer's Chromecast guide fed Ziggo's chatbot (LinkedIn, 2019). Members also produce test reports in the Testpanel and long troubleshooting threads; a modem thread by MR_CHIP had 2,073 posts and 273K views (Mar 2026 snapshot). No evidence was found of a formal member-content reward beyond badges, levels and event invitations.

People, programs and governance

  • Team. A Community team of moderators (some full time, some sharing time with webcare, chat, mail and phone), product-test moderators, developers who maintain the Khoros skin, a Product Owner Community, and the 2018 innovation team (moderators topic; Khoros entry). No headcount was published. The 2021 team-intro topic has since been moved to a hidden "Zolder" (attic) board.
  • Superuser program history. Ranks (Ontdekker, Liefhebber, Raadgever, Expert) plus the elected/invited Super Experts (crown) ran until 2022. In Dec 2022 ranks became levels, Expert became automatic at level 8, and Super Expert kept a separate icon. The colourful levels drew "an enormous amount of feedback" and the colours were toned down within a month. A February 2023 poll asked members to rename Expert and Super Expert (ranks thread), and Super Experts became T.E.A.M. members. A closed Experts area around 2022 "was no success and disappeared quickly" (members RoWi and Bert, Mar 2026). By September 2026 all such titles had been stripped, leaving only levels (Totaal TV, Oct 18 2026). Totaal TV calls this "a removal for show", because the closed Lounge remains.
  • Rules. House rules ban politics, religion and sexuality, and violations risk a ban (huisregels). In February 2024 four core values were added (respect, cooperation, integrity, positivity) because "some discussions get quite heated", with an explicit line that rules apply "regardless of your level, expertise or experience" (Dit is de Community, Feb 15 2024). Edits are marked "Moderator: Aangepast wegens [reden]" (edited because of a stated reason). There is an AI-text rule: use AI as a tool, but avoid fully AI-generated posts.
  • Feedback into product. The Testpanel and closed test groups, plus Jira tickets raised from community findings (the company's ~90% claim). A Testpanel customer survey has run each year (2024, 2025).

Growth loops

  • SEO and chatbot reuse. The main acquisition loop for readers: forum answers rank in Google, and community content was wired into ziggo.nl, the chatbot and Messenger. Colours mark Vodafone or Ziggo pages "if you land on a topic via Google" (Aug 2025).
  • Newsletters and IVR-style redirects. The launch was promoted through the subscriber newsletter (2014), and ziggo.nl's customer-service pages push the community (ziggo.nl).
  • Prospect conversion, now switched off. Members describe joining a provider after reading its community ("through the community I became a customer years ago"). Since June 2026 non-customers can read but can no longer register or post, and former customers must ask to keep access (Totaal TV, Oct 2026; thread).
  • No referral or ambassador-acquisition program found.

Evidence of business impact

  • Company claims (unaudited, from an awards entry): €5.3M in cost deflection in 2021; ~90% of Jira issue tickets from the community; 1,200+ Mediabox Next testers since 2019, linked to the company's highest product NPS; community input in the go/no-go launch decision; 6% higher first-time fix for the staff Wi-Fi guild (Khoros entry, 2022). The entry did not win; Three Ireland won that category (Khoros press release, Sep 29 2022).
  • Hard, independent evidence: none on retention or churn. VodafoneZiggo is a private JV and files nothing with the SEC, and no academic study was found. The proxies are the volume table above and accepted solutions (about 2,400 between Aug 2025 and Mar 2026, roughly 4,000 a year).
  • Reputational cost (qualitative): recurring negative trade-press coverage (Totaal TV/DPG: Oct 2022, Sep 2025, Mar 2026, Jun 2026, Oct 2026). The October 2026 piece argues that "a community is also a shop window" and that how customers treat each other there can change a prospect's mind.

What they did right

  1. Treated the community as infrastructure, not marketing. Dedicated product ownership, an innovation team, a platform migration when the old vendor limited them (2021), and content syndicated into the chatbot and website. The result is roughly 700k posts of indexed answers by 2021 and a credible deflection claim.
  2. Built a real co-creation channel. The Testpanel gives members something more meaningful than badges (early hardware and a voice in launches) and gives the company in-house research. VodafoneZiggo says it replaced outsourced market research, and the community tested DELTA Fiber modems "from day one" (Testpanel).
  3. Steady IRL and behind-the-scenes programming. A predictable quarterly cadence and an annual flagship day at HQ, using the brand's own assets (Ziggo Dome, Ziggo Sport) as rewards that cost little and that money can't buy.
  4. Listened on gamification details. It rolled back the garish level colours within a month and let members vote on new tier names (Jan–Feb 2023).
  5. Survived two mergers without losing history. The UPC users in 2015 and the full Vodafone account, topic and like merge in 2025 kept continuity for regulars ("everything under one roof", Aug 2025 replies).

What they did wrong

  1. A visible, privileged inner circle that polices other customers. Crowned Super Experts, a private board, NDA briefings and dinners. Critics read this as company-aligned volunteers defending Ziggo, and some veterans agreed: "we notice plenty of friction when people respond too critically about Ziggo's policy" (RoWi, Oct 2022). Totaal TV (Oct 2026) says fewer than ten regulars "openly lecture" complaining customers, wave the house rules and signal that continued criticism "is not desirable". Consequence: four years of negative press, and the community stands as an example of provider forums becoming a "headache dossier".
  2. Churning the status system again and again. Ranks became levels (2022), Super Experts became T.E.A.M. (2023), a closed Experts area came and went (around 2022), titles were removed (2025–26), and the closed Lounge arrived (2026). Each change reopened the "two classes of customer" fight ("Is this a new way to get things behind closed doors?", Mar 2026). Consequence: the company lost credibility without gaining equality.
  3. Opaque moderation and critics' disappearance. Members documented posts removed with and without public notice, unevenly (Oct 2022). A May 2025 thread complained of an "unreasonably burdensome moderation regime" (search snippet). After the August 2025 merger "more than ten" critical former members were no longer present (Totaal TV, Sep 30 2025). Reactions to the Lounge launch were deleted for "not contributing to a positive atmosphere" (Lounge FAQ replies).
  4. Shrinking staff presence. Totaal TV reports waits of "more than three days" before a moderator notices a question, often followed by a thin reply, with resolution moved out of public view (Oct 2026). A member: "moderator presence has already declined again… the days when you got better help on a community are about two years behind us" (Jun 2026). Consequence: the vacuum is what lets superusers "take power".
  5. Closing the door to non-customers (Jun 2026), with no announcement. The login option some ex-customers used vanished "without any communication" (account thread, Jun 27 2026). This cut prospects, recent switchers and long-time helpers who are not customers, at a time when Ziggo was losing customers. It is the only Dutch provider to do so (Totaal TV).
  6. Gamification that rewards volume. Members say levels track post count and likes ("chat a lot and start topics about nothing and you'll rise automatically", Sep 2026), and one veteran calls the badges addictive by design.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Wire community answers into every self-service surface (help center, chatbot, Messenger, search). The deflection value comes from reuse, not from the forum's own traffic.
  2. Give superusers meaningful insider work, not just titles. A test panel with closed groups, early hardware and real influence on launch decisions binds experts and produces product value.
  3. Run a predictable event cadence that uses the brand's own assets. Quarterly webinars with internal specialists, behind-the-scenes visits, and one annual flagship day at HQ.
  4. Version the reputation system with members. Roll back fast when a change annoys people and let members name the tiers.
  5. Keep history intact through migrations and mergers. Carry over accounts, posts and likes so the regulars' status survives.

Avoid these

  1. Don't let volunteers enforce the rules or defend the company in public. Keep moderation visibly staff-owned and staff-present (response-time targets, weekend cover), or the superusers fill the vacuum and become the story.
  2. Don't create hidden back rooms for an elite while claiming every customer is equal. If you need a private space, make the entry criteria public, put members' entry in their own hands, and publish what goes on there. Otherwise each new closed board reignites distrust.
  3. Don't close your support community to prospects and ex-customers without saying so. A support forum is also a shop window and a source of conversions, and quietly cutting off non-customers removes your most loyal helpers and your best social proof.

Score rationale

  • Retention mechanics 3/5: a full kit (levels, about 60 badges, an elite tier, the Lounge, the Testpanel, events) keeps a small core of regulars, but members themselves criticise the gamification and ordinary customers have little reason to stay.
  • Content engine 3/5: a staff and member Kennisbank, the Producttips blog and quarterly webinars are solid but modest. Most of the value is Q&A threads reused in the chatbot.
  • Member ownership 2/5: volunteers write most answers, but governance is top-down and opaque (secret level thresholds, invite-only tiers, unexplained removals, a rule change made without notice).
  • Staff investment 4/5: a dedicated team with a product owner, an innovation team since 2018, Testpanel moderators, two platform migrations and a yearly events program, offset by reports of slower moderator response in 2025–26.
  • Business impact evidence 3/5: specific figures (€5.3M deflection, ~90% of Jira tickets, Mediabox Next NPS), but only from a company awards entry, with no independent data on retention.
  • Durability 4/5: 12 years through two vendors and two mergers and still active, though posting volume is about 40% below its 2020–22 peak.
