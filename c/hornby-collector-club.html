TL;DR

  • What it is: two linked spaces. The Hornby Collector Club started in 1997 (taking over from the "Team Hornby Club"; Hornby's first member scheme, the 1928 Hornby Railway Company, had 12,000 members in its first year). It was a paid annual membership with a magazine called The Collector, a free club locomotive each year, members-only limited editions and a discount. The Hornby forum has been on hornby.com since at least 2009 and has been part of the multi-brand Hornby Hobbies Community (an Invision forum) since 2022–24. Both are still active, but the club has been cut back hard.
  • Why people came back: the yearly collecting cycle. A new dated pin badge and 0-4-0 locomotive every year, numbered limited editions only members could buy (for example Locomotion No. 1, 730 units), the January range launch, and four magazines a year. On the forum, volunteer experts answer DCC, TT:120 and repair questions, and the yearly Wishlist threads give members something to argue about.
  • What Hornby fed it: a magazine running since 1997 (issue 141 in Feb 2025), the fortnightly Engine Shed blog written by the developers (from ~2015), live Q&As on YouTube with the brand MD and head of development at each range launch, two TV series on the Yesterday channel, and a virtual Members' Day (2023).
  • The biggest mistake: downgrading without telling members why, and leaving the forum to volunteers. The free club locomotive went in 2024, then the discount, the printed magazine and the membership card in January 2025 ("free, digital-only"), with the digital magazine already months behind. Moderators said for years that "Hornby do not read the Forum", and the forum administrator left in 2020. Members of the enthusiast community talk on RMweb, which Hornby doesn't own.
  • The lesson: an identity ritual you can collect (a dated badge or object, a members-only edition) gives a hobby club unusual staying power. But once the club becomes a line item in a cost-cutting plan, without a staff voice explaining the trade-offs, members feel the downgrade more than the free entry price.

What the community is

Official spaces

  • Hornby Collector Club (uk.hornby.com/community/hornby-club). Run by Hornby Hobbies Ltd in Margate. Its terms now describe a free, 12-month, adults-only membership giving "four digital issues per year" of the club magazine, access to "select Club-exclusive models", free entry to The WonderWorks attraction in Margate, competitions, and "occasional member-only offers" [1]. Print copies are sold separately through a third party (Publishing Works), and Hornby says it has "no involvement or responsibility" for them [1]. Hornby can revoke membership if a member resells club exclusives or shares member content [1].
  • Hornby Hobbies Community (community.hornbyhobbies.com). One Invision forum with Hornby, Scalextric, Airfix and Pocher sections. The Hornby section has General Discussion, HM|DCC (HM7000), TT:120, DCC, RailMaster, International, The Engine Shed, Live Steam, Thomas, Wishlists, Hornby Collector Club, and Forum & Website Feedback [2]. Volunteer "Community Moderators" run it day to day [3][4].
  • Sibling clubs: the Hornby TT:120 Club (launched Oct 2022, paid £30 from Feb 2023, free and digital since 2025) [5][6]. The Airfix Club is still paid in 2026 [7]. The Scalextric Club has been archived, and Scalextric was sold in March 2026 [2][8].
  • Content and loyalty around them: the Engine Shed blog [9], the Hornby Model Railways YouTube channel [10], the Hornby: A Model World TV series [11], and the Hobby Rewards points programme (Nov 2022) [12].

Unofficial spaces that matter more

  • RMweb, the dominant UK railway-modelling forum (run by the publisher of British Railway Modelling), has its own busy Hornby sub-forum on the same Invision software [13]. Hornby's range launches, club exclusives and the free-club change were all argued over there [14].
  • The Hornby Railway Collectors' Association (founded 1969, 2,000+ members) is an independent club for vintage Hornby, with exhibitions and an AGM [15].
  • Hornby Magazine is not made by Hornby. Key Publishing licenses the name (it was launched by Ian Allan in 2007) [16].
  • Reddit hardly matters. r/Hornby has 4 subscribers, though r/modeltrains carries 100+ Hornby-titled posts a year, mostly repair questions (Arctic Shift, Jun 2024–May 2025) [17].

Origin and strategy

Hornby has run member schemes for almost a century. The 1928 Hornby Railway Company reached 12,000 members by the end of its first year, alongside Meccano Magazine [18]. The modern club dates from 1997, when "the 'Team Hornby Club' was replaced with 'The Hornby Collector Club', which included a bi-monthly magazine" [19].

The business logic changed over time:

  1. The collector economy (1997–2018). In 2008 the club cost £23 (£27 Europe, £29 rest of world) and "thousands of members" got The Collector six times a year, two issues of The Collector Plus, a welcome pack and a "fully working Hornby locomotive, exclusive to club members" [20]. Notably, it also pushed members towards independent shops: a "Collectors Club Discount Locator" listed stockists giving 10% off to members [20]. By 2010 the fee was £24.50, and the 10% applied at hornby.com too [21].
  2. Rebuilding collector trust (2018–2022). After years of losses, the 2018 annual report (chairman/CEO Lyndon Davies' turnaround) said discounting had wrecked collector trust. If a collector who paid full price on release day sees "the item on sale for half price a few months after release… they probably won't buy other products from that brand at the time of release again". Davies argued that "if you destroy the trust in the brand and collectors no longer see the products as a store of value, they will switch" [22]. The club, with its scarce members-only editions sold on hornby.com, fits that thesis. It was relaunched for 2019 at £28 with the 10% discount restored [23].
  3. D2C and data (2022–). In 2023, under the new CEO Oliver Raeburn, the focus moved to the website and loyalty data: digital revenue grew 49% and direct-to-consumer reached about 15.5% of sales [24]. Hobby Rewards, "a loyalty currency", signed up 36,000 customers, and "spend from Hobby Rewards members is c2.5 times greater than that of non-members" [24]. The annual report doesn't mention the Collector Club at all. That suggests why it was later made free and folded into loyalty and CRM.
  4. Cost-cutting and break-up (2024–2026). In FY24 revenue was £56.2m and the loss £11.4m [25]. Headcount cuts were expected to save £1.5m a year [26]. Hornby left AIM in April 2025 under Castelnau Group (54.9% holder) [27], sold Scalextric for £20m in March 2026 [8], and Raeburn's directorship ended on 30 June 2026 [28]. The free digital club launched in January 2025, in the middle of this.

No internal community champion is named in any source. The visible people are Simon Kohler (marketing and development director, the face of range launches and TT:120) [29], the developers who write the Engine Shed [9], and later the brand MD "Martyn" and head of development "Carl" on the live Q&As [30].

Club membership numbers have never been published; "thousands" (2008) is the only figure found. Forum activity between the two 2025 snapshots: the Hornby sub-forums added roughly 11k posts in 16 weeks. That's an estimate of about 35k posts a year, mostly TT:120 and HM7000 digital-control help. The Collector Club, Wishlists and Engine Shed sub-forums barely moved [2][36]. A returning member wrote in March 2025: "There seems to be little recent activity on the general discussion forum" [38]. Trajectory: the club peaked as a paid, collectible product between the 2000s and 2023. The forum is now a fairly busy support board for new product lines, with social energy moving to RMweb.

What keeps people coming back

  • Things you can collect for each year of membership. Every year brought a dated pin badge and an exclusive 0-4-0 club locomotive (the 2008 Blue Pullman Class 06, the 2010 BR 0-4-0, R3953 in 2021, R30088 in 2023) [20][21][31][39]. Members catalogue them: one 2018 thread asks for "a comprehensive listing of all the models" [40], and an RMweb thread running since 2014 ranks them [14]. When the locomotive disappeared in 2024, members noticed straight away: "There used to be some variant of an 0-4-0, but not this year. Sadly." [41]
  • Scarce members-only editions. These include the P2 Thane of Fife revealed at the 2023 virtual Members' Day [42], Coronation 46220 [14], and Locomotion No. 1 (730 units, hornby.com only, one per member) [33]. These are the main reason serious collectors pay, and the terms say exclusives may be limited per member [1].
  • The release calendar. The January range launch, quarterly magazines (January, April, July, October) [32], and the fortnightly Engine Shed give members a steady rhythm of things to look forward to.
  • Discount and points. A 10% member discount from 2008 to 2023, plus flash events such as 20% off on hornby.com from 31 May to 5 June 2022 [43]. Later, a 15% bonus on Hobby Rewards points for club members, which was quietly dropped by July 2024 [44].
  • Peer help and status on the forum. Volunteer moderators and long-standing experts answer DCC, wiring and servicing questions. When regulars vanished after the 2024 platform change, members missed them by name: "TC and Paul… brought some valuable electrical knowledge" [45]. Invision ranks and badges split opinion: "I do not wish to be ranked higher than anyone" [46].
  • Wishlists as a yearly ritual. "2026 Wishlist" and "2027 Wishlist" threads run for pages, and Hornby promises posts "will be passed to our Development team" [2][47]. On YouTube, Hornby reads out "Dear Hornby" letters to Santa [48].
  • Ties to the people who make the models. Developers write the Engine Shed, and the live range-launch Q&As put the brand MD and head of development in front of fans [9][30]. Two TV series showed the design team at work [11].
  • Switching costs: low. The only lock-ins were the collection of yearly badges and locomotives and access to exclusives. The free club removed the sunk-cost hook.

People, programs and governance

  • Moderation is volunteer-run. Moderators describe themselves as "the Community Moderators". In early 2020 they posted that "Adam the forum administrator has left Hornby to seek new pastures", which delayed fixes [3]. In February 2024, brand managers gave the moderators "formal guided tours" of the new platform [52]. An "Official Hornby Hobbies" account posts announcements [53], and "Hornby Technical Services" now posts in General Discussion [54].
  • Hornby didn't listen. In January 2020 a member wrote: "It has been pointed out many times by the Moderators that Hornby do not read the Forum. I think if I were running Hornby I would want to know what people are saying about the Company especially on my own website" [4]. In 2009 an outside forum complained that on Hornby's forum "any hint of praise for others or a hint of criticism of Hornby appears to be silently deleted" [55]. Back then, the official forum said a questionnaire was "the only way to suggest new products" [56].
  • Feedback channels today: Wishlist sub-forums ("passed to our Development team throughout the year") [2], occasional outreach such as RMweb's "Hornby Hobbies – 'We would love to hear from you!'" thread (Feb 2022) [57], and range-launch Q&As [30]. No public evidence links a forum wishlist to a product decision.
  • No ambassador, MVP or superuser programme was found apart from volunteer moderation. Hornby's community recognition goes to retailers instead (the Model Shop of the Year awards, first held in 2025) [58].

Growth loops

  • Retailer loop (2008–10): the club discount was honoured at participating independent shops, so the club sent people into stores [20][21].
  • Launch loop: TT:120 started as a web exclusive with a club offering 15% off the new range. The first wave of sets "all but sold out in a matter of weeks" [24][59]. The TT:120 sub-forum became the fastest-growing part of the forum [2][36].
  • TV and attraction loop: A Model World and the Margate visitor centre (opened 2010, reopened as The WonderWorks in Oct 2023) bring in casual fans, and club members get free entry [1][60].
  • SEO and support: forum threads rank on Google for repair and DCC questions. That's a support-deflection asset, not a social one.
  • Lowering the barrier: the free club (2025) aims to maximise sign-ups and CRM reach. No sign-up figures have been released.

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence (company-reported): Hobby Rewards had 36,000 sign-ups by mid-2023, and members spent about 2.5 times as much as non-members [24]. That shows correlation, not causation: loyal customers sign up. D2C was about 15.5% of sales with digital revenue up 49% [24].
  • Vendor case study (treat with caution): Antavo reports 2.87 average purchase frequency, 58% of points redeemed, 13% more transactions in 2024, and 59% of members "likely to recommend" [61].
  • The club itself: no revenue, retention or membership figures appear in any annual report found. The 2018 report's argument about collector trust is the only strategic link [22].
  • Indirect: the January 2025 email said the free club followed "discussions with valued Club members, alongside running industry insights" [62]. No data was given.

What they did right

  1. Rituals you can collect. A dated badge and locomotive each year, plus numbered members-only editions, turned membership into a collection. Members still catalogue and trade them years later [14][40].
  2. Behind-the-scenes content from the people who build the product. The Engine Shed, written by developers fortnightly since about 2015, and live Q&As with the head of development use exactly what enthusiasts want: early CAD renders and samples [9][30].
  3. A magazine that lasted. The Collector ran from 1997 to at least issue 141 in 2025 [19][35]. Few brand clubs keep a publication going for 28 years.
  4. Protecting collector value as strategy. The 2018 annual report spelled out the link between scarcity, full-price launch buying and trust [22]. Club exclusives sold only on hornby.com put that into practice [33].
  5. Using the club to launch new lines. The TT:120 Club and its 15% discount turned early adopters into a forum community that drove most of the forum's 2025 growth [2][36][59].

What they did wrong

  1. Cutting benefits one by one, with little explanation. The free locomotive went (2024) [41]. The Rewards bonus disappeared without notice ("it seems that the 15% bonus to Rewards Points is gone?") [44]. The discount, print magazine and card were dropped in January 2025, framed as eco-friendly ("helping to reduce our environmental impact") [62]. A TT:120 member had already written "£30 pa for nothing!" in 2023 [5]. Result: the club is now a free email list, and its sub-forum stalled at ~3.5k posts [2][36].
  2. Service failures that hit the most loyal members. Membership packs and activation took weeks to arrive or never came [63][64]. Magazines arrived late [49]. The 2019 magazine PDFs vanished from the members' area [65]. The new free club launched with the latest downloadable issue still Spring 2024 [34].
  3. The forum was left to volunteers, and Hornby didn't listen. Moderators said "Hornby do not read the Forum" [4]. The administrator left [3]. Criticism was reportedly deleted (2009) [55]. Customer-service complaints keep coming back ("Does Hornby Customer Services Exist?", 2023) [66].
  4. Repeated platform migrations. The forum was switched off during a website rebuild ("by the time they do switch it on again most of the regulars will have given up") [67]. Another "useless and unworkable" change came in 2011 [68], the February 2024 multi-brand rebuild ("The 'new' forums are now even worse than before") [52], and a June 2026 update that hid the shop and rewards links [69]. Each one lost regulars [45].
  5. No international plan after Brexit. Overseas joins and renewals were suspended in 2021 because customs charges on the free locomotive and badge fell on members [31].
  6. Corporate churn. CEO changes (2023, 2026), delisting, the Scalextric sale and a reported break-up of the group [8][27][28][70] leave the community without a steady sponsor.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Give each membership year a dated, collectible token (a badge, an object, a numbered edition) so staying a member builds a visible collection.
  2. Reserve real scarcity for members (limited, one per member, sold only through your own channel). It rewards loyalty and protects full-price launch buying.
  3. Put your builders on camera and in print regularly. A fortnightly developer blog and a live Q&A at launch beat polished marketing for enthusiasts.
  4. Spin up a dedicated club for each new product line. TT:120's club and forum gave early adopters somewhere to gather and quickly became the most active area.
  5. Keep a printed or downloadable publication going for decades. Back issues become a reference library that keeps members around.

Avoid these

  1. Don't take perks away one at a time without a plain explanation. Each cut reads as a broken promise. Say what's changing, why, and what members get instead.
  2. Don't leave an official forum to volunteers with no staff listening. If "the company doesn't read this" becomes common knowledge, the energy moves to an independent forum you don't control.
  3. Don't re-platform without bringing your regulars along. Every migration here cost expert members.
