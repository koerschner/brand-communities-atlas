TL;DR

  • What it is: Naim Audio's own forum, started in the late 1990s by founder Julian Vereker and now run on Discourse at community.naimaudio.com. It has 18,052 accounts, 1.78 million posts and 5.4 million likes, with about 2,600 people visiting each month and about 1,130 each day. That is an unusually sticky 44% daily-to-monthly ratio for a hardware brand's forum (about.json, 8 Nov 2026).
  • Why people come back: member-run rituals. Every year a new "What are you listening to and why might anyone be interested?" thread fills with 28,000 to 39,000 posts. There is a daily Wordle thread (38,719 posts) and yearly "System Pics" threads, and almost half of all posts are in the off-topic Lounge and the Music Room. Members stay for the friends and the shared hobby rather than for product support.
  • What the company does: very little content, but steady staffing. Richard Dane, a former Naim export sales manager who now freelances for the company, has moderated the forum for decades and acts as the link to R&D and support. Staff post product and firmware announcements and run numbered feedback megathreads, and a lottery picks 8 members for each factory visit in Salisbury, two or three times a year since 2025.
  • Biggest mistakes: heavy, opaque moderation (deleted critical threads and bans, a long-running complaint on rival forums) and a weak product and IT backbone. The October 2024 firmware update set off "Unhappy with the new NAIM update" threads with more than 8,500 posts and 140,000 views. The website relaunch was poorly received, and the forum archive went offline in 2025 because "it's something managed in France."
  • The lesson: forums that last are built around habits and identity (my system, my music, my mates), not around the product. A small, trusted staff liaison who is visibly connected to R&D does more than a content team. But tight control over criticism pushes the most critical owners to independent forums, where the brand can't answer them.

What the community is

The Naim Forum is Naim Audio's owned discussion board. Naim is a British high-end audio maker founded in Salisbury in 1973 (50th-anniversary thread). The forum is organised into "rooms" that have barely changed since at least 2014: Hi-Fi Corner (17,293 topics / 630,564 posts), Music Room (2,470 / 399,679), Lounge (3,151 / 375,197; called the "Padded Cell" until about 2021), Streaming Audio (9,093 / 223,482), Home Theatre (510 / 12,243), and a FAQ room (categories.json, 8 Nov 2026; 2014 snapshot of the old forum). Hi-Fi Corner welcomes "any and all hifi, regardless of age or brand", but bans company reps, unauthorised commercial links and for-sale posts (Hi-Fi Corner).

Official spaces: community.naimaudio.com (Discourse, live since 15 Jan 2019; the site was created 10 Oct 2018 per about.json), and forums.naimaudio.com, a static, searchable archive of the previous forum (archive). The Freshdesk support portal also has a "Forums" tab (support.naimaudio.com), but it looked empty when checked. Unofficial spaces: Pink Fish Media, the independent UK hi-fi forum where critics of Naim gather; the Naim Audio Owners & Want To Be Owners Club Facebook group (link); the Naim category on the Roon Labs Community (link); and Reddit. Reddit barely matters: r/naimaudio was created in January 2026 and the Arctic Shift archive shows only 3 posts, while r/naim is a 338-post subreddit unrelated to Naim (Arctic Shift). Status: active and busy every hour, but slowly declining from its 2021 and 2025 peaks (see below).

Origin and strategy

  • Founder's hobby, late 1990s. Brand ambassador Jason Gould told SoundStage! Simplifi in April 2025 that the forum "dates back to the late 1990s". Julian Vereker "had this love of computers… he thought it would be really cool to be in touch with people who like music, good hi-fi, and Naim Audio in particular. So he started the forum in that vein" (SoundStage! Simplifi, Apr 2025). One early member recalls that the first version was hosted on CompuServe and started with four members (archive thread, 12 Jan 2019). Vereker's own posts survive from at least April 1998 (quoted in a 2008 archive thread). He died in 2000.
  • Four platforms. The forum has run on (1) CompuServe and an early web board, (2) a second web version, (3) Hoop.la (Social Strata, hosted in the US) until January 2019, and (4) self-controlled Discourse since then. Dane admits that "it couldn't be done", meaning nothing was archived, "for the first and second iterations of the forum, although at least for the first one, Julian's contributions were saved" (Aug 2025).
  • The 2019 relaunch was a management decision. Managing director Charlie Henderson (MD 2018–2021, per Wikipedia) announced it as "Evolving the Naim Forum". He called the forum "one of the foundations of our world" and "the heart of our community", and promised mobile access, image posting and polls that "we intend to use… intensively as a feedback tool" (announcement, 10 Jan 2019). Dane gave the practical reasons: more back-end control, no longer hosted in the US, a chance to clean out dead accounts, and lower running costs, "meaning more budget can be spent on the moderators" (17 Jan 2019).
  • Business model link. Naim sells expensive components through dealers, and customers move up an upgrade path (Nait → Supernait → separates → 500 series). The forum supports that path with advice from other owners. It also serves as an early-warning and feedback channel: Gould says forum discussion about Qobuz, which Naim's old streaming card couldn't support, was "partly" why Naim moved to a new streaming module with over-the-air updates in the New Classic range. He also says forum threads helped diagnose a UK ISP router that dropped streamers (SoundStage! Simplifi). Naim's turnover was £39m in 2021 and £41.8m in 2022 (Wikipedia).
  • Ownership changes. Naim merged with Focal in 2011 (later VerVent Audio Group). Barco completed its purchase of VerVent for about €135m in August 2026 (Barco; StereoNET). Members now see Focal-era central IT in things like the website and the archive hosting, and complain about it (see "What they did wrong").

Trajectory. The forum grew fast after the 2019 relaunch and through lockdown. Activity peaked around January 2021 at roughly 3,250 monthly actives and 24k posts a month, then held at about that level through January 2025. (The January 2025 figures were inflated by the firmware row; see below.) Since then it has fallen about 20% on monthly actives and about 40% on posts. New-topic creation is down about 40% from 2019 (363 to 211 per 30 days), and sign-ups are now about 3 a day. Engagement among those who remain is still very high: DAU/MAU is about 44%, and 45% of monthly visitors post. The ranks show a small core: Discourse trust levels have been granted to 38 "Leaders", 160 "Regulars", 2,777 "Members" and 12,492 "Basic" users (badges.json). These are estimates from public Discourse counters. Some 2025–26 Wayback pages mislabel the column headers, so I read them as 24 hours / 7 days / 30 days.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Year-long ritual threads. Each year a new "What are you listening to in 20XX and why might anyone be interested?" thread opens on 31 December, continuing a tradition that ran to "Vol. XIV" on the old forum by 2018 (2018 snapshot). Each one gets 23k–39k posts and around 190k–300k views. There are matching yearly System Pics threads (5k–8k posts each; 2021) and "Pics from the Listening Position" (476k views). Annual resets give people a fresh start and a calendar milestone without losing continuity.
  2. Daily micro-habits. "Anyone 'Wordled' yet?" (since Jan 2022, 38,719 posts) is now the single longest thread. Others include "Things I've done in the last 24 hours" and "Which wine are you drinking?" (10.8k). These give people a reason to visit every day that has nothing to do with hi-fi, which explains the 44% DAU/MAU.
  3. An off-topic home. The Lounge and Music Room hold about 775k of the 1.64M category posts, roughly 47%. The biggest threads include "Best jokes" (18,168 posts, 908k views), "Show us your pets" (11k), "Cricket" (11k) and "Your Watch & Naim" (8.4k). The company kept the off-topic room through two migrations and renamed it from "Padded Cell" to "Lounge" because of mental-health connotations after Covid (Dane, Apr 2022).
  4. Identity through owning the gear and through long tenure. Members define themselves by their "system" (signature lines, system pics, "Show us your Sondek"). They wear their joining dates with pride: "I joined 17th March 2001… I bought my first Naim equipment… directly from… Julian himself… Round about 1976" (archive), or "January the 5th it will be my 20 years here" (Dec 2024). Owners of the NAIT 50 limited edition (1,973 units) built a 10,641-post thread (link).
  5. Status systems. The old forum ranked members by points ("Community Rank #142"). Henderson joked that "to some, their Community Ranking is much more important to them than is strictly healthy" (archive). Discourse replaced it with trust levels and badges: 20,914 Anniversary badges and 178,359 "Nice Reply" badges (badges.json). Old ranks were not migrated, and some members mourned their history ("all previous members have to start from scratch", HiFi Kabin).
  6. A personal tie to the moderator. Members talk about "Richard" as a person: "If you lived next door, I'd take you out for a few pints tonight" (Jan 2023). He is also the route into the factory.
  7. Scarce access. The forum factory visit is limited to 8 people chosen "from a hat". The 2025 call drew 297 replies to gauge interest and 234 replies to sign up (interest, summer 2025, spring 2026, summer 2026).
  8. Switching costs. Two decades of peer knowledge about Naim's DIN cables, power supplies, legacy streamers and network quirks live here and in the archive. Users treat the archive as a manual: "I learnt a lot from digging old posts daily!" (Aug 2025).

The content engine

Company-made content is thin. Staff post launch announcements, such as the NAIT 50 and the Focal & Naim app 6.0 (496 posts), and numbered feedback-and-requests megathreads. For example, "Naim Internet Radio – Feedback & Requests" reached Part 15 in January 2026, each part reposting the original launch text (link). Dane also opens feedback threads for corporate changes, like the April 2024 website relaunch, which drew 518 posts in a week. The FAQ room has only 31 topics, and the old FAQ is just linked to the archive (2023). Naim does have a YouTube "How To Videos" playlist (link) and its own record label, Naim Records, founded in 1993 (Naim). I found no evidence that either is systematically fed into the forum, and no evidence of webinars, a newsletter built for the community, or AMAs with engineers.

Members make nearly all the content: listening logs, system photos, cable and switch experiments ("Ethernet Switch and Cables Mania", 7.3k posts), polls (for example a 739-post poll on whether the 2024 firmware improved sound), show reports (Audio Show and Event Reports, 2026) and troubleshooting. The company curates through moderation and pinned topics rather than rewards. Recognition is mostly informal, plus Discourse badges.

Score: content_engine 2/5. The forum is a strong UGC engine, but Naim itself publishes little beyond announcements and megathreads.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. The admins and moderators listed in September 2026 were Richard.Dane, Paul.B, Stevesky (titled "Naim Staff"), Tom, roland.brewer and NaimMarketing, plus admins "Paul", "angelac" and NaimAdmin (Wayback /about). Dane is "once Naim's export sales manager, who now works freelance from his home in Kent, but commutes to Naim's headquarters in Salisbury for regular meetings with R&D and sales". He has "contacts in the building so he doesn't have to wait until Monday morning". Before launches, Naim brings Dane and the five-person tech-support team in for two- to three-day briefings (SoundStage! Simplifi). The site's contact email is at an outside agency domain (mindbyte.co.uk) (about.json), so it is probably hosted by an agency (an inference).
  • No formal ambassador or MVP program. I found no evidence of one. Trust Level 4 "Leader" (38 users) is the closest thing, and veteran members do a lot of support work.
  • Rules. English only, "the best place for definitive advice… is from your Naim dealer or Naim HQ", no reps, and no buy/sell posts (forum rules, Jan 2019). Bans have a long history. One was "at Les's request", concerning Avondale, a maker of upgrades for Naim gear (Pink Fish Media, Feb 2019).
  • Feedback loop. It runs through Dane's meetings, staff drop-ins (e.g. Stevesky explaining why Naim won't roll back firmware, Oct 2024), feedback megathreads, and invited listening tests. One member took his ND555 to the factory "for listening tests" during the firmware dispute (May 2025).
  • Score: staff_investment 3/5. One long-serving liaison with real access, plus some staff, factory days and a paid platform. There is no dedicated community team and no content budget.

Growth loops

  • Search traffic from 1.78M posts and an indexed archive. Long-tail queries ("NDX2 and Supernait", router dropouts) land on forum threads. Feedspot lists Hi-Fi Corner among the top hi-fi forums (Feedspot, 2026).
  • Upgrade advice that becomes word of mouth. "In [six months I] moved from Myryad… to NDX2/SN3… thanks to 3 excellent Naim dealers and these excellent forums" (Nov 2019).
  • Joining at the point of purchase. Many members say they registered when buying, for example "at the time of buying an ND5XS" (archive).
  • A weak loop now. Sign-ups have fallen to about 83–153 per month in 2025–26, and the Arctic Shift archive and searches show the brand has almost no Reddit presence, so younger buyers have little way to find the forum.

Evidence of business impact

  • Company claims only. The forum helped shape the choice of streaming module (Qobuz capacity, OTA updates). It surfaced a UK router issue that was then pushed to dealers. Users answer each other "at midnight" on weekends (SoundStage! Simplifi). Henderson called it "the heart of our community" (2019).
  • No hard numbers. I found no retention, NPS or revenue figures linked to the forum, and VerVent and Barco filings don't mention it. Naim is not SEC-listed, and Barco's acquisition release is about portfolio, not community. Score: business_impact_evidence 2/5.

What they did right

  1. They kept a single owned home for 28 years and preserved its history. In 2019 they archived the Hoop.la forum instead of deleting it. Members contrasted this with Linn, which closed its forum: "A big thanks! An example for Linn" (Jan 2019); "I think the Scottish guys made a mistake getting rid of theirs" (archive). durability 5/5.
  2. They let the community be about life, not just the product. Wordle, jokes, wine, pets and cricket drive daily habit, which explains the 44% DAU/MAU. retention_mechanics 4/5.
  3. They staffed it with a connected human, not a bot or an agency. Dane's link to R&D, and the pre-launch briefings, mean answers are "the right answers rather than making something up" (Gould).
  4. They used physical access as a recovery tool. After the 2024 firmware row, the 2025 factory visit gave "some priority to those who have suffered with performance issues after the Firmware update", at the request of a senior executive ("Cedric", probably site director Cédric Magnaud) (May 2025). The visits then became a recurring series.
  5. They gave anger somewhere to go. "Happy" and "Unhappy" update threads stayed open for months, and members credited the moderator's restraint: "showing some great restraint in not using your moderator's magic powers" (Dec 2024).
  6. They chose a better platform when moving. Members praised Discourse in 2019: "the first forum upgrade I've seen anywhere for a while that's worthy of the word" (Jan 2019).

What they did wrong

  1. Heavy-handed, opaque moderation. Rival forums have complained about it for years: "the moderation on the Naim forum is pretty heavy-handed" and "Naim have their own dedicated forum for circle jerking and back slapping… the mods will delete anything nasty" (Pink Fish Media, Dec 2023). Another: "I have also had many posts and threads deleted over the years" (PFM, Apr 2024). Other examples: a member's thread about Naim leaving VerVent was removed (PFM, Aug 2024), and a website-critique thread was pulled (PFM, Apr 2024). In April 2026 a banned member publicly accused the moderator of bias against women on the What Hi-Fi forum (What Hi-Fi). That is an unverified allegation, but it is visible. Consequence: critical owners argue on independent forums where Naim can't respond, and the forum gets an "echo chamber" reputation that may deter buyers who are still deciding.
  2. Firmware and app changes landed badly. The October 2024 streamer update produced two "Unhappy with the new NAIM update" threads with 2,801 and 5,753 posts and about 140k views combined (part 1, part 2), against 979 posts in the "Happy" thread (link). Staff refused rollbacks (Stevesky) until a replacement firmware arrived around 19 December 2024, initially without release notes (3.10.1). Earlier, the 2022 merged Focal & Naim app dropped some legacy devices (Audiogon, 2023). Consequence: the forum turned into a complaints desk, and monthly posts fell about a third over the next eight months.
  3. Central IT neglect under Focal. The forum archive went offline in 2025 because "it's something managed in France", and a member wrote "Just presumed it was a Focal thing, like the unmaintained naim website" (Aug 2025). The 2024 website made basic contact details hard to find (May 2024).
  4. History lost in earlier migrations. Two forum iterations were lost, and the 2019 move reset everyone's rank and post history, which some members experienced as "Loyalty well rewarded all round" (HiFi Kabin, Jan 2019).
  5. An ageing, closed circle with no new on-ramp. Even veterans said in 2019 that the place had become "populated by people wanting instant fixes" and that the Padded Cell was "mindless jokes, endless reactionary drivel" (archive). Sign-ups are now about 3 a day, and Naim has done little on Reddit or Discord or through content to bring in newer streaming-era buyers.
  6. Company content is underused. The polls Henderson promised as a feedback tool in 2019 mostly ended up as member-run polls. No evidence found of structured product research using them.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Build annual and daily rituals that members own. A yearly "what are you listening to" or "show your setup" thread with a 31 December reset, plus a daily low-stakes game thread, makes visiting a habit. Naim gets about 44% DAU/MAU from this with almost no staff content.
  2. Make room for life beyond the product. An off-topic lounge kept through every migration turns customers into friends, and friends don't churn.
  3. Have one trusted liaison with real access to R&D. One credible person who gets pre-launch briefings and can get answers on a weekend beats a content team for trust.
  4. Use scarce, physical access as a reward and as repair. A lottery-based factory day (8 seats, two or three times a year) creates anticipation. Prioritising those hurt by a bad update turns detractors into insiders.
  5. Never delete your history. Archive old platforms in a searchable form. Years of user knowledge are a moat and a source of search traffic, and losing them breaks trust.

Avoid these

  1. Don't moderate away criticism. Deleting critical threads sends the debate to independent forums and earns you a "cult" label you can't answer.
  2. Don't let a community live on infrastructure nobody owns. Archive outages, website rewrites and app merges run from a distant parent company show members that the brand doesn't care.
  3. Don't confuse a sticky core with a healthy funnel. High engagement among veterans hid a 40% drop in new topics and very few sign-ups. Plan on-ramps for the next generation of customers, such as streaming-first buyers and younger owners.
