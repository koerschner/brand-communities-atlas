How it pays off for the company

Call and chat deflection: SFR's own 2015 award entry says the forum 'started as a cost saving initiative project to reap call deflection for Customer Care each month'. RED by SFR, a low-cost brand with no phone line, built its forum and FAQ as a 'structural pillar' of a 100% digital support model. No deflection, cost or retention figures were ever published.

TL;DR

  • What it is: SFR's official peer-to-peer help forum. It relaunched on Lithium (now Khoros) on 15 Jan 2013 at forum.sfr.fr, replacing a 2008-era forum where paid web advisers answered questions. It was renamed La Communauté SFR (la-communaute.sfr.fr) in May 2020. RED by SFR, the low-cost brand, has run a separate forum and FAQ since 16 Feb 2016 [2][6][47]. As of 8 Nov 2026 the SFR site shows 70,579 conversations and 32,564 "SFR-certified" answers [1].
  • Why people come back: status and access for a small core. At launch about 40 superusers wrote half of all content. They held "Founders" badges, then the title Membre d'Honneur (later Client Top Contributeur). They had a private room with staff, video calls with SFR teams, and full days at the Campus SFR headquarters with directors (2014, and again in 2023) [2][3][11][12]. Ordinary visitors come once from Google and leave.
  • What SFR feeds it: community managers who moderate, route and certify answers; "Team SFR" product and technical experts; and a blog. Over the years that blog has run expert Q&A ("Que voulez-vous savoir ?"), member portraits, cybersecurity explainers and, since Oct 2026, a monthly "astuces du mois" that credits members by name [8][11][16][24][49]. Nobody on the forum can open a customer's account, so account problems are sent to the app's callback feature [23][30].
  • Biggest mistake: treating the community's history and the members' investment as disposable. SFR wiped content at the 2013 relaunch. It closed the Numericable forum in Nov 2017 with about 8 days' notice to the superusers and no consultation [17]. The public conversation counter fell from 117,763 to 27,208 between June and November 2020, and the RED forum went from 37,004 topics (2019) to 2,174 (2024) while its member count dropped from 575,577 to 167,293 [26][27]. Activity is down about 90% from the mid-2010s, and SFR itself is being split up among Bouygues, Free and Orange after a June 2026 deal [41].
  • The one lesson: SFR's 2013 playbook still works: recruit about 30 superusers by phone, run a closed beta with them, give them founder status, a private room and real access to executives. But a support community only keeps paying off if the company keeps its archive, consults the core before structural changes, and keeps paying staff to certify and close the loop.

What the community is

La Communauté SFR is an owned Q&A forum on Khoros. Anyone can read it. Only SFR subscribers can post, after signing in with their SFR account [13][30]. The homepage splits it into five forums: Téléphonie Mobile (8,102 questions), Internet & Téléphonie Fixe (17,885), Télévision (11,571), SFR Mail & Applications (10,375) and Gestion Client (21,396). Next to them sit a "La Vie de la Communauté" blog and a "Comment gagner des badges" guide [1].

The charter names four roles [13][30]:

  • Members: registered SFR subscribers.
  • Clients Top Contributeurs (CTC): unpaid volunteers "passionate about SFR's products" with "a large activity of answering questions and writing articles" [13][14].
  • Experts SFR: paid community managers who moderate, plus SFR employees with expertise in one area. Only Experts may ask a member for personal details, and only by private message [13].
  • Product managers, who answer in their own area [30].

Answers come in three tiers: certified by SFR (an Expert has confirmed it is correct), approved by the customer who asked, and plain member replies [13]. A key design constraint: the community managers "have no access to customer files or data" [30]. A 2016 welcome post said outright that "there are no web advisers on the Forum" [15]. That is the main difference from Orange's forum, where paid webconseillers can open accounts (see orange-communaute.md).

RED by SFR runs its own Khoros site, communaute.red-by-sfr.fr. It has an FAQ knowledge base and an 11-board forum organised around the customer journey (plan and options, billing, network, box, cancellation, loss/theft, order tracking) [28][29]. As of 8 Nov 2026 it showed 212,855 members and 2,820 topics [28]. SFR members who post RED questions on the SFR forum are routinely sent there: "you are at SFR here, not RED" [50].

Status (8 Nov 2026): still online, with fresh resolved threads and a revived monthly blog (27 Oct 2026) [1][24]. Volume is a fraction of its peak, and the company is being dismantled: on 6 June 2026 Bouygues Telecom, Iliad (Free) and Orange signed a €20.35bn agreement with Altice France to buy and split SFR [41].

Origin and strategy

  • 2008-2012, the "web advisers" forum. In SFR's words, "SFR Forum exists since 2008. It's a place where web advisors used to respond automatically to customer requests either in a public or private mode" [2]. Alongside it, SFR ran the Atelier SFR, a beta-test and co-creation platform (about 50,000 members and 50 beta tests a year, per its former manager), and a "HUB" [2][37]. A brand-level social team set up in spring 2010 had six people answering questions on the Facebook "Communauté d'entraide" tab and @SFR_SAV on Twitter by 2012 [31]. The Neuf Telecom side had its own lineage (the unofficial n9ws.com forum, then official "Neuf TV" forums), according to the SFR technical expert Thierry [16].
  • Spring 2012 to January 2013, the pivot to peer support. SFR decided on "a complete break": moving "from an online customer services model to a 100% peer-to-peer Support Community model". It "kept the name but started from a white sheet without capitalizing on the old threads" [2]. The project was led by Sandie Hodiesne (Social Media Manager) with community managers Cindy, Manuela, Fabien, Fanja and Emmanuelle [2]. Hodiesne later described it as a program "launched in 2013" of online and IRL "customer ambassadors... a driving force for recommendation, co-creation and product teaching" [38].
  • Business case. SFR's 2015 Lithy entry is blunt: the forum "started as a cost saving initiative project to reap call deflection for Customer Care each month". It adds that SFR now wanted the community to be "part of the whole customer experience" [3]. In 2014 SFR's social team called the forum the "véritable socle communautaire" (the real community foundation) of its "Communautés Digitales" [32].
  • RED by SFR (2016): community as the support model. When SFR relaunched its low-cost brand as RED by SFR on 16 Feb 2016, Laurent Partouche ("Community Strategist") and Stéphane Guillemot designed a model "where the community dimension would be a structural pillar from our customer proposition", with two goals: "mutual assistance between our customers and commitment to the brand". Two employees built it in six weeks [6]. Partouche came from Joe Mobile, SFR's 100%-digital MVNO (2012-2015), which he says ran on "a community that solved 95% of requests" [33][34]. SFR shut Joe Mobile in May 2015 after the Numericable takeover [35][36]. RED still has no phone line; its X bio says community managers answer publicly 9am-6pm and customer service works through the app [46].

Trajectory. The mid-2020 counter drop and RED's topic collapse are archiving or purging, not lost demand. So the cleanest trend is new activity:

  • Messages: roughly 230k/yr (2015-16), ~115k (2017-18), ~85k (2019-21), ~40k (2023-24), ~19k (2025-26).
  • Net new conversations: about 20k/yr (late 2020-21), 7.4k (2022-23), 3.8k (2024-25), ~2k (2025-26).
  • SFR certifications: about 12k/yr (2021), 2.7k (2023-24), under 1k/yr since 2024.

All three are my estimates from Wayback counters and Lithium message IDs, and all point the same way: activity down roughly 90% from the mid-2010s, with staff curation falling even faster. Over the same period SFR still had 19.4M mobile and 6.1M fixed customers (end 2025) [42], so the decline is not mainly a shrinking customer base.

What keeps people coming back

For the core (a few dozen people), status, access and belonging:

  • Founding myth and badges. The 2013 beta was run with about 30 superusers recruited from SFR's other spaces, first by email and then by phone; 29 said yes. They had two video calls with the social media manager to calm fears ("it was better before with the old forum") and a one-month closed beta. At launch they got special "Founders" badges and, by SFR's account, decided "the choice of discussion to highlight, what to post on the blog" [2]. In 2017 kendokanne, one of the founders and still a CTC in 2023, wrote: "I am part, with a number of members, of the creation of this Forum SFR... I love this forum" [17].
  • A named role with real jobs. By 2014 Membres d'Honneur came in five roles: Secouristes (answerers), Guides (welcomers), Power Rangeurs (who move threads to the right board), Reporters (who write about digital life) and Digitalistes (who help improve SFR's digital support tools). They got a custom avatar (ordinary members could not choose one), product badges (RED, TV, Internet, Home by SFR) and a green rank frame [9][10][21].
  • Face time with executives. Superusers visited the Campus SFR in spring 2014 and again on 10 Oct 2014. Sessions included the digital customer-relations director, network-renovation briefings, an incident-management walkthrough, and a preview of the mobile site with the iPhone 6 in hand [11]. The 2015 entry says SFR invited them "twice a year for a real-life event" and held video calls with product teams before launches [3]. After a COVID gap, a two-day Campus meetup in October 2023 brought in the marketing director, the customer-relations director, the network executive director and the consumer executive director, and for the first time "three active members of the RED forum". "Since 2020, we had been waiting impatiently for a new Rencontre," kendokanne wrote [12].
  • Gifts that made contribution visible. In 2015 SFR gave superusers a connected "Merci!" counter (a Smiirl device) that showed each person's lifetime kudos live. SFR said most were "close to tears" and called it the first object connected to a Lithium platform [3][7]. A 2013 plan included T-shirts printed with each superuser's username [2].
  • Private room. A space hidden from other members where staff warned superusers about recurring cases and asked for feedback on the platform [2].

For everyone else, the standard gamification layer: a rank ladder, which in 2015 used fantasy-builder names (Apprenti Bâtisseur, Herboriste talentueux, Grand Enchanteur) and is now Contributeur en Herbe → Contributeur Elite [13][21]. Add badges for posting, liking and solving; "mercis" (likes); a monthly "la communauté remercie..." leaderboard; and the approved/certified answer marks [13][20][25]. Email alerts on replies are the only in-product hook. The forum is not built into the SFR & Moi app [30], and members complained in 2022 that alert emails had become "minimalist and empty of content" [18].

The content engine

Most of the content is member answers. SFR's own production has come in waves:

  • 2013-2016: CM news posts, member portraits, a forum Instagram (350 posts by Jan 2015), "Que voulez-vous savoir ?" (topic-of-the-hour Q&A with SFR experts) and "Personnalités du Net", where members questioned outside web figures [8][11].
  • 2015-2019: "Team SFR" interviews presenting in-house experts such as Thierry, a technical MOA manager. He says he can help only with "internal functioning" questions because he has no customer reference to diagnose [16].
  • 2020-2021: after the Khoros relaunch, the "Dossiers de la Cybersécurité" and "Paroles d'Expert" series. These also promoted SFR's paid security product ("a good anti-virus like SFR Cybersécurité is essential") [49].
  • Gap: the homepage's newest community articles were from Sept 2024 to Jan 2025 (largely promotional, such as Galaxy S25 pre-orders and "SFR, N°1 Internet Mobile") until October 2026 [1].
  • October 2026 revival: a monthly "Les astuces du mois" and "enquête" (investigation) stories that retell real member threads and credit both the asker and the answering member by handle [24].

On RED, content is FAQ-first. Its Lithium FAQ was rebuilt as one searchable pool tagged by product and by customer-journey moment, plus a Tumblr-fed "RED Lab" stream [6]. Member-made content is mostly tutorials posted as answers by top contributors, which CMs certify and point newcomers to (for example a member's email-backup tutorial, certified by SFR and linked by a CM in 2023) [52].

People, programs and governance

  • Staff: a social team of six in 2013 (one manager and five CMs) [2]; seven named CMs and "Team" members in Jan 2015 [8]; small CM rosters in the 2020s (Yann, Samantha, Ilan, CMael and others answer and route threads) [1][23]. No headcount has been published since 2015.
  • Superusers: 30 recruited → 40 (Mar 2013) → 53 Membres d'Honneur (Jan 2015) → a handful of named CTCs in the 2020s (Ryry, Ricouz, kendokanne, Benjamin, jnq35) whom CMs @-mention for hard technical cases [2][8][19][51]. Recruitment is by invitation from a CM ("I didn't expect at all to receive a message offering me to become a CTC") [19].
  • Moderation: done by Experts SFR under a charter that bans trolling, outside advertising, deliberately wrong answers and personal data [13]. Moderation has drawn censorship accusations. In 2014 a Linux user wrote that five technically worded posts about the box's outdated Samba server were removed as "inciting practices contrary to the law" [39].
  • Feedback loop: superusers flag outages early. SFR credits kendokanne with pinning down an SMS-billing bug that was then fixed and announced publicly [2]. Campus days give the core direct Q&A with network and product leaders [11][12]. There is no public ideas portal; co-creation lived on the separate Atelier SFR [37].

Growth loops

  • Search: open reading and indexed threads ("certified" answers rank as an official-looking result). Third-party guides now explain the forum to searchers [30].
  • Routing from support: SFR's help pages and charter send people to Assistance FAQs first and the forum second [13]. The Twitter support account @SFR_SAV (staffed since about 2012) and the forum sent people to each other; in 2020 members were still telling forum users to reach an adviser via @SFR_SAV [31][53].
  • Anniversaries and events: Khoros's Lithy index (in text that appears to come from SFR's 2015 entry, which can't be read directly) says the anniversary events raised traffic 9% and registrations 33% and reactivated dormant members [3][4].
  • RED: the forum doubled as a pre-sales space ("Choisir RED" FAQ labels) for a brand with no shops or phone line [6].

There is no referral or ambassador-for-acquisition mechanic tied to the forum.

Evidence of business impact

  • Company claims only. "Cost saving initiative... call deflection for Customer Care each month" (2015, no figures) [3]. RED: "95% of discussions stem from members" in month one [6]. Joe Mobile: "95% of requests resolved by the community" (founder's résumé) [33].
  • Activity proxies: 7,000+ approved solutions in six months and 5,050 thanks a month (Jan 2015) [8]; 32,564 SFR-certified answers in total (2026) [1].
  • Recognition: Lithy finalist for Marketing Champion 2015 (Barclaycard won) [5]; entries in 2013 (Superfan) and 2016 (RED, Community Design) [2][6].
  • Context that cuts the other way: SFR had the lowest customer-service satisfaction of the four French operators in ARCEP's 2024 survey (7.3/10) [40], and users still post "even if no one from SFR customer service looks at the forum" [23]. No SEC-style disclosure exists (Altice France files no 10-K), and I found no academic study. Business impact evidence score: 2, because there are award-entry metrics but no cost, deflection or churn numbers.

Other scores.

  • Content engine 2: the output is mostly CM news and promotion, with long gaps (2025-26). Members' tutorials are certified but not systematically curated.
  • Member ownership 3: the founders shaped the 2013 launch and superusers move threads and write articles, but the big decisions (purges, the Numericable closure) were imposed.
  • Staff investment 3: CMs, certification, Team SFR experts and executive-level Campus days, but no account access and falling certification volume.
  • Durability 2: online for 13+ years, but activity is down about 90% and the parent company is being broken up.

What they did right

  1. A textbook superuser-first launch. They recruited about 30 superusers by phone, ran a one-month closed beta, gave them Founders badges and editorial say, and opened with a meetup and a naming mission for the program. Superusers produced 50% of content within six weeks [2]. Retention mechanics score 3: strong for the core, thin for everyone else.
  2. Role-based superuser design. The five Membre d'Honneur roles turned "power user" into concrete jobs (welcome, move threads, report, improve tools), so people other than answer machines had a way in [9].
  3. Executive access as the reward. Campus days with the network and customer-relations directors in 2014 and 2023 gave volunteers what they valued most: inside information and a voice [11][12].
  4. Certified answers. Since 2020, the three-tier answer system (SFR-certified, customer-approved, plain) makes old threads trustworthy for searchers without letting staff take over conversations [13].
  5. RED's journey-based IA and FAQ-in-forum design, built in six weeks by two people, with members producing 95% of discussions at launch [6].
  6. The 2026 "astuces du mois" format credits members by name and turns resolved threads into stories. It is cheap and transferable [24].

What they did wrong

  1. Repeatedly throwing away history. The 2013 relaunch began "from a white sheet" [2]. The visible conversation count fell from 117,763 to 27,208 in mid-2020 [26]. RED's forum fell from 37,004 topics to 2,174, and its member counter from 575,577 to 167,293 within a year (2023-24) [27]. Archiving had annoyed members since 2014: "I imagine other topics are moved to the archives for no reason. Why archive it?" [22]. Consequence: less search equity, and long-time contributors see their work vanish.
  2. Structural changes imposed on the core. The Numericable forum was closed on 2 Nov 2017. Superusers were told about eight days earlier and were not consulted: "Do you think we were consulted? Dream on!... the CMs are not the big decision-makers at SFR" [17]. Displaced Numericable volunteers called the SFR forum a "palliative" [17].
  3. No path to the account. Volunteers and CMs cannot see customer files [15][16][30]. So the forum can only redirect angry customers to callbacks, which feeds the "nobody at SFR reads this" perception [23]. Orange's paid webconseillers solved this; SFR never did.
  4. Opaque gamification. In 2015 members asked for a month why ranks did not match contribution and got no answer from CMs, even by private message [21]. A navigation thread, "how to create a topic in the new forum?", ran to 25 pages [48].
  5. Shrinking curation. SFR certifications dropped from about 12k a year to under 1k a year after 2023, and the community blog went quiet for most of 2025-26 [1][26]. Under Altice's cost cutting and the 2026 sale, there is no public sign of community investment beyond the core CM role.
  6. Mixing marketing into help content, such as security dossiers that sell SFR Cybersécurité and phone pre-order posts [1][49]. These are minor, but they dilute the space's helper identity.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Recruit your first 30 superusers by hand, by phone, before launch, and run a closed beta where they shape the space. Give them a founders' badge that never expires [2].
  2. Turn "superuser" into named jobs (answer, welcome, tidy, report, improve tools), each with its own badge and permissions [9].
  3. Pay volunteers in access: a private room with staff, previews, and at least one yearly in-person day with the executives who own the product and the network [2][11][12].
  4. Make good answers official. A staff "certified" mark on member answers keeps volunteers credited while giving searchers confidence [13].
  5. Retell solved threads as content that names the members who helped, as in the 2026 "astuces du mois" [24].

Avoid these

  1. Never purge or reset the archive in a relaunch, merger or cleanup. Migrate it and keep URLs. Every reset in SFR's history cost trust and search traffic [2][22][26][27].
  2. Don't make structural decisions (merging forums, closing spaces) without consulting the core and giving weeks of notice and a welcome plan [17].
  3. Don't build a support community that can't reach the account. Staff it with someone who can resolve account cases, or members will see it as a dead end [15][23].
