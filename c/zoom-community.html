TL;DR

  • What it is: community.zoom.com, Zoom's official peer-support forum. A team formed in March 2021, it launched on Khoros on August 12, 2021, moved to Gainsight (inSided) in January 2026, and passed 500,000 registered members in mid-2026. It won Khoros Kudos 2024 Best Customer Support Community and CMX Best Customer Support Community in 2024 and 2025.
  • Why people come back: mostly they don't. Most visitors arrive from Google or the help center with one problem, read an accepted solution and leave (1M+ monthly unique visitors against 500K registered members). The return visitors are a small core of "Community Champions": around 150 in 2023, only about 20 of them customers, and they wrote more than 40% of the solutions. Badges, ranks, weekly leaderboards, a private chat channel, monthly calls and swag keep that core going.
  • What Zoom does to feed it: a support-org team of about five, employee "champions" who answer in public, SEO work (Google clicks up 54% year over year), a new product forum for every launch, customer-success webinars and a Customer Success Hub, monthly "listening reports" that feed the knowledge base, and new tiers for advocates (Super Champion, MVP, Education Ambassadors, user groups).
  • Biggest mistake: the forum is cut off from the two things angry users want, product changes and paid support. Feature requests get bounced to a write-only form (zoom.us/feed) with no public status. Billing and ticket failures pile up in threads the community team can't fix. Both leave visible, years-long threads of "still not implemented" and "no response."
  • The one lesson: a support community can grow big and profitable for the company in five months if it plugs into product SSO, search and support operations. But without a public loop back to the roadmap, and without enough customer volunteers (not employees), it stays a help desk with badges.

What the community is

The Zoom Community (community.zoom.com) is Zoom's owned support forum. It has 20+ product forums (Meetings, Phone, Contact Center, ZoomMate/AI Companion, Docs/Canvas, Clips, Webinars, Events, Rooms and more), a Billing & Account Management board, a Customer Success Forum, a Social Corner, a Community Center (help and onboarding), and "User Groups": Australia/New Zealand, India, Japan and Singapore by region, and Education, Healthcare, Nonprofit and Zoom Events Experts by industry, plus closed groups for program members (nav captured from community.zoom.com, 2026). Members sign in with their Zoom account (Zoom blog, Aug 12 2021). The community team sits in Zoom's Digital Customer Support organization next to the knowledge base, chatbot and support site (Khoros Kudos 2024 entry).

Other spaces nearby:

  • Zoom Developer Forum (devforum.zoom.us), a separate Discourse forum for APIs and SDKs. Its first topics date from January 16, 2017. In November 2026 it had 48,672 users, 318,928 posts, 740 active users in the past 30 days and 1,411 posts in the past 30 days (devforum about.json; latest by created). Developers also have their own Zoom Developer MVP program.
  • Zoom Learning Center and Customer Success Events Hub (success.zoom.us): live trainings, a monthly What's New, weekly open office hours and deep dives (Zoom CS Events Hub).
  • Unofficial: r/Zoom, which describes itself as "Unofficial subreddit of the video conferencing platform Zoom" and has 33,419 subscribers (Arctic Shift). Also champion Denise Lahat's Facebook group Zoom Events Pros, and "Lord Zoom" John Drinkwater's weekly live training show (lordzoom.com), which Zoom now promotes inside the community.

Status today: active and still growing in members. It moved platforms in January 2026 and was featured at Zoomtopia Live on October 20, 2026 (community post).

Origin and strategy

Zoom had no public user forum before 2021. Only developers had one. In 2020, daily meeting participants grew from 10 million to 300 million. Zoom says users "desired a 24/7 accessible online forum," so "in March 2021, we put together a team and tasked them with creating the Zoom Community," and it went live "in less than five months" on Khoros Communities and Khoros Care (Khoros Kudos 2022 entry by Alexis Brown). Before that, support meant tickets, bots and the knowledge base.

The business case was deflection, stated plainly: "The more conversations with helpful solutions, the more people can benefit from viewing these posts... more support deflection can occur, freeing up our live agents to assist with high severity issues and premier accounts" (Khoros Kudos 2023 entry). That fits Zoom's model: a huge free and low-ARPU base that can't get a human for free, and enterprise accounts that do. Later the charter grew to voice-of-customer reporting, knowledge-base gap analysis, outage communication, new-product forums and customer-success content (Kudos 2024).

Champions inside Zoom: Alexis Brown, Senior Manager, Global Community (later "Global Community and Social Media"), who wrote the launch post and won the CMX 2023 "Executive Leader of a Community Team" award; and Briuana Green, moderator, then senior community manager (Kudos 2023; Zoom blog, Aug 15 2023). Zoom says the community "has been continuously supported by Zoom's senior leadership." It has published no named executive sponsor.

Trajectory. Registered members have grown every year: 100K, 250K, 365K, 500K. Traffic and activity look flat to declining, though. Cumulative page views went from 39M (fall 2024) to 62.4M (Aug 2026), roughly 11-12M a year. That matches the "12M+ annual page views" claim and is below the ~19M added between mid-2023 and fall 2024 (estimate from Zoom's own cumulative figures). Reported posts fell from 173K (2024) to 140K-150K (2026), probably a cleanup during the platform migration or a change in how posts are counted; Zoom has not explained it. Champions fell from ~150 to 120. The unofficial subreddit shows Zoom's pandemic-era interest fading (812 posts in March 2021, 128 in March 2026). The forum is growing as a searchable archive more than as a live conversation.

What keeps people coming back

Most traffic is one-and-done search traffic. The retention machinery is aimed at the few people who answer.

  • Points, ranks, badges, leaderboards ("Happy Rewards"). These were there at launch: "gain points for helpful contributions, climb up the reputation level ranks, earn badges, and be spotlighted on our leaderboards" (launch post). In 2024 Zoom added weekly leaderboards based on accepted solutions, updated rank titles and icons, and raised badge thresholds (Kudos 2024). By August 2026 it had awarded 30,000 badges (five-year post).
  • Champion status and a micro-community. Champions get yearly badges, digital certificates for LinkedIn, a monthly Champion call with the community team and employee champions, a welcome gift plus "quarterly surprise and delight gifts," a private forum and Zoom Team Chat channels (Kudos 2023). Customer champion Ray Harwood said: "When I was invited to become a community champion, I became part of a fairly tight-knit group... We help each other out, helping everyone else out" (Zoom blog). A Super Champion tier is decided by annual review (program page).
  • Recognition. A monthly Champion Spotlight series started in 2024 (example, July 2025). In July 2026 came a surprise "Recognition Program": a digital badge and a free merch item, with no application (post, Jul 15 2026).
  • Intrinsic and career motives. Champions describe puzzle-solving ("I answer one or two questions a day, partly because it's a puzzle — it's fun"), being the "go-to person," and finding clients ("I have also found new clients this way") (Zoom blog). The MVP program, launched in early 2026, pitches "amplify your personal brand as a recognized Zoom thought leader" and "direct engagement with Zoom's technical teams" (MVP launch post).
  • Rituals: there are few. The closest are event-driven: a one-day 5th-anniversary badge earned by posting on August 12, 2026 (post), get-out-the-vote drives for awards, and Zoomtopia tie-ins. No weekly community ritual run by Zoom staff was found. The weekly cadence that does exist is volunteer-run: Super Champion John Drinkwater's show, promoted site-wide as tackling "the biggest questions being asked in the Zoom Community forums" (event post).
  • Product hooks: Zoom-account SSO (a JWT login flow built with Khoros professional services), links from the support site, and Zoom's footer "Zoom Community" link (Kudos 2024). No in-app community features (inside the Zoom client) were found.
  • Switching costs: small for askers. For champions, accumulated rank and status. Zoom did not say whether ranks carried over to Gainsight.

The content engine

  • The peer Q&A archive is the product. Zoom argues that "a solution that was posted in 2021 will continue to benefit users in 2023 and beyond," and tracks solution views (up 50% YoY by Aug 2024) (Kudos 2023; Kudos 2024). A custom activity feed sorts posts into Latest, Trending, Solved and Unanswered.
  • SEO is a deliberate program. "Our threads now consistently rank higher in Google search results." Organic traffic rose 53.6% (Kudos 2024). The 2026 Gainsight move added "AI-powered search" and machine translation of user posts (new-experience post, Jan 2026; Zoom on X, Jan 23 2026).
  • Staff content: a dedicated forum at each product launch (AI Companion, Docs, Clips, and later Sheets, Slides, Paper, My Notes), service-impact and status posts, product-launch announcements, and Customer Success Forum posts written by CSMs (AI Companion summary templates, Hub tips). It also has Customer Success Events and On-Demand Learning spaces with live and recorded webinars (Kudos 2024; CS forum). In May 2026 Zoom launched a Customer Success Hub inside the community that gathers onboarding guides, deep-dive webinars, learning clips, adoption guides, Learning Center courses, What's New, support and status (post, May 4 2026).
  • Member-made content is tolerated and borrowed more than built: Lord Zoom's weekly show, employee champion Josh Stanley's 60+ AI Companion short videos (spotlight), and MVPs who are expected to create content and speak. There is no template or extension gallery that members contribute to.
  • Listening Reports: monthly reports on growth, top discussions and contributors that "have directly influenced new knowledge base articles, product features, and content updates." Qualtrics surveys run inside the community and Tableau is used for analysis (Kudos 2024).

People, programs and governance

  • Team: the Zoom Community and Social Media team in Digital Customer Support. The 2024 award post thanked five people by handle: @alexisbrown, @Bri, @VA11_2, @CarlaA and @MGSR (post, Nov 2024). Moderators sign as "Zoom Community Moderator" or "Zoom Community Team."
  • Community Champions: opened to employees in June 2021 and to customers and partners in August 2022, by invitation or nomination (Kudos 2023). Champions "volunteer their time... without the expectation or retrieval of compensation" (program page snippet). The program is labeled in public as "Community Champion | Employee" or "Community Champion | Customer." In 2023 about 130 of the ~150 champions were employees or partners (Zoom blog).
  • Other tiers: Super Champion (annual review); Zoom MVP Program (early 2026, "our highest level of recognition for community leaders who are making waves beyond the Zoom Community," by application) (post); Education Ambassador Program (announced July 31, 2024, 30+ educators and administrators at launch) (Zoom press release); and user groups by region and industry, launched about early 2025 (Japan group opened Feb 13, 2025) (launch post; Japan group).
  • Moderation and tooling: Khoros Care routed "thousands of discussions... every month" into a prioritized queue (Kudos 2023).
  • Feedback to product: done in private. Champions "constantly give us feedback — they are the voice of our customers" (Zoom blog). Bugs were sometimes "first identified through reports from community members." For everyone else, the standard moderator reply is that "the Zoom Community is not a place where feature requests are tracked" and they should use https://zoom.us/feed (example, Apr 2022; Feb 2026). There is no public ideas board.
  • Platform: Khoros, August 2021 to late 2025. IgniteTech acquired Khoros on May 27, 2025 (PR Newswire). Zoom relaunched on Gainsight Customer Communities (inSided) around January 21-23, 2026 (new-experience post), and Gainsight named Zoom as a CC customer in May 2026 (GlobeNewswire). Whether the Khoros acquisition caused the move is not documented.

Growth loops

  1. Search, then archive, then more search. Every solved thread is a landing page. Google clicks per month went from 320K to 494K in a year, and clicks from Zoom's support site also feed in (Kudos 2024). Members arrive by search far more than by referral.
  2. The product account is the membership. SSO with Zoom credentials means any of Zoom's hundreds of millions of users is one click from posting (launch post).
  3. Zoom's owned channels. At launch Zoom promoted the community on its blog, LinkedIn, Twitter, Instagram, TikTok and Zoomtopia (Kudos 2022), and in 2026 again at Zoomtopia Live.
  4. Advocates beyond the forum. The MVP and Education Ambassador programs and the regional user groups aim to carry Zoom into social media, events and campuses. No outcome data has been published yet.
  5. Award campaigns as marketing. Zoom asks members to vote in the Khoros and CMX awards (2024 vote post; 2026 CMX nomination) and then announces the wins.

Evidence of business impact

Hard, company-reported (award submissions to Zoom's own platform vendor, so best read as claims):

  • Time to first reply fell 48.6%, from 36 h to 18.7 h (Aug 2023 → Aug 2024).
  • Forum CSAT rose from 84.5% to 93.6%.
  • Google clicks rose 54% YoY; solution views rose 50%; members rose 28.6% (Kudos 2024; repeated in Khoros customer story).

Asserted but not quantified: "reduced the reliance on direct support channels, leading to cost savings in support operations." No deflection rate, no tickets avoided, no dollar savings, and no churn or retention link has been published.

Filings: EDGAR full-text search found no "Zoom Community" mentions in Zoom's 10-K or 10-Q filings (2021-2026), and the FY2024 10-K uses "community" only for DEI and employee resource groups (EDGAR FTS). At Zoom's scale the community is an operational tool, not something investors hear about.

Third-party recognition: Khoros Kudos 2022 (Keep Calm and Carry On), Khoros Kudos 2024 (Best Customer Support Community) (Khoros blog), CMX Best Customer Support Community 2024 (post) and 2025 (post), and CMX 2023 Executive Leader for Alexis Brown. In 2026 Zoom was nominated, but TaxDome won Best Customer Support Community (TaxDome). Some of these awards include public voting.

No academic research on the Zoom Community was found.

What they did right

  1. Built for one clear job, and shipped fast. A five-month build tied to a named business problem (no free 24/7 support for a base that grew 30x) got 100K members in five months (Kudos 2022).
  2. Used employees to seed answers, then recruited customers. Employee champions answered from June 2021 and customers joined from August 2022. The 2023 champion group (mostly staff) produced more than 40% of solutions (Zoom blog). An empty Q&A forum dies; this one never was empty.
  3. Treated it as a support operation with service levels. A prioritized moderation queue, first-reply time as a KPI (halved in a year), a forum CSAT survey, and monthly listening reports into the knowledge base (Kudos 2024).
  4. Invested in being found. Deliberate SEO (+54% Google clicks) and, in 2026, AI search and translation to reach non-English users.
  5. Gave power users a real inner circle. A private chat channel, monthly calls with staff, gifts, certificates and spotlights. Champions describe a "tight-knit group" and peer help between themselves (Zoom blog).
  6. Added tiers for different motivations: answerers (Champions, Super Champions), outside advocates and creators (MVPs), a vertical (Education Ambassadors), and place and industry (user groups).
  7. Moved the community along with the product. It opened forums on launch day for AI Companion, Docs and Clips and later the AI productivity apps, and posts outage and status updates there.

What they did wrong

  1. No public loop to the roadmap. Moderators send every idea to zoom.us/feed, which gives no status and no votes. Members asked for status tracking as early as 2022 ("they go off into the ether never to be spoken of again") (thread, Aug 17 2022). Long +1 threads build up, like the request to auto-save closed-caption transcripts, "still outstanding over 2.5 years after the initial request" (thread), and the raise-hand controls thread (thread, 2022-2024). The result is visible frustration on pages that rank in Google, the very SEO asset the team is proud of.
  2. The forum soaks up anger it can't resolve. Billing and ticket failures ("the case/ticket is closed," "no response," "impossible to reach them") have their own threads (2023, 2024, Jan 2026, Apr 2026). Moderators can only post a KB link. When a member observes that "posting here in community sometimes gets attention faster than normal support," the forum has become an escalation channel by accident, not a deflection channel.
  3. The volunteer base is thin and shrinking. Of ~150 champions in 2023, only about 20 were customers, and the count was 120 by August 2026. The "community that runs itself" Briuana Green described is still mostly staff-run. The new MVP and Recognition programs in 2026 look like an effort to fix this. Their results are unknown.
  4. Vanity metrics in the headlines. Registered members (a byproduct of SSO) and cumulative page views lead every announcement. Meanwhile annual page views appear to have slipped (an estimate from Zoom's own cumulative numbers) and reported posts fell from 173K to 150K without explanation.
  5. Two platform changes in less than five years of life. Zoom moved from Khoros to Gainsight in January 2026, shortly after Khoros changed owners. Members ran into friction such as needing staff to change usernames ("I'm getting strong gainsight vibes") (thread, Apr 2026), and URLs changed from /t5/ paths to new slugs. No large backlash was found.
  6. Weak social layer, plus commercial messages in it. The Social Corner is mostly staff announcements, self-introductions from SEO marketers and empty posts. One 2022 thread body even contains a spam link (thread). Staff celebration posts double as promotions: the 15-years post carries a 15%-off upgrade offer (post, Jun 2026).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Make your product login the community login, and link the forum from the help center, so the first post costs nothing.
  2. Seed answers with an employee champion bench before you recruit customers. Label employees publicly (for example "Champion | Employee") and measure first-reply time as a service level.
  3. Treat every solved thread as an SEO page. Track Google clicks and solution views, not just new posts.
  4. Give your top answerers an inner circle: a private channel, monthly calls with staff, early info, swag and public spotlights. This is cheap, and it is what keeps your 1% active.
  5. Run a monthly listening report (top issues, unanswered questions, knowledge-base gaps) and route it to support content and product. Then tell members what changed.

Avoid these

  1. Don't send product feedback into a black box. Use a public ideas board with statuses, or at least publish "you asked, we shipped." Otherwise your best-ranking pages become complaint archives.
  2. Don't let the community become the escalation path for a weak paid-support process. Fix the ticket loop, or give moderators a real escalation lever, or the forum will gather the anger.
  3. Don't confuse registered members with community. SSO inflates members. Watch active answerers, the customer share of champions and annual traffic, and plan for search and AI answers eating the "read one thread and leave" visit.
