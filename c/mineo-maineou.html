TL;DR

  • What it is: the official community site of mineo, an Osaka-based budget mobile carrier (MVNO) run by Optage, a Kansai Electric subsidiary. It opened on 13 Jan 2015, about six months after mineo itself, explicitly modelled on the UK's giffgaff. It holds Q&A, chat boards, a staff blog, the Idea Farm suggestion portal, the Free Tank shared data pool, games, and since 2026 user-run Circles. It had 938,470 registered members in Nov 2026 (Commune, 2019 interview; Maineou stats).
  • Why people come back: the community is part of the phone plan. Spare data can be dropped into a national Free Tank, sent to other members as tips (10 MB "chips"), won in member-built maze games, or claimed in disasters. Add rank titles, Nice! reactions, staff who post under playful nicknames, and twice-yearly off-kai meetups where staff share "only-here" business information (KARTE CX 2023; off-kai #23).
  • What the company feeds it: about 7 full-time staff out of a ~40-person mobile strategy department (2019), weekly meetings where managers review every idea (1,351 implemented so far), open dashboards on network congestion and community metrics, and real-world events from 10-person meetups to a 2,700-visitor festival (Commune; Idea Farm; Hakuhodo DY ONE).
  • Biggest mistake: in April 2018 mineo quietly began "traffic optimisation" (recompressing users' images) without telling anyone. The community caught it and a backlash followed, which contradicted the "Fun with Fans!" brand and forced a public apology from the business head on Maineou (ITmedia, 2018-05-07). The slower problem is that the mechanics have drifted: the Free Tank now holds about 5 PB that hardly anyone may withdraw, idea feedback has become terse, and comments are down ~70% from the 2024 peak.
  • The one lesson: tie the community to the product's core unit of value (here, data). Make giving visible and reciprocal, and make the people running it visible humans. That is what turned a commodity utility into a "fan" brand with industry-leading satisfaction. But such economies need re-tuning or they hollow out.

What the community is

Maineou (a pun on "mine-o" + 王, king; members are jokingly 王国民, "kingdom citizens") lives at king.mineo.jp and inside the mineo app. Anyone can register, but data features need an eoID linked to a mineo line. Its spaces:

  • Q&A (peer support with best answers) and boards (掲示板), which began as IT/telecom only and grew into hobby and chat categories. These were re-categorised from scratch in March 2026 (staff blog 3073).
  • Staff blog written by the 運営事務局 (operations office), each writer under a nickname ("Natural Perm", "Spice Egg", "Akashiya Kyon"). Since early on, posts need no manager approval (KARTE CX 2020).
  • Idea Farm (アイデアファーム): suggestions with a visible status pipeline (proposed → checked → under review → implemented) (Idea Farm).
  • Free Tank (フリータンク): a shared reservoir of data, with a disaster-relief tank opened to affected regions. Chips let members tip each other data (Free Tank).
  • Kingdom Dungeon (王国ダンジョン, Jan 2018): members build mazes and seed them with data "gemstones" for others to find (K-tai Watch, 2018-01-12).
  • Yuzurune (ゆずるね。, Mar 2020): a pledge to stay off the network during the noon congestion hour, with rewards for 20 successful pledges (Nikkei xTECH, 2020-01-29).
  • Ambassadors (2019): Co-creation, Support (1:1 setup chat) and Referral (K-Opticom press release, 2019-02-18).
  • Circles (サークル, 31 Mar 2026): user-founded interest groups (Optage, 2026-03-25).
  • Public stats pages (公開情報): network congestion, Free Tank levels, membership, activity counts, staff policy and event history (stats).

Unofficial spaces are small by comparison. 5ch ran long-lived gossip threads about Maineou's own "residents" (e.g. "mineo マイネ王 Part27", c. 2018, which mocks heavy users' daily Nice!/chip routines) (5ch). There is no mineo subreddit (Arctic Shift returns none), and English discussion is scattered across r/japanlife. On social media, mineo's Facebook page has ~42.7k likes (Facebook) and @mineojp points users to Maineou. Maineou is the centre of gravity, and it competes with 5ch rather than feeding off it.

Origin and strategy

mineo launched in June 2014 as a late entrant to a crowded price war among roughly 1,000 MVNOs. While studying foreign MVNOs, the team found giffgaff and decided "this is the strategy!" They chose to compete on emotional value ("I like it, it's fun, it's reassuring") instead of price alone (Commune). Then-head Akiho Ueda (seconded from Kansai Electric, 2016–2019) gave the same giffgaff origin in 2017 (ITmedia, 2017-07-12). A 2026 interview adds that staff visited giffgaff around launch (Real Sound, Mar 2026).

Maineou began modestly as a staff blog for announcements. Management "didn't think it would take many resources," and no KPI was set at first beyond mineo's own contract count. Formal metrics came in July 2016: CSAT, NPS, referrals, churn, and ideas proposed and implemented (Commune). Under Ueda the brand statement "Fun with Fans!" was written down. In 2019 it was extended with a staff code of conduct, "mineo way", shared with fans, and with the three ambassador programmes (K-Opticom, 2019; Nikkei xTrend, 2020-11-11). The company frames value as a pyramid: functional (cheap, stable lines), emotional (connection, fun) and social (helping others, e.g. disaster relief). The community is meant to supply the top two (KARTE CX 2020).

The business link is explicit. A no-contract telecom lives or dies on churn and word of mouth. Current lead Hidenobu Okinaka: "because a telecom customer doesn't cancel right after signing, making them like mineo more during the contract lowers churn and drives referrals." He also says advertising (functional value) and fan marketing (retention) are separate jobs (Hakuhodo DY ONE, Dec 2024).

Trajectory. Registrations grew fast from 2016 to 2018. Since 2020 they track mineo's new subscriptions, because churned users rarely delete accounts (staff blog 2403). New sign-ups have fallen from 10–20k a month in 2016–18 to 2.5–5k in 2024–26. Monthly comments held at 45–80k from 2017 to 2023, then spiked to 120–149k in Aug–Dec 2024, which coincided with the 10th-anniversary campaigns. They then fell steadily, to 41–48k a month from March 2026, below any month from 2017 to 2025. Nice! reactions fell less (about −40% from peak), so light reacting continues while writing declines. Context: mineo itself plateaued at ~120–137 万 lines (1.2–1.37M), though it holds about 8–10% of the MVNO market and ranks around third (ITmedia, 2024-06-26; Real Sound).

What keeps people coming back

  1. Data as the community's currency. The Free Tank (Dec 2015) let anyone pour expiring data into a national pool and withdraw it at month-end. Okinaka says growth was slow until "we released member-to-member data transfer and the Free Tank, and users jumped at once. To gather people, solve it with a mechanism … an easy-to-understand incentive" (Hakuhodo DY ONE). Deposits come with thank-you messages. Staff deliberately refused an "auto-deposit at month end" feature to keep "the communication and gratitude born from putting in, taking out and tipping" (staff blog 1244). Disaster opening is the social-value proof point: 18 activations and ~30,000 users by Jan 2020 (Nikkei xTECH).
  2. Reciprocity loops. Optage's 2023 n=1 analysis with PLAID found that "the source of enthusiasm is the joy of reactions": members who keep giving Nice!s and chips are the ones who also receive them. It also found a journey from 相談 to 雑談, from support questions on the IT boards to chat in the social boards, and from content to people (following favourite posters, even a "master" whose events a member admired). The company then built a growth model around activity, reactions, connection and habit, with a "wai-wai" (group) route and a "shippori" (1:1 DM) route, each with metrics (KARTE CX 2023).
  3. Status ladders. Activity ranks run Beginner → Rookie → Regular → Ace → Veteran → Master → Grand Master → Super Grand Master → King, each with Lv1–3 (Q&A thread, 2023). There are also medals (Best Answerer, Nice! Collector, Popular), visible on every post. Ranks gate privileges: founding a Circle requires Regular Lv1 (staff blog 3073).
  4. Nudges. KARTE pop-ups showing first-timers the Nice! button lifted Nice!-per-view by 37%. A "you can comment" prompt shown at the end of a blog post lifted comment rate by 22%. One packet-reward creative got 4.2x the CTR (KARTE CX 2020).
  5. Parasocial ties to staff. Everyone in the office has a Maineou account and posts in their own voice. One staffer, "Akashiya Kyon", debuted as a company "idol" on YouTube and met ~3,500 users in three years. A member later wrote that the idol video is why they joined (staff blog 2873; board post).
  6. Off-kai as the conversion moment. Small (26 users + 13 staff in Osaka, June 2025) and dialogue-heavy, with candid slides on strategy, network and promotions. "Once someone attends an off-kai they tend strongly to become a core fan." Co-creation ambassadors must have attended at least one (staff blog 2854; Hakuhodo DY ONE; ambassador page).
  7. Collective quests. For 500k members (May 2019) mineo ran a puzzle hunt whose clues unlocked only when someone physically visited sites from Hokkaido to the Goto Islands. A Tokyo member flew to Goto, for a reward of just data (Commune; 50man site). The "takoyaki topping" radar battle split Japan into 4,760 squares for three teams to capture with the app (Hakuhodo DY ONE).

Switching costs: accumulated rank, data tips, friendships and a chip/tank history all live in a mineo-linked account, and long-tenure perks (ファン∞とく, an Idea Farm idea) reward staying.

The content engine

  • Staff blog under nicknames, covering service news, the "why" behind decisions, demographic reports ("mineo user profile 2023"), off-kai result reports with every user suggestion listed, and apologies. Its comment sections are the main staff–member channel (staff blog 2403).
  • Radical stats transparency: hourly Free Tank levels, network congestion "weather" by hour, unfiltered 1-star reviews on the sales site, staff moderation policy, and the full event history. Ueda: "we never hide low ratings" (Nikkei xTrend, 2020).
  • Video: the Maineou YouTube channel ("マイネ王調査団" investigation squad, staff idol documentary) (YouTube). Live streams follow big announcements, e.g. the night of the Jan 2026 full-MVNO reveal (Real Sound).
  • Members as ad talent: the 2019 TV and online ads used real users, unpaid and unscripted (staff blog 1046; Commune).
  • Member-made content: Q&A answers, dungeons seeded with members' own data, guides (e.g. a 2017 member write-up of rank levels), and now Circles such as "Maipyon Fan Club" and "mineo PC Club" (staff blog 3150).

Compared with YNAB, there is little structured "how-to" curriculum. Education is mostly peer Q&A plus Support Ambassadors' 1:1 chats.

People, programs and governance

  • Team: ~7 dedicated of ~40 in the mobile strategy department (2019). Moderation runs 24/365, with an outside contractor nights and weekends that can only hide content provisionally, and decisions are judged on rule content, not report counts (Commune; office page). Cadence promised: off-kai about twice a year, a fan gathering in summer, and a "kingdom event" for long-tenure users in autumn.
  • Idea Farm governance: originally "Idea Lab", where user votes decided which ideas staff would consider. Too many good ideas were voted down, so voting was dropped and managers (about 10, including the business head) review every idea weekly, roughly 20+ a week (Hirukawa, Nishikawa & Yonemitsu, Japan Marketing Journal 43(1), 2023; staff blog 552, 2017). Ideas implemented: ~400 of 4,000 (2019) → ~1,100 of 9,100 (2023) → 1,351 of 11,328 (2026). Examples include the long-tenure reward programme and 10-minute free calls (Idea Farm).
  • Ambassadors: Co-creation requires an Idea Farm proposal plus an off-kai, an application and an NDA, and grants access to "important information" on strategy and promotions. One early ambassador stressed it gives "no special power … if anything you're in the line of fire" (board, 2019-05-30). Support ambassadors give live or booked 1:1 chat help for "a modest perk". Referral pays the referrer an e-gift after six months and waives the new user's ¥3,850 fee (support page; GameWith, 2026).
  • Beta testing: Circles were designed from off-kai and ambassador round-table input, then beta-tested by members for two weeks before launch (staff blog 3073).

Growth loops

  1. Referral: 30% of new contracts came from referrals in Jan 2020, and 30–40% per the 2019 interview (Nikkei xTECH; Commune). Core users refer about 1.5x the average (Optage, 2026).
  2. Pre-sales help: member-taught "budget smartphone consultation" events from 2016 to 2019, and the open Q&A, where non-customers can read and ask. Comparison and affiliate sites routinely present Maineou as a reason to pick mineo (event list; Mobareco, 2026).
  3. Testimonial advertising (2019), plus PR value. The community itself became a story told in Nikkei, a Hakuhodo book and conference talks. Okinaka's caveat: the community does not sell to strangers. "Telling someone who doesn't know mineo 'there's this lively community' gets 'so what?'" Acquisition comes from function and price, and the community's job is retention and referral (Hakuhodo DY ONE).

Evidence of business impact

All figures are company-reported (Optage is unlisted, so there are no SEC-style filings).

  • Monthly churn ~2.0% → ~1.0%; 30–40% of new contracts by referral; ~10% of all inquiries covered by Q&A (2019) (Commune).
  • 2023: an analysis of ~2M lines, including churned ones, found that higher Maineou activity correlates with lower churn and more referrals. The team turned it into a profit-uplift figure for executives (not disclosed) (KARTE CX 2023).
  • 2026: ~4,000 core users churn at about one-fifth of the base rate and use referral codes about 1.5x as often (Optage). This is correlational: loyal people both post and stay.
  • Market share: MVNO share rose from ~5% to ~9% in 2016–19 under Ueda (Nikkei xTrend, 2020).
  • Third-party satisfaction: JCSI MVNO #1 in 2018 and again in 2025 (satisfaction and "delight" index); NTT Com Online NPS benchmark #1 in 2019 (Commune; mineo support, 2025).
  • Academic: a 2023 Japan Marketing Journal case describes Idea Farm as a rare crowdsourcing success, citing that ~90% of firms that try idea crowdsourcing get only about one idea a month (J-STAGE). OpenAlex was over budget during this research; no other peer-reviewed study was found.

What they did right

  • Put the community inside the product's economics. Data sharing, tips, dungeon prizes and Yuzurune made participation materially useful, and the founders credit the Free Tank with the growth inflection (Hakuhodo DY ONE).
  • Executive-level idea review every week, with public status. This yielded 1,351 shipped ideas and an academic case study (J-STAGE).
  • Humans with names, not a brand account, and no approval layer on staff posts (KARTE CX 2020).
  • Offline first contact, started three months in, when the team admitted it "didn't understand community or users"; it ran 25 off-kai over ten years, rotating to new cities (Commune; off-kai #25). "Offline makes online livelier … harsh critics become supporters."
  • Shared hard decisions before making them. When the Free Tank neared empty (Dec 2019), staff published the data, took ideas and changed rules with explanations (staff blog 1244).
  • Measured it rigorously, with monthly activity-vs-churn/referral reports (2019), a 2M-line study (2023) and n=1 interviews to build a growth model (KARTE CX 2023).

What they did wrong

  • The 2018 secret throttling. On 10 Apr 2018 mineo began compressing images and pacing video without notice. Users spotted it, and the backlash ("contrary to Fun with Fans!") made national tech news. The business head apologised on Maineou on 7 May (ITmedia, 2018-05-15; K-tai Watch). Okinaka still cites it as the moment they "vowed never again" (Hakuhodo DY ONE). Consequence: a lasting disclosure norm.
  • Commons economics swung from scarcity to glut. Abuse and resale forced restrictions in Feb 2017 (staff blog 539). The tank nearly emptied in early 2020, which brought tighter rules. Then it overcorrected: by Nov 2026 the tank held ~5.1–5.3 PB, with ~110–155 TB a month deposited in 2026 but only ~9–17 TB withdrawn, because withdrawals are limited to month-end, users under 1 GB, and 1 GB a month (Free Tank; stats API). A best-answer reply says it's now "far from mutual aid … the tank is bloated" (Q&A, 2025-05-07).
  • Gameable status and clique formation. Ranks could be farmed by spamming Nice! (members reached Grand Master with zero comments, one of them in three days: Q&A, 2017). Early on, members complained of a pecking order and quit. The Idea Farm of 2017 turned into "a lawless zone" of debate and personal attacks, which led to a "support-only comments" rule that members still challenge in 2026 (board, 2017; staff blog 552; Idea 346129).
  • Feedback got terser as volume grew. Leadership says "we give honest feedback on why something is hard" (Real Sound). Members in 2026 say most ideas just get "Checked" with no reason. Staff replied that comments are now given only where a meeting decides one is needed, and floated a yearly roundup instead (Idea 346081).
  • Infrastructure and staffing shocks. Push notifications were cut from 10 Sep 2025 to around March 2026 because a vendor service ended (staff blog 2887). Comment volume fell over the same stretch, though no causal link is established. Key personas rotate out under Kansai-Electric-group job rotation. The idol-staffer moved to the fibre business in Aug 2025, and her user farewell event was cancelled "due to internal circumstances" (staff blog 2873).
  • Internal legitimacy is a constant fight. Staff say events "can look like playing" to executives who never attend. Only when decision-makers came to the 2024 festival did support firm up (Hakuhodo DY ONE).
  • Brand humour misfires pushed into the app, such as the 2021 April Fools "Hollywood Zakoshisyoh branch" pop-ups ("mineo suddenly feels creepy … must I cancel?") (Q&A, 2021-04-01).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Make your product's core unit giftable (data, credits, minutes, storage) and route the giving through the community, with thank-you notes. It creates reasons to visit, reciprocity and a social-good story.
  2. Have decision-makers read every idea on a fixed weekly cadence, publish status, and show shipped ideas as a running count.
  3. Run small, candid offline meetings early (they started at 10 people, three months in) and share real business information there. Make attendance a prerequisite for the inner-circle programme.
  4. Let staff be people: nicknames, their own voice, no approval queue, and executives apologising in the community itself.
  5. Measure activity against churn and referral on real account data and present it to leadership, so the community survives budget cycles.

Avoid these

  1. Changing the product behind members' backs. One silent change can undo years of "co-creation" trust overnight.
  2. Shared-economy rules left on autopilot. Rebalance commons, tips and rank formulas as usage shifts, or they turn into farming exploits or meaningless sinks.
  3. Letting feedback decay to "acknowledged". As volume grows, budget for explanations or batch them openly, and protect the community from loss of key staff and vendor outages.
