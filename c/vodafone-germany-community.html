Peak

Most likely 2015 to 2022, when staff moderators still handled account cases by private message and Vodafone ran yearly SuperUser events. Registrations kept rising to 517,537 by Jan 2026 (Wayback homepage counter), but post volume was already shrinking.

Now

2,229,464 posts and 2,576 users online (Wayback, 12 Oct 2026). About 65k new posts in 2025, down from about 83k in 2024 and roughly 100k a year in late 2023. Since 15 Aug 2022 it has been a pure 'customers help customers' forum with no 1:1 staff support.

How it pays off for the company

Support deflection and self-service: the 2015 Lithy entry cites 'service quality, brand reputation and cost reduction'. Also image repair ('improve the image through positive service experience and generate insights', 2017), product feedback from SuperUsers to product managers, beta and crowd testers for cable hardware, and SEO traffic to solved threads. No published savings figure for Germany.

TL;DR

  • What it is: Vodafone Germany's official German-language customer forum at forum.vodafone.de. It opened in October 2009 with 244 users, became "an established Vodafone service channel" on Lithium (now Khoros) around 2012, and absorbed Kabel Deutschland's "Kabelhelden" (2015) and the Unitymedia Community (2020). It counted about 518,000 registrations and 2.18M posts in January 2026.
  • Why people come back: Most visitors are one-off askers with a broken cable line or a billing dispute. The forum lives on a small group of SuperUsers (30 to 40 at most, 22 named in 2025). Vodafone hand-picks them and gives them moderation rights, a private VIP board, early product tests, a two-day yearly meetup with the moderators, and personal ties to staff. Several have posted since 2009.
  • What Vodafone feeds it: staff moderators who screen posts and maintain "accepted solutions", outage alerts (Eilmeldungen), #ServiceZoom help videos, a community blog, crowd tests of routers, sweepstakes, and public praise from the CEO. Until August 2022 the moderators also solved individual account cases by private message.
  • Biggest mistake: on 15 August 2022 Vodafone stopped all individual support by private message. Members now tell newcomers "there is no support here anymore", and the main unofficial forum called the moderators "the only ones you could still rely on". Post volume has fallen every year since (about 83k in 2024, about 65k in 2025). Telekom kept agents in its own forum, and its community is roughly eight times as active.
  • The one lesson: a support community is only as valuable as the path from a public question to someone who can actually fix the account. Remove that path to save money and the volunteer core still answers, but the askers stop coming.

What the community is

The Vodafone Community (slogan "Wissen. Teilen. Helfen.", "Know. Share. Help.") is an owned Khoros forum with boards for mobile, DSL, cable internet and TV, devices, MeinVodafone and e-mail, network outages ("Störungen im Kabel-Netz", "Eilmeldungen"), a Plauderecke (chat corner), Feedback, Hilfe-Videos, a Community-Blog and Gewinnspiele (sweepstakes) (vodafone.de explainer). Anyone can read without an account, and non-customers may post. The forum can also be read inside the MeinVodafone app (Böning interview).

Roles:

  • Moderators, Vodafone employees marked "Moderator/in" with a logo. Until January 2020 their names carried a "VF_" prefix (forum).
  • SuperUsers, private customers with extra rights who are never allowed to touch customer data (SuperUser page).
  • Everyone else, climbing a ladder of about 18 joke ranks from "Youngster" and "Rookie" to "Daten-Fan", "Smart-Analyzer", "Host-Legende", "Royal-Techie" and "Giga-Genie" (ranks post, 2019).

Status today: active but shrinking. The forum still runs on Khoros: the October 2026 homepage carries Lithium markup and showed 2,576 users online (Wayback, 12 Oct 2026). That number likely includes guests. Recent threads are mostly unresolved cable outages and contract disputes. The forum itself blocks automated fetches (Cloudflare), so live pages were read through search snippets and the Wayback Machine.

Unofficial spaces. The largest is the Inoffizielles Vodafone-Kabel-Forum (vodafonekabelforum.de, formerly kdgforum.de). Its members have registered since at least January 2007, and in November 2026 it counted 39,736 members, 770,433 posts and 46,049 topics, with a record of 55,615 simultaneous visitors on 17 May 2026 (homepage snippet). It runs its own wiki "Helpdesk" and is home to the most knowledgeable critics. Some of its people are also official SuperUsers: DerSarde is co-admin there and appears on Vodafone's SuperUser list. Others: kabeluser.de (since 2005), forum.kabel-helpdesk.de, the teltarif forum, and r/Vodafone, a global subreddit with about 2,000 subscribers and 4,708 posts in Arctic Shift that is mostly British.

Origin and strategy

  • 2009 launch. Vodafone announced a customer forum in July 2009 as part of a new strategy and launched it on 2 October 2009 at vodafone.de/forum. Staff moderated it and were meant to help with problems. Spokesperson Carmen Hillebrand said the goal was to let customers "exchange with us, but also with each other" and to "improve service further". On launch day it had 244 users and 349 posts (teltarif, 2 Oct 2009). The 2018 interview says it began on "an open-source solution" (Böning). By 2012 it ran on Lithium and was "an established Vodafone service channel", and Vodafone Group gave its community agents the internal global "Hero Award" (Lithys 2015 entry, Wayback).
  • Strategy in Vodafone's words. Communities "offer significant opportunities in terms of service quality, brand reputation and cost reduction … the portion of self-service is increased" (Lithys 2015). In 2017: "Our goal is to improve the image through positive service experience and to generate insights" (Lithys 2017 snippet). Organisationally it sat in customer service under "Customer Insight Management / Digital Services". Department head Georg Böning ran an operational service team of 25 and was functionally responsible for about 70 social-media service staff (Böning profile).
  • Kabel Deutschland's parallel community. Kabel Deutschland launched "Die Kabelhelden" on 5 February 2013 (Telecompaper). A year later it had 13,000 registered users (handy-dsl-tarif, Feb 2014). Customers wrote 80% of posts and 50% of traffic came from search engines (Smarter Service snippet). Its own KPI deck set targets of at least 30% call deflection on generic topics (slides, Apr 2014).
  • Merger 1 (2015). After Vodafone bought Kabel Deutschland, the two communities merged on Lithium in late 2015. Agents from both teams ran workshops on how their moderation styles differed and visited each other. All SuperUsers were invited to a merger event in Düsseldorf, where they co-wrote the slogan "Know. Share. Help." SuperUsers helped tidy and move content, all users and posts were migrated automatically, and each SuperUser got a personalised mug. The result was ">200,000 users … almost 1 million posts" (Lithys 2016, via r.jina.ai). An agency consultant led the project (Wencour snippet).
  • Merger 2 (2020). Unitymedia, bought from Liberty Global, ran its own community. Its content up to 12 July 2020 was moved over on 20 July 2020, and former Unitymedia users had to register again (forum announcement; vodafonekabelforum).
  • Retreat (2022). "From 15 August 2022 we will no longer offer individual support by private message (PN). Our SuperUsers will gladly help with all public topics … please note they are not Vodafone employees" (official post). Account issues went to the hotline, chat, WhatsApp, Facebook or X. The cut came as Vodafone Germany moved toward automated care: the TOBi chatbot handled about 8M contacts in 2022 (CE-Markt, Feb 2023), followed by a €140M investment in AI customer care in 2024 (Digital Fernsehen) and 2,000 job cuts in Germany (ruhr24). No source states that the forum change was a cost decision. The timing points that way, but that is an inference.
  • Redesign (2023). On 25 July 2023 the forum got a new look modelled on Vodafone's help pages, simpler login and more blog content on the homepage. Members voted on one design option beforehand, until 31 March 2023 (forum).

Net new posts ran at roughly 100k a year from August 2023 to January 2024, then 83,174 in 2024, 64,621 in 2025, and about 46,800 from January to mid-October 2026, roughly 60k a year or 5,000 a month. New registrations fell from 21,127 in 2024 to 13,692 in 2025. The counter shows net posts, so archiving and deletions push it down somewhat. For scale, the Telekom hilft community logged about 520,000 posts a year in 2025 with about 1.7M registered members (see the telekom-hilft report). Vodafone's forum is about one-eighth as active. One solutions leaderboard covering six months (snapshot date unknown, 2025–26) shows how concentrated the work is: one Giga-Genie member had 152 accepted solutions and the next three SuperUsers 71, 59 and 53 (leaderboard snippet).

What keeps people coming back

  1. Climbing to SuperUser. Ranks come from a weighted formula of logins, posts, likes and accepted solutions, which Vodafone keeps secret "to keep it exciting" (Karriereleiter, Dec 2017). The top is not automatic. Moderators "coordinate and appoint" SuperUsers (Böning), although one consumer article says the community can "vote" people in. The perks: a VIP board, "even closer contact with our moderators", event invitations (Karriereleiter), and moderation powers such as closing and moving threads and marking solutions, including enforcing the rules when no staff are online (Lithys 2015).
  2. The yearly SuperUser-meets-Mod weekend. Two days every year. In 2018 that meant the Vodafone Campus, 5G sessions, the Vodafone Innovation Lab, an evening in Düsseldorf's old town and a quiz run by an event agency on day two (forum, Sep 2018). Vodafone called it "a solid part of the community loyalty strategy". A wild card invited one non-SuperUser, and that person reached SuperUser status within six months (Lithys 2015). No evidence was found of events after 2018. The 2016 page still promised a yearly meetup when it was archived in 2025.
  3. Insider access and status. SuperUsers were invited to product, service and software tests and once met "the top managing director" (SuperUser page). CEO Hannes Ametsreiter thanked them publicly in 2018. One SuperUser said the title mattered less than "the possibilities it brings (direct contacts, product tests etc.)" (SuperUser – Wer bist Du?).
  4. Friendship with staff. SuperUsers write about a "Superuser/moderator community that has grown together as people" and name moderators ("die Susi, die Lottis") as the reason they stay (same thread). Moderators and SuperUsers both published "Steckbrief" profiles (2016, 2019).
  5. Light gamification for everyone else: likes, accepted solutions, the leaderboard, and "most active members" and "latest logins" on the homepage.
  6. Outage subscriptions. During big outages a prominent Eilmeldung thread goes up that users can subscribe to (explainer). Askers have a functional reason to come back.

What is missing: there are no rituals for ordinary members (no weekly events, challenges or seasons). After 2022, ordinary askers lost the main reason to return, which was a named moderator who could look into their contract.

The content engine

  • Curated answers. Moderators screen every post, check user-accepted solutions and rewrite them when facts change ("what was right five years ago needn't be current today") (Böning). This is the forum's real asset: long-lived search answers.
  • #ServiceZoom help videos (2023). A presenter, "Melissa", explains digital tools such as the Online-Hilfe, speed tests, the returns portal and the GetConnected app (Hilfe-Videos board).
  • Community-Blog and Vodafone-News. Since 2023 these posts mostly steer users to self-service, for example "Faster to a solution with TOBi" (Feb 2026) and "Report an outage … without a queue" (blog; news).
  • Tests. A Vodafone Crowd Tester sign-up for cable customers started in May 2021 (forum), followed by router crowdtests in 2021–22 (a WiFi 6 Vodafone Station, a FRITZ!Box 6690). Members discussed those tests as much in the unofficial forum as in the official one (vodafonekabelforum).
  • Member content. SuperUser how-tos and long technical threads (signal levels, DOCSIS) exist, but no formal programme rewards guides. Contests ran at the 2015 merger and there is a Gewinnspiele board.

Score 2/5: curated solutions are useful, but there are few original formats, no recurring live programming, and the post-2022 output is mostly deflection.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. A moderator team inside customer service / digital services. Around 2018–2020 the department ran 25 operational staff and coordinated about 70 social-care staff (Böning profile). No current headcount was found. Since 2022 moderators still post, curate and moderate, but they no longer have account access in the forum: "Nobody here, not even the moderators, can see your data" (member, Aug 2026).
  • SuperUsers. About 30 to 40 at their height, 22 named on the official list in 2025. Expectations: follow the rules and netiquette, "a watchful eye, an open ear". Some have stayed for very long: akoch has been a member since 1 October 2009 and has 10,788 posts and 1,721 solutions (profile snippet). SuperUsers mark their official actions in italics to separate them from their private opinions (thread).
  • Rules. Forum rules forbid sales activity and publishing correspondence without consent. Customer data may be requested only by a moderator (rule 1.11), which has been a dead letter since August 2022 (Forenregeln).
  • Feedback loop. SuperUsers sit in on internal events and give "neutral customer feedback" to product managers (Lithys 2015). There is a Feedback board, and members voted on the 2023 redesign. No public idea portal or "you asked, we built" reporting was found.

Growth loops

  • SEO. At Kabel Deutschland, half of community traffic came from search and single threads drew 20,000 to 50,000 views (Smarter Service; Wencour). Vodafone does not publish search figures, but solved threads rank for cable and router error queries.
  • Owned funnels. The forum appears in the MeinVodafone app, in vodafone.de featured articles and in the help pages' design.
  • Acquisition by merger. Most of the registration jumps came from absorbing the Kabelhelden in 2015 and Unitymedia in 2020, not from organic growth.
  • No referral or ambassador-led acquisition was found.

Evidence of business impact

Hard evidence is thin. Vodafone Germany never published a deflection or savings number, unlike Vodafone New Zealand (174,000 calls deflected, Lithys 2015 NZ) or Telekom (>350,000 calls a month, a company claim). What exists: award-entry claims about cost reduction and self-service (2015, 2016, 2017); the CEO's ">5,000 issues solved by SuperUsers" (2018); Vodafone Group's internal Hero Award for the community agents; and Kabel Deutschland's 2014 targets (≥30% call deflection on generic topics) and 80% customer-written posts. Decisions say more than claims: in 2022 Vodafone chose to drop the costly part, staff resolving account cases, and keep the cheap peer layer. That suggests management valued it as deflection, not as a premium service channel. Score 2/5.

Score notes. Retention mechanics 3: a strong SuperUser ladder, events and tests, but nothing for ordinary members. Content engine 2: curated answers plus a few videos and blog posts. Member ownership 4: since 2022 the volunteers carry almost all the answering. Staff investment 2: moderators remain, but 1:1 account support was withdrawn in 2022 and no events are visible after 2018. Business impact evidence 2: award-entry claims only, no German savings figure. Durability 3: 17 years on one domain, but activity has fallen every year since 2023.

What they did right

  1. Started early and stayed. A staff-moderated forum open to non-customers since 2009, still running in 2026 on the same domain. Some SuperUsers have been there the whole time.
  2. Gave super-users real power and face time. Moderation rights, a VIP board, product tests, a yearly two-day event with an Innovation Lab visit, a CEO meeting, public thanks from the CEO. The wild-card invite turned a regular member into a SuperUser within six months (Lithys 2015).
  3. Handled the 2015 merger well. Agents from both companies met, SuperUsers co-created the slogan and helped clean up content, nothing was lost, users moved over automatically, and each SuperUser got a personalised mug (Lithys 2016). It is a good template for any community migration.
  4. Curated answers as a living knowledge base. Moderators verify and update accepted solutions over time.
  5. Outage threads users can subscribe to, giving people one place to watch during an incident.

What they did wrong

  1. Pulled staff support out of a support forum (Aug 2022). Members immediately read it as the end of support: "From 15.08.2022 it will no longer be possible to clear up problems with one of the moderators … only via the hotline or social media" (vodafonekabelforum, 13 Aug 2022). Volunteers now spend their answers redirecting people: "This is a customers-help-customers forum and Vodafone's support here was discontinued in 08.2022" (forum, Jul 2024); "Here in the forum there is no support anymore" (forum, 2024). Consequence: falling post volume (about −22% from 2024 to 2025) and slowing registrations.
  2. Kept making the old promise. Vodafone's own explainer, still live in 2025, says that if nobody replies "a service employee steps in immediately" (vodafone.de), and forum rule 1.11 still describes moderators requesting customer data. Askers still post "I urgently need a moderator's help" in 2026 (homepage snippet).
  3. Ongoing censorship and SuperUser-power disputes. Threads accuse moderators of censorship in 2014, 2019 and 2023, and critics call SuperUsers "self-appointed know-it-alls" with "frightening power" (2018). In 2014 one member complained that "no code of conduct seems to exist for SuperUsers" (2014; 2018; 2023). In a forum full of angry cable customers, hand-picked volunteers with moderation powers look like company enforcers.
  4. Let the expert crowd drift to an unofficial forum. The independent Vodafone-Kabel-Forum (770k posts) has more technical depth on cable and openly says Vodafone showed "no willingness to accept any criticism, least of all from amateurs like us" (vodafonekabelforum, Jul 2023).
  5. Repeated migrations. Two platform merges (2015, 2020) plus a re-registration requirement for Unitymedia users cost history and goodwill. A Unitymedia convert asked why he was still filed under an old customer number (forum).
  6. Opaque ranks. A secret rank formula, plus confusion about what the "gamification options" even mean (Dec 2022 question).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Give super-users real rights. Moderation tools, a private board and a direct line to staff keep a 30-person core active for a decade or more.
  2. Meet in person once a year and invite one outsider. A two-day event with a behind-the-scenes lab visit, plus a "wild card" seat for a rising regular, turns appreciation into loyalty and recruits the next generation.
  3. Bring the volunteers into migrations. Let super-users name the merged community, help clean up content, and move everyone and everything over automatically.
  4. Treat accepted answers as maintained documentation. Staff verify and update solutions so old threads stay correct for search traffic.
  5. Give outages one subscribable home. It turns crisis traffic into a reason to come back.

Avoid these

  1. Don't remove the path to someone who can actually fix things. Members ask in public because they hope a person with account access will see it. Without that, a support community becomes a waiting room, and the volunteers end up as the ones delivering the bad news.
  2. Don't leave promises up after a policy change. Old explainers, rules and FAQs that still promise staff help turn a policy change into a credibility problem.
  3. Don't give volunteers enforcement power without a visible code of conduct and appeals. Otherwise the angriest customers will see them as company enforcers and call it censorship.
