TL;DR

  • What it is: FootJoy's own forum and blog at footjoy.com/community, opened in beta in July 2009 on Telligent community software. Its centerpiece was the FJ Ambassador Program, a points ladder (Member, Bronze, Silver, Gold, then "FJ Ambassador" at 6,000 points). Acushnet's 2016 IPO filing claimed "50,000 committed golfers." The site was switched off in mid-2022 (Acushnet S-1, 2016; Wayback CDX).
  • Why people came back: to earn the ambassador title, which was rare (about 53 people by 2011 and "over 70" by 2022) and gave visible status: a bag tag, an "FJ Ambassador" logo you could put on your shoes and shirts, and ambassador-only content. Ambassadors were also invited on FJ Ambassador Invitationals (FJAI), trips that included golf, a tour of the FootJoy factory and product testing in the company's "Shoe Box" lab. Members later named the Invitationals as the glue: "FJ needs to look no further than the Invitationals and how much continuity an cohesiveness it fostered!" (FJ Community, Jan 2022).
  • What FootJoy fed it: a staff blog (mostly tour-win recaps and product launches, about 30 to 70 posts a year at its height), community-only sweepstakes, "Ask the Tour Players" Q&As, a "Meet the FJ Ambassadors" interview series, and staff answering product questions in the threads. About 10 staff accounts were active in 2011, but one person, digital coordinator Devin Gmitter, effectively ran it.
  • Biggest mistake: everything depended on one dedicated employee and one events budget. The Invitationals were cut for cost. The coordinator left (about 2017). The blog's last post was in September 2017, and it was still the top story in 2022. Every post still needed a moderator's approval, so with no moderator posts simply went unpublished. Activity fell about 95% between 2015 and 2021 (estimate), and FootJoy replaced the forum with FJ Insider, a shipping-and-perks loyalty program, without moving members' points over.
  • The lesson: a scarce, earned status plus in-person access can build a deeply loyal core for very little money. But if the community isn't tied to a metric leadership watches, and isn't staffed beyond one person, it dies when that person leaves. Members who were "all good customers to start with" can't prove incremental revenue on their own (Chris C, Team Titleist, Nov 2022).

What the community is

The FootJoy Community (members called it the "FJC") was the footwear-and-apparel sister of Acushnet's Team Titleist. Both ran on the same Telligent ("Community Server") platform. In 2011 its navigation was Home, Blog, Forums, FJ Ambassadors and Sweepstakes, plus "Invite Your Friends," a Twitter feed, a "Featured Ambassadors" roll and a "Meet the FootJoy Team" panel (Wayback, May 2011). There were seven forum categories: General, Tour, Shoes, Gloves, Apparel, Socks, and Courses & Travel. Small regional spin-offs (FJ Community Canada, with its own bag-tag sign-up, and UK and Canada blogs) appear in search results and on a leftover test server, but no size data survives (footjoy.ca bag tag post; test.footjoy.com nav).

Status today: defunct. The community home page answered normally on May 11, 2022 and redirected (301) by July 14, 2022 (Wayback CDX). On Team Titleist, a member noticed on August 22, 2022 that "the FJ community is gone," and an ambassador replied that "it did go about a month ago" (Team Titleist thread). The old ambassador-program URL now redirects to the FJ Insider page (footjoy.com). FJ Insider launched on January 20, 2022 and offers a release calendar, exclusive products, free express shipping on orders over $149, an Insider customer-service line and "maybe even the chance to test new products," but it has no forum (FJ Insider; Milled, Jan 20, 2022).

Unofficial spaces: no FootJoy subreddit or fan forum emerged (the Arctic Shift archive has no r/footjoy; its comment search timed out, so wider Reddit coverage is unverified). After the shutdown, FJ talk moved to Team Titleist, where many ambassadors were already active ("A lot of us were always Acushnet loyalists and participated on both sites"), and to independent forums such as The Hackers Paradise (Team Titleist thread, Nov 2022).

Origin and strategy

FootJoy's blog began in March 2008, with video of FJ president Jim Connor and "FJ TV" tour pieces (Wayback blog URL list). The social layer opened in beta in July 2009. Golf blogger Tony Korologos called it "a social networking engine similar to my other site The Golf Space, only obviously with a FJ flare," and noted beta registration bugs (Hooked On Golf Blog, July 22, 2009). Most early ambassadors joined in July and August 2009. From launch, the welcome text promised that members could "ultimately, unlock exclusive opportunities by becoming an FJ Ambassador!" (Wayback, Nov 2009).

Business logic. The 2016 IPO prospectus is the only official statement of purpose: "What began 10 years ago as a method of reaching golfers on a direct basis has evolved into an open community of 50,000 committed golfers who connect with the brand and one another. As engagement levels increase, members earn access to new product introductions and opportunities to participate in unique promotions and events" (S-1, June 2016; repeated in the FY2016 10-K). The points table shows what FootJoy wanted from members: data and commerce actions (email opt-in 100 points, complete profile 200, register a product 10, write a review 20, email a wish list or MyJoys design to a friend 10, and buy a pair of custom MyJoys for 500 each, up to 10,000) (ambassador page, Wayback May 2013). EDGAR full-text search finds "FJ Community" only in the 2016 IPO documents and the FY2016 10-K. It is gone from later 10-Ks (for example the FY2019 10-K), while Team Titleist stayed. That is a quiet sign of when it stopped being a strategic asset.

Champions. Chris Garrett wrote the blog and, according to a manager's recommendation on his LinkedIn, "shepherded our new online community" and ran FootJoy's social media (LinkedIn). Devin Gmitter (Senior Digital FootJoy Coordinator, Acushnet, 2011 to 2017, per ZoomInfo, a secondary source) became the day-to-day owner. He wrote blog posts, answered product questions and ran sweepstakes (Wayback 2011 home; sweepstakes, Nov 2015). Members say: "Devin was pretty much in charge of the community and when he left the company it all went down hill. He was never replaced" (Team Titleist, Nov 3, 2022).

The ambassador ladder saturated early: about 53 ambassadors within 22 months of launch, then only about 20 more over the next 11 years.

What keeps people coming back

  • A scarce, earned title. The 6,000-point FJ Ambassador threshold was steep. At 3 points per reply, that is about 2,000 replies, though bonus actions sped it up. Promotion was a public rite: when Brian D reached it in April 2013 he announced it on Team Titleist ("a significant milestone for me. I reached the FJA status") and drew 15 congratulations (Team Titleist, Apr 2013). The rank icons (bronze, silver, gold, ambassador) appeared next to every post.
  • Status you could wear. Ambassadors could buy "FJ Ambassador" logoed merchandise and put the ambassador logo on custom MyJoys and shirts. Members chasing the rank talked about it ("wait until you make FJA and then order a pair with the Ambassador logo!") (thread 7385). An ambassador bag tag was the proof ("I have the bag tag to prove it") (Team Titleist, Mar 2011). Ambassador polos still resell on eBay as "rare" (eBay).
  • In-person Invitationals (FJAI). These were invitation-only trips for ambassadors: golf (one at Rhode Island Country Club), a tour of the factory and original buildings in Brockton, a visit to the Shoe Box test lab, FJ gear, and briefings on roadmap items such as a mobile app ("We talked about it at the FJAI in September") (thread 9594; thread 13438; thread 12010; thread 10497). At least two were held ("FJAI 2"). Dating them from thread IDs puts them at about 2011 to 2013 or 2014 (estimate). Members credit them with the community's cohesion, and their end with its decline: "Really declined when they stopped doing the invitationals!" (Team Titleist, Sep 2022).
  • Insider product testing. Ambassadors tested shoes at the Shoe Box against competitors' models ("I was just there for a test of some new FJ's and a competitor's new shoe... you will all be happy come the 2014 releases") (thread 12223). Brian D recalled "when I did receive something to test, I took it seriously and took honor in being selected" (FJC, Jan 2022).
  • Small rituals. Automatic "Member Milestones" posted join anniversaries ("It is badputter's 6 year anniversary on the FootJoy Community") and first posts (Wayback, Aug 2015). There were birthday threads for staff ("Happy Birthday Devo!", thread 8764), community-only sweepstakes, and a member-run FJC fantasy golf league that kept going into 2022 after staff had gone (thread 18436).
  • Show-and-tell of product. The forum worked as a collectors' wall. "It was indeed fun posting the products and the coordination of apparel and shoes. Like a store display online for others to look at" (Team Titleist, Nov 2022). The busiest threads were about collecting, such as "2019 Shoe Additions" with 186 replies (Wayback, Oct 2019).
  • Friendship. "I've met actual, real human friends from this forum" (joshgiano, Jan 2022). Members also held a non-sponsored get-together in Kansas City after the official events ended.

The content engine

The company's output was thin and promotional: tour-win recaps ("Kisner Closes the Deal..."), product launches, photo-shoot "behind the scenes" posts, Masters and PGA interview clips (2009), "Ask the Tour Players," where members sent questions to tour pros (2009), FJ "challenges" and quizzes, MyJoys design contests ("2010 Most Unique MyJoys Winners"), and "community-only" sweepstakes from 2013 to 2016 (blog URL list). The one piece of community-centered editorial was the "Meet the FJ Ambassadors" interview series: "an opportunity to go behind the screen name and find out more about these passionate golfers who contribute unparalleled time and resources to the Community" (FJ blog; tag link in thread 6377). There was no how-to or fitting education program comparable to Titleist's, no webinars, and no podcast. Members could keep personal blogs ("Working on becoming a FJ Ambassador!!", footjoy.ca), and most of the real content was member-made: collection photos, product IDs and sale alerts. Score: content engine 2/5, because the staff content was mostly press releases and stopped in 2017.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff: ten "Meet the FootJoy Team" accounts in 2011 (Chris Garrett, Chris Kerr, Devin Gmitter, FJICON, Fougere18, JayCon, Kenny DeAlmeida, MyJoysGirl, Nick Gilliam, Rob Kelley) (Wayback, May 2011), but in practice there was one full-time owner. After he left, "FJ did have other associates assigned to the FJ Community... the new ones had other responsibilities in addition to FJC" (Chris C, Nov 2022). Score: staff investment 2/5.
  • Ambassador program: the points ladder described above. The rules changed over time: by 2016 the commerce and data actions had been removed, leaving only posting and MyJoys sharing (Wayback 2016). By 2022 "1 point per dollar" spent on FootJoy.com had been added, capped at 4,000 (Wayback 2022). Perks: ambassador-only content, logo merchandise, "other exclusive opportunities" (Invitationals, tests).
  • Moderation: every forum was pre-moderated ("You have posted to a forum that requires a moderator to approve posts before they are publicly available"). Trusted members could be whitelisted ("I was given the ability to post things immediately without moderation," Aloha Bruce, Nov 2022). Once no one was watching the queue, posts disappeared: "I tried posting many times this year and none were published" (William B, Sep 2022).
  • Feedback loop: informal. Staff answered fit and model questions in threads (thread 11995), and ambassadors fed lab tests. Members also used the forum to vent: "Sorry FJ need to be honest. Next time consult your community!" (thread 8800), and in 2019 a thread was titled "2019 PRO/SL NOT WATERPROOF/FOOT MOLD/CRACKS GALORE" (thread 18114). No evidence was found that FootJoy reported back on changes made because of the community.
  • Member ownership: 3/5. Members ran the fantasy league and meetups and kept the forum alive for five years without staff, but they had no moderation powers.
  • Superusers as staff: after the closure, at least three ambassadors worked as FootJoy.com chat agents: "I'm chatting for FJ.com along with Bobby Swain and Ted Davies, both FJAs" (Chris C, Nov 2022).

Growth loops

Points paid for referrals: emailing a wish list, a MyJoys design or a newsletter to a friend earned 10 points each (capped at 100), and the 2011 sidebar had an "Invite Your Friends" link. The biggest cross-pollination was with Team Titleist: ambassadors recruited there ("I would definitely recommend signing up for the FootJoy community and working your way towards FJ Ambassador status," Mar 2011). FootJoy.com banners pulled shoppers in: "I learned about the FJC when I visited FootJoy.com and saw a banner for the community and ambassador program" (ambassador interview). Forum pages ranked in search for product questions (many still appear in Brave results), but there is no evidence the community was a meaningful acquisition channel. In 2018 FootJoy UK put community money into someone else's forum instead, becoming title partner of the Golf Monthly forum with "Tech Talk" Q&As, "First Look" previews, "Post of the Month" gear prizes and "FJ Forum Experiences" (HQ visits, prototype focus groups). Those were the same tactics the FJ Community had run, now borrowed on a third party's audience (Golf Monthly, Oct 13, 2018).

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: none found. Acushnet has never published retention, revenue or NPS figures for the community.
  • Company claims: "an open community of 50,000 committed golfers" that "connect with the brand and one another" (S-1 and FY2016 10-K). The section was dropped from later 10-Ks.
  • Insider skepticism: "I'm not sure how much additional business it generated for FootJoy outside the Community Members - who were all good customers to start with! Budget cuts and costs wiped out the Invitationals" (Chris C, longtime member and later FJ.com chat agent, Nov 2022). An ambassador countered that it "had to do good things for business" (Military, Nov 2022).
  • Proxy: the points table tied status to MyJoys purchases (up to 10,000 points, or 20 custom pairs), and later to dollars spent. That suggests FootJoy saw it as a high-value-buyer program, but no outcome data exists. Score: business impact evidence 1/5.

What they did right

  1. A clear, aspirational status goal with public rites. The ambassador title was hard to get, visible on every post, and wearable (logo merch, bag tag). It created years-long engagement among about 70 superfans, many of whom were still posting in 2022 (ambassador page; Team Titleist 2013 thread).
  2. In-person access as the top reward. Factory tours, lab testing and golf with staff turned online acquaintances into friends and brand evangelists. Members still cited the FJAI eight or more years later (threads 13438 and 18423).
  3. Members included in product testing. Inviting ambassadors into the Shoe Box made them feel like insiders and gave R&D real users (thread 12223).
  4. Staff present in the threads in the early years. Named employees answered product questions and joined in socially (birthday threads, the "Meet the FootJoy Team" panel), which built ties to real people at the company.
  5. Cheap, durable rituals. Anniversary milestones and the member-run fantasy league needed no staff and outlived the staff.

What they did wrong

  1. Key-person dependency. One coordinator ran the community. When he left (about 2017, per ZoomInfo and members), he "was never replaced," and the replacements had other jobs. Consequence: the blog stopped in September 2017 and activity fell about 90% within two years (ID estimates).
  2. Cutting the highest-value ritual first. The Invitationals were "wiped out" by budget cuts and costs. Members date the decline to that moment. The status ladder then promised "exclusive opportunities" that no longer existed, which "lessened the value of being a FJ Ambassador" (pulplvr, Jan 2022).
  3. A ladder that saturated and stopped renewing. About 53 ambassadors in the first 22 months, then about 20 more in 11 years. The core aged in place, and newcomers saw an unreachable 6,000-point wall and a stale front page (a 2017 blog post at the top in 2022).
  4. Pre-moderation without moderators. A setting that made sense with staff became a silent failure: members' posts were never published in 2022, which drove away the last active members.
  5. Moving attention to social media and a coupon-style loyalty program, and leaving members behind. Members objected that the focus on Facebook, Instagram and Twitter "obviates the community that developed over ten years here." FJ Insider launched in January 2022, and the forum closed about six months later with no announcement found and no transfer of points: "What happens with our ambassador points?" (Anthony C, Aug 2022). Consequence: the loyalists moved to Team Titleist, a sister brand, or third-party forums.
  6. Never proving value. The community appeared in the IPO story, then vanished from filings. With no metric connecting it to revenue or R&D, it had nothing to defend it at budget time.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. A scarce, named top tier with a visible badge that members can wear or show off outside the app. Status that travels (logo gear, bag tag) does marketing for you.
  2. Make in-person access the top reward (HQ visits, lab tours, rounds with staff). It turns users into friends, and friends keep each other coming back for years.
  3. Bring top members into product testing and tell them it matters. It's cheap R&D and the strongest "insider" feeling you can give.
  4. Automate small rituals (join anniversaries, first-post shout-outs) and support member-run traditions (leagues, meetups) that can survive without staff.
  5. Link the points system to business actions (reviews, registrations, referrals, purchases), so the program has measurable outputs to show leadership.

Avoid these

  1. One-person communities. Budget for at least two owners and write down the playbook. Report results (test-panel output, buyer LTV versus non-members) every year so the program survives the next cost review and the next departure.
  2. Cutting the flagship ritual while keeping the promise. If the perks behind a tier go away, rework the tier openly. A ladder to nothing breeds resentment.
  3. Quiet sunsets and settings nobody owns. Don't leave pre-moderation on without moderators, and don't replace a community with a loyalty program without migrating members' status and telling them first.
