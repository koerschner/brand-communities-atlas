How it pays off for the company

The community is the audience Joyrun sells to brands. Advertising is the largest revenue line (brand marketing to runners, race sponsors' promotional budgets, shoe launches), with paid online-marathon medals, race registration and operations, membership (RMB 19/month, 218/year), and e-commerce/affiliate on top. Clubs and races replace paid user acquisition: the company says it does not buy traffic.

TL;DR

  • What it is. Joyrun is a Guangzhou-built running app (launched Feb 2014) whose community layer is in-app running clubs (跑团), a WeChat-Moments-style runner feed, leaderboards, badges, and the "online marathon" it invented in January 2015. It reported more than 130M registered users, over 2.2M daily actives and 29,000 registered clubs in 2021-22, and it is still shipping updates in November 2026 (21jingji; App Store CN).
  • Why people come back. Local clubs give runners people to run with and a daily check-in that teammates see. Paid online marathons give them a physical, city-themed medal that they only get if they actually finish, and anti-cheat makes the medal mean something. Pledge runs (约定跑) put money on the line. Years of run history, PBs and shoe mileage pile up in the app, and it is hard to export them.
  • What the company feeds it. It vets every club (leader's ID card, 3-5 day review) and purges inactive ones. It runs a club league, an annual gala for club leaders, women-only city races, a medal design team, editorial columns, race "big data" reports, and a gear-tester program in which brands give members shoes in exchange for reviews.
  • The biggest mistake. It tied the business to race-season brand marketing, so COVID wiped out about 40% of ad revenue and halved total revenue in 2020. The company answered with spreading ads and pop-ups, a run of side bets (a cycling app, an "RFT metaverse" of mythical beasts, NBA FIT, rehab capsules), and a closed data policy that power users resent.
  • The one lesson. Make your community's smallest unit a real, local, accountable group that you curate, and make your status object (here the medal) costly to fake. That combination got Joyrun from 10-something-percent 7-day retention to category leadership in under two years without buying traffic.

What the community is

Joyrun (悦跑圈, "joyful running circle") is a GPS run tracker with a social layer. The founders treated that layer as the product from day one: "From the name you can see we are doing social, a circle that belongs to runners" (Sina, 2016). The official spaces are:

  • Runner feed (跑友圈). A WeChat-Moments-style timeline of runs, photos, likes and comments. It also has an editor-picked "精选" stream, columns (赛事资讯 race news, 装备测评 gear reviews, 运动科普 sports science), and official accounts you can follow (sspai, 2017).
  • Running clubs (跑团). Groups built around a place, school or employer, with a notice board, photo album, member rankings, daily check-in and club events. Clubs are ranked against each other by average mileage per member, so small clubs can compete with big ones (woshipm analysis). Creating a club takes a formal application: club name, location, the leader's real name, Joyrun ID, phone, email and both sides of their ID card, reviewed in 3-5 working days (Joyrun FAQ). A separate club-function agreement lets Joyrun suspend or dissolve clubs that don't take adequate safety measures (agreement).
  • Online marathons and themed online runs. Sign up, run the distance anywhere on race day with the app recording, and receive a physical medal, an engraved name option, a badge and an e-certificate (Joyrun OLM page).
  • Pledge runs (约定跑, from Oct 2017). Members join a "running class" with a deposit. People who complete the agreed runs get their deposit back and split the forfeits of those who don't (Sina, 2017).
  • IRL. The Pink Women's Run (粉红女子跑, from April 2015; 2,000-runner city editions at RMB 128), "悦跑客栈" race-weekend hospitality at the Beijing Marathon, the Running Club League (跑团联赛, from 2019), and annual club-leader galas (about page; zuicool).

The most visible unofficial spaces are runner discussion on Zhihu, Xiaohongshu, Hupu and V2EX, and the many independent clubs that use Joyrun as their tool. Changsha's "悦跑团", with 8,800 registered members and 2,500-3,000 active runners in 2022, is one example (voc.com.cn). Reddit is essentially irrelevant for this subject, and Arctic Shift queries timed out, so no Reddit evidence is used here.

Status today: active. Version 5.47.15 shipped in early November 2026, the App Store CN rating is 4.9 from 330k ratings, and the company's news account was still posting in September 2026, including a new "团队PK" (team versus team) mode and a story contest for Shanghai Marathon entries (App Store CN; 163.com account).

Origin and strategy

Co-founders Liang Feng (梁峰, CEO, a former telecom-standards engineer), Qi Yu, Wu Wenye and Lu Jia first ran "爱运动" (2011-13), a football and basketball venue-booking and ticketing startup. It lost about RMB 3M: "we wanted to do everything and did nothing well" (Pencil News, 2016). In that company's data, marathon-entry bookings were about 30% of volume, and when the founders embedded themselves in runner chat groups they found strong social demand ("a photo of a pretty runner or a scenic trail gets a row of likes"). They picked running because it had a big base, was still growing, and was lightly controlled by the state, unlike stadium sports (Sina, 2016).

The strategy was community first, monetization designed in. Liang: "Every feature I build is ultimately for monetization, including gear discussion and how users share" (Sina, 2016). His model of sports revenue has four parts: tickets, merchandise, sponsorship and IP licensing. On the user side he named three drivers: participation, achievement ("vanity"), and winning. The online marathon was built straight from the achievement driver.

Early growth came from app-store featuring, which brought 600-700k sign-ups in three months, but "stickiness was low, 7-day retention was only in the teens." The team decided it had to "go where runners are" through races (Pencil News). In January 2015, at the 10th Xiamen Marathon, sponsor Xtep (特步) asked Joyrun to give medals to half-marathon finishers who had recorded their run in the app. The booth was mobbed: about 2,000 medals were handed out on site, 6,000+ engraved medals were mailed afterwards, and nearly 20,000 people completed the race remotely. Daily sign-ups went "from thousands to tens of thousands" (Pencil News). That became the online-marathon format.

Funding: angel round from Qihoo 360 (Aug 2014), a USD 3M A round led by Innovation Works, a USD 18M B round from 动域资本 (Dec 2015, explicitly to build the "running-club economy" 跑团经济), a RMB 100M C round led by 创世伙伴 (Mar 2018), and a C+ round (Sep 2019) for "scenario e-commerce" (Sina, 2016; Baidu Baike; TechNode, 2018; about page). Joyrun also bought into or acquired race-operations, trail, training, rehab, travel and magazine companies: about 10 by 2016, 11 acquisitions by 2018, and "more than 20" invested by 2020 (Sina 2016; TechNode; Lanxiong Sports, 2020, snippet only).

Trajectory. Explosive growth from 2015 to 2017 came on the back of China's marathon boom (races grew more than 30x from 2014 to 2019). Joyrun claims DAU kept rising through 2021, but the headline user count has been frozen at "130M" since 2022, and no third-party MAU figure (QuestMobile, for example) was found. All user numbers are company-reported registrations, not active users. The about page also contradicts itself, giving 2,500 cities in Chinese and 2,100 in English.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Curated local clubs as the atomic unit. Liang's club doctrine is "庙、僧、经、戒、事": a temple (an online club plus a physical route), monks (veterans who can run, organize or sponsor), scripture (a purpose), precepts ("often say no; kick out rule-breakers so those who stay know their value"), and events ("constant activities keep the group alive") (Jiemian, 2018). Joyrun pushes clubs to stay at 50 members or fewer and to split into sub-clubs, because "large clubs that last have actually split into sub-clubs" (cyzone, snippet). Inactive clubs are cleared out regularly (Sina 2016). Inside a club, every member's daily run shows up as a check-in, and member rankings and per-capita inter-club rankings supply peer pressure. Club events can be promoted into the app's official activity feed (CSDN changelog).
  2. Medals that require effort. Joyrun launched with anti-cheat as a headline feature ("the first running app with anti-cheating", Feb 2014), because "we wanted to gather people who truly love running" (about page; Pencil News). Its slogan is "不完赛，无奖牌" (no finish, no medal). The first online marathons were free, but only a few tens of thousands of more than 100k registrants finished, so Joyrun moved to paid entry and refused medals to anyone who paid but didn't run, or ran but didn't pay (cyzone, snippet). Liang: "Users aren't buying a medal; we want it to be a race" (Sina 2016).
  3. Collectible, local, ritualized medals. A dedicated medal-design team made each medal a city symbol: a Lanzhou noodle bowl, a Dalian conch, a Beijing candied-haw stick, and two January 2016 medals that join into a heart. Online entrants got bib numbers and entry certificates "so they feel they're in a real marathon, not a game" (Pencil News; Digitaling). Collectors were rewarded: anyone who bought all eight 2015 medals at RMB 19.9 received a medal-shaped mooncake box (smzdm, 2020).
  4. Money on the line. In pledge runs, finishers split the forfeited deposits; a 10,000-person pledge class ran in February 2018 (Sina, 2018). One user: "several times I wanted to quit because of weather or work, and the deposit kept me going" (Baijiahao, 2021).
  5. Levels, badges and streaks. There is a runner progression ("一级跑者 → 二级跑者 → 超凡跑者"), and top-tier runners are encouraged to volunteer and help others (cyzone, snippet). The app also has an honor badge wall, distance and streak badges, and counts of consecutive weeks run.
  6. Gear identity. "My Shoes" (Jun 2015) logs mileage per shoe. AI shoe recognition (2020) and a crowd-sourced shoe library with member reviews make Joyrun the place where runners' gear opinions live (about page; China Daily).
  7. Switching costs. Years of runs, PBs and race memories sit in the app, and export is limited. One 2026 user still opens it after every watch run "because almost all my running data is on Joyrun" (Zhihu, Jan 2026, snippet). Joyrun has also turned other apps' shutdowns into acquisition. When Nike Run Club left mainland China (Jul 8, 2022; 8M+ registered users there), Joyrun offered a migration path and a commemorative badge for the "movers". When Migu 善跑 shut down (Aug 31, 2024), Joyrun was named one of the official destinations for its data (Tencent News, 2024; Tencent Cloud).

Score, retention mechanics: 4/5. Clubs, effort-gated medals, deposits, badges and lock-in form a strong, layered system. A 5 would need evidence of member-to-member ties outside clubs, and the general feed is described as quiet.

The content engine

  • Editorial. "悦跑说" (Aug 2015) covers race news, gear, training, runner stories and running culture. Its columns were judged "much more useful" than the picked feed (Pencil News; sspai). The company produced a marathon documentary, Running Diary (跑步日记, Nov 2016; one runner doing 16 marathons in 6 days across Taiwan), and a print culture magazine, 跑将 (Douban).
  • Data as content. The "悦跑数据研究院" (data institute) publishes shoe-share reports for big races, such as "51.93% of sub-3 runners at Qingdao wore Li-Ning" or Xtep's 43.8% share among Xiamen sub-3 runners in 2024. These reports trend on Weibo (one hashtag had 68.8M reads) and double as brand sales collateral (Sina Finance 2021; 36Kr). A former Runner's World China GM runs it as VP (Guancha, 2024).
  • Live and tools. Race Live (Sep 2017), in-app livestreaming (2020), training plans, warm-up videos, and run-track videos (the most-used feature of 2019) (about page; China Daily).
  • Member content. 全民体验官 ("everyone's a tester", Sep 2020): members put down a deposit, receive new gear, complete set review tasks and get the deposit back. HOKA's first drop gave 1,000 people the Speedgoat 4. Brands get site-wide placement, UGC and sales commission (China Daily). Club leaders and elite runners act as KOLs, but Liang says elites "are KOLs here but don't represent everyone" (Sina 2016).

Score, content engine: 3/5. The data reports are distinctive. In-app education is thin compared with Keep's course library, and the curated feed was criticized as motivational filler.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. More than 200 at peak and 160 by 2022, with departures described as natural attrition (21jingji). Named roles include a creative director, the COO (Wu Wenye), the data-institute VP and a medal-design team. No figures were found for a dedicated community or club-success team. By contrast, Codoon ran a "sports group service team", club grading and a free online "running-club business school" (Jiemian).
  • Club-leader programs. "享跑荟" sharing sessions for club leaders, elite runners and brands. A Club Gala (Jan 2018, Guangzhou, "hundreds of club leaders"; Mar 2019, Beijing with Nike, ~80 outstanding clubs). The Running Club League with Buick, certified by the Chinese Athletics Association: six city legs in 2019, more than 200 clubs at the 2021 Chongqing leg, and about 150k runners claimed (about page; Jiemian; Sina Finance).
  • Rules. Real-identity club leaders, safety obligations, the power to dissolve clubs, and content review (agreement).
  • Feedback. The founder is explicitly not user-led: "Examples of changing based on feedback exist but are few, because many requests aren't representative." The team segments runners by level and builds for the common need (Sina 2016). Stride and cadence metrics were added because users asked (Pencil News). No public ideas portal was found.

Score, member ownership: 4/5. About 29,000 member-led clubs, some with thousands of members, run weekly IRL routines on Joyrun's tooling. Score, staff investment: 3/5. The galas, league and vetting are real, but there is no evidence of dedicated club-success staffing on Codoon's scale.

Growth loops

  • Race-day acquisition. Partnering with race sponsors (Xtep at Xiamen) and later the Chinese Athletics Association (sole official online-marathon partner from Sep 2016) turned every big race into an acquisition event. Overseas, Joyrun became Nagoya Marathon's online partner in 2020 (about page).
  • Shareable proof. Medals, certificates and run-track videos get posted to WeChat Moments.
  • Clubs recruit locally. Club leaders bring their members onto the app.
  • B2B mini-programs instead of ads. "We don't advertise or buy traffic." Joyrun builds small apps for public walking and running events and harvests those participants: "precise and cheap; these people already have the habit" (21jingji).
  • Absorbing competitors' orphans. NRC (2022) and Migu 善跑 (2024) migrations, plus anecdotal Keep defections. In October 2026 a Keep runner angry about paywalled goal runs tagged Joyrun as the fallback (smzdm, Oct 2026). This is thin, anecdotal evidence for the coordinator's "Keep runners say they will defect" note.
  • Brand campaigns. adidas Run for the Oceans in China (2018-22) ran partly on Joyrun (Sina Finance; SocialBeta).

Evidence of business impact

Everything here is company claims. Joyrun is private and no filings exist.

  • Profitable from early 2016 at about RMB 4M monthly revenue, with the B-round cash "unused" (Sina 2016; Pencil News).
  • Advertising is the largest revenue line. Race-sponsor promotion plus brand marketing to runners made up 40% of ad revenue and "basically disappeared" under COVID. Revenue halved in 2020, with losses of several million RMB in 2020 and 2021, though the company was "overall profitable" by 2022 (21jingji).
  • Joyrun's recommendations drove "about RMB 100M a year" of adidas sales before COVID (CEO claim, same source), while Liang admitted "e-commerce hasn't been done well."
  • Income from users was "very little" in 2018 (event fees and merchandise), and the community's value was sold to B2B buyers: "we help B-side make money" (Jiemian).
  • The best hard evidence of community-driven retention is the founder's own before-and-after: 7-day retention in the teens with app-store users, then 10x daily sign-ups after the race and club pivot (Pencil News). No cohort or churn data was found.

Score, business impact evidence: 2/5. It is all founder interviews.

What they did right

  1. Invented a ritual that scales. The online marathon turned a scarce, lottery-gated event into something anyone could earn on race day, without diluting it (paid entry, anti-cheat, real medals). Competitors copied it within months: Codoon's first was April 2015, and Keep followed in 2016 (Zhihu, 2015; CBNData).
  2. Curated clubs over open groups. Vetting, purging and the ≤50 guideline kept clubs real. Codoon had far more "groups" (200k+), but Joyrun's feed is described as runner-only and "where the running gods live" (Baijiahao 2021).
  3. Stayed vertical. Joyrun refused hardware ("fake smart") and fitness classes, so the app stayed the default for serious runners and the channel brands use for shoe launches: "shoe launches will always come to us" (Sina 2016; 21jingji).
  4. Turned community data into a product brands pay for. Shoe-share reports earn press for Joyrun and give brands proof points they can use.
  5. Welcomed refugees. The NRC and Migu migration badges are cheap, kind gestures that recruit users at the moment they are most open to switching.
  6. Didn't over-monetize the core. Tracking, clubs and the feed stayed free. Membership is optional, and members' own comparisons describe the shop as "tucked in a submenu" (Baijiahao 2021).

What they did wrong

  1. Business tied to the race calendar. "Being professional was both our harbor and the main cause of this heavy blow." Liang described the company as "苟延残喘" (barely surviving) and in "a serious wall phase" in 2022 (21jingji).
  2. Ad creep. "I reinstalled it and found N more ads… it's become an ad-receiving platform, 99 official ad messages!" (Zhihu, Jun 2021, snippet). Pop-up ads arrived around v5.45.1, even though "for most of the past it had few ads" (Zhihu, c. 2025, snippet). The App Store privacy label now lists tracking across other companies' apps.
  3. Closed data. No export, and an unofficial route was later blocked (V2EX, Nov 2020, titled "Never use Joyrun as your running app"). Lock-in keeps some users, but it angers the power users who are a vertical app's evangelists.
  4. Scattered bets. A 2015 headline already said Joyrun "wants to do too much" (cyzone). By 2020-22 the list included the ImIN cycling app with the Tour de France China series (2021), NBA FIT, "rehab capsules", "RFT metaverse beasts", smart running tracks and a physical runner station (China Daily; Sina 2021). No evidence was found that any of these grew the community.
  5. Reliability breaks trust in streaks. A streak bug wiped out 10 weeks: "2016-2022, 1,038 runs, 9,387.96 km, 304 weeks, goodbye Joyrun" (Zhihu, 2022, snippet). Apple Watch sync complaints also led to uninstalls (Zhihu, 2021, snippet).
  6. Platform compliance. In Nov 2018 Apple pulled the app for using hot-update code (guideline 2.5.2) (Sina Finance). A 2022 claim that regulators also delisted the Android app could not be verified.
  7. A quiet general feed. "On Codoon and Keep a run gets dozens to hundreds of likes; on Joyrun only a few" (Baijiahao 2021). The social energy lives in clubs, not in the app-wide feed.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Make the smallest unit a curated, local, accountable group. Vet the leaders, cap group size or encourage splitting, prune dead groups, and rank groups by per-capita activity so small ones can win.
  2. Make your status object costly to fake and worth displaying. Charge for it, verify the effort, and design it so it is local and collectible.
  3. Borrow someone else's calendar. Attach your ritual to real-world events your users already care about (race day), then add your own recurring events.
  4. Invest in leaders, not just members. Galas, leagues and sharing sessions for group leaders cost little compared with the number of members each leader brings.
  5. Welcome refugees. When a competitor shuts down, offer migration plus a symbolic welcome such as a badge.

Avoid these

  1. Don't let community value depend on one external revenue cycle (race sponsorship). Diversify before a shock hits, not after.
  2. Don't fund the business by degrading the space with pop-ups and ad pushes. Core users notice, and they are the ones who would otherwise defend you.
  3. Don't use data lock-in as your retention strategy. It angers power users, and a single streak bug can turn years of loyalty into a public goodbye post.
