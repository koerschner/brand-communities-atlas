TL;DR

  • What it is: Lynk & Co's Chinese owner ecosystem, branded Co:Club. It has 300+ dealer-anchored city owner clubs (Co客领地), an app community with 7.2M registered users, an annual owner conference (Co客大会, every year since 2018), an elected owner council (Co客理事会, 2021), a network of 12,000+ owner-run shops that give perks to anyone holding a Lynk key (领地伙伴), an owner-written magazine and co-designed merchandise. None of this went to Europe, which got clubhouse showrooms instead (lynk-co-clubs report).
  • Why people come back: a shared identity ("Co客"), local clubs that run something almost every day (5,521 events in 2023 alone), a points currency worth real money (10 points = RMB 1) earned by checking in and posting, and the feeling of being listened to through round tables and the council.
  • What the company feeds it: heavily. A GM-level champion (Lin Jie) runs it, there is a yearly festival with product launches and giveaways worth hundreds of millions of yuan in "owner benefits", and the brand pays for co-created products, magazines, films, charity, AED and first-aid training.
  • Biggest mistake: when owners used the official app in March 2023 to publish a joint statement about abandoned infotainment updates, Lynk & Co shut off posting in the app's community the same night. Owners published two more statements in five days and called it arrogance. A second owner joint statement followed in August 2024.
  • The one lesson: build the community around owners' own lives and businesses, not just your product. The partner-shop network turns customers' cafés and guesthouses into club venues. But a community this engaged will also organise against you, so prepare a way to answer collective complaints instead of muting them.

What the community is

"Co客" (roughly "Co-ers") is what Lynk & Co calls its Chinese owners; "Co" is glossed as collaboration, community and connection (NetEase, Dec 2025). The official umbrella is Co:Club 领克车主俱乐部. Its public page lists the parts (lynkco.com.cn/coclub):

Unofficial spaces are the big third-party car platforms rather than Reddit: Dongchedi model circles (领克01 circle "210k car friends", 05 ~170k, 09 ~99k, per Dongchedi, 05, 09, accessed 2026-11-07) and Autohome model forums, where owners trade "how to farm points" guides and file complaints. Circle owners there run their own regional WeChat groups. r/LynkCo (about 531 subscribers) is European and barely touches China (lynk-co-clubs report).

Status: active and still growing on every metric the company publishes. The model has also spread inside Geely: Lin Jie, the Lynk & Co sales chief who championed it, chaired a group-level "user committee" after the 2025 Zeekr merger, and Zeekr now runs its own user committee meetings (Sina, May 2025; Weibo, Sep 2026).

Origin and strategy

Lynk & Co was launched in Berlin in October 2016 as a Geely–Volvo brand for "young, connected urbanites". In China it sold through dealers, but it rebranded the dealer model as "6S": 4S (sales, service, spares, survey) plus Social and Share. When 32 Lynk & Co Centers opened together in 2018, the brand announced that "every Lynk & Co Center and Space will have its own owner club, named Co客领地". The clubs would be the "third space" carrying the Social and Share functions, "with regular social and sharing activities combined with participation incentives", turning owners into "direct touchpoints" with non-owners and creating a "three-way win for users, the brand and dealers" (Lynk & Co, 2018). The Co:Club member platform went live on 29 June 2018 inside the new-retail system Lynk built with Alibaba Cloud. Co coins earned by posting and attending events could be spent in the Lynk mall (Alibaba Research via JN Expert, 2019).

The business logic is explicit:

  • Word of mouth for a new premium brand. Lin Jie's motto is "product is the ship, marketing the sail, word of mouth the wind". He says he wants Lynk to be "a user company, a consumer company… what we least want is a one-time buy-and-sell relationship" (Sina interview, Aug 2020).
  • Sales through the app. In 2020 Lin Jie said app-driven campaigns let Lynk lock in orders 3–4 months ahead (same).
  • Feedback. Lin Jie called the third owner round table, held the day after a launch party, "actually more important" than the party (same).

Champion: Lin Jie, a Geely veteran since 1994 who has run Lynk & Co Sales since 2018. A 2026 profile credits him with "leading and deeply participating in user operations from the start, meeting users face-to-face at the Co客 council" and calls Co客 and NIO owners "the two best-known high-activity communities in China's car industry" (NetEase, Apr 2026; Sina, Aug 2026). In August 2026 he was named head of Geely Auto Group's new national sales company (same).

Reading the trend. App registrations rose about eightfold from 2020 to 2026, roughly in step with owners (400k to 1.68M), and MAU now exceeds the number of owners. Most of that MAU is probably car control (remote start, digital key) rather than community use. The company does not split it out. The two app outages (13 Dec 2024 and 29 Apr 2025) became national news because owners rely on the app as a key (Sina; Sina). The offline layer looks steadier than the headline numbers suggest. Cumulative events went from 12,000 (Oct 2021) to 34,000 (Dec 2025), about 5,500 a year, which matches the 2023 figure. The 2021 claim of "110 events a day" (about 40,000 a year) does not fit. The number of clubs has stayed at about 300 since 2022, and partner shops at about 12,000 since 2024. So the growth is in app usage, not in local organisation.

What keeps people coming back

  1. A name and a tribe. From delivery day, owners "share one name: Co客" (CPQR, Oct 2021). Media and owners use "这很领克" ("that's so Lynk") as a shared adjective (Xchuxing). Launch events set aside seating for Co客 (Weibo, Sep 2026).
  2. Local clubs with a steady stream of events. Each club is tied to a dealer, which hosts and funds activities: road trips, family camping, track days, World Cup parties (Alibaba Research; Autohome, 2025). Peer networks go beyond leisure. Owners say they organised flood relief for Henan through their Co客领地 and collected 400 cases of water in a day (NetEase owner profiles, Dec 2024).
  3. A yearly ritual. Co客大会 has run every year since 2018, online in 2022 (Sohu, 2022). It grew from about 100,000 owners to "ten thousand people" on site in 2025 (OFweek). Owners walk the runway, give "user TED" talks, volunteer and design the conference hoodie. The 2021 edition was organised by the owner council itself (CPQR). Each year brings giveaways: in 2023 every one of the million owners got a RMB 1,000 car voucher; in 2024 RMB 300 service vouchers for everyone ("RMB 400M in benefits") and 15,000 points for repeat buyers (China Auto Trends; Xchuxing).
  4. Points worth real money. Co积分 converts at 10 points = RMB 1 in the Lynk mall, with a two-year expiry (Tencent News, Oct 2024). You earn them by daily check-in (5/day), posting, surveys, events, referrals (200 points per test-drive referral, 3,000 per sale, up to six) and trade-ins (15,000) (BitAuto). One owner calculated about 54,745 points (≈RMB 5,475) a year for a diligent user (Autohome forum). The habit loop is a daily one.
  5. Perks wherever you go. Showing the app and a Lynk key at any of 12,000+ owner-run shops (cafés, guesthouses, skate shops, e-sports hotels) gets a discount. The 2020 aim was to create "Co客 gathering places beyond dealerships" (Sina). In 2021 one in 35 owners had checked in at a partner shop (CPQR). Owners also vote on a yearly "领选榜单" list of favourite shops (Xchuxing).
  6. Being heard. Round tables with executives (Lynk claimed 90%+ of round-table suggestions were "reflected in our service", Dec 2020), the elected council, and product co-creation: 5,271 people joined "09club" and 18 elected "智选官" negotiated the Lynk 09's production specification with engineers (CPQR). Owners vote on the livery of the 2026 children's go-kart (Autohome, Dec 2025).
  7. Status and recognition. Owners get featured in magazines, films and the 2024 "Co客 family portrait" shot by a celebrity photography team (Xchuxing). Their art goes on merchandise; one owner's sketches of 11 Lynk stores became a cup and shirt line (NetEase).
  8. Switching costs. Points that expire, partner perks, friendships and a role in a club or the council all stay behind if you sell the car. Lynk reports 39% of owners who change cars stay with the brand (NetEase, Jan 2026). It does not show how this compares with other brands.

The content engine

Lynk produces lifestyle and owner-story content more than how-to content. Owner how-to material mostly lives on Autohome and Dongchedi.

  • Owner magazine: issue 1, 不确定性 (Uncertainty), 2020. Issue 3, 自己人 (One of Us), a book of drawings by 35 owners for the fifth anniversary. Issues 4–6, Co客生活指北 city guides to Chengdu, Guangzhou and Shanghai, December 2022 (Sohu; NetEase). No later issues found.
  • Owner films: a 2023 feature-length owner-story film (couples who met at Co客大会, modders in Foshan) that the ad trade press called a best-practice case (Digitaling). An owner-written racing film, 速度三个半, was in post-production in late 2025 (NetEase).
  • App feed UGC: 2M+ posts by Oct 2021 (CPQR), rewarded with points. A user co-creation fund pays for photography, design, improvement ideas and collaborations (Sohu case, 2023, snippet).
  • Distribution: a December 2024 "Lynk × Xiaohongshu territory plan" brought partner shops onto Xiaohongshu with traffic support for owner posts (Xchuxing). Executives post on Weibo too; deputy GM 穆军 has 725k followers (Weibo).

Content engine score 3/5: the owner-made publications are good and unusual, but they come out irregularly, and Lynk produces little education content.

People, programs and governance

  • Co客理事会 (owner council): set up May 2021. Owners publicly elected 26 directors as their "spokespeople". The council has five portfolios (clubs, partner shops, cultural co-creation, digital, volunteers), submits proposals and "participates in planning and decisions" for official events (CPQR). A spin-off Co客 Volunteer Council has run Red Cross first-aid training since April 2023: 80+ sessions and 1,800+ owners trained by 2025, with AEDs in about 200 stores (OFweek). Lynk promises 10,000 points to owners who carry an AED and will pay for a car window broken to save a life (Tencent News). The digital council meets engineers. One April 2023 session went through infotainment complaints one by one, with a published list of "how many requests collected, how many solved, which are in progress" (Autohome, Apr 2023). No evidence was found of later council elections or terms, or of how directors are replaced.
  • Club leaders: each Co客领地 sits at a dealer. Dealer staff and owner organisers share the work, but there is no public charter or funding formula.
  • Owner-initiated programs adopted by the brand: the Lynk 09 Sichuan club's "Co客索玛班" scholarships for children in Daliangshan started in 2021–22. Lynk formally joined in 2025, and 120 students have been helped so far (Autohome, Dec 2025).
  • Moderation: posting in the app is rewarded with points, and the company controls the forum, as the March 2023 shutdown showed (below).

Member ownership score 4/5: elected council, owner-run clubs and businesses, owner-started charity. One point off because the company kept a kill switch and used it. Staff investment score 5/5: a top-level executive champion, a national event every year, a points budget in the hundreds of millions, and dealer staff in 300+ cities.

Growth loops

  • Referral: Lynk has claimed for years that 71% of new buyers come from owner referral (Sina, 2020; Lynk & Co, Dec 2020; Autohome, Dec 2025). Points pay per referral.
  • Non-owner fans in the app: in 2020–21 Lynk boasted that app fans outnumbered owners 2.6 to 1, and called the community a funnel (OFweek, 2021).
  • Partner shops as a sales channel: Lynk claimed partner shops sent 1.2M visitors a month its way and had sold 20,000+ items for partners by 2021 (CPQR).
  • The conference as a launch event: the Z20 (2024) and the 03+ TCR (2025) were launched at Co客大会, so owner stories and product news reach the press together.

Evidence of business impact

Hard numbers (sales): cumulative deliveries passed 1M in Nov 2023, under six years from launch, which Lynk calls a record for a Chinese premium brand (Tencent). Sales reached 350,495 in 2025 (+22.8%), the brand's best year, with a weighted average transaction price above RMB 200k (NetEase, Jan 2026). Lynk's three-year residual value of 54.58% put nine models in their segment's top ten (China Automobile Dealers Association data cited by NetEase). Company claims: 71% referral; 39% of owners who trade in buy Lynk again; 16.4% repurchase rate; 90%+ of buyers of the flagship 900 are existing owners adding or upgrading (PCauto, Dec 2025; Autohome). The 71% referral figure has not changed between 2020 and 2025, which suggests a fixed survey statistic ("willing to recommend", as the Dec 2020 release words it) rather than measured attribution. Missing: Lynk & Co is not separately listed. Zeekr's and Geely's filings found no community retention metrics, and no third-party or academic study separating the community's effect from the product's. Business impact evidence score 2/5.

What they did right

  1. Clubs tied to dealers, so every city had a host from day one. Making every dealer a club in 2018 gave the community 130 chapters within about a year and 300+ by 2022 (Alibaba Research; Tencent). Europe's few company-run clubhouses never got this (lynk-co-clubs).
  2. The partner-shop network is new. Turning owners' own businesses into perk venues gives owners with shops customers, gives everyone else a reason to use their membership weekly, and builds a "trust network" in which the car key works as a "social password" (NetEase, Dec 2025). It grew from 200 to 12,000 shops in four years.
  3. Points with real value create a daily habit. In 2020, 80% of 230k mall orders were paid purely in Co coins (Lynk & Co). Owners write guides on how to farm them (Autohome).
  4. Real governance and co-creation. An elected council, the 09 spec negotiators and owners organising the 2021 conference go beyond a feedback widget (CPQR).
  5. Backing owners' own initiatives. Lynk adopted the owner-run Daliangshan scholarships and the owner-taught first-aid program rather than creating its own versions, a model media now cite as "user-initiated, brand-escorted" (Autohome).
  6. Consistency. Eight conferences in eight years under the same champion, through a merger with Zeekr and the departure of Volvo as shareholder (Sina).

What they did wrong

  1. Muting the forum during a protest (March 2023). After Lynk released LYNK OS N for 8155-chip cars only, owners posted a 《领克车主联合声明》 in the official app on 23 March. They said OTA updates had been advertised and promised as "lifetime free system upgrades", yet still required dealer visits and left old units on outdated maps (NBD). That night Lynk "closed the posting function of 智慧车圈 in the app" (Xcar). Owners issued a second statement on 25 March and a third on 27 March (Autohome). Commentators called the move "corporate arrogance" (Zhihu). Consequence: national bad press while sales were down 20% year on year in Jan–Feb 2023 (Autohome). Lynk then changed course with a 1,000-person legacy-upgrade team, paid hardware upgrades for older chips and a council session with engineers (Autohome, Apr 2023).
  2. The same complaint came back (August 2024). Owners of 8155-chip cars issued a new joint statement saying OS N had gone "nearly a year without substantive change" and asked to switch to Flyme Auto, which other Geely cars were getting. Executives answered that it was a hardware-allocation limit (IT之家; Sina). The community can surface problems, but it cannot fix product roadmaps.
  3. Paying compensation in community points. When the Lynk 09's advanced driver-assist package arrived late, Lynk offered 90,000 Co points (nominally RMB 9,000). Owners objected that the points only work in a mall priced "30% to 40% above market" (VOC, Nov 2022). Lynk also gave delivery-delay compensation as up to 100,000 points (Sina, Oct 2023). Using the loyalty currency to settle grievances cheapens it.
  4. Price cuts that undercut recent buyers. Official cuts of RMB 10,000 on 2023 models in March 2024 (IT之家) and a reported RMB 40–50k cut on the Z10 weeks after deliveries in early 2025 ("unacceptable after less than a month", Sina Finance snippet). In 2023 Lynk did offer price protection for buyers (Autohome).
  5. Treating the community app as a car key without making it reliable. Outages in December 2024 and April 2025 left owners unable to use app keys and led to calls to carry physical keys (Sina).
  6. The offline layer has stopped growing. About 300 clubs since 2022 and 12,000 partner shops since 2024, while app numbers keep rising (sources above). The magazine has published no new issues since 2022 as far as we found.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Give every local touchpoint a club charter on day one. If you have stores, partners or resellers, make each one host a chapter with a small budget and an event cadence. Coverage beats a few flagship spaces.
  2. Let members' own businesses become venues. A "show your membership, get a perk" network of member-owned shops gives the community a reason to exist between events and gives members with businesses a commercial reason to stay.
  3. Pay a daily-habit currency that converts to something real. Check-in, posting and referral points that spend like cash drive daily opens. Keep the store prices fair, or the currency loses its value.
  4. Elect a member council with named portfolios and let it run something visible (the annual event, the volunteer program). Publish its request tracker.
  5. Adopt member-started initiatives instead of competing with them. Fund the charity or skills program members already run, and credit them.

Avoid these

  1. Never shut off the forum when members organise a complaint there. Pin an official thread, answer it on a timeline, and bring the organisers into the room. Lynk's turnaround (council session, roadmap) worked; the shutdown that came first made it worse.
  2. Don't settle grievances in loyalty points. It tells members the points are worth less than cash.
  3. Don't let app vanity metrics hide a stalled grassroots layer. Track active chapters, events per chapter and organiser turnover separately from registrations and MAU.
