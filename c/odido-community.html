TL;DR

  • What it is. The Dutch operator's official peer-support forum. T-Mobile Netherlands launched it on 19 January 2009 with the Dutch startup inSided, and it has run on inSided (now Gainsight) ever since. It was renamed with the Odido rebrand in September 2023 and absorbed the Tele2 and Ben forums in January 2026. It has 350,419 members and 71,424 visible topics (Nov 2026) (Marketingfacts 2009; homepage).
  • Why people come back. Two groups come back for different reasons. Ordinary customers arrive from Google with a broken modem or a billing problem and leave once it's fixed. A small core of volunteers (15 "Klant Experts" in 2013, 25 at the peak, about 13 named Superusers today) stays for years: they like helping, they get a title, insider news, a private area and regular meetups at headquarters (Klantgemak 2013; Superusers page).
  • What the company feeds it. Moderators drawn from the webcare team who can look up accounts, a staff-written wiki, news and phone-launch posts, training and quarterly sessions for experts, a 2025 Open Day with the CEO, and an API that turned accepted forum answers into the official device-help pages on t-mobile.nl (Ruud Huigsloot, 2016).
  • Biggest mistake. Odido let the forum drift from "the customer service channel" back to "a forum". After the February 2026 hack of 6.2 million customer accounts, moderators could for a while give "only general information". Staff left, and new topics in 2026 are running about 40% below 2024. Meanwhile an inner circle of Superusers who defend the company draws press criticism (thread, Feb 2026; Totaal TV, Jul 2026). It also closed the Tele2 forum without moving its accounts or posts.
  • The one lesson. A support community pays off when staff with real account powers sit in it and a small, trained, well-treated superuser cohort does most of the answering. Take away either one and it becomes a complaints board that the company is quietly glad to see shrink.

What the community is

  • Official space: community.odido.nl, a Dutch-language Gainsight (ex-inSided) forum. It has two halves: the Community (questions, conversations, events, an ideas board called "Wensen & suggesties", groups) and the Wiki (staff-written knowledge articles for Community, Internet + TV and Mobile) (how it works). Internet + TV dominates: Bekabeld Internet 9,669 topics, TV 7,833, Van bestelling tot installatie 5,361, while mobile categories are each about 400–2,500 (homepage, Nov 2026). New in October 2026: a Startphone section (a parental phone app) with a parent wiki and a "think along about Startphone" feedback call (post).
  • Roles: "[name] van Odido" moderators (webcare staff) under Community Manager "Jason van Odido". Members carry "Odido Superuser" titles, an honorary member ("Erelid TAVL"), and automatic ranks such as "Doet lekker mee", "Master Poster", "King Poster" and "gewaardeerde antwoordgever" (Superusers page; thread).
  • Absorbed spaces: the Tele2 Forum (forum.tele2.nl, also inSided) closed on 1 January 2026. The Ben Community (community.ben.nl) became a news-and-wiki subcategory on 12 January 2026; it says "Need help? Contact my customer service" and takes no questions (Tele2 notice, Dec 23 2025; Ben subcategory; Ben notice).
  • Unofficial spaces: the long "[Odido Glasvezel/Mobiel] Ervaringen & Discussie" topics on Tweakers' Gathering of Tweakers, and Providerforum.nl. Reddit is negligible (r/odido: 7 posts per Arctic Shift). Totaal TV notes that as the official forums close up, customers drift to these independent spaces "where the provider has even less grip on the conversation" (Totaal TV, Jul 18 2026).

Origin and strategy

  • 2007–2008, webcare first. T-Mobile NL had a webcare team from late 2007, and it ran an "iPhone blog" when it was the Dutch iPhone carrier. The blog drew a flood of uncategorised comments, many negative and from non-customers, which the company "could barely keep up with". The forum was designed in response: log-in to post, categories, tags, nicknames, a 1–5 peer rating (Marketingfacts, Apr 2009).
  • Stated goals (2009): communicate innovatively, know what's going on with customers, let customers learn from each other, learn from customers. The company said explicitly that the forum is "not built to generate sales." Staff were limited to factual information, with no commercial advice or comments on competitors (same source).
  • The insight. Customer contact innovation manager Ruud Huigsloot wrote: "In 2008 it became gradually clear that knowledge, especially about smartphones, was shifting from operator to customer." He planned the forum "with Wouter from inSided" in 2008. Moderators deliberately took "a waiting stance" on questions they expected customers to answer, mainly smartphone questions, and those sections became the busiest (Huigsloot, Jan 2016).
  • No business case at the start. Community manager Arianne Heij said: "At the start we couldn't make a business case for the community in any way. We started anyway with a 'coalition of the willing' based on our belief in the medium. Now we can make a good business case." The call reduction was part of that case, and total cost of ownership remained a KPI (Klantcontact.nl interview, via Wayback). Customer-operations director Marieke Snoep framed it within a strategy of "referral, ambassadorship and lasting relationships" (same source).
  • Organisational home. The forum is run by webcare inside Customer Operations. In 2021 webcare managers called the community "the great pride of Webcare… a kind of free customer service staff". They said moderation costs "don't outweigh the benefits" and that 70% of T-Mobile's customer contact was outsourced (KSF webinar write-up, Apr 2021).

Trajectory. The visible-topic counter grew by about 6,300 in 2023, 9,400 in 2024 (the Odido fibre push), and 8,800 in 2025. From December 2025 to October 2026 it grew by only about 4,500, an annualised ≈5,400, roughly 40% below 2024. Member sign-ups fell the same way: about +11,000 in 2024 against about +4,400 in the ten months to October 2026. Topic IDs tell the same story (my estimate; IDs can include deleted topics): about 9,700 new IDs in 2025 against about 4,900 between 15 January and 6 November 2026. The visible counters are smaller than the 2013 totals (56k topics), so old content has been pruned or archived over the years and today's counters understate lifetime activity. The peak by reach and staffing was 2013–2017: 400k monthly visitors, 25 trained experts and showcase press coverage.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Helping as an identity (the core). The volunteers say it plainly. Klant Expert Dave670 posted 9,100 times in under three years: "I mostly do this just to help people answer questions" (Klantgemak 2013). Heij: "The main driver of customers on our forum really is helping others and continuing to learn" (interview). Today's Superusers have 9,000–26,500 replies each (thread).
  2. A status ladder with a real top rung. In 2012, three years after launch, T-Mobile invited a small group to help moderate. They were trained at headquarters and titled Customer/Klant Expert, with "no financial reward" (inSided case). In April 2013 dozens of staff, including directors, lined up applauding at the HQ entrance to welcome the experts (Klantgemak). Below that sit automatic ranks and badges (planned as "reward management" in the 2011 relaunch) (Marketingfacts 2011).
  3. Insider access and a seat at the table. Superusers are promised "extra short lines" to staff, first news, regular meetings ("mostly very gezellig") and influence: "By 'sitting at the table' you influence both the course of the Community and our services" (Superusers page). Experts had a private area. In the T-Mobile era they had a closed Facebook group and chat with staff (Huigsloot, via search snippet), and they met about four times a year at HQ for network-centre tours and device demos (inSided case). Quarterly sessions let experts test new products first, and staff invited them to "be critical" (Klantgemak; interview). There were field trips too, such as an August 2014 visit to the repair partner Dynafix (post).
  4. IRL rituals revived for everyone. In June 2025 Odido held its first Community Open Day at the Hague HQ, open to all members: CEO Søren Abildgaard and the COO spoke, followed by lunch, a 5G talk and a Tech-hub tour. It recruited one new Superuser (recap; sign-up). The events page lists Open Dag, Meetup, Webinar and Gaming types, but I found no evidence of a 2026 Open Day.
  5. Personality and belonging. Superuser bios read like a team roster: the "connection guru", the "handyman", the "young hack-god", a member with a health condition who contributes "my bit" through the forum, and Eric, who has helped since 2010 on Tele2's forum (Superusers page). A small Entertainment Lounge and "Fun!" board exist, but off-topic socialising is minor (28 topics in the Lounge).
  6. For ordinary customers: utility. Most visitors don't come back. They arrive via Google (more than 60% of traffic) with a single problem (inSided case). The hook that kept them using it was that moderators could actually act on their account: identity checks through private profile fields and private messages, then fixes in the thread. Customers on Tweakers recommended it because "the moderators there can do a bit more than the helpdesk staff" (GoT, Feb 2025).

The content engine

  • Staff content: the Community/Thuis/Mobiel wikis built from the most-asked questions, about 165 news posts (price changes, inflation adjustments, new phones such as the Pixel 11 and Galaxy Z Fold8, Videoland and ESPN add-ons), outage megathreads, and a contact-options guide viewed 173,658 times (wiki guide; news board; contact post). The company also used the forum to break bad news openly. When BlackBerry's network failed, a staff post explaining the cause was read 40,000 times on its first day and tripled registrations (inSided case).
  • Member content made official (the standout tactic). In 2015 T-Mobile merged its moderators, knowledge team and website content managers into one team. With inSided it built an API so that "all T-Mobile website pages about smartphones are fully dynamically built device-help pages whose content comes from the forum" (Huigsloot, 2016). Only answers marked by an employee were syndicated (inSided case).
  • Superuser-written content: how-tos (for example a guide to using your own router on Odido fibre), phone reviews, and "get more out of your subscription" topics (Superusers page; how-to). Writing how-tos and reviews is one of the three stated routes to becoming a Superuser.
  • Feedback loop: the ideas board has statuses (New, Under investigation, Completed, Parked, Not possible) (ideas). T-Mobile's internal "T-Mobile Experts" (billing, ordering, network staff) answered on the forum and fed fixes into their own processes, and the forum layout was reviewed twice a year (Klantgemak).

People, programs and governance

  • Team: community manager plus webcare moderators who sign "van Odido". Named leads over time: Arianne Heij (community manager, 2010s), Ruud Huigsloot (customer contact innovation manager and co-founder of the forum), Marieke Snoep (director customer operations, executive sponsor), and today "Jason van Odido". Webcare had a 4–5-person "social content hub" and sat in a glass "Social Hub" in the HQ atrium (KSF 2021).
  • Superuser programme: 15 experts (2013) → 23 (2014) → 25 (c. 2017) → about 13 named Superusers plus an honorary member (2025–26 page). The route is invitation-based ("We have eyes everywhere"): answer questions, write topics, and "show in a positive way that something can be improved" (Superusers page). Perks are non-cash: training (via the T-Mobile Academy), meetups, early access, a private area.
  • Service level: "24–48 hours" for a moderator in 2016, "1 or 2 days" in 2026 (handleiding, 2016; contact post). In a sampled Nov 2026 outage thread a moderator replied after about 29 hours, asked the customer to verify by private message, and marked that reply as the best answer (thread).
  • Staff churn: moderator signatures announce departures: "no longer working at Odido as of 11 September 2025", "no longer working on the community from 14 January 2026", "since 1-7-2026 no longer a moderator" (the Open Day host) (1; 2; 3).

Growth loops

  • Search. More than 60% of traffic came from Google, which also brought in non-customers researching the brand (inSided case). Snoep: "Google functions as the main gate to T-Mobile content" (interview). Prospects read provider forums before switching ("Before I switch provider, these forums are very handy", a Totaal TV reader, Jul 2026).
  • Channel routing. Customer service, Instagram and X agents send people to the community (thread, Mar 2026), and mobile customers sign in with their operator account through single sign-on (inSided case).
  • Ambassadors. One expert alone is credited with bringing in 100 new customers (a vendor claim) (inSided case).
  • Mergers. These brought in whole member bases: Tele2 (bought 2019) and Ben in 2026, though without account migration.

Evidence of business impact

  • Vendor and company claims (not audited): $4M a year saved through peer-to-peer support; 65% of questions answered by members; a 60% fall in support calls the community "contributed to"; 25 experts answer more than half of questions and save more than $2M in call deflection (inSided case). The 60% call drop was first claimed in June 2014 as having happened "partly due to the success of the forum" (Emerce). The same period also saw app self-service and webcare growth, so attribution is shared.
  • Internal measurement (2021): customers searching the community find an answer "in 52% of cases via webcare and 67% via customer-helps-customer" (KSF).
  • Revealed preference: 17 years on one vendor, two absorbed brand forums, a CEO appearance at the 2025 Open Day, and a new Startphone section in October 2026 all suggest Odido still sees value. Against that, Totaal TV argues providers now prefer private channels so that "one customer's compensation" can't become everyone's (Jul 18 2026).
  • No hard retention, churn or NPS link found. Odido's NPS fell from 26 to 20 in Q3 2025 after network problems (Telecompaper). Nothing ties it to the community.

What they did right

  1. Started early and kept going. Launched in 2009 with no business case, "coalition of the willing", and kept on one platform through two relaunches (2011, 2018: "members don't need to re-register") and a rebrand (Telecompaper 2018). Contrast the four platform migrations at T-Mobile US.
  2. Admitted customers know more. Moderators held back on smartphone questions to let members answer, which made those boards the busiest (Huigsloot).
  3. Invested in a small elite instead of mass gamification. Training, quarterly HQ visits, early product tests, a private channel, public recognition (the applause line), and at the peak 25 people doing more than half the answering (inSided case; Klantgemak).
  4. Made member knowledge into official content. Staff-verified forum answers powered the device-help pages via an API (Huigsloot).
  5. Gave moderators account powers and was honest during incidents. Linking to the CRM let moderators actually fix things, which is why customers chose the forum over the phone line. Proactive incident posts built trust and sign-ups (BlackBerry outage) (inSided case).

What they did wrong

  1. Service capability pulled, not explained. After the February 2026 cyberattack (6.2M accounts; Wikipedia), moderators said "at the moment [we] can only give general information and cannot look at your data", and pointed people to the phone line, Facebook or X (thread, Mar 25 2026). Members concluded the community was "completely pointless" for service (thread). The forum had also been collecting customer numbers and partial IBANs in private profile fields for verification, which members raised after the hack (thread, Feb 2026). By November 2026 private-message verification was back, but new-topic volume had fallen about 40%.
  2. The inner circle became the face of the brand. Superusers who defend Odido in hack threads ("I think Odido is already doing the maximum possible, right?") read as company apologists. Totaal TV grouped Odido with KPN and Ziggo, where "superclients sometimes play moderator", with "superclients, ambassadors and expert badges" holding "a privileged position" and private corners outsiders can't see (Oct 2026; Jul 2026). One long-time member blamed the decline on "the introduction of ranks, crowns and whatnot" (thread).
  3. Mergers that threw away members' history. The Tele2 forum (110k members, 390k replies in 2022) closed with "your account details and contributions will not be transferred automatically". Its top helper replied "Oh what a pity! I had so many posts :(" (notice). The Ben forum became a read-only corner, and a Ben customer noted that "with the closing of the Ben community, the (little bit of) support has gone too" (thread, Jan 2026).
  4. A shrinking expert bench and moderator churn. The expert cohort fell from 25 to about 13, and at least three moderators left between September 2025 and July 2026. The same few names now answer everything ("I keep seeing the same names", Mar 2026).
  5. Moderators crowning their own answers. "Beste antwoord door [moderator]" on scripted replies weakens the signal that rewards real helpers (example, Nov 2026). The same grievance demotivates helpers at KPN.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Recruit a named elite of 15–25 helpers and treat them like staff without paying them: training, a private channel to the team, early access, quarterly in-person days, and public thanks from executives.
  2. Let staff hold back on questions customers answer better, and step in only where staff powers matter (account, billing, orders).
  3. Pipe verified member answers into your official help content through an API, so community knowledge compounds beyond the forum.
  4. Stay on one platform and one URL scheme for as long as you can. Seventeen years of SEO equity gave the forum more than 60% search traffic.
  5. Post bad news first and openly in the community. Incident threads are acquisition moments.

Avoid these

  1. Don't quietly withdraw the capability that made the space useful. If a security event forces you to, say what's changing, why and until when, or the community will declare itself pointless.
  2. Don't let a closed inner circle become your public defence force. Keep superuser discussions visible where you can, reward accuracy over loyalty, and let askers (not staff) choose best answers.
  3. When merging communities, migrate accounts and post history. Making your best helpers start from zero is the fastest way to lose them.

Score justifications. Retention mechanics 3: strong for a small expert core, purely transactional for everyone else. Content engine 3: useful wiki and news plus the standout forum-to-website API, but little cadence or programming. Member ownership 3: members write how-tos and reviews and influence products, but staff mark answers and run a closed tier. Staff investment 3: historically high (training, HQ days, CRM-linked moderators), now thinning. Business impact evidence 3: specific numbers, but vendor and company claims only. Durability 4: 17 years, one vendor, survived a rebrand and two mergers, though volume is now falling.
