How it pays off for the company

Support deflection and self-service: the Lithium platform hosted ScottishPower's entire help knowledge base (single articles had 0.9 to 1.4 million views), with the peer forum attached. The 2015-16 Lithium social-care program aimed to repair reputation after an IT/billing failure that cost an £18m Ofgem penalty. TalkEnergy is a market-research panel. No forum-level deflection, churn or NPS numbers were ever published.

TL;DR

  • What it is. In October-November 2017 ScottishPower, the UK energy supplier owned by Iberdrola, launched an owned peer-support forum on Lithium (later Khoros) at community.scottishpower.co.uk. The same platform hosted its whole help "Support Centre" knowledge base [5][6][9]. The forum reached 15,928 members and 22,883 posts by 7 December 2022 [9]. Around 15 December 2022 the URL was redirected to a new Support Centre on the main site, and the forum disappeared without a public farewell [10][11][12]. The "Lithy 2016" finalist entry the coordinator flagged was not for this forum: it was for ScottishPower's social-media customer care on Twitter and Facebook (Lithium Social Web), which ran a year before the forum existed [1][2].
  • Why people came back. Most didn't. Energy customers show up with a problem (smart meters, bills, refunds, prepayment) and leave. The forum's real retention story is one volunteer, "Davc"/"DavC1", a long-time member of ScottishPower's TalkEnergy panel. He had 86 of the forum's 179 accepted solutions in 2019, was still the main answerer in December 2022, and kept answering for years after members concluded ScottishPower staff no longer read the site [6][13][17][21].
  • What the company fed it. Two or three named community managers (Gordon, Eilidh, Mike), welcome guides and guidelines in 2017-2018, a burst of staff blogs (an EV series, solar, the PowerUp prepayment app), and staff stepping in on threads early on [6][7][8][14]. After 2018 the staff content almost stopped: the last company blog, in 2021, was a press release about call-waiting times [11][15].
  • Biggest mistake. ScottishPower staffed a public complaints venue at launch and then walked away from it. By 2020 members were telling newcomers "SP do not read this Community site," and by 2022 "The community site is run by an external company called Khoros… Scottish Power do not read any of the messages" [18][19]. A public forum with no staff became a permanent record of unresolved complaints, and the company removed it with no notice or archive.
  • The lesson. A support forum for a low-interest, high-pain product only works if staff visibly close the loop. Without that it becomes a complaints wall held up by one unpaid expert. Measure deflection, fund the moderators, and treat the super-user as the asset, or don't open the forum.

Origin and strategy

The crisis came first. ScottishPower moved to a new billing and CRM system in 2013-2014 and its service collapsed. In April 2016 Ofgem made it pay £18m over failings in "complaint resolution, call handling and billing processes, during and following the supplier's implementation of a new IT system" [24][25]. ScottishPower's own Lithy entry describes what that did to social media. It had been "a healthy and vibrant small community of engaged advocates", which "rapidly grew and became steadily more negative as customer service complaints and media focus increased. With only two full-time staff operating during business hours our situation quickly became unmanageable" [1].

The 2015-16 response was social care, not a forum. Chris Brennan (then Senior Search and Social Strategy Manager, Digital Marketing; later Digital Director) entered a "Customer 360°" program in the 2016 Lithys. It used Lithium Social Web to filter noise, route power-cut and vulnerable-customer keywords first, and rework repeat complainers' cases [1]. ScottishPower was a finalist for Excellence in Customer Satisfaction, alongside Comcast; TELUS won [2]. In September 2016 ScottishPower gave Lithium its supplier "Innovation" award, saying it was now "the fastest to respond to customer service queries in the industry" [3]. On a February 2017 Lithium/ClickZ webinar Brennan framed the goal as going "from 'fighting fires to total community'": "You can do the best social marketing in the world, but if your social care is not working then your efforts are worthless" [26].

The forum came next (late 2017), as part of a site rebuild. The "total community" step was the Lithium community. Knowledge-base articles on it are dated 22 October 2017, "How to Post and Reply" was posted on 3 November 2017, and on 24 December 2017 community manager Eilidh wrote "Our Community is just over a month old" [6][13]. She described its purpose as peer support with "business experts" plus "a platform to discuss any topics of interest… relating to energy such as electric vehicles, renewable energy and smart home technology" [13]. The site's main navigation sent every "Help" link (bills, meter reading, moving home) into community.scottishpower.co.uk [5]. Self-service deflection was therefore built into the plan from the start, even though no deflection target was ever published.

A self-inflicted split. ScottishPower already ran TalkEnergy, an agency-managed research panel with its own forum. Its most engaged member, Davc, found the new community on 22 December 2017 and asked: "It is confusing to have two very similar services running at the same time - what did SP think when they launched both?" [13].

What the table shows (my calculations):

  • Two growth phases, both driven by pain. 2018-2020 added about 500 posts a month: the smart-meter rollout, the PowerUp app shutdown [20], and COVID call-centre closures [18]. Late 2020 to late 2021 fell to about 130 posts a month. The 2022 energy-price crisis (Energy Bills Support Scheme credits, Warm Home Discount, price cap) brought it back to about 500 a month: 3,191 new members in the last six months [9f][9][17].
  • Tiny reach. ScottishPower supplied "over 5 million homes and businesses" in 2016 [1]. At its peak the forum had about 0.3% of that as members.
  • Very low resolution rate. 287 accepted solutions against 22,883 posts. In August 2019 the "Most helpful members" widget showed Davc 86 solutions, Jonnel 19, Eilidh (staff) 18, Gordon (staff) 11, mickeydee2018 6 [6]. One volunteer had about half of all solutions.
  • The knowledge base was the real traffic. Lithium knowledge-base articles carried huge view counts: Warm Home Discount Scheme 1,362,802 views; ScottishPower Complaints 920,101; Smart Pay As You Go FAQs 142,597 [27]. Most people used the platform as a help centre, not a community.

What keeps people coming back

Very little was designed for return visits. The evidence:

  • Status and recognition: Lithium defaults only. There were likes, accepted solutions, a "Most helpful members" leaderboard and tag leaderboards [6][27]. I found no custom ranks, badges, member-of-the-month posts, superuser program, perks or events (no evidence found).
  • Peer support from a single anchor. Davc (later DavC1) answered from launch until the last week. In August 2021 a member asked "Did you use to work at SP?" and he replied "no I've never worked at SP but I've been reading this forum for 5 years", meaning since the TalkEnergy days [21]. In 2022 he was still posting most of the latest accepted solutions on the homepage [9]. Members thanked him personally ("Thank you very much DavC1…") [21].
  • Staff ties: early, then gone. In 2018-2019 staff replied in threads and their smart-meter team chased posters ("our smart team have been trying to get in contact with you") [18]. By March 2020 regulars were telling newcomers "This Community is a forum for customers, it is not monitored by Scottish Power" [18].
  • Switching costs: none for the forum. TalkEnergy is the only place with a retention-shaped rule: "Should you decide to switch suppliers, we will withdraw your membership from the panel" [4]. That is a condition of membership, not a reason to stay a customer.
  • TalkEnergy's mechanics are paid-research mechanics: every activity (survey, poll, forum reply) earns an entry into a monthly three-winner prize draw, and online focus groups pay £15-£30 in Amazon vouchers [4][22].

The content engine

  • Knowledge base ("Support Centre"). Staff accounts (SP_Help, SPHelp, Eilidh, "ScottishPower") wrote it from October 2017: bills and payments, meter readings, complaints, Extra Help (Hardship Fund, Warm Home Discount), smart-meter FAQs [27]. Councils linked to it; Swansea's Hardship Fund page pointed at community.scottishpower.co.uk [28]. This was the platform's most valuable content, and in December 2022 it moved to the new Support Centre.
  • Blogs. There was a burst at launch: "20 Years of ScottishPower on the Web" (Nov 2017), "Meet Gordon, the Community Manager!" (Nov 2017), "Life with solar panels" (Dec 2017), "A New Day for Energy" promoting the PowerUp app (Dec 2017), "Meet Eilidh, the Community Manager!" (Feb 2018), and an EV explainer series by 2019 ("What is an EV?", "Is an EV right for me?", "Will an EV save me money?", "Top 10 EV models") [7][8][14]. Then little. One post, "The Day I Had A Smart Meter Installed", appeared in March 2021 [29]. The final staff blog, "How our call waiting times measure up" (August 2021), celebrated a Which? call-wait result (2 min 28 s) to a forum full of people who couldn't get through. It drew 14 comments and about 20,000 views by December 2022 [11][15].
  • Onboarding content. Welcome post, Community Guidelines ("a light-hearted place… having some fun along the way"; "don't openly discuss the moderation process"), "Top tips", profile and "Likes and Accepted Solutions" FAQs, signed "Gordon & Eilidh, Community Managers" [6][30][31].
  • Member-made content. Long how-to threads on SMETS1/SMETS2 meters, Economy 7/10 wiring, solar and DCC communication faults, mostly written by DavC1 and a few regulars (e.g. "HairyFool") [17][21]. Nothing shows the company curating, featuring or rewarding this (no evidence found).
  • No video, webinars, podcasts or live events tied to the community (no evidence found).

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. Gordon and Eilidh (community managers, 2017-18) and Mike (posted the FAQs in 2018) [6][7][8]. In 2016 the social team "trebled in size" and went 24/7 [1]. No headcount was ever published for the forum.
  • Moderation. Visible "Mod note: private information removed" edits continued through 2022 [32]. Members believed moderation was outsourced: "The community site is run by an external company called Khoros. They never respond to messages to a moderator" (March 2022) [19]. I could not verify this, but it is how members saw it.
  • Superusers or ambassadors. No formal program (no evidence found). The de facto superuser came over from the research panel.
  • Feedback into the product. There was no ideas board. Product feedback went through the separate agency panel, TalkEnergy, under a confidentiality agreement that assigns IP to ScottishPower [22]. The public forum and the feedback channel never met, which is what Davc complained about in 2017 [13].
  • Off-platform presence. Official representatives on MoneySavingExpert in 2010-2018 handled complaints publicly and moved them to email [16]. By 2022-23 MSE threads read "Scottish Power literally uncontactable by any means" and "Scottish Power 'Live chat' is quite dead" [33][34].

Growth loops

  • SEO. The forum and knowledge base ranked for long-tail energy problems ("Economy 7 smart meter", "Why use Data Services?", "Too Many Meters"). MSE posters linked to its threads as references in 2018-2022, including to show "how bluddy awkward some of the energy companies are about complex meters" [35]. That is acquisition of visitors, not of customers.
  • Forced inflow. New members arrived through supplier failures (customers "assimilated" from Extra Energy in 2019, from British Gas meters, and from suppliers that collapsed in 2021). These were reluctant customers who came to complain [17][36].
  • Referral. The "Refer a friend" terms lived on the community knowledge base [35], but nothing ties referrals to community activity (no evidence found).

What they did right

  1. Triage by severity on social (2016). Keyword routing put power cuts and vulnerable customers first and cut P1 response from an hour to 20 minutes [1]. For a utility, that is the right first job for "community".
  2. One home for help and peers. Putting the knowledge base and the forum on one platform and one search box ("Support Centre / Blogs / Discussion Forum" results side by side) gave articles and threads shared SEO and very large reach [9][27].
  3. Good launch hygiene. Named, human community managers with intro blogs, plain-language guidelines, a privacy warning, and FAQs on likes and solutions [7][8][30][31].
  4. Picking the right expert. Whether by luck or design, the panel's most knowledgeable customer became the forum's main answerer and stayed for five years [6][13][21].
  5. Privacy moderation kept up. Personal details were scrubbed from public posts to the end [32].

What they did wrong

  1. Staff withdrew from a complaints venue. Once the launch team faded, nobody official answered. Members themselves set expectations: "SP do not read this Community site" (2020), "Scottish Power do not read any of the messages" (2022) [18][19]. The result was a public, indexed archive of grievances (threads like "Are Scottish Power the hardest Company ever to try and get to" ran to eight or more pages) [39].
  2. Two parallel communities that never talked. TalkEnergy (agency panel, feedback, rewards) and the public forum (support, no rewards) split the engaged customers from the struggling ones. This was flagged on day one and never fixed [13].
  3. Content stopped after 2018. The blog went from weekly at launch to one PR post in 2021 [11][15].
  4. Tone-deaf PR content. Celebrating call-wait times to an audience complaining they couldn't get through undercut credibility [11][15][34].
  5. A silent shutdown. The forum, with years of answers and 15,928 accounts, was redirected to a help centre around 15 December 2022. The only notice I found was a banner promoting the new Support Centre ("Launching 15th December 2022, it'll be a quick and easy way to find answers") [10][12]. The archive vanished, and as of Nov 2026 the Google Play listing still tells customers to "Contact ScottishPower customer services… on the Community" [40].
  6. A single point of failure. With no superuser program, recognition or perks, the forum's answer supply depended on one volunteer [6][9].

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Triage before you talk. Route the urgent and vulnerable cases first (ScottishPower's P1 routing) before investing in "engagement".
  2. Put help articles and peer threads in one search. The knowledge base drew more than a million views per article. Make sure every thread can lead to the canonical answer, and back.
  3. Launch with named humans and clear norms. Intro posts and simple guidelines lowered the barrier in year one.
  4. Recruit your super-answerers from your most engaged customers (panels, betas), and then formally recognise and support them.
  5. Use a separate research panel for structured feedback. TalkEnergy's paid focus groups and prize draws are a cheap, durable way to get product input, provided it connects to the public community.

Avoid these

  1. Don't open a public support forum you won't staff. For a high-pain product, an unanswered forum is worse than none: it publishes your failures and teaches members that the company isn't listening.
  2. Don't run disconnected communities. If panel members and forum members are the same people, merge the venues or link them clearly.
  3. Don't shut down without migration and notice. Archive the best threads, thank the volunteers, update every link (including app-store copy), and say why.

Scores (justification)

  • Retention mechanics 1/5: only Lithium defaults (likes, solutions, a leaderboard); no rituals, ranks program or events.
  • Content engine 2/5: a strong, heavily used knowledge base and a short 2017-18 blog burst, then almost nothing.
  • Member ownership 2/5: members did nearly all the answering, but one person carried it, with no governance or recognition.
  • Staff investment 2/5: two or three community managers at launch, a well-funded social team in 2016, and a forum members believed was unstaffed by 2020.
  • Business impact evidence 2/5: credible-looking but self-reported social-care metrics; nothing for the forum.
  • Durability 1/5: about five years (2017-2022), then a silent redirect.
