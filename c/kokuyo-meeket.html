TL;DR

  • What it was: meeket! was KOKUYO's free app for reviewing study tools. It ran from 1 June 2022 to 21 December 2023 and was aimed at Japanese middle- and high-school students. Members posted photo reviews of stationery from any brand, followed each other, saved posts, and asked "教えて!" ("teach me!") questions. A small student panel helped KOKUYO design limited-edition products. It peaked at about 10,000 downloads and 1,300 reviews (November 2022), then shut down. Fan-marketing listicles still present it as a "success case" (e.g. https://media.f-marketing.jp/article/fan-community-site-case/).
  • Why people came back: to browse honest, photo-heavy reviews from peers ("meeket!-buying", meaning buying something because of a post) and to show off their own "partner stationery" (相棒文具). The only real hooks were social-network basics: likes, comments, follows and a ranking. The app had no rituals, streaks or recurring events.
  • What KOKUYO put in: a three-person launch team, with two more staff joining through KOKUYO's internal side-job program. The team said they read every review. They ran launch campaigns (a 20%-off coupon for signing up, a notebook giveaway), an Instagram account with Instagram Lives, a feature in KOKUYO's own magazine, and three limited products co-created with students (a paperweight, rising-ruled loose-leaf paper, and a hinoki-wood desk slope).
  • Biggest mistake: KOKUYO built a standalone, closed app for a small, short-lived audience (students age out of the target every three years) when that audience already lived on Instagram and X. The parent brand's X account had about 134,000 followers, while meeket!'s own Instagram had 587. The team's own interview called the user base "small", and the app was closed within 18 months.
  • The lesson: the co-creation panel outlived the app. The paperweight first sold as a 300-unit test run and sold out in about a month. Relaunched nationally in January 2024, it hit its annual sales plan in about a month, placed third overall in Japan's "Stationery General Election 2024", and has since spun off new models. A small, close panel of real users was worth more than the UGC app around it.

What the community is

meeket! (pronounced "mee-ket", a play on "meet" and "market") was a review-and-Q&A app focused on study tools, built and run by KOKUYO, Japan's largest stationery maker (Campus notebooks). It launched on iOS and Android on 1 June 2022, with a web version at meeket-st.com (KOKUYO newsroom, 2022-06-01). It was built on Customedia, a white-label platform for members-only communities and social networks from Kobe-based Customedia Inc., which says it powers more than 800 sites (Customedia case study, 2023-08-18).

Features (Customedia; KOKUYO newsroom):

  • Reviews posted with photos and text, Instagram-style, and filed automatically under product categories.
  • Search by "people like me": a profile records grade, target school, personality type and best subject, so users can filter reviews by people similar to themselves.
  • "My study tools", a collection of the tools you own; clips to save posts; likes, comments and follows.
  • Rankings based on how many users own, favourite, like or clip a tool.
  • "教えて!" ("teach me!"): when search fails, post a question. Others can tap "私も知りたい!" ("I want to know too!") to add weight to it.
  • A recommendation tab, plus news pushed through the @meeket_st Instagram account.

Reviews of competitors' products were explicitly welcome. KOKUYO's own X account promoted the app as a stationery-review app that is "OK with other companies' products" (@kokuyo_st, 2022-12-05).

Status: defunct. The login page carried a notice that "meeket! will end service on 21 December 2023" (Wayback, 2023-12-10). KOKUYO's archived magazine interview carries an editor's note that the service ended in December 2023 (KOKUYO Magazine via Wayback, captured 2024-03-03). A user wrote in October 2023 that new installs would stop at the end of November (@peco_kco, 2023-10-28). Both store listings are gone (Google Play returns 404 and the iTunes lookup for id1623547844 returns nothing; checked 2026-11-07). No official explanation of the closure was found.

Unofficial spaces: no evidence of a fan-run subreddit, Discord or forum. Arctic Shift and Brave searches found nothing. Outside the app, discussion was a few dozen posts on X and one note.com essay.

Origin and strategy

  • Pandemic channel problem. Yoko Takeichi (武市陽子), who ran meeket!, told Senden Kaigi that stationery's value used to reach teens through trying products in stores and seeing classmates' gear. COVID cut off both, so meeket! was started "as one new place to convey the value of stationery" (Senden Kaigi, June 2023 issue).
  • Instagram roots. Takeichi joined KOKUYO in 2018 and was the "person behind" the brand's Instagram account (コクヨのぶんぐ). In 2021 she moved into a new-business project and became a founding member of the meeket! team (KOKUYO Magazine interview, 2022-11-30). The idea came from an internal question: beyond making and selling stationery, what more could KOKUYO do for students?
  • Research thesis. The team ran Instagram Stories polls and interviews, and found that teens' study problems change by stage ("can't start", "can't stay focused", "results don't follow"). A KOKUYO survey found only a little over 10% of students felt they studied well and got results, and most of that group had settled on favourite go-to stationery. From this came the "partner stationery" (相棒文具) thesis: help students find their partner tool sooner, and peers sharing recommendations is the fastest way to do that (same interview).
  • Operating principles per Senden Kaigi: (1) stay completely close to what users (teens) say; (2) never forget the sense of "making KOKUYO together". The article also asks why KOKUYO chose a closed app rather than open social media. The answer is behind the paywall and was not read (Senden Kaigi).
  • Business model link: indirect. The app had no monetization. The launch coupon sent users to KOKUYO's own online store, Kokuyo Showcase (newsroom). Fan-marketing write-ups list the outcomes as "sales promotion of KOKUYO study tools" and "more interaction between users", without numbers (f-marketing.jp).
  • Related context: in February 2021 KOKUYO bought CLEAR, maker of Clearnote, a note-sharing app for students with 4M+ users across Japan, Thailand, Taiwan and Indonesia (CLEAR release, 2021-02-12; Clearnote deck, 2023). meeket! had an official Clearnote account (Clearnote author page) and cross-posted picks there (example, 2022-12-04). So KOKUYO owned a teen community roughly 400 times larger, yet launched a separate app anyway.

Trajectory: an early spike from the seeded beta and launch campaigns, then slow growth. The posting rate in the first five months (about 60 reviews a month across 10,000 downloads) suggests most installers never posted. KOKUYO published no MAU or retention figures. No post-mortem was found.

What keeps people coming back

The evidence for a retention loop is thin. Here is what existed:

  • Discovery loop ("meeket!-buying"). Takeichi said products bought because of a meeket! post were called "meeket!買い", and the purchases came back as new reviews ("products that got buzz are 'meeket!-bought', then become new posts") (interview). This is the app's only documented loop, and the term never spread: Brave finds it only in KOKUYO's interview and in listicles that copy it.
  • Identity and pride. Members made elaborate posts: pen-colour swatch charts, side-by-side tests of red-marking pens, note-taking methods (interview). One user said a masking-tape review was "my hardest-ever post" and that she had written the fountain-pen posts herself (@pon_dentallll, 2023-02-28).
  • Peer matching. Searching by grade, target school and personality type was the distinctive idea. It tied into KOKUYO's "Manabi Style Diagnosis with music" learning-style quiz, relaunched in March 2022 (ICT教育ニュース). Whether people used the filters is undocumented.
  • Status signals: likes, clips and a popularity ranking. No badges, levels, streaks or featured-member programs were found.
  • Contact with staff. All team members read every review, and staff appeared on Instagram Live (interview). One early user thanked meeket! for a prize and for meeting "planner friends" (@yu__tree, 2022-07-31).
  • Co-creation. Students who helped shape products saw them in KOKUYO's shop. This is the strongest retention lever, but it reached only a small panel.
  • Missing: no weekly ritual, no exam-season challenges (despite a natural calendar of tests and entrance exams), no notifications strategy on record, and no switching cost: reviews could not be exported, and the content was lost at shutdown.

Score: retention mechanics 2/5. The app had generic social-network features plus one thin purchase-and-review loop, with nothing recurring.

The content engine

  • Member content was the product. The launch was seeded with 1,000+ reviews from pre-launch "advance members" (@ohatamayu: "as an advance member I spent time building something good together with KOKUYO").
  • KOKUYO channels: the @meeket_st Instagram (188 posts), Instagram Lives with the team, a long feature in KOKUYO Magazine (2022-11-30) promoted from @kokuyo_st, cross-posts on Clearnote, and press releases. A design studio made the logo and mascot (@kyoda_cr, 2023-03-10).
  • Campaigns: an Instagram follow-and-share giveaway (100 notebook sets, 1–12 June 2022); a 20%-off Kokuyo Showcase coupon for downloading and registering (all of June 2022); and the "#コクヨのマナビ / #コクヨに届け" stationery-set campaign in 2023 that referenced meeket! (newsroom; @peco_kco, 2023-07-31).
  • Co-created products (the real content engine): (1) the 本に寄り添う文鎮, a brass paperweight curved to fit an open book, ¥1,650, 300 units; (2) the 右上がり罫線ルーズリーフ, loose-leaf with sub-rules rising about 6 degrees to the right, ¥495 (newsroom); (3) a 12° hinoki-wood desk slope made from Shimanto thinned timber, with the student testers' comments quoted on the shop page (Kokuyo Showcase).
  • Cadence: no regular editorial schedule was found. The company-made content was mostly launch marketing.

Score: content engine 2/5. Strong seeding and co-created products, but little ongoing staff content inside the app.

People, programs and governance

  • Team: three people from the stationery division: "Tetchan" (strategy and management), "Zushimi" (product development) and "Take" (Takeichi, customer contact). Two more joined through KOKUYO's internal side-job program, one from stationery sales and one from the work-style seminar unit. A manager oversaw them (interview). That is small and part-time for a standalone app. Staff investment 2/5.
  • Student panel: "a group of middle and high school students who cooperate with interviews every month", with whom KOKUYO develops products (same source). By 2024 this was formalized as "Campus Classmate" (キャンパスクラスメイト), about 40 student "supporters" who meet KOKUYO monthly, online or at KOKUYO's office (Nikkei xTrend, 2024-03-04). In 2025 it was described as a student community used for Campus-notebook roundtables (qo-dozo interview). Perks and selection criteria: no evidence found.
  • Moderation: the platform included an NG-word (banned word) filter. Its stylesheet _ng_word_register.css appears in the archived site assets (Wayback CDX). Staff read everything. Given the minors in the audience, a closed app gave KOKUYO control, but the published rules were not found.
  • Feedback into the product: direct and visible. Students' habit of clamping textbooks open with big binder clips became the paperweight (xTrend; Impress Watch, 2024-07-02). Member ownership 3/5: members owned the content and shaped products, but had no governance role and lost everything at shutdown.

Growth loops

  • Acquisition: launch press across Nikkei, education outlets and PR Times; a sign-up coupon; an Instagram share giveaway; promotion from @kokuyo_st. There was no referral program.
  • SEO: weak. Reviews sat inside a members-oriented app on the Customedia platform, and Google indexed mainly the login page (the Wayback CDX is dominated by /login captures).
  • Word of mouth: small. The full X archive has 41 matching posts in two years. A typical sign of the reach problem: five months after the shutdown, someone "discovered" that "there seems to be a stationery review app called meeket" (@kamotoayako, 2024-05-23).
  • Products as the growth loop: the co-created paperweight drew more attention than the app ever did. Stationery commentators noted it "sold like crazy and went out of stock" and "came from here" (@takokabeuji, 2022-12-11).

Evidence of business impact

Hard evidence (about the co-created product, not the app):

  • Test run: 300 units at ¥1,650 sold out in about a month (xTrend). The test price was a loss-leader meant to measure demand (Impress Watch).
  • National launch in January 2024 (brass ¥5,500, iron ¥2,200 including tax) "in response to calls for a re-release" (KOKUYO release, 2024-01-16). It hit its annual sales plan in about one month (xTrend) and placed third overall in GetNavi's "Stationery General Election 2024" (Impress Watch).
  • The line kept growing: a bronze collaboration with Shumi no Bunguhako (June 2026) and the larger "もっと本に寄り添う文鎮" ("even more book-hugging paperweight"), launched September 2026, whose release text traces it back to the June 2022 limited sale (KOKUYO, 2026-09-02).

Company and vendor claims: "promotes sales of KOKUYO study tools" and "more interaction between users" (f-marketing.jp); the Customedia case study. Neither gives numbers, and both were published or kept up without noting the closure.

For the app itself: no retention, MAU, conversion or revenue data. A shutdown 18 months in is the clearest signal. Business impact evidence 2/5. Durability 1/5.

What they did right

  1. Built it on a real, researched pain. The "partner stationery" thesis came from Instagram polls and a survey showing that the roughly 10% of students who succeed have settled tools (interview).
  2. Kept it brand-neutral. Allowing reviews of competitors' products made the reviews credible, and outside reviewers praised it (note.com: "how generous of them").
  3. Seeded before launch. 1,000+ reviews from advance members meant nobody arrived to an empty app (Customedia PR).
  4. Made co-creation real and fast. Two products shipped on launch day and a third followed, each quoting the student testers. The paperweight became a national hit and a product line (xTrend; KOKUYO 2026).
  5. Kept the useful part after the app died. The monthly student panel continued as Campus Classmate, and Takeichi moved to KOKUYO's D2C direct-marketing unit (xTrend).

What they did wrong

  1. Built a separate app instead of meeting teens where they already were. The audience lived on Instagram, X and KOKUYO's own Clearnote (4M+ users). meeket! peaked at about 10,000 downloads and its Instagram reached 587 followers, against about 134,000 for @kokuyo_st. Consequence: the team admitted the scale was "small" five months in, and it closed after 18.
  2. Picked an audience that keeps leaving. Teens studying for exams cycle out every three years and, after entrance exams, lose interest in study tools. The team was already broadening to "all lifelong learners" by November 2022 (interview), and adult fountain-pen fans were posting. That points to a positioning that never settled.
  3. No recurring reason to open the app. Japan's exam calendar (term tests, entrance exams) was an obvious ritual hook, but there were no seasonal challenges, streaks or events.
  4. Undersized, part-time team for a standalone consumer app, staffed partly through an internal side-job program. UI problems were acknowledged ("many things to improve in layout, flow, usability").
  5. Shutdown with no explanation and no export. No public announcement of the reasons was found. Members' 1,300+ reviews vanished, and archives keep only the login page. Their documented labor ("my hardest-ever post") was lost. Consequence: goodwill lost, and the content's search value was never captured.
  6. Stale case studies. Vendors and fan-marketing media kept listing meeket! as a success after it closed (f-marketing.jp; Customedia, 2023-08-18). That is a warning for anyone researching from such lists.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Seed the space with recruited advance members before launch, so day one has hundreds of real posts.
  2. Let members review competitors' products. Neutrality is what makes brand-run review content credible.
  3. Run a small, standing co-creation panel (meeket!'s monthly students, later about 40 "supporters") and ship what they suggest quickly, as limited test runs.
  4. Use test runs to measure demand, then scale what sells out. A 300-unit sell-out turned into a multi-year product line.
  5. Credit members publicly in the product story (tester quotes on the shop page). Recognition costs nothing.

Avoid these

  1. Don't build a standalone app when your audience already gathers on platforms you control or that are free (here, Instagram, X and KOKUYO's own 4M-user Clearnote). Bring the community features to where the audience already is.
  2. Don't launch without a recurring ritual. Tie the community to the user's natural calendar (exam seasons, in this case).
  3. Don't shut down silently. Explain why, give notice, offer export or migration, and keep the content's archive and search value.
