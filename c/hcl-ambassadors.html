TL;DR

  • What it is: HCL Software's yearly recognition programme for non-employee experts. It started in 2019 as HCL Masters, the home for IBM's collaboration (Lotus/Notes/Domino) Champions when HCL bought those products. It was renamed HCL Ambassadors in mid-2020. Each year about 90 to 115 people are named. In 2026 they come from just two product areas: Digital Solutions (Domino, Sametime, Connections, DX, Volt) and, since 2021, BigFix.
  • Why people come back: the yearly October nomination and December "class" announcement, a permanent Lifetime tier, a private Slack, monthly calls with product management under a personal NDA, beta access, faster support tickets, free licences and the in-person "Ambassador drinks" at Engage. Year-to-year member retention runs at 70 to 87%. Of the 112 people in the first HCL-selected class (2020), 48 are still named in 2026.
  • What HCL puts in: one programme manager (Tim Clark, then Maria Nordin, herself a former Ambassador) plus a BigFix lead (Rhonda Studnick Kaiser), early roadmaps and betas, and product managers' time. Almost all the content is made by the members: blogs, conference talks, user groups.
  • Biggest mistake: HCL never took the programme beyond the two inherited and acquired product lines. Unica, Commerce, AppScan, Workload Automation and the Actian data products have no Ambassadors. The 2023 class shrank 23% (114 to 88), BigFix nomination windows keep getting extended, and the ecosystem the programme serves is getting older (Engage had about 400 attendees in 2020 and 250 in 2026).
  • Lesson: an acquirer can keep a community it acquires by changing the badge, not the people. HCL honoured IBM's status on day one (Champions became Masters, Lifetime Champions became Grandmasters), ran its own competitive selection within six months, and hired from the community to run it.

What the community is

HCL Ambassadors is a status programme, not an open forum. HCL defines it as "a distinction that HCL awards select members of the community that are both experts in their field and are passionate about sharing their HCL knowledge with others" (HCL). The one hard rule is that Ambassadors are "not HCL employees". They are customers' staff and business-partner consultants who blog, write books, speak, podcast, run workshops, answer forum questions and organise events (Class of 2026).

Structure (2026):

  • HCL Ambassador: a one-year term (January to December), renewed through self- or peer-nomination each October and announced in mid-December (become-an-ambassador).
  • HCL Lifetime Ambassador (originally "HCL Grandmaster"): no renewal needed, for people "proven, over several years, to be the best of the best". There are 11 in 2026, all from Digital Solutions (Class of 2026).
  • Tracks: "HCL Digital Solutions" (Notes/Domino, Sametime, Connections, DX, Leap/Volt) and "HCL BigFix". No other HCLSoftware product line has a list.

Official spaces: the class pages on hcl-software.com, the X account @HCLAmbassadors (created June 2019; 1,791 followers and only 249 posts by Nov 2026, from the X API), a private Slack with product channels, monthly virtual meet-ups, a private BigFix Forum channel, Ambassador lounges at user-group conferences (Lisa Towles, HCL, 2021), and since March 2025 an "HCL Ambassador" group (46 members) on HCL's new Discourse-based Digital Solutions Community (developer.ds.hcl-software.com groups.json; Hablewitz).

Unofficial but central spaces: Engage (a user group in Belgium and the Netherlands, founded and long run by Lifetime Ambassador Theo Heselmans and handed in 2024 to Ambassadors Kris De Bisschop and Tom Van Aken; Domino Elf), OpenNTF, Let'sConnect/Social Connections, DNUG, RNUG and CollabSphere. Much of the programme's value is lived out inside these member-run events.

Origin and strategy

  • Dec 2018: IBM agrees to sell seven products to HCL, including Notes/Domino, Sametime, Connections, Portal (DX), Commerce, Unica, AppScan and BigFix. The deal closed on 30 June 2019 (IBM; ibs.bg). HCL had already run Domino development under an IP partnership since 2017. Its "Factory Tours" (Milan in Feb 2019, Japan later in 2019, Beijing in 2020) courted the community before the close (Lichtenberg; C3UG).
  • May 2019, Engage (Brussels): HCL announces that "IBM Champions will becoming HCL Masters" (BCC), and that "anyone currently with the 'IBM Champion' accolade will become an 'HCL Master'" (Oval).
  • Aug 2019: a transitional class of about 111 names is published on HCL's partner microsite cwpcollaboration.com. IBM Lifetime Champions in the collaboration area become "HCL Grandmasters" (Wayback: Find a HCL Master; msbiro).
  • Oct to Dec 2019: HCL's first competitive nomination (window 1 to 31 October, judged "by a panel of HCL employees") and the Class of 2020 announcement on 16 December (Wayback: nomination page; @HCLAmbassadors).
  • Mid-2020: the programme is renamed HCL Ambassadors and Grandmasters become "Lifetime Ambassadors". The Masters page redirected by 26 June 2020, a June 2020 code of conduct uses the new name, and members blogged "I became HCL Ambassador 2020" on 19 June 2020 (Wayback CDX; Lichtenberg). No official reason was found. The timing matches the industry-wide retirement of "master" terminology in June 2020, but that is an inference.
  • Oct 2020: the programme opens a BigFix track (first class 2021), run by BigFix marketing (BigFix Forum).

Strategy. The programme sat beside a paid-for "Client Advocacy" programme (a named HCL developer per customer) on the same microsite (Wayback: advocacy). Together they were a plan to stop customers leaving a franchise IBM had starved. In 2019 HCL reported "customers coming back in form of reinstatements of software maintenance" (C3UG). The Ambassadors give HCL credibility from people who are not its own sales staff. Ambassadors describe their job as to "bring light into the darkness when involved in competitive discussions" (Lotus Evangelist, 2020), and members write rebuttals to "ill informed opinions of HCL Domino" (Domino People). For HCLSoftware, whose annual recurring revenue (ARR) was $1.03B in FY25, up only 1.8% in constant currency (HCLTech), keeping legacy maintenance revenue matters. The internal champion was the Digital Solutions leadership under Richard Jefts, who ran the Factory Tours and still gives the Engage keynotes (magerman; Domino Elf).

What keeps people coming back

  1. The annual class ritual. Nominations open on 1 October and the "Welcome to the HCL Ambassadors Class of 20xx" email lands just before Christmas. A public class page follows in mid-December or January, and dozens of members blog or post about it every year (Heiko Voigt; Boccadoro; Brandl; Ktrick). Because the term is a single year, members have to earn the title again every October.
  2. Status that can be lost, plus a permanent top tier. Selection is competitive: "never should there be a situation… where all who were nominated were selected" (Paul Withers). The Lifetime tier (9 people in 2020, 11 in 2026) gives the long-term goal. Verified digital badges (Badgr) can be shown on LinkedIn (Lausten).
  3. Insider access. This includes monthly virtual meet-ups with "product updates, guest speakers, and open discussions", exec and product-management sessions, roadmaps and betas (Towles). All of it sits under "a personal NDA between you and BigFix…it's not with your company" (Rhonda Studnick Kaiser). Partners turn this into business: "Without our advanced warnings and meetings with HCL, we can't provide you with the full first-class service" (Lotus Evangelist).
  4. Hard perks that lock members in. Free HCLSoftware licences, "immediate escalation of HCL support tickets", content syndication, exclusive forums and early betas (HCL). Support escalation and free licences are worth real money to a consultant, and members lose them if they stop contributing.
  5. The low time cost. The only requirement is a once-a-year January welcome briefing. Taking full part costs about 60 to 90 minutes a month (Towles).
  6. Belonging that started under IBM. Members call the group "the who's who" and compare it to "a huddle with the Boston Celtics" (Hablewitz). The in-person moments, like the Ambassador drinks and lounges at Engage, are what members remember most (Javier Sánchez). The "yellow" identity (Lotus yellow, "#Dominoforever", the "yellowverse") predates HCL, and HCL took it over rather than replacing it (Boccadoro).
  7. Seeing their feedback used. HCL has named a member's idea in a webinar, and "if you put feedback in the beta forum… a developer will reply to you promptly" (Domino People). In August 2026 HCL asked Ambassadors to test Domino Workspace and a product owner replied point by point (DS Community thread).

The content engine

HCL produces little content about the programme. It publishes the yearly class page, a benefits page, nomination posts (on the BigFix Forum every October since 2020) and an X account that averages about 35 posts a year. One explainer video by programme manager Tim Clark appeared in 2021 (Brandl). Recently HCL has used Ambassadors as marketing, for example "HCL Ambassadors Share Real-World Domino Experiences" (Let'sConnect 2025), which is embedded on the Domino product page (YouTube; HCL Domino).

The engine is the members. They write technical blogs (Martijn de Jong, Rainer Brandl, Daniel Nashed and many others), run #HCLAmbassadorTips posts (Lotus Evangelist), give user-group talks (28 Ambassadors led sessions at Engage 2026), host podcasts and video series, and run the conferences themselves. The nomination rules turn this into a cycle. Nominees must list "3-6 detailed accounts of their community contributions" from the past 12 months with links, and the rules reward "contributions to HCLSoftware Academy" and "participation in Beta programs with contribution evidence". They explicitly exclude reshared LinkedIn posts and "items that are considered part of your daily business activities" (become-an-ambassador). The nomination app itself runs on HCL's own Domino Leap, with tabs for "Nominations · Evidence · Votes · Countries" (hclambassadors.com).

The yearly test does not guarantee that members keep producing. One Ambassador renewed for 2024 wrote "I wrote 13 articles in 2023… considerably less than in the years before" (Martijn de Jong).

People, programs and governance

  • Staff: Tim Clark was the HCL Ambassadors Program Manager around 2020 and 2021 and later moved to Notes product management (Brandl; Domino Elf). Maria Nordin, an IBM Champion and HCL Ambassador since 2016 who worked at partner ISW/Kudos, became "Program Manager HCL Ambassadors and User Groups" by 2022 (RNUG; Let'sConnect interview; HCL blog author page). BigFix: Lisa Towles and Rhonda Studnick Kaiser in 2021, and Rhonda still in 2026 (BigFix Forum). This is a staff of one to three people for about 100 members (estimate).
  • Hiring from the community: Paul Withers, an IBM Lifetime Champion and HCL Grandmaster, joined HCL Labs in 2019 and is listed "(retired)" from the Lifetime tier because employees cannot hold the title (Wayback: Grandmasters post; RNUG).
  • Selection: self- or peer-nomination with consent, judged on merit by an HCL panel (Wayback). Lifetime status is granted at HCL's discretion (two new Grandmasters in 2019; Jesse Gallagher and Wannes Rams by 2024).
  • Rules: a three-page code of conduct (June 2020) covers law, harassment and confidentiality. It also includes "Protecting HCL's reputation. Do not make statements… that would be considered libel or slander towards HCL", and any leak of alpha or beta material is "passed along to HCL's legal department" (Wayback PDF).
  • Feedback into the product: monthly calls, Slack, beta forums, the Domino Ideas portal (domino-ideas) and invited hands-on reviews such as Domino Workspace in 2026.

Growth loops

  • Member events bring newcomers. Ambassador-run conferences bring in new people (about 30% first-timers at Engage 2020) and act as the stage HCL uses for launches.
  • Partners sell through status. Partners such as Belsoft and Ktrick publicise how many Ambassadors they employ (Belsoft; Ktrick). That makes the badge a sales credential and keeps partners building on HCL.
  • Nomination cycle. Every October members ask their audiences to nominate them, which keeps the programme visible (Lotus Evangelist).
  • A weak loop for new talent. The member pool is mostly long-time Notes veterans. With no free "Community Edition", few young developers reach the platform: "An aging team is being seen as a risk for the future" (Kwintessential Notes). No evidence was found that the programme brings in new customers in any measurable way.

Evidence of business impact

Hard evidence: none found. HCLTech filings and press releases report HCLSoftware ARR ($1.02 to 1.03B in FY25, up 1.8% in constant currency) but never mention Ambassadors (HCLTech). HCL has published no retention, NPS or pipeline attribution, and no academic study was found.

Soft evidence:

  • HCL reported maintenance reinstatements during the 2019 handover (C3UG).
  • HCL now counts Ambassadors as a headline metric of community "momentum" ("28 HCL Ambassadors" at Engage 2026; HCL).
  • Ambassadors provide unpaid beta QA and UX reviews (DS thread).
  • HCL's renewed investment in BigFix Ambassadors (8 to 35) suggests the BigFix team sees value.

What they did right

  1. Honoured existing status on day one. Every IBM collaboration Champion became an HCL Master and every IBM Lifetime Champion became a Grandmaster, announced at the community's own conference before the deal closed (BCC; Oval). No one lost a title because of the sale.
  2. Then made the title HCL's own. A competitive selection within six months let members feel chosen by the new owner: "Obviously I value this one more as I was selected by HCL this time" (Wannes Rams). Continuity stayed high, with about 68% of transfers renewed and about a third still Ambassadors seven years later.
  3. Gave real perks, not just swag. Support escalation, free licences, NDA roadmaps and early betas are worth money to practitioners and give partners an edge (HCL).
  4. Hired from the community. Maria Nordin went from Ambassador to programme manager, and Paul Withers from Grandmaster to HCL Labs. Members dealt with people they already trusted.
  5. Supported member-run events instead of replacing them. After IBM folded Lotusphere into Think, HCL backed Engage, Let'sConnect and the others rather than building its own conference, and let the community hand Engage to two Ambassadors in 2024 (Domino Elf).
  6. Set clear, evidence-based criteria. The do's and don'ts list (links, last 12 months, nothing that is just your day job) keeps the title tied to contributions others can check (become-an-ambassador).

What they did wrong

  1. Never scaled across the portfolio. HCLSoftware sells Unica, Commerce, AppScan, Workload Automation, DX, Volt MX, Actian/Informix and more, yet the 2026 lists cover only Digital Solutions and BigFix (Class of 2026). The marketing and security products bought in the same IBM deal have no advocate base. The result is a programme for a legacy franchise rather than a company-wide engine.
  2. Shrinking pipeline in its core. Non-lifetime Digital Solutions members fell from about 103 (2020) to 62 (2026). The 2023 class fell 23% with only 10 newcomers. BigFix nomination windows were extended in 2020 (to 11 Dec), 2022 (to 18 Nov) and 2025 (to 14 Nov) (BigFix Forum 35945, 43190, 53019), which suggests too few nominations.
  3. Thin official voice. The X account has posted about 249 times in seven years, and the public Digital Solutions forum shows 93 posts in 30 days (about.json). Little programme content reaches non-members, so the status means little outside the existing tribe.
  4. Unexplained churn and renaming. The Masters-to-Ambassadors rename (mid-2020) and the Grandmaster-to-Lifetime change happened without a public explanation found. The quiet removal of a group of members in 2023 (including several in Russia) was never addressed publicly. Consequence: no evidence of backlash, but members learned about the changes from their badges.
  5. A gag clause sits uneasily with "honest feedback". The code of conduct forbids statements "considered libel or slander towards HCL" and routes leaks to legal. In practice Ambassadors still criticise in public (for example "I don't think it would be usable by many actual end users as is"; DS thread). No sanction case was found.
  6. Too little effort on the next generation. Long-standing requests for a free Community Edition to bring in young developers have "stranded" (Kwintessential Notes), and Engage attendance fell from about 400 to 250 between 2020 and 2026.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. When you acquire or relaunch, carry status over one-for-one, then reselect within a year. Keeping the titles avoids a shock to members' identity. A fresh, competitive selection makes the title the new owner's.
  2. Make the top tier one-year and earned again, with a small lifetime tier above it. The October nomination and December announcement give a yearly beat, a reason to blog, and status worth defending.
  3. Pay in access and practical perks, not swag. NDA roadmaps, monthly product-management calls, early betas, faster support and free licences matter most to experts and partners.
  4. Write evidence-based criteria with explicit don'ts. Ask for linked contributions from the past 12 months and exclude day-job work and reshares. This keeps the badge credible and pushes members to produce content.
  5. Hire your programme manager from the community, and back member-run events instead of competing with them.

Avoid these

  1. Don't let an ambassador programme stay tied to the product line it was born in. If the company sells 20 products, a two-product advocate base will shrink with those products.
  2. Don't let the visible layer go quiet. A private Slack and a yearly list do not bring in newcomers. Without public content and an easy free version for new developers, the pool gets older and smaller.
  3. Don't rename or prune silently, and don't pair "give us honest feedback" with a no-disparagement clause. Explain changes and keep honest criticism protected.

Score notes. Retention mechanics 3: a solid yearly ritual, a lifetime tier and practical perks keep 70 to 87% of members each year, but there are no finer-grained progress mechanics. Content engine 2: HCL publishes little and the content is almost all members' own. Member ownership 4: members run the conferences and the user groups. Staff investment 2: one to three part-time owners for about 100 people. Business impact evidence 1: no figures. Durability 4: seven unbroken classes since the handover, though tied to legacy products.
