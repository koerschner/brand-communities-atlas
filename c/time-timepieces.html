How it pays off for the company

Primary NFT sales plus a built-in royalty on every resale (split 50/50 with artists, 1% to charity) produced a company-claimed $10M+ profit in roughly the first year. A secondary aim was digital-subscription acquisition: holders connected wallets to TIME.com to remove the paywall, reaching a crypto-native audience with under 1% overlap with TIME's readers. Also a showcase for crypto advertisers (Galaxy, Grayscale) and brand partnerships (Galaxy, Sandbox, City of Miami/Mastercard, Deepak Chopra).

TL;DR

  • What it is: TIME's NFT "community initiative," launched September 2021 with a 4,676-piece Genesis drop by about 40 artists under TIME's red border, then six-plus more collections (Nyla Hayes, Slices of TIME, Timbaland's Beatclub, Deepak Chopra's Seven Collection, partner drops). The community lived on a Discord, on @TIMEPieces on X, and in a weekly Spaces called TIME Hall (TIME; Digiday).
  • Why people came back: each token was a pass to the next drop. Holding a piece, or a POAP from attending TIME Hall, put you on the allow list. A public "utility matrix" set red-border holders above white-border ones, and long-term holders got free airdrops, raffles for TIME events and meditations with partners. A president who personally showed up every week added a parasocial tie (NFT Studios; nft.time.com).
  • What TIME fed it: artist curation by TIME's creative director, collaborations that borrowed other NFT communities' audiences, a Community Council of 30, an anniversary airdrop program (Artist Spotlights), and from 2023 to 2026 a weekly show produced by an outside studio (Good News Studio).
  • Biggest mistake: the core holder benefit, free access to TIME.com, was erased when TIME dropped its paywall for everyone on June 1, 2023 (TIME). That came five months after the founder-champion Keith Grossman left for MoonPay, and after January 2023 TIME stopped running new collections. Holders were left with tokens worth about 93% less in ETH than they paid at mint and a community run mostly by volunteers and a contractor.
  • The lesson: a weekly live ritual hosted by a senior, visible owner, combined with access passes that you earn by showing up, will keep a community alive through a 90%+ market crash. But benefits that the parent company can switch off on a whim are not utility, and a community tied to one champion needs a succession plan.

What the community is

TIMEPieces is TIME magazine's Web3 program: a set of Ethereum NFT collections, all bearing TIME's iconic red (or white) border, plus the community of holders, artists and fans gathered around them. TIME described it as "a new NFT community and initiative" and "an innovative extension of our current Digital Subscription efforts" (TIME launch post, Sep 22 2021).

Spaces:

  • Discord, "TIMEPieces Community" (server created Sep 24 2021, the day after the Genesis mint): "Community HQ for our amazing TIMEPieces NFT holders, artists, members and supporters" (Wayback capture of the invite page, Jan 2022). The vanity invite discord.gg/timepieces now points to a different, newly created server (created Jun 28 2026; 439 members, 28 boosts). This report could not tell whether that is TIME's own migration or a third party (see Open questions).
  • X/Twitter @TIMEPieces (joined Sep 2021; 36,198 followers and 19,465 posts as of Nov 2026, via the X API). Its bio still reads "Powered by 60K Community Members | 158 Artists | 30 Community Council Members."
  • TIME Hall, a weekly Twitter Spaces "town hall" with staff, artists and guests (already 600+ attendees in Jan 2022 per a member blog). It was still running as "our weekly TIME Hall" in March 2026 (@TIMEPieces, Mar 13 2026).
  • nft.time.com, the registration, allow-list and mint site, plus time.com collection pages for each drop (time.com/collection/timepieces-nft).
  • Satellite spaces: TIME Square in The Sandbox metaverse, billed as "an inclusive environment that will provide unique experiences to TIMEPieces holders" (Sandbox blog, Jun 2022), and IRL showings such as Dreamverse NYC (Nov 2021).

Status today: semi-dormant. TIME has not run a new collection since January 2023. The weekly show produced by an outside studio "sunset in 2026" (Good News Studio), and the X account has posted nothing since March 13, 2026. The original Discord still shows 12.5k members and a few hundred online (many likely idle).

Origin and strategy

  • Champion: Keith Grossman, TIME's president from 2019 to 2022, formerly at Wired and Bloomberg. In early 2021 owner Marc Benioff raised NFTs with CEO Edward Felsenthal and Grossman after the Nyan Cat sale. "I looked at it, and I was like, 'We could do this'" (The Fine Print, Oct 25 2021). Grossman learned by spending months on Clubhouse, then minted TIME's first 1-of-1 covers on SuperRare himself in March 2021 (nft now, Dec 2022).
  • Funding: TIME's SuperRare sales earned it about $1.4M of $RARE tokens in the DAO airdrop. Grossman sent $1M to TIME and kept $400K, which "paid for the launch of TIMEPieces… I never had a conversation with a business partner or a single meeting with anyone" (nft now; Fortune, May 2023). In other words, the community never had to win a budget fight, and never had a sponsor in the business beyond Grossman.
  • Business model: 1) primary sales at about $300-400 each, with resale royalties shared 50/50 with artists and 1% set aside for charity (Fine Print); 2) subscription acquisition: holders connected their wallet to TIME.com for unlimited access "through TIME's 100th Anniversary in 2023" (TIME). Grossman said he wanted all future subscriptions to become tokens (CryptoSlate, Jul 2022).
  • Positioning: "values-based community" vs. "greed-based communities." Grossman repeated that "TIMEPieces was not built around prices or trading" and that "values create value over time" (Fortune). He deliberately kept it separate from TIME's mass audience: "What would actually undo the success of TIMEPieces would be if we tried to dilute TIMEPieces with the larger Time brand" (Digiday).
  • Brand-authentic concept: TIME has given its cover to artists for a century (Chagall, Lichtenstein, Warhol, Basquiat), so "wouldn't it make sense to give [the next generation of artists] a cover?" (Maya Draisin, Edge of NFT podcast, 2023). Creative director D.W. Pine picked 19 artists, and each of them nominated a peer, which gave 38-40 Genesis artists, half of whom had never minted an NFT (Fine Print).
  • Succession: Grossman announced in Nov 2022 that he was leaving for MoonPay (effective Dec 31 2022). Chief Brand Officer Maya Draisin, his longtime colleague from Wired, took over TIMEPieces on top of subscriptions, retail, licensing and TIME for Kids (CNBC, Nov 28 2022; Decrypt).

Trajectory: very fast growth from September 2021 to mid-2022, a plateau in holders while trading volume collapsed (2022), a slow decline after the champion left and TIME stopped running drops (2023 onward), and near dormancy in 2026. The "40K" and "60K" community figures are company claims, and the actual Discord count was never above about 12.5k in any capture found. Treat them as cumulative reach across Discord, X and allow lists, not as active members.

Reddit: no official or significant fan subreddit. Arctic Shift searches of r/NFT, r/opensea, r/NFTsMarketplace and others found essentially no TIMEPieces threads. The community lived on Discord and X.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Access you earn by holding and by showing up. For the Nyla Hayes drop (Nov 2021), only wallets holding a Genesis, Inspiration or TIMECat NFT or a POAP claimed at a TIME Town Hall could enter the raffle for 1,000 mint spots (NFT Studios). Attending the weekly show literally earned a place in line. Later drops auto-registered red-border holders of every collection (nft.time.com).
  2. A public status ladder (the "Utility Matrix"). Red border beats white border, Genesis beats later drops, and each collection was slotted into a column of the matrix. Seven Collection red-border holders got a private meditation with Deepak Chopra and a raffle for signed books. Genesis holders who minted three got an on-chain art catalog, and every minter got a free 3D logo (nft.time.com; Artifex, Jan 2023).
  3. Gifts for loyalty. The TIMECat airdrop compensated people whose Genesis transactions failed and then became a minting pass. The Inspiration airdrop went to Genesis holders, and the Artist Spotlight airdrops ("sent to longstanding holders in celebration of TIMEPieces 1-year anniversary," Oct 6 2022) were explicitly meant for "deepening the relationship between TIMEPieces artists and collectors" (OpenSea; time.com).
  4. Collectors deciding rarity. In Slices of TIME (Feb 2022), holders could trade and burn during a 10-day window, and "the rarity of this collection was determined completely by Slices of TIME collectors" (OpenSea), a game mechanic that kept people in Discord daily.
  5. The weekly ritual and the executive in the room. TIME Hall was hosted by Grossman himself, who was "always fun and engaging" (web3growth, Dec 2022) and announced news there (Coinbase NFT, JR as an artist, airdrops). Digiday tied performance to "access to exclusive events, raffles and regular contact with Grossman and his team." Later Maya Draisin hosted, and members asked her personal questions (@RoseHorowitz31, Feb 2026).
  6. Identity and values. The community was positioned as "a community of shared psychographics—not demographics or geographics" and as values-based, with charity moments such as Artists for Peace: 61 artists donated 1/1s over a weekend and raised about $400K for Ukraine in 48 hours (Fortune). There were also community awards (e.g. "TIMEPieces Entertainer of the Year 2022," Edge of NFT).
  7. Surprise drops tied to TIME journalism. The Vitalik Buterin cover issue became "the first-ever fully readable magazine on the blockchain," airdropped as a surprise to the community (nft now, Mar 2022; Ledger podcast).
  8. Switching costs were financial (illiquid tokens) and social (artist relationships and council seats) more than product lock-in. The wallet-connect paywall benefit was the only true product hook, and it died in 2023.

The content engine

  • Live audio as the spine: TIME Hall (weekly, 2021-2026) plus themed series (an August 2022 "#SummerCamp" calendar of Spaces, e.g. mindfulness with Maloka; Social Media Today).
  • Outsourced weekly show (2023-2026): Good News Studio produced TIMEPieces x The Good News Show "every Monday from 2023 to 2026": 90-120 minute live Spaces, a weekly written roundup of Web3/AI news, and live coverage from Christie's Art+Tech and an NYSE digital-assets event. It claims 1,000+ hours and 75,000+ listener sessions, with guests including Tyler Hobbs, Refik Anadol, Claire Silver and Sasha Stiles (case study). This is effectively how TIME kept the community fed cheaply after the in-house team shrank.
  • Editorial pages on time.com for each collection and artist profiles by D.W. Pine; a hardcover Genesis book (TIME).
  • Artist amplification: the X account is largely retweets of TIMEPieces artists' work and news (X API sample, Feb-Mar 2026), plus an open "Submit Art" call.
  • Member-made content: the art itself is the UGC. Genesis artists nominated peers, and the roster grew from 38 to 150-158 artists (Fortune). Holders blogged recaps (e.g. cybervenus). There was no evidence of a structured holder-content program (guides, fan art contests).

Content engine score 3: a reliable weekly cadence for five years, but narrow (audio and retweets), outsourced after 2023, and never connected to TIME's large editorial machine.

People, programs and governance

  • Team: until August 2021, "Grossman, creative director D.W. Pine, and the lawyers," with Grossman doing the minting himself. By October 2021 up to 16 people, none full-time (Fine Print). By late 2022 there was a named Web3 team (William Ban, Lane Lytle, Jason Metz, Tameka Bazile, Ben Murphy, Hannah Robinson, Kaili Nishihira, plus community mods "JPEG HODL" and "BigWiseGuy") (web3growth). Community staffer Tyler Morgan left in March 2023 and stayed on as a council member (LinkedIn). From 2023, a CBO-led part-time effort plus a contractor.
  • Community Council (Sept 2022 onward): "a committee comprised of TIMEPieces community members, artists, and partners who have supported the growth of TIMEPieces since the beginning" (LinkedIn, Sep 2022), 30 invitees "selected to help shape the future of TIME's Web3 strategy," with a member badge (Good News Studio). It was advisory. No evidence was found of formal voting or published decisions.
  • Fair-launch governance: after the Genesis gas war, TIME moved to registration, raffles, 24-hour mint windows, one per wallet, and multi-day timelines "so that people have a better chance… regardless of where they are in the world" (Bharat Krish, Digiday).
  • Feedback into product: mostly through TIME Hall and the council. Grossman's claim that "engagement in the community increase[d]" during the 2022 crash was "a key metric for me personally" (Fortune). No published data backs it up.

Staff investment score 3: real executive time and a named team in 2021-22, but never full-time at the start, and it shrank to part-time leadership plus a vendor after 2022.

Growth loops

  • Borrowed audiences through collaborations: Robotos (70,000-member community; TIME Studios developed a TV show from its IP), Timbaland's Beatclub (2,000 pre-registered for 252 NFTs), Cool Cats, ToyBoogers airdrops, the Galaxy Explorer Collection (3,210 1/1s, Oct 2022), Seva.Love/Deepak Chopra, FOTO, Artifex, and City of Miami x Mastercard. "Time has used the collaborations as a way to reach other crypto-native audiences" (Digiday; Galaxy; web3growth).
  • Artist networks: every artist brought their own collectors, and the nominate-a-peer model scaled this.
  • Media flywheel: TIME's brand drew press coverage of every drop (CNBC, Digiday, Fortune, CoinDesk), and the launch site got over 1 million visitors in a day (MomentRanks).
  • Deliberately not used: TIME's 100M monthly audience. Grossman refused to push TIME readers into TIMEPieces, which protected the culture but capped growth once crypto inflows stopped.

Evidence of business impact

Hard or near-hard evidence:

  • Genesis primary sale: 4,676 × 0.1 ETH, about $1.4M gross, split with artists (Chaincatcher/The Block).
  • Trading volume of 14,504 ETH (about $18.4M) by December 2022 across collections (CoinDesk), on which TIME earned royalties.

Company claims (unaudited; TIME is private, so there are no SEC filings):

  • "More than 20,000 individual NFTs that have netted the publisher a profit exceeding $10 million," with 60% of sales happening on the secondary market and secondary volume of $50M (Grossman, Digiday, Apr 2022). The figure covers all TIME NFTs since March 2021, including 1/1 covers, and Digiday corrected it to primary plus secondary.
  • $600,000 to charities; about $400K for Ukraine (CNBC; Fortune).
  • Subscription effect: nearly 10,000 connected wallets, "the equivalent of a digital subscription," with an estimated 1% or less overlap with existing readers. The free access cost TIME little, but this is a count of free access, not revenue. Crypto-paid subscriptions were "not that many. A few thousand" (Fortune).

There are no retention, churn or LTV figures, and nothing on whether TIMEPieces members converted into paying TIME subscribers. Business impact evidence score 2: the headline profit is an executive's unaudited claim from the bubble peak, and no later revenue was ever disclosed.

What they did right

  1. Fixed the launch fast and publicly. After the Genesis bot and gas fiasco, they compensated failed minters with the TIMECat airdrop and, within two months, shipped an on-chain raffle (registration, POAP or holding required, 24h windows, one per wallet) (NFT Studios). Krish admitted the "naive approach" on the record (Digiday).
  2. Made attendance a currency. POAPs from Town Halls counted toward allow lists, which turned a weekly show into a habit with a payoff.
  3. Brand-authentic premise. "Give the next generation of artists a TIME cover" fit a century of TIME practice. It drew artists who had never minted ("I don't know anything about it, but I trust Time," illustrator Tim O'Brien via Fine Print) and made the red border the status object.
  4. Artists as co-owners. A 50/50 primary and royalty split plus peer nomination made artists evangelists. The roster quadrupled to 150+.
  5. Consistency outlasting the hype. TIME kept a weekly live show going for about 4.5 years, well into the bear market, after most brand NFT projects had gone silent. The vendor's line: "In a space defined by hype and disappearing acts, consistency became the most radical thing we could offer" (Good News Studio).
  6. Recognition structures: the Community Council, anniversary Artist Spotlights, community awards, red- vs white-border tiers.

What they did wrong

  1. Utility the parent could, and did, revoke. The headline benefit (TIME.com access "through 2023") became worthless when TIME made the site free for everyone on June 1, 2023 (TIME; Axios). Grossman's plan to turn subscriptions into tokens died with it. No replacement benefit was announced in anything found.
  2. Single-champion dependency. TIMEPieces was funded without a business case and ran on Grossman's personal passion. When he left (Dec 31 2022), it went to a CBO with half a dozen other portfolios. Within weeks the last TIME-run collection minted (Seven Collection, Jan 2023), and nft.time.com still showed that drop as the latest in 2025 (Wayback captures).
  3. Chaotic Genesis launch. Bots took most of the supply, gas fees often exceeded the $300 price (one buyer paid about $60K in fees for 10), the top 100 addresses ended up with 24% of supply, and Ethereum fees spiked network-wide (Business Insider; BanklessTimes). The founding cohort was partly flippers.
  4. The "not about price" message vs. a price-shaped reality. The community was sold as values-based, but status, allow lists and the $10M profit all rode on secondary trading. Genesis fell from a roughly $9,500 floor hours after mint to about 0.0068 ETH (OpenSea, 2026). Collaborations multiplied supply (20,000+ NFTs by April 2022, 30,000+ by end of 2022), which diluted scarcity.
  5. Shiny side bets that went nowhere. TIME Square in The Sandbox (June 2022) was announced as the holders' destination. No evidence was found that it became an active space.
  6. Inflated, unclear membership claims. "40K" and "60K community members" were never reflected in the Discord, which peaked around 12.5k in the captures found. That makes it hard for anyone, TIME included, to manage by real engagement.
  7. A quiet ending. The vendor show ended in 2026, the X feed stopped after March 13, 2026, and there was no public sunset post or roadmap for holders (none found). The discord.gg/timepieces vanity now leads to a different, months-old server.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Make showing up earn something. TIME's POAP-for-attendance rule turned a weekly show into the gate for the next drop. Any app can give early access, beta seats or limited items to people who attend live sessions.
  2. Put a senior, real person in the weekly room. A president hosting town halls built more trust than any campaign. Plan who inherits the mic.
  3. Publish a status ladder. The utility matrix (red vs. white, Genesis vs. later) told every member exactly what each tier gets, and made loyalty legible.
  4. Gift long-term members on anniversaries. Artist Spotlight airdrops to "longstanding holders" were cheap and memorable, and they rewarded tenure rather than spending.
  5. Recruit creators who recruit creators. Peer nomination plus revenue share took TIMEPieces from 38 to 150+ artists, each bringing an audience.

Avoid these

  1. Don't make your core perk something the business can give away to everyone. TIME's paywall removal wiped out the holder benefit overnight. Member value must be exclusive to members by design.
  2. Don't let one executive be the whole program. Unbudgeted, passion-funded communities stall when the champion leaves. Give it a P&L line, a full-time owner and a successor.
  3. Don't claim reach you can't show. "60K members" beside a 12.5k Discord erodes credibility. Measure and report active members honestly.
