How it pays off for the company

Advertising-department-funded loyalty site for a retail-sold candy maker: it aims to turn impulse purchases (an estimated 80% of candy buying is unplanned) into 'named' purchases of Morinaga brands, and doubles as an always-on research panel for brand managers and R&D. Members report buying 2.6x more Morinaga sweets than general consumers (vendor survey), which Morinaga estimates is worth 'hundreds of millions of yen' in sales against a running cost of 'tens of millions of yen' a year.

TL;DR

  • What it is: Angel PLUS (エンゼルPLUS) is Morinaga & Co.'s own member website for fans of all its sweets (Hi-Chew, Choco Ball, Choco Monaka Jumbo, Morinaga Milk Caramel and more). Since November 2013 members post photos, chat on a message board, answer polls and quizzes, collect "Angel Points" and meet staff at small events around Japan. It had about 870,000 members in December 2024 and is still growing (Kyodo PR Wire, 2013; Fanbase Company, 2025).
  • Why people come back: cheap daily habits (5 points for logging in, a once-a-day lottery that costs 100 points, a monthly product-review prize draw), long-running rituals that members started themselves (a candy word-chain thread with 108,909 comments since 2014), and a named, illustrated staff host, "KAZ," who also hosts every in-person event (points page; shiritori thread).
  • What the company feeds it: at least one new staff-started board thread a week, employee blogs with product development stories, quizzes and polls, a goods design vote every winter, 30-person workshops in seven cities a year, and (since 2024) real co-development of products with members. It runs on a budget held by the advertising department, so no single brand can cut it (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019; ExchangeWire Japan, 2019).
  • Biggest mistake: early on, recruiting members with prize campaigns and links from sweepstakes sites brought in prize hunters who harassed regulars until some regulars quit. Morinaga dropped member count as a KPI, but it still requires Angel PLUS registration to enter its national campaigns, so the member base keeps filling with people who come for prizes. The share of members active each month fell from about a third in 2019 to about a fifth in 2023 (estimate from published figures) (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019; DIGIDAY Japan, 2024).
  • The one lesson: fund the community outside any product line's budget, measure depth (monthly actives and NPS by engagement level) instead of headcount, and show a real employee's face. Morinaga did all three with one in-house staffer and an outsourced moderation team, and kept it alive for 13 years.

What the community is

Angel PLUS lives at ap.morinaga.co.jp. Signing up is free; since 25 March 2025 new members register through the shared MORINAGA ID, which also logs into the Morinaga Direct Store online shop (Angel PLUS notice, 2025). Its main parts:

  • Angel Gallery (エンゼルギャラリー): members post photos of Morinaga sweets they ate or recipes they made with them. According to the advertising department head, this is the most active area (DIGIDAY Japan, 2024).
  • Angel PLUS board (掲示板): threads started by members or staff, some with close to 100,000 comments.
  • Angel Blog and "KAZ no tsubuyaki": campaign news, product development stories from employees, and the admin's own diary.
  • Polls, quizzes, a fortune-telling widget, speed-kuji instant lotteries and members-only campaigns.
  • "in Talk!" and "in your health": wellness content tied to the in Jelly line, added as Morinaga repositions itself as a "wellness company" by 2030.
  • IRL: the "Oyatsu Summit" (おやつサミット) workshops, renamed "Angel Fan Meeting" (エンゼルファンミーティング) in 2025 after members submitted 860 name ideas (Angel PLUS, Feb 2025), plus an annual tour of the MORIUM museum and the Tsurumi factory (Fanbase Company, 2025).

The site is run by Morinaga's Marketing Division, Advertising Department, with the agency eLife (イーライフ), whose "eLife Community Suite" platform has hosted it since launch and whose remote staff do daily moderation and replies (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019). Unofficial spaces: I found no notable fan-run subreddit, Discord or forum (the Arctic Shift archive can't search all of Reddit by keyword, and Brave turned up nothing). The visible off-site activity comes from Japanese sweepstakes (懸賞) bloggers and prize-listing sites that repost Angel PLUS campaigns (ameblo example, 2024; ken-kaku listing, 2026), plus Morinaga's own X account, which is named @morinaga_angel.

Origin and strategy

  • Predecessor: before Angel PLUS, Morinaga ran a "Biscuit board" (ビスケット掲示板) community for about five years, paid for out of the Morinaga Biscuit brand budget. Moving it into a company-wide site meant Angel PLUS launched without a large up-front system cost (Fanbase Company, 2025). Biscuit fans were welcomed with their own sub-board (Angel PLUS).
  • Trigger: in July 2013 the parody site Kyoko Shimbun "reported" a 144-piece "Darss Gross" chocolate. Morinaga replied on Twitter that it would make it, and released a limited run. The exchange got more than 10,000 retweets. Morinaga concluded that dialogue with consumers creates buzz and business, but that social media is "unstable," so it wanted a lasting place of its own (Nikkei MJ, 2014).
  • Business logic: mass advertising was losing reach to "second-screen" smartphone use. Candy is hard to differentiate on technical merit, and about 80% of purchases are unplanned, so Morinaga wanted more "named purchases" (指名買い) at the shelf (Nikkei xTrend, 2021). Morinaga has said plainly that it does not want to sell directly: "the main battlefield is retail," and Angel PLUS is "just one measure to raise mind share" (ExchangeWire Japan, 2019). Sweets also generate little contact with consumers. A 2014 manager said that even after 100+ years, "we couldn't see the everyday consumer" (Nikkei MJ, 2014).
  • Champions: the Corporate Communications department launched it (Iwasaki Ikuo, Hashizume Fumie, 2013-14) (internet.com, 2014). Kazuhito Matsuno (松野員人), a 1994 hire who moved from Southeast Asia sales into the Advertising Department in October 2016, took it over and became "Admin KAZ" (MarkeZine, 2021). Takehiro Inose (猪瀬剛宏), head of the Advertising Department since April 2018, is the executive owner (DHBR, 2023).
  • Strategy shift: from "enclosing customers" (囲い込み) and growing member numbers to "pursuing depth, not numbers" and "loose connection" (ゆるくつながる). The reasoning: candy lovers also buy competitors' sweets, so the goal is to be remembered at the shelf (MarkeZine, 2021; DIGIDAY Japan, 2024).

Trajectory: membership has grown every year and roughly tripled between 2019 and 2023. Monthly actives grew more slowly (80,000 in 2019, 100,000 in 2021, 140,000 in 2023), so actives as a share of members fell from about 33% to 21% to 19% (my estimate from the published figures). Page views have stayed flat at 1.1-1.2M a month since 2021. No 2025-2026 member or activity figures have been published; I could not confirm whether the site has passed 900,000.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Angel Points, built to deepen engagement. Points reward harder actions more: 5 for a daily login, 30 for starting a thread, 15 for a comment, 10 for a quiz answer, 5 each time someone reacts to or replies to your post, all with daily caps (points page). Nikkei described it as "the higher the hurdle, the more points" (Nikkei xTrend snippet). In June 2014 Morinaga tripled thread and comment points after members asked what points were for. It added a monthly 100-point "most lively thread" bonus (most likes and most comments) and a staff-picked "Kirari prize," and used points as the entry ticket for its "new product tryout squad" monitor program (Angel PLUS notice, Jun 2014).
  2. A daily lottery as the points sink. The speed-kuji costs 100 points per draw, allows one draw a day, and has about 50 winners a month. Nikkei noted the odds are tiny against ~100,000 monthly actives and called that "deliberate," presumably to keep the habit going without making the site a prize factory (Nikkei xTrend snippet; kuji page). Members have rituals around it: a 2023 thread swaps lucky draw times (board).
  3. Monthly product-review campaign. Each month one product is picked; members buy it, eat it and post a review, and 10 are drawn for a gift box. One-word comments ("tasty") don't count (Oct 2026 campaign). This links the habit to an in-store purchase and produces UGC brand managers can use.
  4. Member-owned rituals. "Okashi de shiritori" (a word-chain game using only sweets) started as a member's post in December 2014 and is still running at 108,909 comments; spin-offs include a 5-7-5-7-7 tanka chain (DHBR, 2023; tanka chain). Morinaga's consultant called this "posts calling forth posts... members spreading the heat on their own."
  5. An annual calendar. The goods co-creation vote runs each December-February in three rounds (item, color, illustration), with entrants in two rounds getting double odds. 2025's winner was an Angel PLUS cooler tote, with 1,472 of 2,519 votes (Angel PLUS, Jan 2025). There is also the half-yearly senryu contest, whose ~20 finalists are voted on by employees at headquarters, factories and branches, and the fan-meeting tour.
  6. Small, warm IRL events. Since 2017: Saturday and Sunday mornings and afternoons, 30 people a session, ~120 per city, in Sapporo, Sendai, Tokyo, Nagoya, Osaka, Hiroshima and Fukuoka. Morinaga moved from one Tokyo event to a tour because members complained about losing the lottery or living far away. In 2019 members voted for host cities and Matsuno promised to visit the top 15; 13 were held before COVID stopped the rest. Quizzes are played in mixed teams of six so strangers talk, and after each event KAZ posts a report thread where attendees reconnect ("I was at your table...") (Fanbase Company, 2025). Tokyo has been over 10x oversubscribed (DIGIDAY Japan, 2024). Events went online as live streams in 2021-2024 (live stream, 2021).
  7. A human host. "Admin KAZ" is Matsuno, shown as a cartoon of his own face. He was uneasy about it at first; his predecessor had made the illustration without telling him. Attendees often say they came "to meet staff member so-and-so" (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019). Other named staff personas (HINA, RIHO, SHIOKEN) carry the voice now. The president answers member questions (President Q&A), as do product teams (in Jelly Q&A).
  8. Collective goals. Newer campaigns pool effort across members: a 2026 "Morinaga snack map of Japan" pays everyone who posts 200 points and holds a prize draw if all 47 prefectures are covered (X, Jul 2026), and an in Jelly step-count challenge has group targets and badges (campaign list).

Switching costs are low by design: points have no cash value (Kyodo PR Wire, 2013). What members would lose by leaving is their point balance, their posting history and their friends.

The content engine

  • Cadence: at least one new staff thread a week, such as "This is the rule for choosing souvenirs!" (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019). Then there are monthly campaigns, monthly speed-kuji prize posts, quizzes, two-choice polls, and blogs whenever a product launches. Staff read every post twice a day (moderation notice, 2014).
  • Formats: employee development stories, such as a serialized "non-fiction development variety" about the Tokyo Peanut Mania test sale (blog, 2020); KAZ's trivia, such as microwaving a Choco Monaka Jumbo for 30 seconds to crisp the wafer (ExchangeWire, 2019); factory reports; event reports; trainer and nutritionist columns in "in Talk!"; live-streamed craft sessions.
  • Member-made content: gallery photos, arranged recipes, threads, senryu and tanka, goods votes and event names. It is curated through monthly "lively thread" bonuses, the Kirari prize and employee-judged contests, and reused in Morinaga's sales materials and brand marketing (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019; eLife case).
  • Weak spot: the wellness content has not caught on. "Most members are sweets lovers, so activating this content is a future challenge" (Inose, DIGIDAY Japan, 2024).

People, programs and governance

  • Team: in 2019 Morinaga had one person on it (Matsuno), working with eLife through a monthly meeting plus daily email and phone (ExchangeWire, 2019). By 2024 it was 2 in-house staff plus about 8 on the external team (Fanbase Company, 2025). eLife has "hundreds" of home-based staff who prompt posts, reply on Morinaga's behalf and handle trouble. It follows a playbook that says "eLife handles general replies, KAZ handles anything about new products." eLife keeps the manual secret, even from Morinaga (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019). Matsuno plans and hosts every IRL event himself.
  • Budget and cost: the Advertising Department's own budget, deliberately not funded by any brand, so that a brand moving its money into TV can't cut the community (Fanbase Company, 2025). The total cost, events included, is "tens of millions of yen, not a hundred million" (roughly US$200-600k a year at 2019 rates; estimate) (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019).
  • Moderation: off-topic, duplicate, medical, defamatory or copied posts are hidden (2014 notice). When a defamatory post is deleted, staff email its author to explain why (ExchangeWire, 2019). There is a dedicated "winner reports" board with posting etiquette rules (notice).
  • Ambassadors: actress Aya Ueto was named Angel PLUS "ambassador" in June 2014 to attract younger members (Nikkei MJ, 2014); I found no evidence of the role after 2014. Angel PLUS has no superuser or member-ambassador program. A separate "Angel Ambassador" fan meeting (60 people, May 2026) exists for Direct Store mail-order customers (Direct Store report, 2026).
  • How feedback reaches the product: brand managers ask for surveys 1-2+ times a month. Surveys draw 5,000-10,000 responses fast (DHBR p.2), and a food-allergy survey drew about 3,000. Results so far: Tokyo Peanut Mania moved from a large jar to a smaller paper pack after event attendees said it was "too much, so too expensive" (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019); a test sale of rice-flour Moonlight cookies; OKOMETO, a rice-milk ice free of 28 allergens that was co-developed with allergy families through surveys, tastings and package votes and launched in March 2024 (PR Times, 2024); and Ōtsubu Ramune Energy Drink flavor (5 March 2024), which came out of an online roundtable with junior-high members (Angel PLUS, Feb 2024). The yearly NPS survey asks "what would you like to do next?" and "help develop products" kept coming up, which is how co-development became the goal (Fanbase Company, 2025).

Growth loops

  • Campaign gating (the main driver): "Entry to national campaigns requires Angel PLUS registration; beyond that we do nothing special," and "few people leave after a campaign ends" (DIGIDAY Japan, 2024). In November 2026 the homepage was mostly brand campaigns (Hi-Chew, Jumbo, Choco Ball x Sanrio, in Bar) (home page). Matsuno said many people "first registered for a present," then found it fun, got replies, and now "look for Morinaga products in stores" (Fanbase Company, 2025).
  • Sweepstakes ecosystem: prize-listing sites and kenshō blogs repost Angel PLUS lotteries and events, which brings in a steady stream of prize seekers (ken-kaku; koubo.jp).
  • Event word of mouth: Morinaga invites attendees to post on social media, and many write blog reports, which bring in new members (ExchangeWire, 2019). Lifestyle media sometimes cover the events (Orange Page, Sep 2026).
  • Social and cross-service: the @morinaga_angel X account promotes registration (for example a 50-plush sign-up draw in 2018) (X, 2018), and staff now reply to hashtag posts from non-fans: ~30,000 conversations a year, "considered AI, decided against it" (DIGIDAY Japan, 2024). The 2025 MORINAGA ID ties Angel PLUS to the D2C store, with 500 bonus points for linking accounts and a store coupon (notice; Morinaga PR, 2025).

Evidence of business impact

  • Purchase gap (company and vendor claim, correlational): members buy 2.6x more Morinaga sweets than general consumers, and NPS is +56 for members vs -31 for the general public, from eLife's survey reported in a sponsored ("Special Report [PR]") DHBR feature (DHBR p.2, Dec 2023). In 2021 the figures were +54.6 vs -33.3, with constant commenters around +80 (Nikkei xTrend snippet). NPS rises with each of four engagement tiers (Fanbase Company, 2025). The published sources don't control for self-selection: people who join a candy maker's fan site were probably already heavy buyers.
  • Revenue estimate: Morinaga multiplies the annual-survey purchase gap by the number of active users and reports the result internally. Matsuno put it at "hundreds of millions of yen" in sales (ExchangeWire, 2019; Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019), against costs in the "tens of millions."
  • Internal validation: the 2023 company President's Award for "raising the quality of fan marketing" over 10 years (Fanbase Company, 2025). Nearly every brand in the company now uses the board for research (eLife case).
  • Hard evidence missing: no retention or churn curve, no panel or POS-matched sales lift, and no mention in Morinaga's investor materials that I found. Score 3/5: specific numbers exist and are repeated consistently, but every one comes from the company or its paid vendor.

What they did right

  1. Ring-fenced, cross-brand funding. Paying for it out of the Advertising Department's budget meant no brand's media plan could cancel it; both Morinaga and its consultant call this the key reason it survived (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019; Fanbase Company, 2025).
  2. Measuring depth, not size. The KPIs are monthly logins and NPS by engagement tier; member count was dropped despite frequent pressure from above to "increase members" (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019).
  3. No selling. No "buy now" messages and no links to the online shop, so members trust the space (ExchangeWire, 2019). Purchases are prompted indirectly, through the monthly review campaign.
  4. A face and a voice. KAZ and named staff, real employees on the blogs, the president answering questions. People come to events to meet them (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019).
  5. Small events that lead back online. Capping sessions at 30 people, mixing tables, and posting a report thread afterwards turn one afternoon into a lasting tie (Fanbase Company, 2025).
  6. Doing what members ask, visibly. Taking events on tour when members complained, visiting the cities members voted for, adding Hi-Chew art workshops after attendees asked (Rakuten blog, 2019), holding a recipe contest on request, and co-developing products (Fanbase Company, 2025).
  7. Making it everyone's job. Employees across headquarters, factories and branches judge the senryu contest; brand teams run surveys; R&D runs workshops. By 2023 the site had moved from "the Ad Department's thing" to a company asset (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019; Fanbase Company, 2025).

What they did wrong

  1. Prize-led recruitment that poisoned the board. Sign-up prize campaigns and a link from a sweepstakes site brought in non-fans who "wrote whatever they liked, increased abuse, and existing users quit." Morinaga switched to "come only if you want to" (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019; DIGIDAY Japan, 2024). Consequence: it lost some core members, and the "失敗" (failure) in Nikkei's 2021 headline refers to this.
  2. …and still running on campaign gating. Requiring Angel PLUS registration for national campaigns works against the "no campaign hunters" rule. It explains the membership tripling while the share of active members fell (estimated ~33% → ~19%, 2019-2023), and why sweepstakes bloggers make up a visible part of the audience (one logs her event visit as "today's loot: worth ¥1,000") (ameblo, 2023).
  3. Points with nothing to spend them on. For the first year and a half members asked what points were for ("points just pile up with no use," May 2015). Morinaga fixed it with bonuses, monitor gating and the daily lottery (notice, 2014; board, 2015). The lottery's deliberately low odds create their own complaints ("lately I never win") (board, 2023).
  4. Audience narrowness. Members are mainly women in their 20s-40s, especially homemakers, because the programming was tuned to mothers (MarkeZine, 2021). The 2014 Aya Ueto ambassador push for younger members left no lasting trace, attendees joke about the "high average age" (board, 2023), and the wellness pivot is struggling (DIGIDAY Japan, 2024).
  5. Key-person and vendor dependence. One in-house owner who is also the persona and the event MC, plus an agency whose moderation playbook is secret even from Morinaga (Web Tantou-sha Forum, 2019). Matsuno has run it since 2016 and says he is "a bit lost" now that co-development, the long-term goal, has been achieved (Fanbase Company, 2025).
  6. Self-graded evidence. The 2.6x and NPS figures come from the vendor and appear in sponsored articles, which makes the case easy to oversell (as the f-marketing roundup does).
  7. Minor friction: a 2025 ID migration with two login buttons and the one-time password removed (notice); occasional lottery bugs (Feb 2026); lottery-only event access and some poorly suited venues (ameblo, 2023).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Give the community its own budget line, outside any product team, and agree on that at launch; it is the single best predictor of durability in this case.
  2. Track monthly actives and NPS by engagement tier, not member count, and design every feature to move people one step deeper (lurk → react → comment → start a thread → attend).
  3. Weight rewards toward effort and toward being responded to (points for receiving replies and reactions), and give them a fun, low-value daily sink so the habit doesn't turn into a prize economy.
  4. Put one real, named staff member at the center and have them host the in-person moments; keep events small (≈30) and post a follow-up thread so offline ties continue online.
  5. Make member input visibly matter: yearly co-design votes, letting members pick locations and names, then real product co-development with recruited sub-panels (OKOMETO).

Avoid these

  1. Buying sign-ups with prizes or sweepstakes traffic: it brought in abuse that drove off regulars, and gating campaigns behind registration still waters down engagement years later.
  2. Points with nowhere to spend them: members notice within months.
  3. Relying on one champion and a black-box vendor: document the playbook in-house and plan for the host persona to hand over.

Score notes

  • Retention mechanics 4/5: a full ladder of points, a daily lottery, monthly campaigns, a yearly calendar and member-owned rituals, but it relies heavily on prize lotteries and has no status levels or superuser tier.
  • Content engine 3/5: steady and authentic (weekly threads, employee blogs, live streams), but low-production and not much beyond the board itself.
  • Member ownership 3/5: members started the defining rituals and vote on goods, cities and names, but there is no member moderator or ambassador role.
  • Staff investment 3/5: only 1-2 in-house staff plus ~8 vendor staff, offset by a ring-fenced budget, a staff persona, hand-hosted events in 7 cities and a President's Award.
  • Business impact evidence 3/5: consistent numbers (NPS +56 vs -31, 2.6x purchase, "hundreds of millions of yen"), but all of them are vendor-measured, correlational and published in PR features.
  • Durability 5/5: 13 years without interruption, still growing, events booked into Dec 2026, and moved onto the company-wide ID in 2025.
