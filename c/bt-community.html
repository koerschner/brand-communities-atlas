TL;DR

  • What it is: BT's official peer-to-peer support forum for UK residential customers. It relaunched as the "NEW BTCare Community Forums" on Lithium (now Khoros) at community.bt.com and was live by January 2010, replacing the older BT Beta forums. Volunteers answer the questions, a small staff team moderates, and a separate BT Business forum runs alongside it. It is still running in November 2026.
  • Why people come back: customers come once to get a fix. A tiny core keeps answering for status: Lithium ranks up to "Distinguished Sage", thumbs-up ratings, accepted solutions and, from December 2020, a "Community Leader" badge with a private area, product trials and meetups. Two founding members, imjolly (user #33, 91K posts) and Keith_Beddoe (user #46, 67K posts, 4K solutions), were still the main answerers in 2024–25. For ordinary customers the real draw was that named moderators could open your account and fix it when the phone line couldn't.
  • What BT fed it: a dedicated moderator team drawn from the social-media care team, a community manager, employee subject-matter experts for TV, devices and Sport, a monthly newsletter (about 49 issues, 2015–2019), BTCare YouTube videos and BT help pages that send people to the forum to "save you having to give us a call".
  • Biggest mistake: BT treated the forum as a deflection channel to be cut back, not as a relationship. It deleted Phorm criticism in 2008 and has run an 18-month archive rule since about 2021, which cut visible threads from 135K to 55K in eighteen months. In 2024 it closed the Enniskillen care site and replaced the long-serving moderators with an EE team that by 2025 said "we have no account access". Members now get "generic copypastas" and are sent back to the phone line.
  • The lesson: a support forum's reason to exist is to work better than the call centre. Give the moderators real power to resolve cases, keep the archive, and look after the handful of volunteers who do most of the work. As the control group for giffgaff, BT shows what you get with status-only rewards and no member ownership: a forum that lasts but never becomes a community.

What the community is

community.bt.com is a Khoros (formerly Lithium) forum. In 2026 it has boards for Broadband (copper, fibre, "Extras & Apps"), Home setup and Wi-Fi, BT Devices, TV (EE TV and YouView, TNT Sports), Home phone and Digital Voice, BT Mobile, Bills & Packages, email, a feedback board and "The Lounge" for off-topic chat (homepage, Wayback Jun 2026). BT describes it as "BT's official support community", where customers "connect with other BT customers" (homepage).

The rules have stayed the same since 2014: "While we have a Community Manager and Moderators, these forums are intended to be peer-to-peer community" (User Guidelines, 25 Sep 2014). The regulars repeat it as a stock reply: "This is just a customer to customer help forum… The only BT Employees are the forum moderators" (Nov 2022 thread).

Related official spaces:

  • BT Business Community at business.forums.bt.com, a separate Lithium forum live by December 2011 (Wayback). A 2015 post sends home users to "the BTCare Community Forum" (2015).
  • Sister forums in the group: the EE Community (community.ee.co.uk) and Plusnet's forum. As BT moved consumers onto "New EE" from 2024, moderators sent migrated customers to the EE forum ("EE have their own forum, so you would be better raising the issue there", Apr 2024). By February 2026 members were asking BT to "get the BT and EE forums merged" (Lounge, Feb 2026).
  • Unofficial spaces: ISPreview's and thinkbroadband's BT sub-forums are where criticism of the BT forum gets aired. I found no meaningful BT subreddit (Arctic Shift returned nothing for r/bt_uk-style names, and no Reddit comments linking to community.bt.com).

Origin and strategy

BT Beta forums (c.2006–2009). BT's first customer forums lived at beta.bt.com (AVForums users were pointing people to "the BT BETA Support forum site… www.beta.bt.com" in March 2008). In November 2008 BT deleted every thread about its Phorm/WebWise ad-targeting trials, including one of almost 200 pages. Its chief press officer told The Register that "the point of the forums is technical support", and when asked whether more threads would go, said "I think they can, yeah" (The Register, 19 Nov 2008). Slashdot, ISPreview and thinkbroadband all covered the deletions.

Relaunch as BTCare Community Forums (early 2010). BT's social customer-service push started with @BTCare on Twitter in April 2009 (X). The forum relaunched on Lithium: on 2 March 2010 the homepage read "Hello and welcome to the NEW BTCare Community Forums!", with an announcement titled "Old forums now closed". It had about 6,800 posts in six boards and linked to BTCare's YouTube how-to videos (Wayback Mar 2010; first capture 31 Jan 2010). In September 2010 the first community manager, Kerry Gulloch (KerryG), wrote that the community "has been re-launched for over 7 months" and added higher ranks (Updated Rank Structure, 20 Sep 2010).

Strategy: deflection and "Net Easy". The fullest statement of BT's thinking is the 2014 IPA/MRS #IPASocialWorks case study, sponsored by BT Customer Services. BT found churn was 40% different between customers with an "easy" and a "difficult" service experience. It therefore built a "Net Easy" score instead of relying on NPS, and set out to "harness crowd-sourcing and customer-generated content", noting that "user-generated content could reduce costs" (MRS/IPA case study, 2014; text captured in sources/bt-community/). The forum was one of four cross-promoted social channels, alongside Twitter, Facebook and YouTube. BT's 2012 Lithy award entry said the same thing: superfans "contributing numerous solutions helping our customers, which deflects contacts from our traditional voice or email channels" (Lithys 2012: BT – Best SuperFan Story, KerryG, search snippet; the page is behind Cloudflare). BT's help site still pitches the forum this way: "that may save you having to give us a call" (BT Help).

Champions: KerryG (community manager 2010–c.2014), then StephanieG and SeanD (SeanD at BT since 2006, community manager in the 2010s–2021), and moderators from the social-media care team. By 2026 the forum is run by a "Community Management Team" (James_B, NickBS, JordanTA) and a "Community Support Team" of about ten staff (Who's who, Jan 2021 version; 2026 version). I found no named executive sponsor beyond the customer-service function.

Trajectory: new joiners peaked in 2010–2012, a forum boom just as BT Infinity fibre launched. The rate then fell by roughly half each era: about 41K a year, then about 23K, about 15K and about 7.8K. Visible content has held steady at around 35–39K threads since 2025, because the archive rule caps it. Verdict on status: still active, but declining on every intake measure.

What keeps people coming back

  1. Getting the problem solved. Most visitors are one-off: they search, find an accepted solution, and leave. BT's help page leads with that (BT Help).
  2. Moderators as the escalation desk (the real hook, until 2024). Users were told to post publicly first. A 2013 change to the "Contact Us" link for mods was made "to ensure the customers join the forum and post their problem first" (2013 thread, snippet). Mods then took the case private and fixed it. A 2015 user thanked "Robbie, PaddyB, David and Olga" for a refund (Aug 2015). In 2022 another wrote: "Where would we be without the mods" (Jul 2022). ISPreview users called the forum "an exceptional place to get dedicated UK-based support when the usual avenues didn't work" (Oct 2024). That was the one switching cost the forum created: a back door into competent support.
  3. Status for the core. There are 17 Lithium ranks from "Visitor" and "Newbie" up to "Distinguished Sage". They are driven by tenure, posts, ratings and solutions, and BT deliberately keeps the formula secret: "visit often, contribute regularly and make use of the ratings system" (Sep 2010). Kudos were renamed "Ratings" (a thumbs-up), and superusers' signatures ask for them.
  4. Community Leader perks (formalised 2 Dec 2020). The criteria are friendliness, "a real desire to help", consistent contribution and more than six months' membership. The perks are a private discussion area, a Community Leader icon, "trials and user groups for new BT products and services" and "face to face meetups with the Community Team". Applications go by private message to the community manager, who calls it "a very small thank you" (How to become a Community Leader). There is no cash, credit or payback, unlike giffgaff.
  5. Belonging, lightly. "The Lounge" has existed since at least 2012 as a place to "unwind… for a virtual cup of tea" (Wayback 2012). Moderators' "Who's who" bios (Liverpool fans, PS4 gamers, a Doberman named Miya) made staff into people (2021). A December 2024 Lounge regular put it this way: "there is also a pretty good crowd here with some regulars" (Dec 2024). There are no rituals, seasons, challenges or events. No evidence found of any live events for members other than the Leader meetups.

The content engine

  • Staff content: a monthly "Community Newsletter" from about 2015 to 2019 (Issue 45 was dated August 2018 and Issue 49 early 2019). It was mostly BT marketing (BT Sport signings, AMC shows such as Snatch "exclusive to BT customers"), plus forum "hot topics" (Issue 45). After Issue 49 I found no sign of it continuing. The other staff content is announcement and guide posts (welcome guide, guidelines, outage notices) and a year-end thank-you (Dec 2021).
  • Adjacent BT content: the BTCare YouTube channel had "over 100 information and explanatory videos" by 2014 (MRS 2014), and bt.com help articles point to the forum.
  • Employee experts: subject-matter experts for BT TV, devices and BT Sport post "in their own time" (Who's who).
  • Member content: accepted solutions and long technical threads (Hub settings, port forwarding, Openreach faults) by volunteers. A 2018 member even built a separate "forumhelp" website (Lounge 2018; btcommunity.weebly.com). BT curates this only through accepted-solution marks. I found no evidence that solutions are folded into BT's knowledge base, as AT&T did.

Score: content_engine 2. The newsletter was promotional and stopped. Almost all useful content is volunteer-made and is then archived away.

People, programs and governance

  • Staff: in 2021 there was one community manager (SeanD) and six named moderators, mostly from BT's Twitter and social-media care team, several with 10–17 years at BT. The 2026 page lists three community managers and about ten "Community Support Team" members whose bios mention Orange, EE, mobile billing and "experienced all the changes through to EE, BT and now New EE" (2026 Who's who). The role changed too: the support team now "connect you to a private place to get support from our Customer Care Team" rather than fixing the account themselves. In September 2025 a moderator wrote "I am sorry we have no account access", and the member replied "shame bt must've done away with that" (Sep 2025).
  • Volunteers: Community Leaders and the high-rank regulars. They are self-selected ("Community leaders are self-selected BT customers", MRS 2014), and BT picks them by invitation or private message. Score: staff_investment 3: a real dedicated team for 15 years, though cut in power in 2024.
  • Moderation and rules: "constructive posts", stay on topic, be specific (2014 guidelines). Moderation has a history of bans and deletions (Phorm 2008; "Just been banned without a reason", thinkbroadband, Aug 2014).
  • Feedback into the product: there is a "BT.com & Community feedback & suggestions" board (board), staff surveys on forum structure (Archive Staging snippet), and Leader trials. There is no ideas portal or public "we built this" loop. Score: member_ownership 2.

Growth loops

  • SEO: accepted solutions rank for BT-specific error queries, and BT Help links to the forum. The archive rule undercuts this loop, though, because old threads drop out of public boards.
  • Cross-promotion across @BTCare, Facebook and YouTube, "each cross-promotes the other" (MRS 2014).
  • No acquisition loop. There are no referrals or ambassadors. It is a post-sale support forum only, unlike giffgaff's member-get-member scheme.

Evidence of business impact

  • Company claims (2014, all social channels together, not the forum alone): about 600,000 contacts a year moved from voice to social, £2M a year lower service costs, and live chat and social scoring 2:1 against bt.com and 4:1 against voice on "Net Easy". "Easy" experiences were tied to a 40% churn difference. Community Leaders produced 20% of forum posts and 28% of accepted solutions (MRS/IPA, 2014). BT measured "call 'deflection' rates" as a KPI (same source).
  • Platform award entry: the Lithys 2012 "Best SuperFan Story" says superfans deflect contacts (vendor-hosted company claim).
  • Filings: no evidence found. EDGAR full-text search for "community forum" in 20-F filings, 2009–2021, returns nothing for BT.
  • Independent evidence: none quantitative. Anecdotally, ISPreview users in 2024 linked the moderators' loss of power to customers leaving ("BT has lost me for good").

Score: business_impact_evidence 2. One credible, audited-style case study, but it covers the social channels as a group and dates from 2014.

What they did right

  1. Kept one platform and one URL for 16+ years. The forum has been on Lithium/Khoros at community.bt.com since 2010, with no disruptive migration, unlike AT&T's 2019 move to Sprinklr. Ranks and post counts from 2010 still stand (imjolly #33). Durability 3.
  2. Put empowered, named staff in the forum. Moderators came from the social care team that was "empowered to 'own' customer issues and provide an end-to-end, one-contact, resolution service" (MRS 2014). They were the reason sceptical customers posted at all (thank-you posts from 2015 and 2022; ISPreview in 2024).
  3. Recognised and used superusers early. Ranks were revised within seven months of launch to keep top members climbing (2010). BT tracked the Leaders' share of solutions (2014) and gave them trials and meetups (2020).
  4. Clear expectations. The "customer to customer" framing was repeated by staff and volunteers, which limited angry "BT isn't answering" posts.
  5. Measured it as a service channel (Net Easy, deflection rate, cost per channel), which kept a budget behind it for a decade.

What they did wrong

  1. Censorship and deletion (2008 onwards). BT deleted the Phorm threads, then said deleting rather than closing them was "insignificant" (The Register). That set an early reputation that "if you're not sucking up to BT over there you're not getting helped" (thinkbroadband 2014).
  2. Archiving away the knowledge base (c.2021 onwards). "Threads that are older than 18 months will be moved into an archive section… any threads not attracting any views will be moved into a permanent archive which is not accessible by any forum members" (Do Old Threads Get Removed?, Jul 2023, snippet). Visible discussions fell from 135K in July 2021 to 55.3K in January 2023, and visible solutions from 29.1K to 15.4K. The consequence: answers that brought in search traffic, and volunteers' life's work, disappear.
  3. Cut moderators' power as the brand merged into EE (2024–25). BT announced on 14 May 2024 that it would close the Enniskillen contact centre (307 jobs), and it shut on 31 October 2024 (BBC; Fermanagh Herald). @bt_uk's social team had said "Our team is based in Enniskillen" (X, 2021). In May 2024 an admin assured members "The BT Community continues to be a valued platform… we'll continue to support in the future" (Lounge, May 2024, snippet). By October 2024, though, users reported "the mods there are just from the EE forum… all you get is generic copypastas", and by 2025 mods had "no account access". The consequence: the forum lost its one unique advantage over the call centre.
  4. Never built belonging or member ownership. The newsletter was promotional and stopped around 2019. There were no events or rituals, and only a "very small thank you" for people writing tens of thousands of answers. Two members from 2010 carry the forum, a clear key-person risk.
  5. Two forums for one customer base. BT moved its customers onto EE but kept the BT and EE forums separate, so migrating customers got passed from one to the other (Apr 2024; Feb 2026).

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Make the forum the place where things actually get fixed. Put named staff with real account powers in public threads, and require a public post first, as BT did in 2013. That one hook outperformed every badge.
  2. Count your superusers' share and protect it. BT knew its Leaders wrote 28% of solutions. Track this, and give them early access, trials and direct lines to staff.
  3. Keep the ranks moving at the top. BT added top tiers after seven months so that power users never "finish".
  4. Measure the forum as a service channel (an effort score, deflection, cost per resolved contact) so it survives budget cycles.
  5. Humanise moderators. Bios and consistent names build parasocial trust in a brand people otherwise distrust.

Avoid these

  1. Don't delete criticism or prune the archive. Both destroy trust and search equity. Lock old threads instead.
  2. Don't take power away from community staff during reorganisations. Members notice within weeks, and the forum turns into a complaints wall.
  3. Don't rely on status-only rewards for a handful of founders. Without ownership, money or belonging (giffgaff's Payback, events, ideas shipped), the volunteer bench never refills.
