Peak

Engagement peak was arguably 2014-2018, when single contests drew 7,000+ teams on real Taobao/Tmall data with RMB 1M prizes (Zhihu; CSDN). By count, 2022 was the prize peak (US$1.49M, 25 competitions) and 2023 the competition-count peak (61) per ML Contests.

Now

Nov 2026: homepage claims 600+ competitions, 1M+ developers, 4,000+ datasets, 4,300+ universities (tianchi.aliyun.com). ML Contests 2025: 51 competitions, US$1.03M prizes, still the #2 platform behind Kaggle on users, competitions and prize money. The international domain was folded into the China domain in 2025.

How it pays off for the company

Talent pipeline for Alibaba (top-10 finishers fast-tracked to final-round campus interviews), crowdsourced solutions on Alibaba's own data (2014 Tmall recommendation winners beat the in-house algorithm by 16.9% on Double 11), developer acquisition for Alibaba Cloud products (PAI, ODPS/MaxCompute, PolarDB, Qwen; some contests require using only Alibaba Cloud models and compute), and a paid competition-hosting service for companies, universities and local governments.

TL;DR

  • What it is: Tianchi (天池, "heavenly lake") is Alibaba Cloud's Kaggle-style platform for data-science, AI and engineering competitions. Jack Ma launched it at Peking University in March 2014. It now claims 600+ competitions and 1M+ developers. ML Contests ranks it the second-largest competition platform in the world, behind Kaggle, by users, contest count and prize money (tianchi.aliyun.com; ML Contests 2025).
  • Why people come back: a credential that Chinese employers recognize. Alibaba itself fast-tracks top-10 finishers to final-round campus interviews. Add real company data, cash prizes, a steady calendar of seasons, and tiered titles running from Challenger to Conqueror (Zhihu; Tianchi About).
  • What Alibaba feeds it: desensitized Taobao/Tmall/Alipay data, free cloud compute (a cluster of about a thousand servers for the first contest, 60 free GPU hours a year now), learning contests with step-by-step tutorials (many co-run with the volunteer group Datawhale), livestreamed walkthroughs, and swag and points economies.
  • Biggest mistake: it treats the community as a funnel more than a place. Members describe contests as "more like an exam" with little sharing. They say rankings were gamed with sockpuppet accounts and that judged defenses were opaque ("I still don't know my objective score"). Recent contests even require participants to use only Alibaba Cloud's Qwen models (CSDN; CSDN/YueTan; Tianchi 532510).
  • The lesson: a competition platform tied to a career outcome (an interview or a job) can sustain a million-user funnel for a decade. Without peer-to-peer sharing norms and transparent judging, though, the conversation moves off-platform (Zhihu, CSDN, WeChat, DingTalk) and the brand keeps only the transaction.

What the community is

Tianchi has several layers (Tianchi About; homepage):

  • Competitions (天池大赛): algorithm contests, engineering/programming contests (cloud-native, PolarDB database, Flink), innovation and application contests, academic contests run with conferences (KDD Cup, IJCAI, CIKM, CCKS, CVPR), "AI large-model" contests, and learning competitions (学习赛/新人赛) that stay open all the time for beginners.
  • Tianchi Lab / Notebook: launched in 2018 on Jupyter. Its launch promo gave away World Cup tins from a condom brand (forum post 6062). It now runs on PAI-DSW, with about 60 free GPU hours a year that members can extend by competing, publishing notebooks or uploading datasets (CSDN, 2024; Tianchi notebook).
  • Datasets: 4,000+ datasets, many released from Alibaba's own business, such as the CBLUE medical NLP benchmark (dataset page).
  • 技术圈 (Tech Circle) forum: articles, Q&A and notebooks, with a points ranking. In Nov 2026 the "Latest" feed ran to 2,571 pages (forum, read via r.jina.ai).
  • Off-platform, staff-run groups: each contest gets an official DingTalk group (for example "天池新人官方钉钉群：21714828" on a beginner contest) (contest 231712). Most informal discussion happens there and on Zhihu, CSDN and WeChat. Reddit and Hacker News barely mention Tianchi: one 2017 HN post, and Arctic Shift full-text searches of r/MachineLearning, r/kaggle and r/datascience timed out or came back empty.
  • IRL: top teams travel to Hangzhou or the annual Apsara (Yunqi) Conference for finals and defenses, plus campus tours and city meetups with Alibaba Cloud MVPs (MyDrivers, Jan 2022).

Alibaba Cloud Computing Co. runs it. The platform head in 2024 was Wang Yiting (王一婷) (cnblogs, Sep 2024; Baidu Baike, via r.jina.ai). Status: active, with about 50 prize competitions a year.

Origin and strategy

On 18 March 2014, at the Fifth Alibaba Technology Forum at Peking University, Ma Yun (Jack Ma), CTO Wang Jian and CPO Dai Shan launched a global student big-data competition "based on its Tianchi cloud computing platform." Ma framed it as data sharing: "If Alibaba became the only one enterprise that owns the data, we wouldn't make much progress." The university's account says Alibaba hoped Tianchi would be both "the testing ground for its opening strategy of big data" and "a platform for cultivating technology talents" (PKU News). The first contest, the Tmall recommendation challenge, was designed and judged by Tmall's recommendation-algorithm team (InfoQ, Apr 2014).

The strategy ties back to the business in four ways, all visible in company material:

  1. Recruiting. Top-10 teams defended in Hangzhou, and every graduating student on them was put straight into Alibaba's final-round campus interview (Zhihu compilation). A 2016 participant wrote that "Top10具备校招直接终面资格" ("top 10 qualifies directly for the final campus-recruiting interview") (Zhihu Q&A, Apr 2016).
  2. Crowdsourced R&D on Alibaba's own data. Six university students born in the 1990s ran their recommendation algorithm on 30% of Tmall's Double 11 traffic in 2014, beat Tmall's in-house algorithm by 16.9%, and split RMB 1M (Zhejiang Online, Nov 2014; CSDN recap).
  3. Cloud product adoption. Contests ran on ODPS (now MaxCompute), PAI, Docker images in Alibaba's container registry, PolarDB and now Qwen. The 2018 relaunch as an "industrial AI crowd-intelligence platform" even added a marketplace that matched enterprises with startups (cnblogs, May 2018).
  4. Hosting revenue. Tianchi sells a one-stop hosting service in three tiers, from self-serve to fully managed, plus a SaaS white-label platform. It pitches the service to companies as a way to "gather AI talent, collect technical solutions, and promote the latest product capabilities" (CompetitionHostIntro).

Trajectory: registrations grew steadily, from about 190k (2018) to 250k (2019), 600k (2021) and 1–1.4M (2023–24). The two sets of numbers don't quite agree: ML Contests uses 1.4M as of April 2024, while the homepage says "1M+". Per-contest intensity peaked early. In 2014–15 single contests drew 7,000+ teams on genuinely novel Alibaba data with RMB 1M prizes. By 2018 a typical contest drew about 3,000 teams, and some registrants only downloaded the data. Big numbers now come from Alibaba-product engineering contests and university leagues. Like Kaggle's account totals, the user count measures reach, not an active community. No DAU/MAU figure has been published.

What keeps people coming back

  • A career credential with a guaranteed payoff. This is the core loop. Alibaba's own recruiting honored contest results (final-round interviews for top-10 graduates), and other Chinese tech firms list "Kaggle、天池等竞赛有获奖经历" ("award-winning results in competitions such as Kaggle or Tianchi") in job ads (V2EX job post, 2022). One member wrote: "比赛成绩好对工作面试极有好处" ("good contest results help enormously in job interviews") (Zhihu, 2016).
  • Seasons and leaderboards. Contests run a preliminary round and a final round, then an offline final and defense. An A-board, scored daily, switches to a hidden B-board in the last days to stop leaderboard overfitting (Zhihu). Long-running learning contests reset each quarter as a "season" with a season leaderboard (contest 532510).
  • Interim rewards. In the 2014 contest, the top-10 teams each month got an Alibaba Cloud phone per member. "Our team won twice, it felt like we were opening a phone shop" (Zhihu).
  • Status tiers. The certification ladder runs Challenger (real-name verified), Rival (500+ points), Duelist (two top-10% finishes), Gladiator (three top-3s) and Conqueror (three wins plus a presentation to the Tianchi Committee). Higher tiers "enjoy more rights" (Tianchi About). A 2021 medal system added points for activity and a currency called 粮票 ("ration coupons") that buys backpacks, keyboards and thermoses (MyDrivers). Periodic "天池达人积分榜" (expert-points leaderboard) campaigns award AirPods and Instax cameras (Alibaba dev community, Mar 2023).
  • Compute as a retention hook. Free GPU hours on Tianchi Lab, extendable by competing, sharing notebooks or uploading datasets (CSDN). Students also get RMB 300 vouchers (contest 532510).
  • Meeting the "大神" (masters). Being in the top 5 means "attending the official high-level defense, and most importantly, getting to know the masters! (said three times)" (Zhihu, 2016). The social payoff is real, but it lives in teams, DingTalk groups and finals, not in the forum.
  • Switching costs: modest. Points and tiers don't travel, but the résumé line does. Many players compete on Tianchi, Kaggle, DataFountain and JData at the same time (CSDN).

The content engine

  • Company and partner education. "Tianchi AI Learning" launched in 2018 with machine-learning courses taught by Tianchi scientists and Alibaba algorithm experts, plus "攻略分享直播" (strategy livestreams) for each contest (cnblogs). "天池大学堂" (Mar 2020) offered free courses, a zero-to-one data-mining contest and Alibaba referral listings (Alibaba dev community). Later came the "AI练习生" (AI trainee) camps and the 2024 large-model "提升营" (improvement camp) for the tenth anniversary (Tianchi trainee; CSDN, Jul 2024).
  • The Datawhale partnership is the most effective piece. From March 2020 Datawhale, an independent volunteer learning community, co-launched the "零基础入门" (zero-to-one) contest series with Tianchi: used-car price prediction, news recommendation and others. Datawhale runs cohort study groups ("组队学习"), and Tianchi hands out official certificates and gifts to the best learners and team captains (Datawhale/CSDN; Datawhale about). The partnership continues into LLM contests in 2024 (BAAI Hub).
  • Member-made content, encouraged with prizes. Beginner contests explicitly reward notebook sharing ("分享激励"). Winners must publish write-ups on 技术圈, and mid-season "excellent player" livestreams explain the problem (contest 231712; contest 531826). A 2018 veteran livestream ("天池老司机在线分享辛酸竞赛路", roughly "a Tianchi old hand shares his bitter contest journey") is still on the forum (forum post 3069). In practice the durable solution archive sits off-platform: CSDN posts, Zhihu columns, GitHub (e.g. alibaba_tianchi_book) and contest aggregators such as Coggle (Coggle).
  • Datasets as content. "Tianchi 2.0" (2021) open-sourced 100+ industry datasets and showed them as a "data museum" at the Apsara Conference (QbitAI).
  • Crowdsourced problems. In 2026 the "学AI，你做主" ("Learn AI, you decide") contest asked members to design learning problems themselves (CompeteHub). It is the first strong sign of handing members ownership of the content.

Score, content engine 3/5: lots of company and partner content and a strong beginner on-ramp, but member-to-member sharing is thin, and members say so (see Member voice).

People, programs and governance

  • Staff. A dedicated Tianchi team inside Alibaba Cloud. Named figures include platform head Wang Yiting, chief scientist Chen Mosha (陈漠沙) and platform engineer Sheng Lele (盛乐乐) (Tencent Cloud repost). No headcount has been published. Contest rules set up an organizing committee with a secretariat, an expert committee and an arbitration committee (Baidu Baike). One member praised the staff: "工作人员的耐心和付出非常值得肯定" ("the staff's patience and effort deserve real credit") (CSDN/YueTan).
  • Fairness infrastructure. TCC (TianChi Computing) runs final-round Docker submissions on uniform hardware. Since 2020 it also offers "streaming evaluation" that deals test items out one at a time like a card dealer. Sheng explained why: a past winning team had trained on 128 P100 GPUs for a week, so Tianchi wanted teams "to compete on algorithms under the same compute" (Tencent Cloud repost). Cheating rules: results canceled, entry in a "dishonesty database," up to a three-year ban (university rules). Real-name Alipay verification is mandatory for prizes (AFAC2026).
  • Ambassadors and volunteers. A small volunteer team existed in the K-12 program (twin fifth-graders became volunteers after a camp). Tianchi also runs joint meetups with Alibaba Cloud's MVP and ACE expert groups (MyDrivers). No formal Tianchi ambassador or MVP program turned up (no evidence found). The role a Kaggle Grandmaster community plays is filled instead by an outside partner, Datawhale.
  • Feedback into product. Indirect. Contests are how Alibaba product teams (PolarDB, cloud-native, PAI, Qwen) test and market products with developers. No evidence turned up of a structured channel from the community back to the platform roadmap.

Score, staff investment 4/5: heavy, sustained corporate investment (a Jack Ma launch, a cluster that alone cost more than the RMB 1M prize, a 12-year team), but little investment in community-led structures. Member ownership 2/5.

Growth loops

  1. The campus pipeline. Launched at Peking University. University leagues, curriculum partnerships and "AI进校园" (AI on campus) tours reach 4,300+ institutions, and the university competition carries a three-star rating in the national university computer-competition index (student competition 2024). Contest credentials feed recruiting, and recruiting stories ("offer收割机", offer harvester) feed sign-ups (forum post 88).
  2. Sponsors bring their own audiences. Governments (Yangzhong power-usage AI contest, 2017), airlines (Xiamen Airlines), hospitals, Intel and NVIDIA, and academic conferences (IJCAI, KDD, CCKS) all market their contests (Baidu Baike).
  3. Referral campaigns. "Invite friends to sign up for designated learning contests" to win iPads and DJI drones (2024) (CSDN).
  4. SEO and secondary content. Thousands of CSDN and Zhihu write-ups rank for "天池 + 赛题" (Tianchi plus the contest name).
  5. The failed loop: global. English pages, KDD/IJCAI contests, Alibaba Cloud "AI Forward" contests in India and Southeast Asia (AI Forward), and a Michigan State hackathon in 2018 (Medium) never built a non-Chinese base. By April 2025 the international domain tianchi.alibabacloud.com showed only a notice: "the main site of this platform has been relocated to a China-based domain" (Wayback, 3 Apr 2025). A 2023 survey notes "the primary audience is Chinese, and not all competitions include English documentation" (arXiv 2312.05185).

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard-ish: the 16.9% Double 11 lift from student algorithms (2014) is company-reported but specific (CSDN recap). There is a firsthand account of the recruiting fast-track (Zhihu). Tianchi says 700+ papers used its datasets (Oct 2021) (Alibaba dev community). The 2015 champions' algorithm "was applied in Alibaba's business and raised recommendation click-through" (Baidu Baike). Tianchi sells hosting at a listed price (CompetitionHostIntro).
  • Company claims only: "China's largest AI developer community" and the user totals.
  • Missing: no revenue, hire counts, Alibaba Cloud conversion or retention figures for Tianchi in Alibaba's filings (no evidence found). Score 2/5.

What they did right

  1. They opened up real, valuable data and production tools. Students got desensitized Tmall data and a cluster of about a thousand servers with Alibaba's internal tools (ODPS, XLib). A participant noted that "the cluster cost alone far exceeded the million-yuan prize" (Zhihu). That made Tianchi results credible in the market from the first contest.
  2. They wired the credential to a concrete career outcome. A final-round interview, not a badge. This is the strongest retention driver in the record.
  3. They closed the loop on impact. Winners' algorithms ran on live Double 11 traffic. That gave participants a story worth retelling for a decade.
  4. They invested early in fairness tooling. Hidden B-boards, Docker reproduction, uniform hardware and streaming evaluation answered the compute-inequality problem Kaggle also faces (Tencent Cloud repost).
  5. They built an on-ramp for beginners through a partner. Learning contests that never close, plus Datawhale cohorts, convert novices who would bounce off a 7,000-team contest (Datawhale/CSDN).
  6. Durability. Twelve years in, it still ranks #2 worldwide, with about US$0.7–1.5M in prizes a year (ML Contests). Durability 4/5.

What they did wrong

  1. An exam culture instead of a sharing culture. "Tianchi contests feel more like an exam: everyone keeps their head down on their own work, with very little exchange... the atmosphere for discussion is not as good as Kaggle's" (CSDN, Feb 2018). Early rules banned sharing outright on some contests ("禁止参赛者之间分享任何解题思路", "participants may not share any solution ideas with each other") (contest 231590). Consequence: discussion and solution archives moved to Zhihu, CSDN and DingTalk, so Tianchi doesn't own the community's knowledge.
  2. Integrity complaints and opaque judging. A three-time top-5 finisher alleged sockpuppet accounts in a co-host university's teams, identical scores, and teams pulling their best results to dodge top-20 code review. In a later contest he said he dropped from 2nd to 5th after the defense with no explanation: "I still don't know my objective score or my defense score... Not participating is better for everyone" (CSDN/YueTan, 2023). A 2016 team that led online and lost at the defense "argued with the judges until midnight" (Zhihu). Write-ups also catalog data leaks in Tianchi contests that organizers sometimes left unfixed (secrss). Consequence: veterans churn, and there is no public appeals process.
  3. Platform lock-in that felt like a product demo. Early final rounds forced SQL/UDF work on ODPS: "a 6,656-line SQL" contest whose experience was "not good: too long, not enough resources, lots of luck, little real learning" (nanshu.wang, Jul 2015). PAI's 800-feature cap was "a hard flaw" (Zhihu). In 2026, an LLM contest requires that "participants must and may only use Alibaba Cloud Qwen models... all compute must come from Alibaba Cloud" (contest 532510). Consequence: it caps Tianchi's credibility as a neutral benchmark and its appeal outside China.
  4. The global retreat. Despite KDD and IJCAI tie-ins and contests in South and Southeast Asia, the international domain was folded into the China domain in 2025. Mandatory Alipay real-name verification adds friction for foreign participants (Wayback; AFAC2026).
  5. Too many brands, and swag in place of meaning. Golden League (2015), "Industrial AI crowd-intelligence platform" (2018), Tianchi 2.0 (2021), medals, 粮票 coupons, points leaderboards and gift campaigns kept changing. Engagement was bought with gifts more than earned through member status.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Tie your top status to a real-world payoff you control, such as an interview, a partnership or a showcase. Tianchi's fast-track is why a résumé line on a Chinese platform matters.
  2. Put member work into production and tell the story. The 16.9% Double 11 win was retold for a decade. Let community output ship in your product, then publicize it.
  3. Keep an always-on beginner track and partner with an existing learning community for cohorts. Datawhale supplied the peer layer Tianchi lacked.
  4. Invest early in visible fairness: hidden final boards, reproducible submissions, equal compute. It protects the credential's value.
  5. Give compute or product credits that grow with participation (GPU hours earned by sharing notebooks). Usage becomes the reward.

Avoid these

  1. Don't let the community become a product-marketing funnel (Qwen-only, Alibaba-compute-only rules). Members notice, and the credential loses its neutrality.
  2. Don't run opaque judged rounds on top of objective leaderboards without publishing scores and an appeals path. Veterans are the first to leave.
  3. Don't rely on swag, coupons and rebrands for engagement while the real conversation happens in other people's channels (Zhihu, CSDN, WeChat). Build norms and incentives for sharing on your own platform.
