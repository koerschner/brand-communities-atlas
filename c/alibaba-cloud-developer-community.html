TL;DR

  • What it is: Alibaba Cloud's Chinese developer portal (developer.aliyun.com, born as 云栖社区 / Yunqi Community in December 2015) bundles blogs, Q&A, ebooks, training camps, livestreams, labs and a points mall. Around it sit three recognition layers: MVP (expert award, launched June 2017, copied almost word for word from Microsoft's MVP), ACE (volunteer city chapters, November 2017) and the 乘风者计划 ("Wind Riders") blogger ladder (August 2021). An international mirror (alibabacloud.com/blog, a forum, a separately branded MVP programme) runs in English (Yunqi 3rd anniversary; MVP launch; ACE origin; 乘风者 launch).
  • Why people come back: mostly extrinsic rewards: 5 points a day for checking in, a lottery after 5 check-ins in a week, points redeemable for thermoses and phone stands via Taobao, blogger tiers with certificates, MVP trips to Hangzhou and the Apsara Conference. For a minority, it's career capital: MVP and "expert blogger" titles, book deals, Alibaba job referrals for ACE officers (points rules; blogger tiers; ACE officer perks).
  • What the company feeds it: an enormous official content pipeline. In 2021 alone it put out 250 ebooks and ran 100 offline events, 100 livestreams and 100 training camps (50k+ campers). On top of that come conference slide packs, product review bounties, and paid writers abroad (US$200–300 an article through Tech Share) (Baidu Baike citing the InfoQ award; Tech Share terms).
  • Biggest mistake: paying for volume with points invited farming. Users openly describe running "several alt accounts" that post lightly rewritten articles from elsewhere to collect points. Searchers complain that the site's misleading titles flood Baidu. When per-article points disappeared and old points were expired (2024–25), the article rate fell by roughly three-quarters (vpssw; Q&A 586706; ID sampling below).
  • The one lesson: recognition programmes (MVP, chapters) and staff-made education travel well. A points economy tied to posting volume buys content, not community. Once the rewards stop, the content stops too, and while the rewards run, the content poisons your SEO and your brand.

What the community is

Official spaces (China):

  • developer.aliyun.com: blogs (Markdown or Yuque editor, every post moderated before publishing), 技术圈 topic circles run by Alibaba product teams and outside media, ebooks (10–20 articles on one theme), Q&A, livestream/video, a skills map, skills tests, training camps, the 云起实验室 hands-on labs, a mirror site, the Tianchi contest link, and a points mall (Baidu Baike; homepage, read via r.jina.ai, Nov 2026). In 2026 the navigation leads with Qwen/百炼 models, 通义灵码 (Lingma) and ModelScope. The community has become a front door for the AI products.
  • MVP (阿里云最有价值专家): an annual, unpaid award for outside experts, with batches announced several times a year (MVP intro, Jul 2017).
  • ACE (Alibaba Cloud Engineer) 同城会: city chapters "self-organized by Alibaba tech fans" but "managed by the developer community". They're led by a 会长 (president), 副会长 (vice-president) and 班委 (committee), with DingTalk as the online layer (ACE charter, Sep 2020).
  • 乘风者计划 bloggers: a four-step ladder, 技术博主 → 星级博主 → 专家博主 → 签约博主 (tech → star → expert → contracted blogger). It was later extended with "mentor" and "Q&A officer" tracks (2023 tiers; programme page).
  • 聆听平台 (connect.aliyun.com): a feedback portal where accepted suggestions earn gifts and "your name permanently listed on the product feature list". It was launched alongside MVP in June 2017 and still resolves (login-gated) (launch article).
  • DingTalk groups: official groups for each technology, promoted as "a big community of 100k developers" in 2019 (article 693792).

Official spaces (international): the Alibaba Cloud Community blog (relaunched for UGC in May 2018; "Community 2.0" with reputation ranks in Jan 2019), the Tech Share paid-author programme, a separate global MVP site (mvp.alibabacloud.com), and a phpwind-style forum at alibabacloud.com/forum. Wayback shows the forum live with HTTP 200 through April 2025 and permanently redirected (301) from June 2025 (Hello World, May 2018; Community 2.0, Jan 2019; Wayback CDX).

Unofficial spaces: practically none of significance. r/AlibabaCloud (created Feb 2017) has 355 subscribers, ~1,991 posts and just 88 comments: a link-dump, not a community (Arctic Shift, Nov 2026). Hacker News has no comments mentioning the aliyun developer community (Algolia, 0 hits). Chinese developer talk about Alibaba Cloud happens on V2EX, Zhihu, CSDN and Juejin, and much of it is about support and pricing, not the community.

Sister communities covered elsewhere: Tianchi (contests; see alibaba-tianchi.md) and ModelScope (see modelscope.md).

Origin and strategy

The lineage goes back to Alibaba Cloud's webmaster and developer conferences (2009–2014, renamed the Apsara/云栖 Conference in 2015) and the old bbs.aliyun.com forum. The Baidu entry says the community "has operated for 12 years" as of the 2021 InfoQ award (Yunqi history; Baidu Baike). 云栖社区 (yq.aliyun.com) launched on 20 December 2015 as a blog and Q&A hub where Alibaba's own engineering teams published "Double 11" war stories next to outside bloggers (Juejin). It was later rebranded 阿里云开发者社区 on developer.aliyun.com; old yq URLs still resolve.

The strategic logic, in Alibaba's own words:

  • "Users taught us how to do cloud." Recruitment posts quote Dr Wang Jian ("是阿里云的用户教会我们怎么做云计算的", "it was Alibaba Cloud's users who taught us cloud computing"). MVP exists to "reflect more developers' voices onto our technical roadmap" (MVP 5th batch, Jul 2018).
  • From PM-led to co-built products. President Hu Xiaoming launched MVP together with the 聆听平台 feedback portal at the Shanghai Yunqi summit (10 June 2017). The pitch was that products would shift "from product-manager-led to co-construction with customers", and the MVP was billed as the first global MVP programme from a Chinese cloud vendor. The beta portal had drawn "several thousand suggestions" in two months (article 96137).
  • A funnel for developers. At the May 2021 developer conference Alibaba claimed "5 million community developers" and announced RMB 1.5 bn of resources over three years under 飞天加速计划 2.0 (Apsara Acceleration Plan 2.0): free compute for students, credits, certification and contest funds (Sohu repost; summit page).
  • Ownership. The community and the training centre sit under one head (孟晋宇) inside the marketing and public-affairs organization. Its 2021 InfoQ award went to VP 刘湘雯 (Liu Xiangwen) of 市场营销与公共服务部 (the Marketing & Public Services department) (Baidu Baike; InfoQ award article). In other words, this is a marketing asset, not a support organization.

What keeps people coming back

  1. A points economy. Check in daily (5 points), get a lottery draw for 5 check-ins in a week, collect small weekly points for watching a video, sharing, liking, following or bookmarking, 20 points for each answer staff "recommend" (up to 10 a day), 10 for a lab, and 300 for a selected product review. Points must be claimed within 7 days and expire on 1 April of the following year. They buy one item of each gift type a month, redeemed through Taobao (rules). The system is pure Taobao-style loyalty mechanics. Points had existed before, were dropped, and came back in April 2022 ("以前阿里云是有积分系统的…后来阿里云积分下线了", "Alibaba Cloud used to have a points system… then it was taken offline") (yangmaodang).
  2. Status ladders. Blogger tiers come with physical rewards: phone stand → 云小宝 plush → thermos and certificate, plus "exclusive interview / ebook / livestream" for experts. The top tier was originally "MVP STAR" (Sina 2021; 2023 tiers). On the international side, a 2019 reputation score ranks "Popular Bloggers" (Community 2.0).
  3. MVP as an annual ritual and a ladder. The career path is ACE → "future MVP" (article 696600). MVP perks: a certificate and swag, a closed-door summit at the Apsara Conference with "product night talks" with Alibaba engineers, speaking slots, HQ visits, and on higher tiers "up to USD 1500 in credits, free ACP" (Spring 2023 call; 2019 summit). Some got stranger perks: four MVPs joined Alibaba's "玄奘之路" Gobi desert trek in 2018 (article 625395).
  4. IRL belonging (ACE). Early chapter events mixed tech talks with badminton, city walks and photo-checkpoint team races. Beijing's first meetup "filled within 2 days" (Zhihu). The name itself is identity work: the founding members chose ACE, "every member is a builder of the cloud… the ace in the pack" (article 316788).
  5. Career hooks. ACE officers get "Alibaba job referral opportunities" and access to MVPs and Alibaba experts (ACE charter). Expert bloggers get book deals and Apsara tickets.
  6. Weak peer support. The Q&A board is mostly answered by points-seekers and staff. Asks show generic, AI-flavoured "recommended answers" (see Member voice). There is no evidence of a Stack Overflow-style culture of peer answers.

The content engine

  • Company-made, at industrial scale: team blogs from Alibaba engineering (100+ team blogs by 2017), slide packs from the Apsara Conference and developer conferences, 100–250 free ebooks a year, livestream "online summits" (300+ in 2017), training camps taught by Alibaba experts, 云起 hands-on labs, a skills map and tests (2017 review; Baidu Baike; conference downloads). The 2017 China Developer Survey (14,992 participants) was its own content product (2017 review).
  • Sponsored member content: themed essay contests are scored 2 (comments) : 3 (reads) : 5 (expert review), with prizes up to air fryers (article 786478; article 1143692). Product review campaigns pay "a 10-yuan voucher + 50 points, guaranteed" for an approved review (SAE review drive).
  • Paid writers abroad: Tech Share pays US$200 or 300 cash (or US$500 in credits) per approved article, US$150 for every fifth article, and referral bounties of US$5 cash + US$20 credits per new paying user, capped at US$1,650 (terms). Country spin-offs exist, such as IDR 2 million in Indonesia (TechShare Indonesia).
  • MVP-as-reviewer: when Model Studio launched in Singapore (2024), Alibaba invited MVPs in ~12 countries to publish review videos and posts in local languages (MVP reviews). This is a cheap, multilingual launch amplifier.

People, programmes and governance

  • Staff: a dedicated operations team (开发者社区运营) runs ACE, bloggers, points and contests. Moderation is pre-publication and opaque (see Member voice). Team size: no evidence found.
  • MVP criteria and terms: experts get no money ("不会得到…任何货币形式的报酬", "will not receive… any monetary compensation"). The award is annual, with quarterly applications at first (MVP intro). The 2017 Chinese FAQ copies Microsoft's MVP FAQ almost verbatim: the same six selection criteria with "微软" (Microsoft) swapped for "阿里云", a leftover reference to "ASDN", and an unfilled "XX元阿里云代金券" ("RMB XX" voucher) placeholder (compare Microsoft MVP blog, 2009). Today's global bar: in the past year, more than 5 tech blogs or a book, an offline talk, forum contributions, or more than 5 accepted suggestions on 聆听 (mvp.alibabacloud.com). 2018 added a "co-creation space" of tasks as an on-ramp to MVP (article 530790).
  • ACE governance: an explicit KPI regime. Presidents in tier-1 cities must self-organize at least 3 events a year (tier-2: 2; others: 1). They're scored every February on a scorecard; 80% of "meets expectations" keeps the role, and "excellent" earns a paid trip to a closed-door officer meeting. Officers who fall short get 3 months to improve, and those who step down become "honorary presidents" (ACE charter). From 2020 events ran under the brand "阿里云开发者 DevUP" (article 767307). The ACE group feed shows its last official event recap in January 2022 (Nanchang, online). After that it's unrelated articles (ACE group feed, via r.jina.ai).
  • Feedback loop: 聆听平台 suggestions earn credit on the feature list. MVPs file bugs and test new products (MVP mission). No public data on how many suggestions shipped after 2017.

Growth loops

  1. SEO on Baidu. Millions of indexed articles and Q&A pages capture long-tail queries. This works for traffic but has become a reputation cost (see below).
  2. Campus and credits. Student compute, the 飞天加速计划 (Apsara Acceleration Plan), training camps feeding certification exams (ACA/ACP/ACE certs), and Tianchi contests.
  3. Referral bounties built into content. Tech Share writers embed referral codes (terms). Third-party "freebie" sites post affiliate-coded links to the points mall (vpssw).
  4. Evangelists abroad. MVPs in India, Pakistan, Indonesia and Latin America run campus talks and Community Days. One Indian MVP describes "inspiring many to begin their cloud careers through Alibaba Cloud's certification programs" (Gavaskar S, Jun 2025).

Evidence of business impact

  • Hard evidence: none found. Alibaba's 20-F filings don't discuss the developer community (EDGAR full-text search for "developer community" + "Alibaba Cloud" returns only Tuya, Lion Group and Baidu). No retention, churn or attribution numbers have been published.
  • Company claims: 5M community developers (2021); "thousands of suggestions" in 聆听's first two months (2017); the 2021 InfoQ "Best Tech Community Operations" award; a 2024 top-30 China tech-brand influence ranking (Baidu Baike).
  • Indirect: May 2025 press releases about the autumn MVP batch read like self-placed promotion on NetEase/Sohu "self-media" accounts. One even claims MVP "cultivated thousands" of experts, which contradicts the official counter of 295 (Sohu; mvp.alibabacloud.com). The title evidently has marketing value to the people who hold it.

What they did right

  1. Recognition tied to product feedback. Launching MVP with a feedback portal, and making "accepted suggestions" an explicit MVP criterion, turned advocates into a QA channel (article 96137; mvp.alibabacloud.com).
  2. Clear, written rules for volunteer leaders. The ACE charter spells out duties by city tier, an annual review, perks, succession, and an honorary off-ramp. Few companies document chapter governance this precisely (ACE charter).
  3. Real staff-made education. Ebooks, camps, conference slides and engineering team blogs give members something useful even when peer activity is thin. That's the reason the site survives its UGC swings (Baidu Baike).
  4. Using MVPs as a multilingual launch network. The 2024 Model Studio review push produced local-language coverage in ~12 countries within weeks (MVP reviews).
  5. Member-chosen identity. "ACE" was named by the first members, and the early chapters mixed fun with tech (city walks, badminton), which filled events fast (Zhihu).

What they did wrong

  1. Rewarding volume, which bred farming and content-farm SEO. At its peak the points scheme paid for 3 articles a day. A public guide explains: "I keep several alt accounts… take articles from the web and rewrite them… one phone number can register three Alibaba Cloud accounts", earning toilet paper, speakers and lamps (vpssw). Consequence: low-quality pages and misleading search titles (see the Q&A 586706 quote below), followed by mass deletions (missing IDs) and a retrenchment in which post rates fell roughly 75% once per-article points ended (ID sampling above).
  2. Moving the goalposts on points. The system was killed and revived (2022), then 2021–22 points expired in Feb 2024 and all pre-2025 points expired on 1 Apr 2025, with "claim within 7 days or lose them" friction throughout (rules; 2024 notice). Consequence: members learn that the currency is disposable, and that's the moment loyalty schemes stop working.
  3. Opaque moderation. Rejected posts get no reason ("能给出具体的原因吗?", "can you give a specific reason?"), a complaint unanswered since 2018 (ask 132293). Internationally, a long-time writer couldn't find how to post (Raja_KT comment).
  4. Programmes launched and left to fade. ACE went from 47 cities (2019) to no official activity write-ups after Jan 2022. The international forum filled with banned-user spam and was redirected in 2025. The MVP counter fell from 419 (2019) to 295 (2026), and recent batches add only ~10 people (article 696600; ACE feed; forum thread; Wayback CDX).
  5. A borrowed programme. The MVP terms were copied from Microsoft, placeholders included. The template wasn't the problem: unedited copy signals a box-ticking launch, and the early "300 MVPs" target pushed toward quantity (MVP intro; article 96137).
  6. Marketing-owned, hence campaign-shaped. Each product launch brings a new contest, review bounty or sub-brand (Yunqi号, DevUP, 乘风者, 测评, 问答官). The community never developed a durable peer-help core of its own.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Launch the expert programme together with a feedback channel, and count accepted suggestions as a qualifying contribution. Advocacy and product improvement then reinforce each other.
  2. Write a charter for volunteer chapter leaders: event quotas scaled to city size, an annual review, perks, succession and an honorary exit. It makes chapters manageable at 40+ cities.
  3. Fund a staff education layer (free ebooks, camps, conference slides, engineering blogs) so the community is useful even when peer activity dips.
  4. Mobilize your recognized experts at launches. Local-language reviews from MVPs in a dozen countries cost far less than ads.
  5. Let early members name and shape their identity (ACE), and mix social activities with technical ones at IRL events.

Avoid these

  1. Don't pay per post, per like or per check-in. Volume rewards attract alt-account farmers, fill your site with rewritten content, damage your search reputation, and vanish the moment you stop paying.
  2. Don't devalue your currency. Points that expire, get reset or get withdrawn teach members that their effort is disposable.
  3. Don't launch sub-brands you won't sustain. ACE, an international forum and a global MVP drive all faded quietly. Retire programmes in public, or keep them staffed.

Scores, justified

  • Retention mechanics 3/5: a full kit of check-ins, points, tiers, an annual award and IRL chapters, but almost all of it is extrinsic and was repeatedly reset.
  • Content engine 4/5: industrial-scale staff content (100–250 ebooks a year, camps, livestreams, labs, conference slides) plus paid and contest-driven UGC. Quality is uneven.
  • Member ownership 2/5: ACE was member-named but run on staff KPIs. UGC follows rewards, and there's no member-led peer-help core.
  • Staff investment 4/5: a dedicated operations team, MVP summits, travel for officers, RMB 1.5 bn developer resource plan (2021).
  • Business impact evidence 1/5: only company claims; nothing in filings, no retention or attribution data.
  • Durability 3/5: the core site has survived 10+ years and two rebrands, but ACE, the international forum and the MVP's scale have all faded.
