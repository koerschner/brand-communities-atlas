How it pays off for the company

Usage expansion for a mature, declining-use condiment: members buy more bottles (8.3 to 9.4 a year vs a household average of 6; graduates 10.7 to 13.6). Fan posts supply insight for TV ads and in-store messaging (the 'Mayo Sauté' campaign), PR material, recipe co-development and a co-created deli product. Since 2025 it also feeds sign-ups to the kewpie ID first-party data and D2C stack (Hi! kewpie, Qummy).

TL;DR

  • What it is: Kewpie's members-only online fan community for its mayonnaise. It opened on 19 Aug 2014 for the brand's 90th anniversary, ran for 11 years on QON's Beach platform and reached about 140,000 registered members. On 1 Aug 2025 it was rebuilt on Asobica's coorum platform at mayofunclub.kewpie.co.jp (Kewpie 2014, Kewpie 2025).
  • Why people come back: a daily "what did you cook" conversation led by a named staff host ("Emi"), plus a school year. The Mayonnaise Academy (2018–2025) ran April-to-March cohorts of quiz lectures and cook-and-post practicals, ending in an exam. Passing made you a certified "Mayoshelju" (mayo concierge), with a magnet diploma, an alumni-only circle and invitations to events and co-creation. Many members re-enrolled year after year (Sankei 2022).
  • What the company feeds it: staff-posted talk themes and quizzes, seasonal recipe prompts, polls and "general elections" (sōsenkyo), points that buy entries into prize lotteries, annual anniversary goods co-designed with members, and events at its Mayo Terrace visitor centre. The company also feeds back what it learns: fan posts showed that cooking with mayo (frying in it, mixing it into eggs) is what turns users into fans, and Kewpie then built TV and in-store campaigns around "Mayo Sauté" (AdverTimes 2024).
  • Biggest mistake (or at least cost): the 2025 migration. Kewpie closed the Beach site, required a new kewpie ID sign-up and reset the points. Its own FY2025 stakeholder table shows 8,059 members, down from 140,265 a year earlier (Kewpie stakeholder dialogue). The registered base was probably padded with dormant prize-hunters, but most of the headline number did not make the move.
  • The one lesson: give a low-involvement product a curriculum and a credential. A yearly cohort with homework and an exam turned "people who like mayo" into self-identified experts. They bought about 1.8x the household average and recruited and welcomed newcomers for the brand.

What the community is

  • Official spaces.
    • Beach era (Aug 2014 to 31 Jul 2025): a member community at beach.jp/community/KEWPIE, on a platform run by QON Inc. (クオン). The site's rules name Kewpie as sponsor and QON as the system provider (guidelines). At launch it had a Talk Room for company-set themes and quizzes, member-created "membership circles", featured Mayoshelju recipes, an "Oishii photo" wall, and points spent on prize campaigns (2014 release). By 2023 it was organised into three circles: the Lounge (open chat), the Academy (annual school, from 2018) and Mayoshelju kitchen (graduates only, from 2020) (AdverTimes 2024).
    • coorum era (1 Aug 2025 to now): mayofunclub.kewpie.co.jp. Its sections are a notice board, Fun Content (campaigns and polls), Mayo School (the successor to the academy), Mayo Kitchen (seasonal vegetable threads), Ohanashi Hiroba (member posts) and Mayo Photo (site). Registration requires a kewpie ID (2025 release).
    • IRL: members-only events at Mayo Terrace (Chōfu, Tokyo; opened June 2014). Examples: a 2016 member tour, previews of new tour courses, a round-table with R&D staff, the 100th-anniversary "fun club Anniversary Party" on 15 Mar 2025, and a 16-member redesign round-table at Kewpie HQ on 24 Jun 2025 (Sankei 2020, Sankei 2022, Beach event report, 2025 release).
  • Who runs it: To Ad Kewpie (トウ・アドキユーピー), the Kewpie group's in-house advertising and brand-management company. Community manager Ayako Osawa described the role in 2023 (Mayo Story No.6). Posts are signed by the facilitator persona "進行役のえみ" (host Emi) from at least 2018 to 2026.
  • Unofficial spaces: no meaningful fan-run community found. The visible unofficial layer is members' own Instagram, X and blog posts (#マヨシェルジュ, #キユーピーマヨネーズアカデミー) and sweepstakes-winner posts. There is no relevant subreddit (r/KewpieBabies is about the dolls).
  • Sibling community: Kewpie also runs "Kewpie Community Ohanashi Dining" (2012, on LA VIDA, 20,834 members per the 2024 sustainability data), a vegetable and cooking community (2012 release).

Origin and strategy

Kewpie launched the club for the 90th anniversary of Kewpie Mayonnaise (first sold in 1925). At the time "mayonnaise-banare" (people turning away from mayo, largely over calories) was a business problem. The stated aim was to "correctly convey the original deliciousness and value of Kewpie Mayonnaise to the future." Executive Shinichiro Yamamoto gave three goals: (1) build bonds with loyal users, (2) work out the mechanism by which people become fans, and (3) project an ideal image of the "mayoler" (mayo lover) (AdverTimes 2024).

The internal champion was Yamamoto. He opened the club as president of To Ad Kewpie, later became a Kewpie board director, and spoke about it at Sendenkaigi's AdverTimes Days in Sep 2023 (QON Journal, AdverTimes 2024). The club sits in marketing, not in customer service or e-commerce.

The business logic is usage expansion in a saturated category. Most Japanese households already own Kewpie, so growth has to come from using it in more ways (frying, marinating, eggs, baking), which means more bottles. The community is a living lab for which new uses stick. From 2022 it also became a first-party data play. Kewpie launched kewpie ID, the Hi! kewpie member site and the Qummy D2C salad store (kewpie ID had 32,000 members in 2023). The 2025 rebuild made kewpie ID mandatory for the fan club (AdverTimes 2024, 2025 release). Asobica markets coorum as a "zero-party / honne (candid) data platform" (coorum release). Kewpie's 2025 Integrated Report says the club is used "not only for building fans but for understanding consumer needs and improving products and services" (Integrated Report 2025 p13-14, search snippet).

Trajectory:

  • Growth, 2014–2023. Membership rose in steps: +27k from Oct 2019 to Jun 2020 (COVID), then flat until mid-2021, then +42k to 2023. Steps like these usually follow campaigns. That is an inference; I found no campaign calendar to confirm it.
  • Plateau. Membership was flat at about 140k from mid-2023, but activity kept rising: about 114k posts a year in 2022–23 and an annualised 146k in the first seven months of 2024, from the counter deltas.
  • Reset in 2025. Membership fell to about 8k after the migration. The new site's top-level volume (about 12,300 posts and photos in 15 months, plus comments: a sample page showed 1 to 48 comments per post) suggests a smaller but still daily core. My rough estimate is a few hundred interactions a day; this is an estimate, not a company figure.

What keeps people coming back

  1. A school year with homework and a credential (the core mechanic). The Academy opened every April (cohorts 1 to 7, 2018–2025). It mixed quiz-style "lectures" with "practicals": staff set a mayo technique every two months, such as frying rice in mayo or folding it into eggs, and members cooked it and posted photo proof. Three or more practicals unlocked a December exam. Passing brought certification as a Mayoshelju, a nickname-engraved diploma magnet, gifts (for example a Kewpie-doll mayo stand, later T-shirts) and access to the graduates-only circle and events (Mayo Story No.6, Sankei 2022, goo blog, cohort 3). Members repeated it like a "life's work" and encouraged other candidates (Sankei 2022). X posts show the same people graduating in cohorts 5, 6 and 7 (@minyazuki 2023, 2025). The successor Mayo School (Oct 2025) keeps the lecture-plus-practical format with half-year mid-term and final exams. The bar is lower (one lecture and one practical per half), and passers are invited to a special event (Mayo School launch).
  2. Status you earn rather than buy. In 2014 the "Mayoshelju" were outside recipe bloggers recruited to post recipes; two of them "graduated" from the role in Feb 2018 (2014 release, Beach Feb 2018). From 2018 the title went to members who passed the academy instead. Members treat it as a real credential ("about as prestigious as a sommelier": @chizutandosu).
  3. A daily conversational ritual with a named host. "Emi" posts the day's or month's theme ("how did you use mayo today?", "your favourite mayo onigiri filling?"), seasonal vegetable threads and quizzes (Mayo Story No.6). Long-time member Akiko Nakagawa set herself a goal of one food photo a day and called the club "like my own place." After her children left home, posting gave her a reason to keep cooking well (Sankei 2020).
  4. Light gamification tied to prizes. On Beach, posts, comments and claps earned points that were spent on lottery entries for Kewpie goods (points guide). On coorum, points (100 for sign-up, 5 per daily login, 30 per post or photo, 15 per comment, 3 per like with caps) raise you through five ranks, Beginner to Expert at 15,000. Ranks reset every 1 August, and the Expert reward is merchandise members co-design: in 2026, an apron whose shape and motif were chosen by member votes (points page, Fun Content). Event badges (for example for Potato Salad Day, 10 Oct) produce a flood of "my badge arrived, thank you!" posts, which work as a recognition ritual in their own right (Hiroba, Nov 2026).
  5. Calendar hooks. Mayonnaise Day (1 Mar), Potato Salad Day (10 Oct), Vegetable Day (31 Aug), club anniversaries (goods made with members every year since year one), and the 2025 centenary (Mayo Story No.6, Beach 100th party).
  6. Ties to staff and the factory. Members meet R&D staff, preview Mayo Terrace courses and eat staff-created dishes. Nakagawa said a 2016 Mayo Terrace event was the turning point that ended her shyness about posting (Sankei 2020).
  7. Switching costs were mostly social: a nickname, a history of posts, rank and certification. Kewpie itself erased the post history and Mayoshelju-era status in 2025 (see below).

The content engine

  • Company-made: daily or monthly talk themes; quizzes on history, ingredients and the "oxygen-free" production method; academy and school curricula; "Mayonnaise Magic" technique messaging; seasonal vegetable know-how (Mayo Kitchen); polls and elections (Mayo Onigiri Diner vote 2022, Oshi-mayo dish election, Mayo snack No.1 election 2026); and diagnostic quizzes (PR TIMES 2022, Fun Content). The cadence on the new site is about 2 to 3 school posts a month (lecture, answer reveal, practical) and 1 to 4 Fun Content items a month (Mayo School).
  • Member-made: recipe photos are the main content. Kewpie curates them into a photo wall, features them, and co-develops recipes with Mayoshelju, published on Kewpie's MayoKitchen recipe site (Mayoshelju recipes). In 2022 Mayoshelju wrote the tagline for the first community-originated product, a limited deli potato salad made by group company Delia Foods (Sankei 2022). A 2026 Mayonnaise Day project published "regional mayo recipes" made with members (Fun Content). The terms allow Kewpie to edit and reuse posts in promotions (guidelines).
  • Inside the company: fan comments go into press releases, which earned media pickup, and a weekly digest of fan voices goes to employees, who reported that it made their work feel meaningful (AdverTimes 2024).

People, programs and governance

  • Team: run by To Ad Kewpie's advertising department. I found no team size. One named community manager (Osawa) says she tracks numeric targets but puts warmth toward each member first: "the community is alive" (Mayo Story No.6). The platform vendors (QON, then Asobica) provide the tooling and, on QON's side, case-study PR (QON case studies).
  • Ambassador tier: Mayoshelju (433 by Aug 2023). Their perks are the alumni circle, exclusive events, recipe co-creation and product input. There is no evidence of payment after 2018. Yamamoto calls them "our ambassadors and reliable partners… comrades who think alongside our employees" (Sankei 2022).
  • Rules: posts must stay on mayo and food. Long off-topic chains, including word games such as shiritori, are discouraged, and members are pointed to member-created circles for side conversations. Promotion, multiple accounts, personal information and disparaging other companies are banned. The operator may delete posts or remove members (guidelines).
  • Feedback loop: analysis of posts and surveys feeds each year's academy syllabus and TV ad strategy (Sankei 2022). A member round-table shaped the 2025 rebuild. Members asked for staff-recommended recipes, usage tips and more real-world events (2025 release).

Growth loops

  • Campaign sign-ups: big giveaways gated on joining and posting, for example 1,000 jars plus a staff recipe book at launch, 1,500 limited-design jars, and 100 aprons for the 2025 relaunch. They were promoted on PR TIMES, X (the relaunch tweet had 17k impressions) and LINE (2014 release, X). The effect is visible in sweepstakes-winner posts (@neko_maomao_a).
  • Earned media: PR built on fan facts and vote results, plus sponsored features in Sankei.
  • Member evangelism: Mayoshelju promote what they learned on their own social media, and some invite friends (Sankei 2022, Sankei 2020).
  • Weak spots: awareness stayed low. A 2025 reader of the relaunch news wrote "I didn't know this existed!" (@affiliate_blues). There is no SEO surface either: Beach threads sat behind a login-style circle structure and are now redirected away.

Evidence of business impact

  • Company survey data (correlational, self-selected):
    • FY2021: fan club members bought 9.4 bottles a year and Mayoshelju 13.6, against 6.0 for the average household in the government Family Income and Expenditure Survey, converted to 450 g bottles (about 1.6x and 2.3x) (Sankei 2022).
    • Sep 2023 figures: general public 6, fan club 8.3, Mayoshelju 10.7. Mayoshelju also use mayo in more ways (AdverTimes 2024).
    • Both gaps shrank between the two readings, around the 2022–23 price rises (450 g from ¥439 to ¥481 in Apr 2023: Kewpie). That is my observation; Kewpie hasn't commented on it.
    • None of this proves causation: heavy users self-select into the club.
  • Marketing insight (company claim, specific): the analysis found that frying with mayo ("Mayo Sauté") and mixing it into eggs are the "entry" uses that make people feel it is all-purpose, and that success experiences (family praise, fluffy eggs) deepen fandom. Kewpie says this gave it the confidence to lead TV ads, social media and in-store promotion with Mayo Sauté, and that it is a "very important discovery for marketing efficiency" (AdverTimes 2024, Sankei 2022, Mayo Story No.6).
  • Category context: household mayo volumes fell under repeated price rises from 2021 and began stabilising in 2024 as "use expansion" took hold (Nihon Shokuryo Shimbun 2025). The community is one input to that strategy, not a measured driver.
  • Not found: revenue attribution, retention cohorts, NPS, cost or ROI. The integrated reports mention the club qualitatively only.

What they did right

  1. A curriculum instead of a feed. The academy gave a low-involvement product something to study, with deadlines, proof-of-work posts and an exam. It produced 433 certified superfans in five years, and many re-enrolled (AdverTimes 2024).
  2. Status moved from hired influencers to earned members (2018). The title stopped being a paid blogger role and became something any member could earn, which gave the ambassador tier a place to grow.
  3. The community worked as a research instrument. Kewpie set "uncover the fan mechanism" as an explicit goal, found an actionable insight (cook with mayo) and spent national ad money on it.
  4. One consistent, warm host voice. "Emi" led for years, the manager watched people's life stages ("I got married", "I had a baby"), and the space felt safe. It retained older homemakers, a group many brand communities never reach (Mayo Story No.6, Sankei 2020).
  5. Real-world payoffs: factory visits, R&D round-tables, the centenary party, co-created goods and a co-created product. Members' own accounts tie IRL events to deeper engagement online.
  6. Fan voices sent inward to employees each week, which helps internal buy-in and protects the budget.
  7. Members asked before the redesign (a 16-person round-table), and co-design continued afterwards (apron votes).

What they did wrong

  1. The migration shed about 94% of members. Kewpie closed the Beach site on 31 Jul 2025 and redirected its old threads to a closure page. The new site required a new kewpie ID account and reset points and history. Membership went from 140,265 (FY2023) to 8,059 (FY2025) (stakeholder page, closure notice). Eleven years of recipe threads (774k+ posts) are gone from public view, Wayback aside. The new member count is far below even the old site's apparent daily-active core.
  2. The flagship program had a gap. Academy enrolment ended in spring 2025: a would-be student found "applications closed" in Apr 2025 (@KeijouAtsushi). Mayo School did not start until Oct 2025, so the centenary year had no school. The prestige "Mayoshelju" title doesn't appear on the new site, where students get the label "マヨ生徒" (Mayo student). The new bar (one lecture and one practical per half-year; 173 passers in term 1) is much lower, which may dilute the credential.
  3. The headline number was inflated by prize-driven sign-ups. Points bought lottery entries, and joining was the entry ticket to big giveaways. That brings in sweepstakes hunters who never return. The flat 140k with rising posts after 2023 suggests the active core was a small fraction. The yearly rank reset on coorum may also frustrate long-time members, though I found no complaints.
  4. Low awareness and no discoverability. It is a closed, login-gated site with no SEO footprint and no presence on Instagram or LINE beyond campaign blasts.
  5. The impact claims are unproven. Only self-reported purchase counts were published, with no control group, and the uplift shrank between 2021 and 2023.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Run a cohort "school" with proof-of-work. Short lessons, hands-on tasks members post as evidence, a yearly exam and a public credential. Make it repeatable each year so veterans come back as mentors.
  2. Let superfan status be earned. Replace hired creators with members who qualify, then give that tier exclusive access (staff, R&D, previews) and co-creation that ships, such as recipes, product copy and merchandise.
  3. Instrument the community for one product question. Kewpie asked which use cases create fans, found the "aha" behaviour (cooking with the product) and pushed it through mass marketing. Treat the community as a research panel with a hypothesis.
  4. One named, consistent host voice with a daily prompt. A light, answerable question every day ("how did you use it today?") is a better retention engine than broadcast content.
  5. Send member voices inward weekly. It builds employee pride and protects the program's budget.

Avoid these

  1. Don't migrate by reset. Moving platforms and forcing a new account system (for first-party data) wiped about 94% of registered members and 11 years of searchable content. Migrate accounts, history and status, or run both sites in parallel, and measure active-member retention rather than registrations.
  2. Don't let prize lotteries define membership. Giveaway-gated sign-ups inflate the count with people who never come back. Report actives and the cohort core.
  3. Don't let a flagship ritual lapse or quietly devalue its credential. Keep the calendar continuous, and keep the earned title (here, Mayoshelju) or migrate it with care.

Score justifications

  • Retention mechanics 4: a strong cohort-exam-credential loop plus daily prompts, points, badges and events. Not 5, because rank resets and prize-lottery points lean extrinsic.
  • Content engine 4: a steady staff-led curriculum and themes, with member recipes curated into brand channels. Limited formats: no video or live classes found.
  • Member ownership 3: members could create circles and co-create recipes and goods, but staff set the agenda and the rules keep talk on-brand.
  • Staff investment 4: a dedicated in-house agency team, a host persona, an executive champion, IRL events and two platform builds.
  • Business impact evidence 3: published purchase-uplift surveys and a concrete insight-to-advertising chain, but correlational, with no revenue or retention numbers.
  • Durability 3: 12 continuous years, but the 2025 rebuild reset membership to about 8k.

Open questions

  • How many of the 140k Beach members were active in a given month? The FY2025 figure of 8,059 sits only about four months after the relaunch; what is the 2026 count?
  • Did Mayoshelju status carry over in any form, and did the Mayoshelju kitchen alumni circle survive the move?
  • Why was Beach dropped (cost, QON platform strategy, or the need to require kewpie ID)? What is the conversion rate from fan club to kewpie ID?
  • What is the size of the operating team, and its budget?
  • Has Kewpie measured purchase uplift causally, for example with matched panel data, rather than through member self-reports?
