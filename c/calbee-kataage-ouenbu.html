TL;DR

  • What it is: Calbee started the Kataage Potato Cheer Club (堅あげポテト応援部, "Kataage Potato Cheer Club") in July 2016 so fans could help design new flavors of its thick-cut chip. An agency hosted it for 8 years. In June 2024 Calbee rebuilt it on Asobica's coorum platform inside its points app, the Calbee Ruby Program, and only verified buyers can join. Membership went from about 16,000 before the move to about 68,000 in September 2026 (16, 11).
  • Why people come back: a predictable yearly cycle. Members submit flavor ideas, vote on a shortlist, taste prototypes, draw package art, and vote on the design. The flavor then reaches every convenience store in Japan with the club's icon on the bag. Pre-launch sampling lotteries, points and ranks, and push notifications fill the time between launches.
  • What Calbee does to feed it: a brand-team owner who changes every few years, backed by an agency (Agile Media Network until 2024, Asobica since). It also runs national press releases for every co-created flavor, a kabuki-actor "club captain" (Matsumoto Kōshirō, since 2022), small fan meetings, photo contests, and a 10th-anniversary campaign in 2026.
  • Biggest mistake: for about 8 years the club sat on a web site that needed its own login and reached members by email. By Calbee's own account, sign-ups flattened and participation fell before the 2024 rebuild. Today the gate (scanning a folded empty bag in the app) is fiddly, and posting on the board has fallen to roughly a ninth of its 2024 launch level even as membership tripled.
  • The one lesson: give fans one real decision a year that ends up on a shelf, and stamp their mark on it. Ten years of results show a single, visible co-creation ritual can hold a community together far better than a busy feed.

What the community is

The Kataage Potato Cheer Club is Calbee's official fan community for 堅あげポテト (Kataage Potato), a kettle-style thick-cut potato chip launched in Hokkaido in 1993, sold nationwide since 2005, and one of six Calbee brands with more than ¥10 billion in sales (16). The club's framing is a school club (部活, bukatsu): members are 部員 ("club members"), there is a 部長 ("club captain"), and the founding "member's creed" (部員の心得) asks people to say proudly that they love Kataage, to give honest requests and criticism because they love it, and to spread the word in their own words on blogs and social media (19).

Spaces, official:

  • 2016–2024: agency-hosted web site at matome.agilemedia.jp/kataage-ouenbu, run by Agile Media Network (AMN, renamed CRAVIA). It carried the creed, project write-ups ("the road to development"), surveys, monitor sign-ups, a "Kataage love level", and hashtag campaigns on Twitter (20). Calbee relaunched the site on July 13, 2020 (3).
  • June 2024 onward: coorum community at kataage-ouenbu.calbee.jp, reached by single sign-on from the Calbee Ruby Program app. It has five sections: announcements; a product archive; the "Launch Hype Squad" (発売商品盛り上げ隊, pre-launch sampling); the "Kataage Potato Lab" (堅あげポテト研究所, co-creation); and boards for talk and photos (22). Asobica runs day-to-day operations: the member contact address is an @asobica.co.jp mailbox (39).
  • Offline: "real club activity" tastings at Calbee's head office (20 members, October 2016) (2), fan meetings with factory tours in 2023 and November 2024 (16, 7), and a fan meeting for about 30 people at the new Setouchi Hiroshima plant in October 2026 (33).

Unofficial spaces: no evidence found of a fan-run forum, subreddit or Discord. Fan talk outside the club lives on personal blogs (for example sweepstakes bloggers writing up sampling wins, 41) and on X.

Status: active and celebrating its 10th anniversary. Calbee ran six anniversary projects in 2026, including a commemorative co-created flavor launched November 2, 2026 (11, 14).

Origin and strategy

The trigger was a flop. In 2015–16 Kataage expanded into "for women" flavors to win back 20–30-year-old female buyers. The second of these, Kuromitsu Kinako (brown-sugar syrup and roasted soy flour, March 2016), drew loyal fans' anger ("this isn't Kataage") and sold poorly. Calbee's launch press release for the club unusually included an apology: the brand manager said the team "deeply regret[s] releasing a product that did not meet many customers' wishes" (1). Founder Aya Kataoka later said that breaking fans' trust in the brand scared her more than the poor sales (13).

The business problems. (a) Kataoka and one manager had to launch more than ten new flavors a year, and she wanted "customers' voices on our side" (13). (b) As the number of products (SKUs) grew, sales per product sagged. The club's goal was to add depth (repeat purchase and product strength) rather than breadth (17, 16, 21). (c) There was an internal precedent: Calbee's Jagarico fan site "じゃがり校" ("Jagari School") had already co-created products that sold well, and earlier Kataage products shaped by survey feedback had beaten ordinary limited flavors (17).

A failed first attempt. In 2015 Kataoka tried a co-creation project that recruited mostly women who were not Kataage fans, and it did not work. She restarted with a space only for real fans (13). The name was chosen on purpose. "堅あげポテ党" ("Kataage Potato Party") was rejected because it implied factions; 応援部 ("cheer club") was meant to put Calbee staff and customers on one team (13).

The 2024 pivot to buyers only. After 8 years, Calbee cited outside factors (changes to the X/Twitter API) and internal ones (flat registrations, falling participation) and moved the club into the Ruby Program. That app, launched in September 2020, gives points for scanning folded empty snack bags and had about 1.4 million members by 2026 (16, 30). Only previous members or people who had scanned a qualifying bag could join. ZDNET describes users who scan a Kataage bag's barcode and serial being invited in, so the community is made up of actual buyers (18, 15). Calbee also gained purchase data for members. Moving a single brand's community into a company-wide app caused internal debate. It was settled as a "test" that also served the Ruby Program's own growth goals, and Calbee's Future Lab team joined for push notifications and data extraction (16).

Champions. Aya Kataoka (founder, 2016). Since then a series of brand-team owners: Ryota Fukuhara (from 2019), Miki Katakura (around 2022–23), Yu Hozumi (2024–25), Suzuki and Yamamoto (coorum era), and brand manager Takahiro Obata (2026) (13, 17, 21, 16).

Posting trend (my estimate, from paging the public talk board at 30 posts per page and reading timestamps): about 1,100 posts in the first 10 weeks after the move (June 30 to mid-September 2024, about 440 a month); about 120 a month from September 2024 to July 2025; about 64 a month from July 2025 to February 2026; and about 50 a month since February 2026. The photo board shows the same curve: about 1,435 photos by September 2025 and about 300 in the 14 months since. Recent talk posts collect about 14–19 likes each (24).

Trajectory: membership grew slowly but steadily for 8 years, roughly tripled after the move into the app, and keeps growing. Open-ended social activity peaked at the 2024 relaunch and has fallen by about 85–90% since. Structured participation (ideas, votes, sampling entries) is what Calbee reports, and it remains in the hundreds to low thousands per call.

What keeps people coming back

  1. The annual co-creation ritual. Since 2018 the club has launched one co-created flavor a year. The order is: theme → idea call → staff shortlist → member run-off vote → prototype tasting (online or at a fan meeting) → member-drawn package illustrations → package vote → national launch (7, 16). Each cycle takes about 1.5 years, so projects overlap and something is always happening. Results so far: ゆず塩レモン (yuzu-salt-lemon, 2018), 海老しお (shrimp-salt, 2019), 梅かつお (ume-katsuo, 2020), 炙り帆立 (seared scallop, 2021), 青じそ明太子 (shiso-mentaiko, 2022), 究極のだし (ultimate dashi, 2023, with the bonito-flake maker Ninben), 柚子香る鯛だし (yuzu sea-bream dashi, 2024), plus fan-idea flavors in 2025 and 2026 and the 10th-anniversary 贅沢海鮮だし (luxury seafood dashi, 2026) (7, 8, 9, 11). Obata calls "the density of co-creation" the main reason the club has lasted ten years (13).
  2. Seeing your work on a shelf. Co-created bags carry a "Fan's #1 flavor" banner (2021) and, since 2025, the club's icon on the front, with the development story on the back (4, 8, 9). Members' sampling comments go onto in-store promotional material (17). Calbee says fans ask every year for co-created flavors to become permanent or come back, and calls this a sign that members feel the products are their own (16).
  3. Pre-launch sampling lotteries. The "Launch Hype Squad" mails unreleased products to lottery winners, who share them with family and fill in a survey (16, 22). On the board, members cheer each other on during draw weeks (26).
  4. Points and ranks. Members earn 100 points for joining, 5 for a daily login, 1–3 for likes, comments and posts (each capped at 3 a day), and 20 for a "best answer". Ten ranks run from Beginner Lv.1 at 0 points to "Kataage Legend" at 50,000 (23). Calbee has said rewards for points are "planned" (22). Separately, the Ruby Program's own currency, "rubies", pays for entry to fan meetings (500 rubies) and other prize draws (31).
  5. Push notifications instead of email. Moving into the app replaced the email newsletter with push notifications that open a survey or vote in one tap. Calbee credits this for 4x the idea submissions and an "action rate" about 7x that of similar-sized communities (16, 18).
  6. Belonging and the club metaphor. The roles (member, captain), the creed, and the cheer-club framing produce what staff describe as a club of teammates. Returning attendees greet each other at events, and some wear handmade chip accessories (13).
  7. Contact with the people who make it. Fan meetings include factory tours and staff conversations. The 2024 project brought in the factory team that actually makes Kataage (7). In a 2024 roundtable, one member said the staff's own love of Kataage was part of what kept them engaged (29).
  8. One-off engagement stunts. A bag-folding "general election" in the Ruby Program, where flavors competed on the number of folded bags scanned, drew about 11,000 people and 28,000 scans in a month with no prizes. About 30% of club members took part, one member logged 116 scans, and about 400 members left comments for their favorite flavor (16).

The content engine

  • Project content is the main content. Every co-creation step is posted in the club (the theme, the shortlist, results, prototype reports, the design vote), and each finished flavor gets a "road to development" write-up (20, 22). Cadence: one major project a year plus sampling rounds.
  • Exclusive news. Collaboration products sold only through certain retailers are announced in the club but not on Calbee's public social accounts (16).
  • Corporate media. National press releases for every co-created flavor are picked up by Nikkei, Sankei, Mainichi and Yahoo! News (9, 11). Calbee's note blog "THE CALBEE" has run long features (a 2024 member roundtable and a 2026 10-year retrospective) (29, 13). The 10th-anniversary site carries anniversary videos and YouTube clips introducing new flavors through fans' comments (14, 11).
  • Celebrity layer. The kabuki actor Matsumoto Kōshirō (the 10th of that stage name) has been Kataage's image character since 2020 and its "club captain" since 2022. He appears at launch events and wrote the tagline for the 2023 dashi flavor (32, 6).
  • Member-made content. Members contribute the flavor ideas and the package illustrations that designers build on, plus a photo board of eating scenes and arrangement recipes (7, 14). Photo contests run with the 東京カメラ部 (Tokyo Camera Club) photography community (autumn 2023, summer 2024) (34). In 2026 members also designed a merchandise item, a sticker (11).
  • What's missing: there is no educational or how-to layer (the product is a snack), no regular live show or podcast, and no member-led programming.

People, programs and governance

  • Staffing: one person on the brand team owns strategy and content plans, and an outside operator produces and moderates. That operator was AMN in 2016–2024 and has been Asobica since 2024. Since the move into the app, Calbee's Future Lab team handles push notifications and purchase-data pulls (16). No headcount was found beyond this.
  • Turnover with continuity: the owner has changed at least five times in ten years. Kataoka credits the club's survival to every successive owner "taking fans' energy seriously" (13).
  • Ambassadors: there are sampling-ambassador drives for the core 焼きのり (roasted seaweed) flavor (2024 and August 2026), and winners post that they will "spread the word" (14, 28). No tiered superuser or MVP program was found. Ranks are cosmetic.
  • Governance of co-creation: fans propose ideas, staff shortlist them (on fit with the theme, the brand's Japanese "和" image and feasibility), and fans vote. In 2024 the factory team was added to the shortlist step (7, 16). Fukuhara said Calbee also asks members to help sell the product, by sampling it and writing comments for in-store displays (17).
  • Feedback into the product: besides the co-created flavors, members ran a "new flavor renewal" survey of existing buyers, so changes to core flavors keep loyal fans (16). A member reports asking for small bags and seeing them made (29). Kataoka says the club broke unwritten internal rules (no more than two ingredients in a flavor name, no katakana) and that Calbee now plans a separate "co-creation line" instead of treating these as one-off novelty flavors (13).

Growth loops

  1. The points app as a funnel. With 1.4 million Ruby Program members, anyone who scans a Kataage bag can be invited in. This is the main reason membership grew from 16,000 to 68,000 in about two years (30, 15, 16).
  2. The package as advertising. The club icon and development story on co-created bags reach every convenience store, and Calbee says co-created flavors bring in new buyers (9, 42).
  3. Press and the celebrity. Every launch gets a national press release, and the captain's launch events earn entertainment coverage (32).
  4. Sampling spreads by word of mouth. Winners share the samples with family and friends and post about them (15, 41).
  5. Early social recruitment. In 2016 Calbee recruited through its then-new Instagram and Twitter accounts. Hashtag campaigns on X (for example #ゆず塩レモン応援部 in 2018) lost force after the API changes (13, 2, 16).
  6. Stated next step: Obata wants members to become the brand's spokespeople and "bring in people around them" (13).

Evidence of business impact

All of the evidence comes from Calbee or its vendors. No public numbers on unit sales, revenue or retention exist.

  • Co-created flavors perform better (company claim). The 2021 炙り帆立 (seared scallop) flavor's unit sales in week 13 beat the average for limited-time flavors (15). All 7 co-created products have "good results" and keep selling well over time, which Calbee reads as repeat purchase (16). Inside Calbee the co-created flavors are now trusted as a "can't-miss" line (13).
  • Selling to retailers. Obata, who was in sales at the time, says the "made with customers" story landed with retail buyers (13).
  • Engagement after the move (company and vendor claims). Membership rose 2.4x, idea submissions 4x, and the action rate is about 7x that of comparable communities (16). The ZDNET figure (33% of June's active members still active in September 2024) is also self-reported (15).
  • Spread inside Calbee. Calbee copied the setup (coorum inside the Ruby Program) for Jagarico's "じゃがりこじま" ("Jagarico Island") in September 2026, and Asobica says the Kataage work "confirmed that turning fan voices into products leads to business results" (35).
  • Measurement gap. Hozumi names measuring brand loyalty as an open problem: Calbee wants to link Ruby Program purchase histories to loyalty but had not done so as of 2025 (16).
  • Score (2/5): the claims are consistent and specific, but there is no third-party or financial evidence.

What they did right

  1. Started from a real failure and said so publicly. The 2016 launch release apologized for Kuromitsu Kinako. That framed the club as accountability to fans rather than marketing (1).
  2. Recruited only real fans. After the 2015 attempt with non-fans failed, the club was built for existing lovers of the product. In 2024 it went further and required proof of purchase. Calbee argues that anonymous social posts can't tell it who actually buys (13, 16).
  3. Kept one ritual going for nine straight years. One flavor a year from 2018 to 2026, through a potato shortage (the 2018 launch slipped a year after typhoons hit Hokkaido; members replied "We'll wait, hang in there!"), COVID (tastings moved online) and a platform migration (2, 4).
  4. Gave members real decisions and credit on the bag. The flavor and package are decided by member votes and built from member illustrations, and the bag carries the club's icon (7, 9).
  5. Fixed the plumbing when it stalled. Moving from email and a separate web login to push notifications and single sign-on inside an app members already used multiplied participation, by Calbee's figures (16).
  6. Kept events small and in person. Fan meetings of 20–35 people are a deliberate choice, so each fan talks more with staff (30).
  7. Rewarded long-time members with a revival. In 2026 members voted the very first co-created flavor, ゆず塩レモン (yuzu-salt-lemon), the one they most wanted back, and Calbee re-released it (12).

What they did wrong

  1. Eight years of stagnation on rented, high-friction infrastructure. On the agency site, joining meant a separate login, reach depended on email and Twitter, registrations went flat and participation fell. Calbee itself lists these as the reasons for the 2024 rebuild (16). The club's activity depended on Twitter, so the X API changes hurt it. Consequence: the club stayed at roughly 15,000 members for about three years (2021–2024).
  2. A clumsy gate. Joining (and earning points) depends on photographing a folded empty bag. App Store reviews of the Ruby Program app (4.46 average from about 17,500 ratings) are full of scan failures ("情報解析エラー", "information analysis error"), forced updates, and no way to move an account to a new phone (31). These reviews are about the whole app, not only the club, but the app is the club's front door.
  3. Membership grows while activity shrinks. Talk-board posting has fallen from about 440 a month at the relaunch to about 50 a month while membership roughly tripled (my estimate from the public board, above). Obata concedes that as the club grows Calbee must re-examine "who the real fans are and how everyone can feel comfortable" (13). Calbee's press releases report total members, not active ones.
  4. Too much depends on scarce lotteries. Most perks (samples, meetings, ambassador slots) are lottery-based, and members post about losing every draw (26, 31). Rewards for club points were still described as "planned" (22).
  5. Fan creations disappear from shelves. Co-created flavors are limited-time (about 3–5 months), while fans ask every year for them to become permanent or come back. As of 2026, the first flavor (ゆず塩レモン, yuzu-salt-lemon) is the only one documented as brought back (2024, 2026) (16, 12).
  6. In-person events are hard to reach. Members watching TV coverage of a fan meeting said you can only join if you live nearby, and that they would feel intimidated without strong opinions (27).
  7. Lost history (unverified). The new site's product archive starts from the June 2024 renewal, and its guide even dates the club's founding to 2017 instead of 2016 (22). No evidence was found that the old site's posts were carried over.

Lessons for building a product community

Copy these

  1. Run one flagship co-creation cycle a year with a public, physical outcome, and show members' credit on the product itself (the icon on the bag, the story on the back).
  2. Let members make real decisions inside clear limits. Staff shortlist for feasibility and brand fit, then members vote. It keeps quality up and makes members' input count.
  3. Put the community inside the app your customers already open, with single sign-on and one-tap push notifications to each vote or survey. Calbee's 4x idea submissions came from removing friction, not from new content.
  4. Use verified customers as your panel. Tie participation to proof of purchase (or real usage) so feedback comes from buyers and can be linked to behavior.
  5. Keep the identity small and warm: a club metaphor, a creed that invites honest criticism, small in-person meetings where staff talk with members, and a willingness to apologize in public.

Avoid these

  1. Don't build your community on an agency's site and email list for years. If people need a separate login and reach depends on another company's API (X), growth stalls. Calbee lost about 8 years to this.
  2. Don't let headline member counts hide falling activity. Track posts per active member and cohort retention. A fast-growing member count from an app funnel can hide a dying conversation.
  3. Don't make the entry gate fragile or make rewards mostly lotteries. Every failed scan or lost draw is a churn moment. Give members guaranteed, earnable rewards alongside the chance-based ones.
